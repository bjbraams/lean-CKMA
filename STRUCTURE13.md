## Spectral structure and variational comparison

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS13.md#spectral-structure-and-variational-comparison).

### Mathlib

**Spectral theorem for Hermitian matrices.** A Hermitian matrix over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\) has real eigenvalues, listed in decreasing order by `Matrix.IsHermitian.eigenvalues₀`, and is diagonalized by the unitary matrix of an orthonormal eigenbasis (`Matrix.IsHermitian.spectral_theorem`); its determinant and trace are the product and sum of the eigenvalues, and its rank is the number of nonzero eigenvalues (`Analysis/Matrix/Spectrum.lean`). A Hermitian matrix is positive semidefinite (positive definite) exactly when its eigenvalues are nonnegative (positive) (`Matrix.IsHermitian.posSemidef_iff_eigenvalues_nonneg`, `Matrix.IsHermitian.posDef_iff_eigenvalues_pos`, `Analysis/Matrix/PosDef.lean`). The continuous functional calculus of a Hermitian matrix is realized as \(Uf(\Lambda)U^*\) (`Matrix.IsHermitian.cfc`, `Analysis/Matrix/HermitianFunctionalCalculus.lean`). For symmetric endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional inner product space the eigenvalues are listed in decreasing order (`LinearMap.IsSymmetric.eigenvalues_antitone`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Spectrum.lean`).

**Eigenvalue localization and nonnegative matrices.** Gershgorin's circle theorem: every eigenvalue of a square matrix over a normed field lies in one of the discs centred at a diagonal entry with radius the sum of the norms of the other entries in its row (`Matrix.eigenvalue_mem_ball`). A strictly diagonally dominant matrix, by rows or by columns, has nonzero determinant (`LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Gershgorin.lean`). The counting statement for disjoint unions of discs is not proved.

The Rayleigh quotient of a self-adjoint operator is \(\langle Tx,x\rangle/\|x\|^2\). On a complete space, a point at which it attains its maximum or minimum on a sphere is an eigenvector, and the supremum or infimum is the corresponding eigenvalue; in finite dimension the supremum and infimum are eigenvalues (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Rayleigh.lean`).

A nonnegative square matrix is irreducible when its positivity graph is strongly connected, equivalently when every entry of some positive power is positive, and primitive when a single power is entrywise positive (`LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Irreducible/Defs.lean`). These are the definitions of Perron–Frobenius theory; the Perron–Frobenius theorem itself is not proved.

### TauCeti

**Normal matrices.** A normal operator on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors (`LinearMap.exists_orthonormalBasis_apply_eq_smul_of_isStarNormal`, [normal operators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/NormalOperator.lean)), and a normal complex matrix is unitarily diagonalizable (`Matrix.exists_mem_unitaryGroup_star_mul_mul_eq_diagonal`, [normal matrices](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Matrix/Normal.lean)). For a Hermitian matrix and a set \(\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R\), the functional calculus of the indicator of \(\Omega\) is the orthogonal projection onto the sum of the eigenspaces at eigenvalues in \(\Omega\), and \(A\mapsto f(A)\) is continuous for continuous \(f\) ([spectral projectors](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Matrix/FunctionalCalculus.lean)).

**Singular value decomposition and polar decomposition.** For a linear map \(A\) between finite-dimensional inner product spaces over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\), the right singular vectors are an ordered orthonormal eigenbasis of \(A^*A\), the left singular vectors are \(u_i=\sigma_i^{-1}Av_i\), \(A\) and \(A^*\) have the same singular values, and \(A=\sum_i\sigma_i\,u_i\otimes v_i\) (`LinearMap.eq_sum_singularValues_smul_rankOne`, `LinearMap.exists_orthonormal_eq_sum_singularValues_smul_rankOne`, [singular system](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/SingularValues.lean)). This is the singular value decomposition in rank-one form. The polar decomposition \(T=U|T|\) is proved for bounded operators between Hilbert spaces, with \(|T|=\sqrt{T^*T}\) and \(U\) the canonical partial isometry with initial space \((\ker T)^\perp\) and final space the closure of the range; \(U\) is the unique bounded operator with \(U|T|=T\) vanishing on \(\ker T\) (`ContinuousLinearMap.polarFactor_comp_modulus`, `ContinuousLinearMap.eq_polarFactor_iff`, [bounded polar decomposition](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Polar/Bounded.lean), [modulus](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Modulus.lean), [partial isometries](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/PartialIsometry.lean)). The finite-dimensional rectangular version is `LinearMap.polarFactor` ([finite polar factor](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Polar/Finite.lean)); between spaces of equal dimension the polar factor can be taken to be a linear isometric equivalence (`LinearMap.exists_linearIsometryEquiv_comp_operatorAbs_eq`, [unitary polar decomposition](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Polar/Unitary.lean)), and for injective maps the isometric factor is unique ([injective maps](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Polar/Isometry.lean)).

**Variational eigenvalue theory.** For a symmetric endomorphism \(T\) of a finite-dimensional inner product space over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\), with eigenvalues \(\lambda_0\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_{n-1}\), the Courant–Fischer theorem states that \(\lambda_k\) is the supremum, over subspaces of dimension \(k+1\), of the infimum of \(\operatorname{re}\langle Tx,x\rangle\) over their unit vectors (`LinearMap.IsSymmetric.eigenvalues_eq_iSup_iInf_re_inner`); the min–max form follows by passing to \(-T\). Consequences are monotonicity of the sorted eigenvalues under quadratic-form domination, hence in the Loewner order, and Weyl's perturbation bound \(|\lambda_k(T)-\lambda_k(S)|\le\|T-S\|\) (`LinearMap.IsSymmetric.eigenvalue_mono`, `LinearMap.IsSymmetric.abs_eigenvalue_sub_eigenvalue_le_norm`; [Courant–Fischer](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/CourantFischer.lean)). The index-shifted Weyl inequalities and Cauchy interlacing are not stated. Ky Fan's trace inequality bounds \(\sum_{i<k}\operatorname{re}\langle Tw_i,w_i\rangle\), over orthonormal families of \(k\) vectors, by the sum of the \(k\) largest eigenvalues (`LinearMap.IsSymmetric.sum_re_inner_apply_self_le_sum_eigenvalues`, [Ky Fan](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/KyFan.lean)).

**Schur–Horn and Hoffman–Wielandt.** The forward Schur–Horn theorem: the diagonal of a symmetric operator in any orthonormal basis is the image of its spectrum under a doubly stochastic matrix, hence lies in the convex hull of the rearrangements of the spectrum, and \(\sum_k\varphi(d_k)\le\sum_i\varphi(\lambda_i)\) for convex \(\varphi\) (`LinearMap.IsSymmetric.re_inner_apply_self_mem_convexHull_eigenvalues_comp_perm`, [Schur–Horn](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/SchurHorn.lean)). The converse (Horn) direction and Lidskii's theorem are not proved. For symmetric \(T\) and \(S\) with eigenvalues \(\lambda_i\) and \(\mu_i\) in decreasing order, the Hoffman–Wielandt inequality \(\sum_i(\lambda_i-\mu_i)^2\le\sum_k\|(T-S)e_k\|^2\) holds for every orthonormal basis \((e_k)\) (`LinearMap.IsSymmetric.sum_sq_eigenvalues_sub_le_sum_sq_norm_apply`, [Hoffman–Wielandt](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/HoffmanWielandt.lean)); the normal case is not proved. Davis's matching lower bound for eigenvalue change under a perturbation with small diagonal part, when the eigenvalues of \(S\) are \(\gamma\)-separated, is also proved (`LinearMap.IsSymmetric.le_sum_sq_eigenvalues_sub`, [Davis](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/DavisEigenvalueChange.lean)).

**Supporting material.** Sylvester's law of inertia is proved for Hermitian matrices over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), through the signature (`Matrix.IsHermitian.signature_congr`, [signature](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Matrix/HermitianSignature/Basic.lean)). For Perron–Frobenius theory, TauCeti proves Birkhoff's contraction of Hilbert's projective metric by strictly positive matrices ([Birkhoff contraction](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Data/Matrix/BirkhoffContraction.lean)), used for Sinkhorn scaling, but not the existence of a Perron eigenvector.

## Singular values, norms, and determinant inequalities

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS13.md#singular-values-norms-and-determinant-inequalities).

### Mathlib

**Singular values.** For a linear map \(T\) between finite-dimensional inner product spaces over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), the singular values are the square roots of the eigenvalues of \(T^*T\), arranged in decreasing order and repeated by multiplicity, then extended by zeros. The sequence is indexed by \(\mathbb{N}\), starting at \(0\), and only finitely many terms are nonzero. They are nonnegative and antitone. The support is exactly the initial segment of length \(\operatorname{rank} T\): the \(n\)th singular value is positive if and only if \(n<\operatorname{rank} T\), and the singular-value sequence vanishes if and only if \(T\) does. The map is injective if and only if every singular value before the dimension of the domain is positive (`LinearMap.singularValues`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/SingularValues.lean`).

**Determinants.** A positive-semidefinite matrix has nonnegative determinant, and the determinant of its square root is the square root of its determinant (`Analysis/Matrix/PosDef.lean`, `Matrix.det_sqrt` in `Analysis/Matrix/Order.lean`). Hadamard's, Fischer's and Minkowski's determinant inequalities are not in Mathlib.

### TauCeti

**Ky Fan sums and Fan dominance.** Singular-value inequalities for linear maps \(A\) between finite-dimensional inner product spaces are developed through Ky Fan sums \(\sum_{i<k}\sigma_i(A)\). Ky Fan's maximum principle bounds \(|\sum_i\langle u_i,Av_i\rangle|\) by the \(k\)th Ky Fan sum for orthonormal families of \(k\) vectors, with equality on the leading singular pairs; it gives the Ky Fan triangle inequality, i.e. the weak majorization \(\sigma(A+B)\prec_w\sigma(A)+\sigma(B)\) (`LinearMap.isWeaklyMajorizedBy_singularValues_add`), and \(\sum_i\sigma_i(A)=\max_Q|\operatorname{tr}(AQ)|\) over unitaries \(Q\) ([Ky Fan](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/KyFan.lean)). Unitarily invariant seminorms on rectangular maps are defined, and Fan dominance is proved: \(A\) lies in the convex hull of the two-sided unitary orbit of \(C\) exactly when every Ky Fan sum of \(A\) is at most that of \(C\), and then \(N(A)\le N(C)\) for every unitarily invariant seminorm \(N\) (`LinearMap.mem_twoSidedUnitaryOrbitHull_iff_kyFanSum_le`, `TauCeti.UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm.map_le_of_kyFanSum_le`); the Ky Fan sums are themselves unitarily invariant seminorms, which gives the converse. The ideal inequalities \(N(CA)\le\|C\|N(A)\) and \(N(AC)\le\|C\|N(A)\) follow ([Fan dominance](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/FanDominance.lean), [unitarily invariant seminorms](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm/Basic.lean), [Ky Fan norms](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm/KyFan.lean)). Every unitarily invariant seminorm is a symmetric gauge of the singular values, and conversely (von Neumann–Mirsky gauge representation, `TauCeti.UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm.map_eq_gauge_singularValues`, [gauge](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm/Gauge.lean)).

**Schatten norms.** The Schatten \(p\)-norms, \(1\le p\le\infty\), are constructed as the unitarily invariant norms attached to the \(\ell^p\) symmetric gauge, with the triangle inequality, domination of the operator norm, adjoint invariance, and the ideal inequalities (`TauCeti.UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm.schatten`, [Schatten](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm/Schatten.lean)). The Schatten Hölder inequality and the duality of Schatten norms under the trace pairing are not proved.

**Trace inequalities for singular values.** For symmetric \(T\) and \(S\) with eigenvalues \(\lambda_i\) and \(\mu_i\) in decreasing order, the von Neumann trace inequality \(\operatorname{re}\operatorname{tr}(TS)\le\sum_i\lambda_i\mu_i\) holds (`LinearMap.IsSymmetric.re_trace_comp_le_sum_eigenvalues_mul_eigenvalues`, [Hoffman–Wielandt](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/HoffmanWielandt.lean)), as does \(|\operatorname{tr}(AQ)|\le\sum_i\sigma_i(A)\) for unitary \(Q\); the form \(|\operatorname{tr}(A^*B)|\le\sum_is_i(A)s_i(B)\) for arbitrary matrices is not stated.

**Approximation numbers and Eckart–Young.** Between finite-dimensional inner product spaces the approximation number \(a_n(T)=\inf\{\|T-R\|:\operatorname{rank}R\le n\}\) equals the \(n\)th singular value (`ContinuousLinearMap.singularValue_eq_linearMap_singularValues`, [Eckart–Young](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/EckartYoung.lean)), so \(\|A\|=\sigma_0(A)\) and \(|\sigma_n(A)-\sigma_n(B)|\le\|A-B\|\) (`LinearMap.abs_singularValues_sub_singularValues_le`). Composing with the projection onto the span of the first \(n\) right singular vectors, i.e. truncating the singular value decomposition, gives a best rank-at-most-\(n\) approximation in operator norm with error \(\sigma_n\) ([attainment](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/Attainment.lean)). The optimality of truncation in other unitarily invariant norms, including the Frobenius error formula, is not stated. The mixed-index inequalities \(a_{m+n}(S+T)\le a_m(S)+a_n(T)\) and \(a_{m+n}(ST)\le a_m(S)a_n(T)\) (`ContinuousLinearMap.singularValue_comp_add_le_mul`, [approximation numbers](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/Basic.lean)) give, through Eckart–Young, \(s_{i+j-1}(AB)\le s_i(A)s_j(B)\) in the one-based notation of the synopsis; the product form \(\prod_{i\le k}s_i(AB)\le\prod_{i\le k}s_i(A)s_i(B)\) is not proved. If \(\|Cy\|\le c\|y\|\) for all \(y\), then \(\sigma_i(CA)\le c\,\sigma_i(A)\) (`LinearMap.singularValues_comp_le`, [singular values](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/SingularValues.lean)).

**Determinants.** Minkowski's determinant inequality \(\det A^{1/n}+\det B^{1/n}\le\det(A+B)^{1/n}\) for real positive-semidefinite \(n\times n\) matrices, \(n\ge 1\), and the concavity of \(A\mapsto\det A^{1/n}\) on that cone are proved from the superadditivity of the geometric mean, with the same statements for positive operators on a finite-dimensional real inner product space (`Matrix.PosSemidef.det_rpow_add_det_rpow_le`, `Matrix.concaveOn_det_rpow`, [Minkowski determinant](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Matrix/MinkowskiDeterminant.lean)). The complex case and the equality case are not stated. The only Hadamard-type bound is \(|\det A|\le\prod_i\sum_j|a_{ij}|\) for an absolute value, which is not Hadamard's inequality for positive matrices ([ℓ¹ bound](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/AbsoluteValue.lean)).

## Positive blocks, products, and factorization

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS13.md#positive-blocks-products-and-factorization).

### Mathlib

**The Loewner order.** On a Hilbert space over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), a linear endomorphism is positive when it is symmetric and \(\operatorname{re}\langle Tx,x\rangle\ge 0\) for every vector \(x\). In finite dimension a positive algebraic endomorphism is self-adjoint. A bounded endomorphism is positive under the same symmetry and quadratic-form hypotheses; on a complete space this is equivalent to self-adjointness together with the quadratic-form bound. The Loewner order is \(f\le g\) if and only if \(g-f\) is positive. The same definition is given for bounded operators (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Positive.lean`).

Bounded operators on a complex Hilbert space, with this order, form a star-ordered ring, and a nonnegative operator has nonnegative real spectrum (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/StarOrder.lean`). The operator-monotone functions in this file are monotone in this order.

For square matrices over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), a matrix is positive semidefinite when it is Hermitian and \(x^*Mx\ge 0\) for every finitely supported vector \(x\). The scoped matrix order declares \(A\le B\) when \(B-A\) is positive semidefinite. The positive-semidefinite matrices are closed in the usual topology on matrix entries. When the index is finite, a nonnegative matrix has nonnegative real spectrum and the matrices form a star-ordered ring, so the functional calculus applies to them (`LinearAlgebra/Matrix/PosDef.lean`, `Analysis/Matrix/Order.lean`). A diagonal matrix is positive semidefinite if and only if its diagonal is nonnegative.

**Schur complements.** If \(A\) is positive definite, then \(\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^*&D\end{pmatrix}\) is positive semidefinite exactly when \(D-B^*A^{-1}B\) is, and symmetrically for an invertible lower-right block (`Matrix.PosDef.fromBlocks₁₁`, `Matrix.PosDef.fromBlocks₂₂`, `LinearAlgebra/Matrix/PosDef.lean`). The determinant of a block matrix with invertible upper-left block is \(\det A\det(D-CA^{-1}B)\) (`Matrix.det_fromBlocks₁₁`, `LinearAlgebra/Matrix/SchurComplement.lean`). The singular-block version with the Moore–Penrose inverse is not stated.

**Schur and Kronecker products.** The Kronecker product of two positive-semidefinite (positive-definite) matrices is positive semidefinite (positive definite), and so is their entrywise (Hadamard) product: this is the Schur product theorem (`Matrix.PosSemidef.kronecker`, `Matrix.PosDef.kronecker`, `Matrix.PosSemidef.hadamard`, `Matrix.PosDef.hadamard`, `Analysis/Matrix/Order.lean`). The spectra and singular values of \(A\otimes B\) are not computed.

**Factorizations.** A positive-definite matrix has an \(LDL^*\) decomposition with \(L\) lower triangular and \(D\) diagonal (`LDL.lower_conj_diag`, `Analysis/Matrix/LDL.lean`); this is supporting material.

**Matrices as C⋆-algebras.** `CStarMatrix m n A` is a type copy of the matrices with entries in a C⋆-algebra \(A\). The operator norm coming from the action on \(C^\star\)-valued functions of the columns makes the square matrices a non-unital C⋆-algebra when \(A\) is non-unital, and a unital C⋆-algebra when \(A\) is unital (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/CStarMatrix.lean`).

### TauCeti

Finite Schur products and Schur powers of positive-semidefinite matrices indexed by arbitrary types are recorded in [positive-semidefinite matrix algebra](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/PosSemidef.lean). The Cholesky factor of a positive-definite real matrix is constructed and shown to be unique ([Cholesky](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Cholesky/Basic.lean), [Cholesky equivalence](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Cholesky/Equiv.lean)); this is supporting material.

## Operator monotonicity and convexity

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS13.md#operator-monotonicity-and-convexity).

### Mathlib

**Positive and negative parts, exponential, and logarithm.** In a non-unital algebra with the non-unital real continuous functional calculus on self-adjoint elements, the positive and negative parts of an element are the functional calculi of the real positive and negative parts. For a self-adjoint element, \(a^+-a^-=a\) and \(a^+a^-=a^-a^+=0\). In the presence of the star order, \(a\le a^+\) and \(-a^-\le a\). If the nonnegative elements are exactly those with nonnegative real spectrum, then \(a^+=a\) if and only if \(0\le a\), and \(a^-=0\) under the same condition. In a Hausdorff semitopological ring, the decomposition is unique: if \(a=b-c\) with \(b,c\ge 0\) and \(bc=0\), then \(b=a^+\) and \(c=a^-\) (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/PosPart/Basic.lean`).

On a real normed algebra with the real functional calculus, the exponential of a self-adjoint element is nonnegative for the star order, and \(\log(\exp a)=a\). If \(a\) is strictly positive and the nonnegative elements have nonnegative spectrum, then \(\exp(\log a)=a\). The exponential and logarithm are inverses in that sense (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/ExpLog/Basic.lean`).

**Operator monotonicity and operator concavity.** Throughout this section the order is the star order: the nonnegative elements are those of the form \(b^*b\), arranged so that the Loewner order on operators and the positive-semidefinite order on matrices are instances.

On a non-unital C⋆-algebra, \(a\mapsto a^p\) is monotone for every real exponent \(p\in[0,1]\), the square root is monotone, and both are concave on the positive cone. On a unital C⋆-algebra the real powers \(a\mapsto a^p\) for \(p\in[0,1]\) are monotone and concave on the positive cone (`CFC.monotone_rpow`, `CFC.concaveOn_rpow`, `Analysis/SpecialFunctions/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Rpow/Order.lean`). The file leaves as TODO the operator antitonicity and operator convexity of these powers on \([-1,0]\), and the operator convexity on \([1,2]\).

The proof for \(p\in(0,1)\) is Löwner-style: \(x^p\) is written as an integral, against a positive measure, of the operator-monotone and operator-concave functions \(t^p(t^{-1}-(t+x)^{-1})\) (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Rpow/IntegralRepresentation.lean`). This is a special case of the integral representation of entry 13.33, not the general theorem.

On a unital C⋆-algebra the logarithm is monotone and concave on the strictly positive elements (`CFC.log_monotoneOn`, `CFC.concaveOn_log`, `Analysis/SpecialFunctions/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/ExpLog/Order.lean`). Operator convexity of \(x\log x\) is a TODO there.

On a non-unital C⋆-algebra, \(x\mapsto 1-(1+x)^{-1}\) is monotone on the positive cone, both for the nonnegative calculus and for the real calculus (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ApproximateUnit.lean`).

On a unital C⋆-algebra, a self-adjoint element satisfies \(-\|a\|\cdot 1\le a\le\|a\|\cdot 1\), and both \(a a^*\) and \(a^* a\) are at most \(\|a\|^2\cdot 1\). For nonnegative \(a\) in a nontrivial algebra, \(\|a\|\) lies in the real spectrum, and \(\|a\|\le r\) for \(r\ge 0\) if and only if \(a\) is at most the scalar \(r\). In particular \(\|a\|\le 1\) if and only if \(a\le 1\). If \(0\le a\le b\), then \(\|a\|\le\|b\|\). If \(a\) is strictly positive and \(a\le b\), then \(b\) is invertible. For nonnegative invertible elements, \(0\le a\le b\) implies \(b^{-1}\le a^{-1}\), with the corresponding equivalence (`CStarAlgebra.inv_le_inv`, `CStarAlgebra.inv_le_inv_iff`), and the real powers of exponent \(-1\) satisfy the same reverse comparison. Inversion is antitone, and operator convex, on the strictly positive elements (`CStarAlgebra.antitoneOn_ringInverse`, `CStarAlgebra.convexOn_ringInverse`). For nonnegative \(a\) and strictly positive \(b\),
\[
a\le b\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\bigl\|\sqrt{a}\,b^{-1/2}\bigr\|\le 1
\]
(`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Order.lean`, `Analysis/SpecialFunctions/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Rpow/RingInverseOrder.lean`).

If \(0\le e\) and \(\|e\|\le 1\), then \(e^n\le e^m\) whenever \(0<m\le n\) in \(\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), and in particular \(e^2\le e\le\sqrt{e}\). On a unital C⋆-algebra, \(0\le a\) implies \(0\le a^n\) for every natural number \(n\); if \(1\le a\) then \(n\mapsto a^n\) is monotone, and if \(0\le a\le 1\) then \(n\mapsto a^n\) is antitone.

**Functional calculus of Hermitian matrices.** The functional calculus \(f(A)=Uf(\Lambda)U^*\) of a Hermitian matrix is recorded under the first heading. The Fréchet derivative formula by divided differences (Daleckii–Krein) and the holomorphic functional calculus of general matrices are not proved.

### TauCeti

For Löwner's theorem, the complex-analytic half of the classical proof is present. A Pick function, holomorphic on the upper half-plane with nonnegative imaginary part, has a Herglotz representation in Cayley coordinates by a finite positive measure on the circle, and conversely ([Pick functions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Pick/Basic.lean)); transporting the circle measure to the line gives the Nevanlinna representation ([Nevanlinna](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Pick/Nevanlinna.lean)), and the representing measure vanishes where the boundary values are real ([boundary values](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Pick/Boundary.lean)). The link from operator monotonicity to Pick functions is not made. The spectral projectors and continuity of the Hermitian functional calculus are recorded under the first heading.

**Synopsis entries.** Covered: 13.29 (Mathlib), 13.30 (Mathlib), 13.31 (Mathlib). Partly covered: 13.28 (the spectral form \(f(A)=\sum_\lambda f(\lambda)P_\lambda\), Mathlib and TauCeti; no derivative formula or holomorphic calculus). Not covered: 13.32–13.38.

## Matrix means and geometry of the positive cone

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS13.md#matrix-means-and-geometry-of-the-positive-cone).

### TauCeti

TauCeti constructs the (unweighted) geometric mean
\[
 a\#b=a^{1/2}(a^{-1/2}ba^{-1/2})^{1/2}a^{1/2}
\]
for strictly positive \(a\) and nonnegative \(b\) in the continuous-functional-calculus setting (`TauCeti.geometricMean`). It characterizes this as the unique nonnegative solution of \(xa^{-1}x=b\), and proves the symmetry \(a\#b=b\#a\) and the inversion identity \((a\#b)^{-1}=a^{-1}\#b^{-1}\) under strict positivity (`TauCeti.geometricMean_comm`, `TauCeti.geometricMean_ringInverse_ringInverse`), together with the operator arithmetic–geometric mean inequality \(2(a\#b)\le a+b\) (`TauCeti.geometricMean_add_geometricMean_le`; [functional-calculus construction](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/GeometricMean.lean)).

For positive-definite \(S\) and positive-semidefinite \(T\), it consequently constructs the unique positive-semidefinite solution of \(ASA=T\), namely \(S^{-1}\#T\); the matrix file gives the square-root formula and congruence identity (`Matrix.PosDef.existsUnique_posSemidef_mul_mul`, [matrix specialization](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Matrix/GeometricMean.lean)). This adds a concrete matrix mean, not the Kubo–Ando theory. The weighted means \(A\#_tB\), the harmonic comparison, and the Riemannian geometry of the positive cone are not developed.

**Synopsis entries.** Partly covered: 13.39 (unweighted mean \(t=1/2\): symmetry and inversion, TauCeti), 13.40 (the arithmetic–geometric inequality for \(t=1/2\), without the harmonic bound or equality case, TauCeti). Not covered: 13.41–13.44.

## Completely positive maps and quantum entropy

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS13.md#completely-positive-maps-and-quantum-entropy).

### Mathlib

Complex square matrices, with the \(\ell^2\) operator norm obtained by identifying a matrix with an endomorphism of Euclidean space, form a C⋆-algebra. The norm and the C⋆-identity are scoped so that they do not choose a global norm on all matrices. Every entry of a unitary matrix has norm at most \(1\) (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Matrix.lean`). A linear map between C⋆-algebras is completely positive when all its matrix amplifications are positive; the type of such maps is defined and star homomorphisms are shown to be completely positive (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/CompletelyPositiveMap.lean`). No Kraus, Choi, Stinespring, or Kadison–Schwarz theorem accompanies the definition, and partial traces, von Neumann entropy, and quantum relative entropy are not defined. A real doubly stochastic matrix has \(\ell^2\) operator norm at most \(1\) (`Matrix.l2_opNorm_le_one_of_mem_doublyStochastic`, `Analysis/Convex/Birkhoff.lean`).

**Synopsis entries.** Not covered: 13.53, 13.54, 13.57–13.62, 13.73, 13.76.

## Trace ideals and determinants

### TauCeti

Approximation numbers \(a_n(T)=\inf\{\|T-R\|:\operatorname{rank}R\le n\}\) are defined for bounded operators between normed spaces (`ContinuousLinearMap.singularValue`), with \(a_0(T)=\|T\|\), monotonicity in \(n\), the mixed-index inequalities \(a_{m+n}(S+T)\le a_m(S)+a_n(T)\) and \(a_{m+n}(ST)\le a_m(S)a_n(T)\), and \(1\)-Lipschitz dependence on \(T\) ([approximation numbers](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/Basic.lean)). ## Perturbation and further trace and entropy inequalities

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS13.md#perturbation-and-further-trace-and-entropy-inequalities).

### TauCeti

**Sylvester equation.** For bounded operators \(A\), \(B\), the Sylvester operator is \(X\mapsto AX-XB\) (`ContinuousLinearMap.sylvester`). In finite dimension, for symmetric \(A\) and \(B\) with disjoint point spectra, it is bijective and the solution is given by dividing eigenbasis coefficients by eigenvalue differences (`ContinuousLinearMap.sylvester_bijective`, [Sylvester operator](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Sylvester.lean)). If the spectra are at distance at least \(\delta\), then \(\delta\|X\|_2\le\|AX-XB\|_2\) in the Frobenius norm (`ContinuousLinearMap.mul_frobenius_le_frobenius_sylvester_apply`, [unitarily invariant Sylvester bounds](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm/Sylvester.lean)); under an interval/exterior separation the bound \(\delta N(X)\le N(AX-XB)\) holds in every unitarily invariant seminorm, and under a separation of quadratic forms the operator-norm bound holds in every dimension. The converse for non-normal matrices and the Bhatia–Davis–McIntosh constant \(\pi/2\) for general separation are not proved.

**Davis–Kahan.** The Davis–Kahan \(\sin\Theta\) theorems hold in every unitarily invariant seminorm with constant \(1\) under an interval/exterior separation of spectra: in the residual form \(\delta N(P_{U^\perp}X)\le N(AX-XM)\) for a trial map \(X\) (`TauCeti.UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm.mul_map_starProjection_comp_le_map_sylvester_apply`) and in the perturbation form \(\delta N(P_{V^\perp}P_U)\le N(B-A)\) ([sin Θ](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm/SinTheta/Basic.lean), [Frobenius and Ky Fan corollaries](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm/SinTheta/Corollaries.lean)). A dimension-free operator-norm version under quadratic-form separation is also proved ([dimension-free sin Θ](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Projection/SinTheta.lean)). The Rayleigh–Ritz compression is the best model among all residuals in every unitarily invariant seminorm ([Rayleigh–Ritz](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/RayleighRitz.lean)).

**Synopsis entries.** Partly covered: 13.66 (for symmetric \(A\), \(B\): bijectivity under disjoint spectra and the Frobenius bound with constant \(1\); not the general criterion or the constant \(\pi/2\), TauCeti), 13.67 (under interval/exterior separation, in every unitarily invariant seminorm, TauCeti; the general-separation Frobenius form is not stated). Not covered: 13.72, 13.75.
