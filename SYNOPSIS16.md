# Synopsis: Locally convex spaces, duality, and distributions

Part of the [mathematical synopsis series](SYNOPSIS.md).
[Bibliographical scope and references](CKMA-bib.md#ckma-16);
[MSC2020 correspondence and cross-subject index](CKMA-MSC2020.md).

This synopsis selects the common structural and distribution-theoretic core of
Section 16 of [CKMA-bib.md](CKMA-bib.md#ckma-16).
It follows the route from seminorms and duality to completeness, nuclear
spaces, test functions, and kernels. Distributional Fourier analysis appears
here as a consequence of this framework; microlocal refinements belong to
Section 30, and fundamental solutions and elliptic regularity continue in
Sections 32–34 (Weyl's lemma is SYNOPSIS32, item 13). Scalars are real or complex, and locally convex spaces are
Hausdorff unless otherwise stated.

A **topological vector space** has continuous addition and scalar
multiplication. It is **locally convex** if zero has a base of convex
neighborhoods. A **seminorm** is subadditive and absolutely homogeneous.
A subset $B$ is **bounded** if every zero-neighborhood absorbs it; it is
**absolutely convex** if it is convex and balanced. The continuous dual is
$E'$. Completeness refers to the natural translation-invariant uniformity;
it is not in general the same as sequential completeness.

## Seminorms, constructions, and completeness

For an absolutely convex absorbing set $U$, its **Minkowski functional** is
$p_U(x)=\inf\{t>0:x\in tU\}$. A **Fréchet space** is a complete metrizable
locally convex space. A **strict LF space** is the locally convex inductive
limit of an increasing sequence of Fréchet spaces, with each stage closed in
the next and carrying the induced topology.

* **1. Seminorm description.** Every locally convex topology is generated
  by a family of seminorms, through finite intersections of sets
  $\{x:p(x)<\varepsilon\}$. It is Hausdorff exactly when the seminorms
  separate points. Minkowski functionals of absolutely convex open
  zero-neighborhoods recover the topology.
* **2. Continuity criterion.** A linear map $T:E\to F$ is continuous
  exactly when, for each continuous seminorm $q$ on $F$, there are finitely
  many continuous seminorms $p_j$ on $E$ and $C$ such that
  $q(Tx)\leq C\max_jp_j(x)$.
* **3. Metrizability criterion.** A locally convex space is metrizable
  exactly when its topology is generated by countably many seminorms. With
  an increasing defining sequence $(p_n)$, one compatible invariant metric is
  $d(x,y)=\sum_{n\geq1}2^{-n}\min(1,p_n(x-y))$.
* **4. Normability criterion.** A Hausdorff locally convex space is
  normable exactly when it has a bounded zero-neighborhood (Kolmogorov).
  The weak topology of an infinite-dimensional normed space is never
  normable, indeed not even metrizable.
* **5. Completion and products.** Every Hausdorff locally convex space has
  a Hausdorff completion, unique up to its canonical isomorphism. Products
  of complete spaces and closed subspaces of complete spaces are complete.
  Countable products of Fréchet spaces are Fréchet.
* **6. Quotients of Fréchet spaces.** If $M$ is a closed subspace of a
  Fréchet space $E$, then $E/M$ with its quotient topology is Fréchet.
  Closed subspaces of Fréchet spaces are Fréchet as well.
* **7. Projective limits.** A projective limit of Hausdorff complete locally
  convex spaces is a closed subspace of their product and is complete.
  A countable projective limit of Fréchet spaces is Fréchet.
* **8. Strict inductive limits.** A strict LF space is complete. Every
  bounded subset lies in one stage and is bounded there; a sequence
  converges exactly when it lies in one stage and converges in that stage.
* **9. Universal property of inductive limits.** A linear map from a
  locally convex inductive limit into a locally convex space is continuous
  exactly when its restriction to every stage is continuous.
* **10. Boundedness by seminorms.** $B\subset E$ is bounded exactly when
  $\sup_{x\in B}p(x)<\infty$ for every continuous seminorm $p$. Continuous
  linear maps preserve bounded sets, and convergent sequences are bounded.

## Separation and compatible dual topologies

A **dual pair** $(E,F)$ is a bilinear pairing separating both spaces. Write
$\sigma(E,F)$ for pointwise convergence on $F$, $\beta(E,F)$ for uniform
convergence on $\sigma(F,E)$-bounded sets, and $\tau(E,F)$ for uniform
convergence on absolutely convex $\sigma(F,E)$-compact sets. For $A\subset E$,
$A^\circ=\{f\in F:|\langle a,f\rangle|\leq1\ (a\in A)\}$.

* **11. Locally convex Hahn–Banach theorem.** A continuous linear
  functional on a subspace extends continuously to the whole space, with
  the same domination by a specified continuous seminorm. Consequently
  $E'$ separates points of $E$.
* **12. Geometric separation.** A point outside a closed convex subset of
  $E$ is strictly separated from it by the real part of a continuous linear
  functional. Thus every closed convex set is an intersection of closed
  affine half-spaces.
* **13. Bipolar theorem for dual pairs.** $A^{\circ\circ}$ is the
  $\sigma(E,F)$-closed absolutely convex hull of $A\cup\{0\}$. In particular,
  absolutely convex weakly closed sets are recovered exactly from their polars.
  For convex sets in a normed space, weak and norm closures coincide.
* **14. Alaoglu–Bourbaki theorem.** The polar of a zero-neighborhood in
  $E$ is compact for $\sigma(E',E)$. A subset of $E'$ is equicontinuous
  exactly when it is contained in such a polar.
* **15. Mackey–Arens theorem.** The locally convex topologies on $E$ whose
  continuous dual is exactly $F$ are precisely those between
  $\sigma(E,F)$ and $\tau(E,F)$. In particular, $\tau(E,F)$ is the
  strongest topology compatible with the dual pair.
* **16. Mackey boundedness theorem.** Compatible locally convex topologies
  have the same bounded subsets. In particular, a subset of a locally convex
  space is bounded exactly when it is weakly bounded against every element
  of its continuous dual.
* **17. Metrizable spaces are Mackey spaces.** Every metrizable, and more
  generally every barrelled or bornological, locally convex space $E$ carries
  $\tau(E,E')$; in particular the norm topology of a normed space $X$ is
  $\tau(X,X^*)$. Weak and norm topologies have the same
  continuous linear functionals and bounded sets, but can have very different
  convergent sequences and compact sets.
* **18. Strong dual of a normed space.** Uniform convergence on bounded
  subsets of a normed space is precisely the operator-norm topology on its
  dual. For general locally convex $E$, the strong dual $E'_\beta$ is
  described by seminorms $f\mapsto\sup_{x\in B}|f(x)|$ for bounded $B$.
* **19. Duals of subspaces and quotients.** For closed $M\subset E$,
  the continuous dual of $E/M$ is its annihilator $M^\perp\subset E'$;
  restriction $E'\to M'$ is surjective with kernel $M^\perp$. These are
  algebraic identifications, and homeomorphisms for the weak topologies.
* **20. Krein–Milman theorem.** A compact convex subset of a Hausdorff
  locally convex space is the closed convex hull of its extreme points.
  Conversely (Milman), if a compact convex $K$ is the closed convex hull of
  a set $Z$, then every extreme point of $K$ lies in $\overline Z$.
  Closure cannot generally be omitted in infinite dimensions; the theorem
  applies to weak-star compact dual balls.
* **68. Grothendieck completeness theorem.** A locally convex space $E$ is
  complete exactly when every linear form on $E'$ whose restriction to each
  equicontinuous set is $\sigma(E',E)$-continuous is itself
  $\sigma(E',E)$-continuous, that is, evaluation at a point of $E$. In
  general the completion of $E$ is the space of all such linear forms. More
  generally, for a saturated covering family $\mathfrak S$ of bounded sets,
  $E'$ is complete for uniform convergence on $\mathfrak S$ exactly when
  every linear form on $E$ continuous on each member of $\mathfrak S$ is
  continuous.
* **69. Banach–Dieudonné and Krein–Šmulian theorems.** If $E$ is metrizable,
  uniform convergence on precompact subsets of $E$, equivalently on null
  sequences, is the finest topology on $E'$ that agrees with
  $\sigma(E',E)$ on every equicontinuous set (Banach–Dieudonné). Hence, for
  Fréchet $E$, a convex subset of $E'$ is weak-star closed exactly when its
  intersection with the polar of every zero-neighborhood is weak-star closed
  (Krein–Šmulian).

## Barrelledness, bornologies, and mapping theorems

A **barrel** is a closed absolutely convex absorbing set. A space is
**barrelled** if every barrel is a zero-neighborhood, and **bornological**
if every absolutely convex set absorbing all bounded sets is a
zero-neighborhood. A **Montel space** is barrelled and has relatively
compact bounded subsets.

* **21. Banach–Steinhaus in barrelled spaces.** A pointwise bounded family
  of continuous linear maps from a barrelled space into a locally convex
  space is equicontinuous. In particular, the pointwise limit of a sequence
  of continuous linear maps on a barrelled space is continuous.
* **22. Barrelledness of standard spaces.** Fréchet spaces are barrelled.
  Locally convex direct sums, inductive limits, and Hausdorff quotients of
  barrelled spaces are barrelled; thus strict LF spaces are barrelled.
* **23. Bornological mapping criterion.** $E$ is bornological exactly when
  every linear map from $E$ into any locally convex space that maps bounded
  sets to bounded sets is continuous.
* **24. Bornological examples.** Every metrizable locally convex space is
  bornological. Locally convex inductive limits and Hausdorff quotients of
  bornological spaces are bornological, so strict LF spaces have this property.
* **25. Fréchet open mapping theorem.** A continuous linear surjection
  between Fréchet spaces is open; a continuous linear bijection is an
  isomorphism of topological vector spaces.
* **26. Fréchet closed graph theorem.** An everywhere-defined linear map
  between Fréchet spaces with closed graph is continuous. Banach-space
  mapping principles therefore extend to smooth-function spaces, although
  they do not extend to arbitrary locally convex spaces without hypotheses.
* **27. Separate and joint continuity.** A separately continuous bilinear
  map from a product of two Fréchet spaces into a locally convex space is
  jointly continuous.
* **28. Montel duality.** Montel spaces are reflexive in the locally convex
  sense: the canonical map onto their strong bidual is a topological
  isomorphism, and the strong dual is again Montel. Since on equicontinuous
  sets the weak-star topology agrees with compact convergence, weak-star
  convergent sequences in the dual of a Montel Fréchet or LF space converge
  strongly; this applies to $\mathcal D'$, $\mathcal E'$, and $\mathcal S'$.

## Tensor products and nuclear spaces

For Banach spaces, the **projective norm** is
$\pi(u)=\inf\sum_j\|x_j\|\|y_j\|$ over $u=\sum_jx_j\otimes y_j$;
the **injective norm** is
$\varepsilon(u)=\sup_{f\in B_{X^*},g\in B_{Y^*}}|(f\otimes g)(u)|$.
Locally convex projective and injective tensor topologies are defined by the
corresponding seminorms. A continuous map to a Banach space is **nuclear**
if it has a representation $Tx=\sum_j\lambda_j f_j(x)y_j$ with
$\sum_j|\lambda_j|<\infty$, equicontinuous $(f_j)$, and bounded $(y_j)$.
A locally convex space is nuclear if all its continuous maps into Banach
spaces are nuclear.

* **29. Projective universal property.** Continuous bilinear maps
  $E\times F\to G$ correspond to continuous linear maps
  $E\otimes_\pi F\to G$. For complete $G$ these extend uniquely to the
  completed projective tensor product.
* **30. Banach tensor duality.** $(X\widehat\otimes_\pi Y)'$ is
  isometrically the space of bounded bilinear forms on $X\times Y$, hence
  $\mathcal L(X,Y^*)$. Also $\varepsilon(u)\leq\pi(u)$.
* **31. Nuclear tensor characterization.** $E$ is nuclear exactly when,
  for every locally convex $F$, the projective and injective topologies on
  $E\otimes F$ coincide.
* **32. Stability of nuclearity.** Subspaces, Hausdorff quotients, products,
  and countable locally convex direct sums of nuclear spaces are nuclear.
  A Banach space is nuclear as a locally convex space exactly when it is
  finite dimensional.
* **33. Nuclear Fréchet compactness.** Every nuclear Fréchet space is
  Montel, and hence reflexive; its bounded sets are relatively compact.
  Nuclear Fréchet spaces are also separable.
* **34. Smooth-function examples.** $C^\infty(\Omega)$ and
  $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)$ are nuclear Fréchet spaces.
  $\mathcal D(\Omega)$ is a nuclear strict LF space and is Montel.
* **35. Tensor products of smooth functions.** The natural maps give
  $C^\infty(\Omega)\widehat\otimes_\pi C^\infty(\Lambda)
  \cong C^\infty(\Omega\times\Lambda)$ and
  $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)\widehat\otimes_\pi\mathcal S(\mathbb R^m)
  \cong\mathcal S(\mathbb R^{n+m})$ as locally convex spaces.
* **70. Köthe echelon spaces and the Grothendieck–Pietsch criterion.** Let
  $(a_j)_{j\geq1}$ be positive weights on $\mathbb N$ with
  $a_j(k)\leq a_{j+1}(k)$. For $1\leq p<\infty$ the **echelon space**
  $\lambda^p(A)=\{x:\sum_k|x_ka_j(k)|^p<\infty\ \text{for every }j\}$,
  with these seminorms, is Fréchet. It is nuclear exactly when for each $j$
  there is $l$ with $\sum_ka_j(k)/a_l(k)<\infty$; the space then does not
  depend on $p$. The weights $a_j(k)=k^j$ give the space $s$ of rapidly
  decreasing sequences, which is nuclear and isomorphic to
  $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)$ through Hermite expansions and to
  $C^\infty(\mathbb T^n)$ through Fourier series.

## Test functions and the local structure of distributions

For open $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$, let
$\mathcal E(\Omega)=C^\infty(\Omega)$ with uniform convergence of all
derivatives on compact sets. Let $\mathcal D_K$ consist of smooth functions
supported in compact $K\Subset\Omega$, with seminorms
$\max_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^\alpha\phi\|_\infty$;
$\mathcal D(\Omega)=C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ has its strict inductive limit
topology over a compact exhaustion. A **distribution** is an element of
$\mathcal D'(\Omega)$, acting linearly, without complex conjugation, on
test functions. The **support** is the complement of the largest open set
where it vanishes.

* **36. Continuity criterion for distributions.** A linear functional $T$
  is a distribution exactly when for each $K\Subset\Omega$ some $C_K$ and
  integer $m_K$ satisfy
  $$|T\phi|\leq C_K\max_{|\alpha|\leq m_K}
       \|\partial^\alpha\phi\|_\infty\qquad(\phi\in\mathcal D_K).$$
  One fixed $m$ for all compact $K$ defines distributions of order at most $m$.
* **37. Regular distributions and measures.** Every $f\in L^1_{\rm loc}$
  defines $T_f(\phi)=\int f\phi$; equality of regular distributions means
  equality almost everywhere. Distributions of order zero are precisely
  locally finite Radon measures, with complex measures allowed.
* **38. Positive distributions.** A distribution nonnegative on all
  nonnegative real test functions is a positive Radon measure. Positivity
  therefore forces order zero.
* **39. Distributional convergence.** If $T_j\phi$ converges for every
  test function, its limit is a distribution. On each fixed $\mathcal D_K$
  the sequence obeys a uniform finite-order estimate, by Banach–Steinhaus.
* **40. Localization and gluing.** Distributions restrict to open subsets,
  and compatible distributions on an open cover glue uniquely. Smooth
  partitions of unity construct the glued distribution.
* **41. Compact support and $\mathcal E'$.** A distribution extends to a
  continuous functional on $C^\infty(\Omega)$ exactly when its support is
  compact. Every such distribution has finite order, with an estimate on
  some compact neighborhood of its support.
* **42. Distributions supported at a point.** If $\operatorname{supp}T
  \subset\{a\}$, then $T=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}c_\alpha\partial^\alpha\delta_a$
  for finitely many coefficients, with $m$ the order of $T$.
* **43. Local structure theorem.** On each relatively compact open subset,
  every distribution is a finite sum of distributional derivatives of
  continuous functions. A compactly supported distribution is globally of
  the form $\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\partial^\alpha f_\alpha$ with continuous,
  compactly supported $f_\alpha$.

## Differentiation, regularization, and convolution

Set $(\partial^\alpha T)(\phi)=(-1)^{|\alpha|}T(\partial^\alpha\phi)$
and $(aT)(\phi)=T(a\phi)$ for smooth $a$. A **mollifier** is
$\rho\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ with integral one, with
$\rho_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon^{-n}\rho(x/\varepsilon)$.

* **44. Continuity of distributional operations.** Differentiation and
  multiplication by a fixed smooth function are continuous on distributions.
  They satisfy the Leibniz rule and agree with classical operations on
  $C^{|\alpha|}$ functions. For a piecewise $C^1$ function on $\mathbb R$
  with jumps of size $c_j$ at a locally finite set of points $x_j$, the
  distributional derivative is the classical derivative plus
  $\sum_jc_j\delta_{x_j}$.
* **45. Vanishing derivatives.** A distribution on a connected open set
  with all first derivatives zero is constant. On an interval, every
  distribution has a primitive, unique up to an additive constant.
* **46. Convolution and smoothing.** If $T\in\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^n)$
  and $\phi\in\mathcal D$, then $T*\phi$ is smooth, and derivatives may
  be taken on either factor. The analogous construction works locally
  wherever translated supports remain inside the domain.
* **47. Approximation by smooth functions.** $T*\rho_\varepsilon\to T$
  in distributions. Cutoffs and mollification show that test functions are
  dense in $\mathcal D'$ for distributional convergence.
* **48. Convolution of distributions.** $S*T$ is defined if one factor
  has compact support, and more generally if addition is proper on
  $\operatorname{supp}S\times\operatorname{supp}T$. It obeys
  $\operatorname{supp}(S*T)\subset\operatorname{supp}S+\operatorname{supp}T$
  and commutes with derivatives; associativity holds under the corresponding
  support conditions.
* **49. Pullback by a submersion.** Every smooth submersion
  $F:\Omega\to\Lambda$ has a continuous pullback
  $F^*:\mathcal D'(\Lambda)\to\mathcal D'(\Omega)$ extending composition
  of smooth functions. For diffeomorphisms the definition uses the
  change-of-variables Jacobian on test functions. Arbitrary smooth maps
  do not admit pullback of every distribution.
* **50. Tensor product of distributions.** $S\otimes T$ exists uniquely,
  agrees with iterated evaluation on test functions, and satisfies
  $(S\otimes T)(\phi\otimes\psi)=S(\phi)T(\psi)$.
* **51. Schwartz kernel theorem.** Every continuous linear map
  $A:\mathcal D(\Lambda)\to\mathcal D'(\Omega)$ has a unique
  $K\in\mathcal D'(\Omega\times\Lambda)$ such that
  $(A\psi)(\phi)=K(\phi\otimes\psi)$. Conversely every such kernel
  defines a continuous map. Differential operators and the identity have
  kernels supported on the diagonal.

## Tempered distributions and Fourier transforms

The **Schwartz space** $\mathcal S$ consists of smooth functions with
$\sup_x|x^\alpha\partial^\beta f(x)|<\infty$ for every pair of
multiindices. Its continuous dual $\mathcal S'$ consists of **tempered
distributions**. Use $\widehat f(\xi)=\int e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,dx$;
thus $f(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int e^{ix\cdot\xi}\widehat f(\xi)\,d\xi$.
On distributions, $\widehat T(\phi)=T(\widehat\phi)$.

* **52. Tempered continuity criterion.** $T\in\mathcal S'$ exactly when
  it is bounded by a finite sum of Schwartz seminorms. Every compactly
  supported distribution is tempered, as are locally integrable functions
  with $(1+|x|)^{-N}f\in L^1$ for some $N$. Every tempered distribution is a
  finite sum $\sum\partial^\alpha f_\alpha$ of derivatives of continuous
  functions of polynomial growth.
* **53. Fourier automorphism.** Fourier transformation is a continuous
  automorphism of $\mathcal S$ and, by transposition, of $\mathcal S'$.
  It satisfies $\widehat{\widehat T}=(2\pi)^nT(-\,\cdot)$.
* **54. Differentiation and multiplication formulas.**
  $\widehat{\partial^\alpha T}=(i\xi)^\alpha\widehat T$ and
  $\widehat{x^\alpha T}=i^{|\alpha|}\partial_\xi^\alpha\widehat T$.
  In particular $\widehat{\delta_0}=1$ and $\widehat1=(2\pi)^n\delta_0$.
* **55. Basic Fourier pairs and singular distributions.** One has
  $\widehat{e^{-|x|^2/2}}=(2\pi)^{n/2}e^{-|\xi|^2/2}$. For $0<a<n$,
  $|x|^{-a}$ is tempered and
  $$\widehat{|x|^{-a}}=2^{n-a}\pi^{n/2}\frac{\Gamma((n-a)/2)}{\Gamma(a/2)}
    |\xi|^{a-n}.$$
  On $\mathbb R$, the principal value $\mathrm{p.v.}\,1/x$ is a tempered
  distribution of order one with
  $\widehat{\mathrm{p.v.}\,1/x}=-i\pi\operatorname{sgn}\xi$, and
  $1/(x\pm i0)=\mathrm{p.v.}\,1/x\mp i\pi\delta_0$ (Sokhotski–Plemelj).
* **57. Fourier transform of convolution.** If $S\in\mathcal E'$ and
  $T\in\mathcal S'$, then $S*T\in\mathcal S'$ and
  $\widehat{S*T}=\widehat S\,\widehat T$; the smooth function
  $\widehat S$ and its derivatives have the growth needed for this product.
* **58. Tempered kernel theorem.** Continuous linear maps
  $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^m)\to\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^n)$ correspond
  uniquely to kernels in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^{n+m})$ by the same
  tensor evaluation formula as in result 51.
* **59. Paley–Wiener–Schwartz theorem.** For compact convex
  $K\subset\mathbb R^n$, Fourier transforms of distributions supported in
  $K$ are exactly the entire functions $F$ on $\mathbb C^n$ satisfying
  $$|F(z)|\leq C(1+|z|)^N e^{H_K(\operatorname{Im}z)},
    \qquad H_K(\eta)=\sup_{x\in K}x\cdot\eta,$$
  for some $C,N$. The distribution is unique.
* **60. Smooth Paley–Wiener theorem.** Fourier transforms of smooth
  functions supported in compact convex $K$ satisfy the preceding estimate
  with $(1+|z|)^{-N}$ for every $N$, with constants depending on $N$.
  Conversely these estimates characterize those functions.
* **61. Periodic distributions.** Distributions on $\mathbb T^n$ correspond
  to Fourier coefficient sequences of at most polynomial growth. Smooth
  functions correspond to rapidly decreasing sequences, and Fourier series
  reconstruct every periodic distribution in the distribution topology.

## Rigged Hilbert spaces and measures on duals

A **Gel'fand triple** (rigged Hilbert space) $\Phi\subset H\subset\Phi^\times$
consists of a nuclear space $\Phi$ continuously and densely embedded in a
separable Hilbert space $H$, and the space $\Phi^\times$ of continuous
conjugate-linear functionals on $\Phi$, into which $H$ embeds by
$h\mapsto\langle h,\cdot\rangle$. The model is
$\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)\subset L^2(\mathbb R^n)\subset\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^n)$,
after composing with complex conjugation.

* **71. Gel'fand–Maurin nuclear spectral theorem.** Let
  $\Phi\subset H\subset\Phi^\times$ be a Gel'fand triple and $A$ a
  self-adjoint operator on $H$ with $\Phi\subset D(A)$, $A\Phi\subset\Phi$,
  and $A|_\Phi$ continuous on $\Phi$. Then there are a finite Borel measure
  $\mu$ on $\sigma(A)$ and, for $\mu$-almost every $\lambda$, generalized
  eigenvectors $F_{\lambda,j}\in\Phi^\times$, $j$ in a countable index set,
  with $F_{\lambda,j}(A\phi)=\lambda F_{\lambda,j}(\phi)$ and
  $$\|\phi\|^2=\int\sum_j|F_{\lambda,j}(\phi)|^2\,d\mu(\lambda)
    \qquad(\phi\in\Phi).$$
  For $-d^2/dx^2$ with $\Phi=\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ the generalized
  eigenfunctions are $e^{\pm i\sqrt\lambda x}$, $\lambda>0$, none of which
  lies in $L^2$.
* **72. Bochner–Minlos theorem.** Let $E$ be a real nuclear space, for
  example $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)$ or $\mathcal D(\mathbb R^n)$ of real
  functions. If $\chi:E\to\mathbb C$ is continuous and positive definite
  with $\chi(0)=1$, there is a unique probability measure $\mu$ on the
  $\sigma$-algebra of $E'$ generated by the evaluations $T\mapsto T(\phi)$
  such that $\chi(\phi)=\int_{E'}e^{iT(\phi)}\,d\mu(T)$. Conversely every
  such characteristic functional is positive definite with $\chi(0)=1$, and
  it is continuous when $E$ is Fréchet. For $E=\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)$
  that $\sigma$-algebra is the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of $\mathcal S'$;
  $\chi(\phi)=e^{-\|\phi\|_2^2/2}$ gives white noise. On an
  infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space norm continuity does not
  suffice: characteristic functionals of Borel probabilities are exactly the
  positive definite functions with $\chi(0)=1$ continuous for the topology
  defined by Hilbert–Schmidt operators (Sazonov).

## Fundamental solutions and elementary regularity

A **fundamental solution** for $P(\partial)$ is a distribution $E$ with
$P(\partial)E=\delta_0$. An operator is **hypoelliptic** if smoothness of
$Pu$ on an open subset implies smoothness of $u$ there.

* **63. Malgrange–Ehrenpreis theorem.** Every nonzero constant-coefficient
  linear differential operator on $\mathbb R^n$ has a distributional
  fundamental solution. A tempered fundamental solution also exists
  (Hörmander, Łojasiewicz).
* **64. Classical fundamental solutions.** The Laplacian has
  $E=(2\pi)^{-1}\log|x|$ for $n=2$ and
  $E=-|x|^{2-n}/((n-2)|S^{n-1}|)$ for $n\geq3$. The heat operator
  $\partial_t-\Delta$ has $E=H(t)(4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^2/(4t)}$, and
  $\partial/\partial\bar z$ has $E=1/(\pi z)$ on $\mathbb C$. These give
  the Newtonian potential, the heat semigroup, and the Cauchy transform.
* **65. Convolution solution.** If $E$ is a fundamental solution and
  $f$ is a compactly supported distribution, then $u=E*f$ solves
  $P(\partial)u=f$ on all of $\mathbb R^n$.
* **66. Elliptic regularity with constant coefficients.** A
  constant-coefficient elliptic operator is hypoelliptic. More quantitatively,
  if it has order $m$ and $Pu\in H^s_{\rm loc}$, then
  $u\in H^{s+m}_{\rm loc}$. More generally, with symbol $p(\xi)=P(i\xi)$,
  $P(\partial)$ is hypoelliptic exactly when
  $\partial^\alpha p(\xi)/p(\xi)\to0$ as $\xi\to\infty$ in $\mathbb R^n$
  for every $\alpha\neq0$ (Hörmander); the heat operator is an example.

## Reference guide

Schaefer–Wolff and Osborne supply the common locally convex theory: seminorms,
inductive limits, compatible dual topologies, barrels, and mapping principles.
The tensor and nuclear-space material links this theory with the programme
developed in Trèves, Köthe, Grothendieck, and Jarchow; Ryan gives the Banach
tensor foundations. Duistermaat–Kolk supplies an independent, concrete route
through distributions, operations, kernels, Fourier transforms, and fundamental
solutions. These themes recur in Schwartz, Horváth, Friedlander–Joshi, and
the other distribution texts in the bibliographies. Gel'fand–Shilov and
Berezansky–Kondratiev develop rigged Hilbert spaces, generalized eigenvector
expansions, and measures on duals of nuclear spaces. Nonlinear generalized
function algebras and specialist splitting theory are beyond the common core
selected here.
