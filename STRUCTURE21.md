## Operator topologies, spectra, and factorization

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS21.md#operator-topologies-spectra-and-factorization).

### Mathlib

**Bounded operators.**

A continuous linear map between normed spaces carries the operator norm, and composition is submultiplicative (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Basic.lean`). If the domain is a nontrivial normed space over a nontrivially normed field and has a separating dual, the space of continuous linear maps into a normed codomain is complete if and only if the codomain is complete (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/CompleteCodomain.lean`).

On a complete normed space over a nontrivially normed field, a continuous endomorphism is a unit of the operator algebra if and only if it is bijective. The spectrum of the operator as a Banach-algebra element therefore agrees with its spectrum as a linear endomorphism (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Banach.lean`). The spectrum of an element of a Banach algebra is section 20. On a complete complex inner product space, the bounded endomorphisms form a C⋆-algebra (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousLinearMap.lean`).

**Self-adjoint operators.**

On an inner product space over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), a symmetric linear endomorphism has real eigenvalues, its eigenspaces are mutually orthogonal, and the orthogonal complement of the sum of the eigenspaces is invariant and contains no eigenvector (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Spectrum.lean`).

In finite dimension that orthogonal complement is zero. The operator is orthogonally equivalent to a diagonal operator on the orthogonal sum of its eigenspaces, and the space has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors whose eigenvalues are listed in decreasing order.

On a complete inner product space, a compact self-adjoint operator has the same conclusion for the orthogonal complement: it is zero. Each nonzero eigenspace is finite-dimensional. The file calls this the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators, and it records the spectral theory of a general bounded self-adjoint operator as future work (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Spectrum.lean`).

For a self-adjoint continuous operator on a complete inner product space, the spectral radius equals the operator norm (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Rayleigh.lean`). Hellinger–Toeplitz holds: an everywhere-defined symmetric operator on a complete space is bounded (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Symmetric.lean`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Adjoint.lean`).

**Continuous functional calculus.**

Let \(a\) be a normal element of a unital complex C⋆-algebra. The continuous functional calculus is a star-algebra equivalence from the continuous complex functions on the spectrum of \(a\) onto the unital C⋆-subalgebra generated by \(a\). It sends the restricted identity function to \(a\), and therefore extends the polynomial calculus. A star-algebra equivalence of C⋆-algebras is an isometry, so the calculus is isometric (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Basic.lean`, `IsStarNormal.instIsometricContinuousFunctionalCalculus`). Applied to \(f(\lambda)=(z-\lambda)^{-1}\), this gives \(\lVert(z-T)^{-1}\rVert=\operatorname{dist}(z,\sigma(T))^{-1}\) for normal \(T\).

Uniqueness is Stone–Weierstrass. On a compact subset of \(\mathbb{R}\) or of \(\mathbb{C}\), a continuous star-algebra homomorphism out of the continuous functions is determined by its value on the identity function. The same uniqueness holds for the non-unital calculus, and for nonnegative scalars when the target is an \(\mathbb{R}\)-algebra (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Unique.lean`).

The generic calculus is instantiated for normal elements of a unital complex C⋆-algebra, for normal elements of a non-unital complex C⋆-algebra by passage through the unitization, and for self-adjoint elements by restriction of scalars along the real part (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Basic.lean`, `Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Instances.lean`). The order, positivity, and the rest of the C⋆-package that use this calculus are section 23.

**Uniform boundedness and operator topologies.**

The Banach–Steinhaus theorem: a pointwise bounded family of continuous linear maps on a Banach space is uniformly bounded (`banach_steinhaus`, `Analysis/Normed/Operator/BanachSteinhaus.lean`); hence strongly convergent sequences are norm bounded, and a pointwise limit of continuous linear maps on a barrelled space is continuous (`continuousLinearMapOfTendsto`, `Analysis/LocallyConvex/Barrelled.lean`). The weak operator topology is defined as a type copy of the operator space, with its seminorm description (`ContinuousLinearMapWOT`, `Analysis/LocallyConvex/WeakOperatorTopology.lean`). Joint strong continuity of multiplication on bounded sets is not recorded.

**Fuglede–Putnam.**

If \(a\) and \(b\) are normal elements of a C⋆-algebra and \(xa=bx\), then \(xa^\ast=b^\ast x\) (`fuglede_putnam_rosenblum`, an alias of `SemiconjBy.star_right`; `Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Fuglede.lean`). For operators this is the case of normal operators on one Hilbert space; commutation with continuous functions of a normal operator follows from the continuous functional calculus.

The spectrum of a bounded operator is nonempty and compact, and Gelfand’s spectral radius formula holds, as for any element of a complex Banach algebra (Section 20); the holomorphic functional calculus, Riesz projections, approximate point spectrum, the numerical range, and the Taylor joint spectrum are not formalized.

### TauCeti

For a normal bounded operator \(T\) on a complex Hilbert space and \(\xi\in H\), the scalar spectral measure \(\mu_\xi\) is the unique finite Borel measure on \(\sigma(T)\) with \(\langle\xi,f(T)\xi\rangle=\int f\,d\mu_\xi\) for continuous \(f\); its mass is \(\lVert\xi\rVert^2\) and it computes \(\lVert f(T)\xi\rVert^2=\int|f|^2d\mu_\xi\) ([diagonal measures](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/BorelCalculus/DiagonalMeasure.lean), `ContinuousLinearMap.diagMeasure`, `integral_diagMeasure_eq_inner_cfc`, `eq_diagMeasure_iff`). Bounded Borel functions are set up as a pointwise star algebra of symbols. Stone's theorem is available in the unbounded-operator development of Section 22. Sources: [bounded Borel symbols](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/BoundedBorel.lean). An operator intertwining two Hilbert-space operators and their adjoints intertwines all their continuous functions (`ContinuousLinearMap.comp_cfc_eq_cfc_comp`, [intertwining](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Intertwining.lean)); this is the two-space form of the calculus that Fuglede–Putnam feeds.

**Douglas factorization, elementary direction.** If \(A:E\to G\) and \(T:E\to F\) are bounded, \(G\) is a Hilbert space, and \(\lVert Tx\rVert\le C\lVert Ax\rVert\) for all \(x\), then \(T=VA\) for a unique bounded \(V:G\to F\) vanishing on \((\operatorname{ran}A)^\perp\), with \(\lVert V\rVert\le C\) (`ContinuousLinearMap.factorThru`, `ContinuousLinearMap.factorThru_comp`, `ContinuousLinearMap.norm_factorThru_le`, [factorization](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Gram/Factor.lean)). Applied to adjoints this gives the implication from \(AA^\ast\le cBB^\ast\) to \(A=BC\) with \(\lVert C\rVert^2\le c\); the range-inclusion equivalence is not stated.

## Fredholm operators and essential spectra

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS21.md#fredholm-operators-and-essential-spectra).

### Mathlib

**The Fredholm alternative.**

Let \(T\) be a compact endomorphism of a Banach space over a nontrivially normed field, and let \(\mu\neq 0\). Either \(\mu\) is an eigenvalue of \(T\), or \(\mu\) lies in the resolvent set. Equivalently, the nonzero eigenvalues are exactly the nonzero points of the spectrum. The preliminary statement that \(T-\mu\) is antilipschitz whenever \(\mu\neq 0\) is not an eigenvalue does not use completeness of the space. The field need not be \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\) (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Compact/FredholmAlternative.lean`).

**Fredholm operators and the index.**

Over a complete nontrivially normed field, a continuous linear map between Hausdorff topological vector spaces is Fredholm when it is strict, its range is closed and of finite codimension, and its kernel is finite-dimensional and topologically complemented. Four conditions are equivalent: that definition; the existence of a continuous quasi-inverse, that is, an inverse modulo finite-rank maps on both sides (`ContinuousLinearMap.isFredholm_iff_exists_isQuasiInverse`, with `LinearMap.IsQuasiInverse`), which is Atkinson's theorem with finite-rank rather than compact remainders; the existence of an isomorphism between closed finite-codimension subspaces; and a Fredholm package, a topological splitting in which the operator is invertible on the essential summand and zero on a finite-dimensional complement. Sources: `Analysis/Normed/Operator/Fredholm/Basic.lean`.

The same file proves that Fredholm operators are stable under finite-rank perturbation (`IsFredholm.add_hasFiniteRange`) and composition (`IsFredholm.comp`), with the index additive on composites (`IsFredholm.index_comp`); that a continuous linear map is invertible exactly when it is Fredholm and bijective; and that closed embeddings, quotient maps, inclusions of closed complemented subspaces, and continuous idempotents are Fredholm under the expected finiteness conditions.

The index is \(\operatorname{rank}\ker - \operatorname{rank}\operatorname{coker}\), an integer. If either rank is infinite, the formula does not carry a separate infinite value (`Algebra/Module/LinearMap/Index.lean`).

The set of Fredholm operators is open in the operator norm, and the index is locally constant on that set, when the scalar field and the domain are complete and the codomain is a normed space. The codomain is not required to be complete, although the module commentary describes the result as a theorem about two Banach spaces (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Fredholm/Open.lean`).

### TauCeti

The additions include the Banach-space Fredholm criterion from finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel (closed range is automatic between Banach spaces once the cokernel is finite-dimensional), the Hilbert-space adjoint closed-range theorem (taking adjoints preserves Fredholmness and negates the index), and compact perturbations: if \(T\) is Fredholm between Banach spaces and \(C\) is compact, then \(T+C\) is Fredholm with the same index (`ContinuousLinearMap.IsFredholm.add_of_isCompactOperator`), and \(I-K\) is Fredholm of index zero for compact \(K\). Combined with Mathlib's quasi-inverse characterization this gives the compact-remainder half of Atkinson's theorem; applied to \(T-z\) it gives invariance of the Fredholm essential spectrum under compact perturbation. Openness of the Fredholm locus and local constancy of the index are already in Mathlib, as recorded in the Mathlib account; TauCeti restates them in \(\varepsilon\)-form ([Fredholm criterion](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/ClosedRange.lean), [adjoints](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/Adjoint.lean), [compact perturbations](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/CompactPerturbation.lean)). For compact \(K\), TauCeti proves finite-dimensionality of the kernel and cokernel of \(I-K\), together with closedness of its range ([Riesz theory](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/Compact/RieszTheory.lean)). The resolvent of a possibly unbounded operator (`LinearPMap`) on a Banach space, in particular of a bounded one, is analytic in operator norm on the resolvent set ([resolvent analyticity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/Resolvent/Analytic.lean)).

A self-adjoint Fredholm operator on a Hilbert space has index zero, and more generally so does any Fredholm operator with the same kernel as its adjoint ([self-adjoint Fredholm](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/SelfAdjoint.lean)). The compact self-adjoint spectral theorem is repackaged as the existence of a Hilbert basis of eigenvectors ([eigenbasis](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Spectrum.lean)); in Mathlib the conclusion is stated as the vanishing of the orthogonal complement of the eigenspaces.

## Compact operators, singular values, and trace ideals

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS21.md#compact-operators-singular-values-and-trace-ideals).

### Mathlib

**Compact operators.**

A map is a compact operator when the preimage of some compact set is a neighborhood of the origin. From a normed space into a Hausdorff topological vector space, this is equivalent to the closure of the image of a ball being compact. For linear maps between normed spaces over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), compactness implies continuity. They form a submodule of the continuous linear maps (`compactOperator`), and the submodule is closed in the operator norm. Precomposition and postcomposition with a continuous linear map preserve compactness (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Compact/Basic.lean`).

The identity is a compact operator if and only if the space is finite-dimensional, when the scalar field is complete and locally compact and the space is a Hausdorff topological vector space over that field (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Compact/FiniteDimension.lean`).

If two continuous linear maps into a Hausdorff space differ by a finite-rank map, one is strict and has closed range if and only if the other is. This is Bourbaki’s finite-rank perturbation lemma (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Perturbation/StrictByFinite.lean`).

**Singular values.**

On finite-dimensional inner product spaces over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), the singular values of a linear map \(T\) are the square roots of the eigenvalues of \(T^\ast\circ T\), arranged in decreasing order and repeated by multiplicity. The sequence is indexed by the nonnegative integers and is finitely supported. Its support is the initial segment of length equal to the rank of \(T\): the first \(\operatorname{rank}(T)\) singular values are positive, and the rest are zero (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/SingularValues.lean`).

### TauCeti

Approximation numbers \(a_n(T)=\inf\{\lVert T-R\rVert:\operatorname{rank}R\le n\}\) are defined for bounded maps between seminormed spaces over any nontrivially normed field (`ContinuousLinearMap.singularValue`), with \(a_0(T)=\lVert T\rVert\), antitonicity, the index laws \(a_{m+n}(S+T)\le a_m(S)+a_n(T)\) and \(a_{m+n}(ST)\le a_m(S)a_n(T)\), the two-sided ideal inequality \(a_n(ATB)\le\lVert A\rVert a_n(T)\lVert B\rVert\) (`singularValue_comp_comp_le`), and Lipschitz dependence on \(T\) ([approximation numbers](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/Basic.lean)). Between finite-dimensional inner product spaces they are Mathlib's singular values (Eckart–Young, `singularValue_eq_linearMap_singularValues`) ([Eckart–Young](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/EckartYoung.lean)). Over a complete field \(a_n(T)=0\) exactly when \(\operatorname{rank}T\le n\), and a bounded operator into a Hilbert space is compact exactly when \(a_n(T)\to0\), that is, exactly when it is a norm limit of finite-rank operators (`isCompactOperator_iff_tendsto_singularValue`) ([compactness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/Compact.lean)). For decreasing diagonal operators on \(\ell^p\), \(a_n\) is the \(n\)-th coefficient. On Hilbert spaces \(a_n(T^\ast)=a_n(T)\), min–max formulas hold, and the Ky Fan sums \(K_k(T)=\sum_{n<k}a_n(T)\) satisfy \(K_k(S+T)\le K_k(S)+K_k(T)\) ([Ky Fan](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/KyFan.lean)).

On these foundations TauCeti builds operator-ideal gauges. The Hilbert–Schmidt energy \(\sum_i\lVert Tb_i\rVert^2\) is independent of the Hilbert basis and equal to that of the adjoint (`hilbertSchmidtEnergy_indep`), its square root is a basis-free Hilbert–Schmidt norm with \(\lVert ATB\rVert_{\rm HS}\le\lVert A\rVert\lVert T\rVert_{\rm HS}\lVert B\rVert\), and \(\sum_n a_n(T)^2=\lVert T\rVert_{\rm HS}^2\) ([Hilbert–Schmidt norm](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/HilbertSchmidt/Basic.lean), [Hilbert–Schmidt and approximation numbers](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/HilbertSchmidt.lean)). A symmetric gauge \(\Phi\) induces \(\Phi(T)=\Phi_\infty((a_n(T))_n)\), a subadditive, homogeneous, adjoint-invariant gauge dominating the operator norm and satisfying the ideal inequality (`TauCeti.SymmetricGauge.opGauge_comp_comp_le`); the \(\ell^p\) gauges give the Schatten classes, and the sum gives the nuclear gauge \(\sum_n a_n(T)\), finite exactly on trace-class operators, lower semicontinuous, and forcing compactness when finite (`nuclearENorm_comp_comp_le`) ([symmetric-gauge ideals](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/Family/SymmetricGauge.lean), [nuclear gauge](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/Nuclear/Basic.lean)). In finite dimension the Schatten \(p\)-seminorms are unitarily invariant norms with the triangle inequality, adjoint invariance and one-sided ideal inequalities ([Schatten seminorms](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm/Schatten.lean)).

## Toeplitz and Hankel operators

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS21.md#toeplitz-and-hankel-operators).

### TauCeti

On the boundary Hardy space \(H^2\subset L^2(\mathbb T)\), with the nonnegative Fourier modes as Hilbert basis, the Toeplitz operator \(T_a=PM_a|_{H^2}\) of a symbol \(a\in L^\infty\) has matrix \((\hat a(i-j))\), adjoint \(T_{\bar a}\), norm at most \(\lVert a\rVert_\infty\), and determines \(a\); the Hankel operator \(H_a=(I-P)M_a|_{H^2}\) satisfies \(T_{ab}-T_aT_b=H_{\bar a}^\ast H_b\), and \(H_a=0\) exactly when \(a\) is analytic, so \(T_{ab}=T_aT_b\) when \(b\) is analytic or \(a\) coanalytic ([Hardy space](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Basic.lean), [Toeplitz operators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Toeplitz.lean), [Hankel operators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Hankel.lean)). The Hankel identity is the algebraic input to compactness of continuous-symbol semicommutators; that compactness, the C⋆-algebra generated by the shift, and its quotient by the compacts are not formalized.

## Reproducing kernels and interpolation

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS21.md#reproducing-kernels-and-interpolation).

### Mathlib

**Reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces.**

Reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, the setting of Paulsen–Raghupathi and Agler–McCarthy, are defined for vector-valued functions (`RKHS`): point evaluations are continuous, the kernel is positive semidefinite (`RKHS.posSemidef_kernel`) and its kernel functions are dense (`RKHS.kerFun_dense`), the kernel factors as \(K(x,y)=k_x^\ast k_y\) through the kernel functions (`RKHS.kernel_apply`), and every positive semidefinite kernel is the kernel of the space it generates (`RKHS.OfKernel`, `RKHS.kernel_ofKernel`; Moore’s theorem) (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Reproducing.lean`). The space of a sum of kernels is identified isometrically with a quotient of the product of the two spaces, with the norm of a sum attained by an orthogonal projection (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Reproducing/Operations.lean`). The Schur product theorem is proved for positive semidefinite matrices over finite index sets (`Matrix.PosSemidef.hadamard`, `Analysis/Matrix/Order.lean`), which gives positivity of pointwise products of positive definite kernels on finite subsets.

### TauCeti

Positive-definite functions on involutive additive monoids are closed under sums, nonnegative multiples, and Schur (pointwise) products ([positive-definite functions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PositiveDefinite/Basic.lean)).
