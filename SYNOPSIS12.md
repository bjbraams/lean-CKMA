# Synopsis: Convexity, convex analysis, optimization, and variational analysis

This synopsis follows the overlapping core of Section 12: convex geometry,
convex optimization, conjugate duality, optimality conditions,

A convex function $f:E\to(-\infty,\infty]$ is **proper** if it never takes
$-\infty$ and is finite somewhere. Its **effective domain** is $\operatorname{dom}f$;
it is **lower semicontinuous** when its epigraph is closed. The conjugate is
$f^*(u)=\sup_x(\langle u,x\rangle-f(x))$, with $u$ in the continuous dual.
The **subdifferential** is
$\partial f(x)=\{u:f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle u,y-x\rangle\ \forall y\}$ at
points of finite value, and is empty elsewhere. Unless specified otherwise,
geometric statements are in $\mathbb R^n$.

## Separation and convex geometry

The **convex hull** is the set of finite convex combinations. A point of a
convex set is **extreme** if it lies in no nontrivial open segment of the set.
A **convex body** is a compact convex set with nonempty interior. Relative
interior means interior in the affine hull; it is often the correct
finite-dimensional qualification when ordinary interior is empty. The
Krein–Milman theorem is SYNOPSIS16, item 20.

* **1. Separation.** A point outside a nonempty closed convex set can be
  strictly separated from it by a continuous linear functional. A compact
  convex set and a disjoint closed convex set can be strictly separated.
  These formulations also hold in Hausdorff locally convex spaces.
* **2. Supporting hyperplanes.** In $\mathbb R^n$, every boundary point of a
  convex set lies in a supporting hyperplane. In a topological vector space
  the same holds for a convex set with nonempty interior. In a Banach space,
  the support points of a closed convex set are dense in its boundary, and
  the support functionals of a closed bounded convex set are norm dense in
  the dual (Bishop–Phelps).
* **3. Carathéodory's theorem.** Every point of a convex hull in $\mathbb R^n$
  is a convex combination of at most $n+1$ points of the original set. The
  dimension can be replaced by that of its affine hull.
* **4. Radon's theorem.** Any $n+2$ points in $\mathbb R^n$ can be partitioned
  into two nonempty groups with intersecting convex hulls.
* **5. Helly's theorem.** For a finite family of convex sets in $\mathbb R^n$,
  if every subfamily of at most $n+1$ has nonempty intersection, the entire
  family does. An infinite-family version requires compactness hypotheses.
* **6. Finite-dimensional extreme-point representation.** A nonempty compact
  convex set is the convex hull of its extreme points; each point is a convex
  combination of at most $n+1$ of them.
* **7. Minkowski–Weyl theorem.** A subset of $\mathbb R^n$ is a polyhedron,
  a finite intersection of closed half-spaces, exactly when it is the sum of
  the convex hull of finitely many points and the cone generated by finitely
  many vectors. In particular, bounded polyhedra are polytopes, and a
  polyhedral cone is finitely generated.
* **9. Choquet representation.** In a compact metrizable convex subset of a
  locally convex space, every point is the barycenter of a probability carried
  by the extreme points. Barycenter means equality after evaluation by every
  continuous affine function.
* **10. Choquet simplex.** For a compact metrizable convex set, uniqueness of
  these extreme-point representing probabilities characterizes a Choquet
  simplex. Thus integral representation can be unique even when the set is
  infinite-dimensional; it is not unique for general compact convex sets.
* **11. John's ellipsoid theorem.** Every convex body has a unique
  maximum-volume inscribed ellipsoid $\mathcal E$. Centering dilations at its
  center, $\mathcal E\subseteq K\subseteq n\mathcal E$. For centrally
  symmetric $K$, the outer factor improves to $\sqrt n$. Equivalently, every
  real $n$-dimensional normed space has Banach–Mazur distance at most
  $\sqrt n$ from $\ell_2^n$.

## Convex functions, derivatives, and conjugacy

The one-sided **directional derivative** is
$f'(x;d)=\lim_{t\downarrow0}(f(x+td)-f(x))/t$ when interpreted in the extended
sense. The **indicator** $\delta_C$ is zero on $C$ and infinity outside;
the **support function** is $h_C(u)=\sup_{x\in C}\langle u,x\rangle$.

* **12. Continuity on the interior.** A proper convex function in finite
  dimensions is locally Lipschitz on the interior of its domain. On a Banach
  space, a convex function bounded above on a neighborhood is continuous and
  locally Lipschitz there; finiteness alone is not an adequate substitute in
  arbitrary infinite-dimensional settings.
* **13. Existence of subgradients.** A proper convex function has a nonempty
  subdifferential at every point in the relative interior of its domain in
  finite dimensions. For a continuous convex function on an open Banach-space
  domain, continuous supporting subgradients exist at every point.
* **14. Directional derivative and subgradients.** At an interior point of a
  finite-dimensional convex domain,
  $f'(x;d)=\max_{u\in\partial f(x)}\langle u,d\rangle$.
  The directional derivative is sublinear in $d$ and determines the subdifferential.
* **15. Differentiability criterion.** A finite convex function on an open
  convex subset of $\mathbb R^n$ is differentiable at $x$ exactly when
  $\partial f(x)$ is a singleton. It is differentiable off a Lebesgue-null
  set, and its gradient is continuous on the set of differentiability points.
  A differentiable function on an open convex set is convex exactly when its
  gradient is monotone; a $C^2$ function, exactly when its Hessian is
  positive semidefinite.
* **17. Fenchel–Young inequality.**
  $f(x)+f^*(u)\geq\langle u,x\rangle$, with equality exactly when
  $u\in\partial f(x)$.
* **18. Fenchel–Moreau theorem.** A proper lower semicontinuous convex
  function on a Hausdorff locally convex space equals its biconjugate.
  It is therefore the supremum of its continuous affine minorants.
* **19. Inverse subdifferentials.** For such $f$,
  $u\in\partial f(x)$ exactly when $x\in\partial f^*(u)$, using the
  canonical embedding in the bidual when necessary.
* **20. Legendre-type functions.** A proper closed convex $f$ on
  $\mathbb R^n$ is essentially smooth exactly when $f^*$ is essentially
  strictly convex. If $f$ is of Legendre type (both properties), then
  $\nabla f$ is a bijection from $\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f$
  onto $\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f^*$ with inverse $\nabla f^*$,
  and $f^*$ is again of Legendre type.
* **21. Subdifferential sum rule.** For proper lower semicontinuous convex
  functions on a Banach space, if one is continuous at a point of their common
  domain, then $\partial(f+g)(x)=\partial f(x)+\partial g(x)$.
  In finite dimensions, intersecting relative interiors is a standard
  alternative qualification.
* **22. Linear chain rule.** For bounded linear $A:E\to F$, if $g$ is proper
  lower semicontinuous convex and continuous at some $Ax_0$, then
  $\partial(g\circ A)(x)=A^*\partial g(Ax)$. Without a qualification one
  generally retains only the inclusion from right to left.
* **67. Danskin's maximum rule.** Let $K$ be compact and $\phi(x,t)$ be convex
  and $C^1$ in $x\in\mathbb R^n$, with $\phi$ and $\nabla_x\phi$ jointly
  continuous. Then for $f(x)=\max_{t\in K}\phi(x,t)$,
  $$\partial f(x)=\operatorname{conv}\{
     \nabla_x\phi(x,t):\phi(x,t)=f(x)\}.$$
  A unique maximizing gradient gives ordinary differentiability, even if there
  are several active indices producing that same gradient.
* **23. Infimal convolution.** For proper convex $f,g$, if
  $(f\mathbin\square g)(x)=\inf_y(f(y)+g(x-y))$ is proper, then it is convex
  and $(f\mathbin\square g)^*=f^*+g^*$. Lower semicontinuity and attainment
  of the infimum require additional hypotheses.
* **24. Support and indicator duality.** $\delta_C^*=h_C$ and, for nonempty
  $C$, $h_C^*=\delta_{\overline{\operatorname{conv}}C}$.
* **25. Bipolar theorem.** For $C^\circ=\{u:\langle u,x\rangle\leq1\ \forall x\in C\}$,
  $C^{\circ\circ}=\overline{\operatorname{conv}}(C\cup\{0\})$ in finite
  dimensions, and with the corresponding weak topology in dual-pair formulations.
* **26. Recession and bounded sublevels.** For proper closed convex $f$ in
  finite dimensions, its recession function satisfies
  $$f^\infty(d)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{f(x+td)-f(x)}t
    =\sup_{u\in\operatorname{dom}f^*}\langle u,d\rangle,$$
  independently of $x\in\operatorname{dom}f$. All sublevel sets are bounded
  exactly when $f^\infty(d)>0$ for every $d\ne0$.

## Minimizers, projections, and monotone operators

Here $H$ is a real Hilbert space. The **normal cone** to a closed convex $C$
is $N_C(x)=\{u:\langle u,y-x\rangle\leq0\ \forall y\in C\}$ for $x\in C$.
An operator $A:H\rightrightarrows H$ is **monotone** if
$\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq0$ on its graph; it is **maximal monotone** if
its graph has no proper monotone enlargement. Its resolvent is
$J_{\gamma A}=(I+\gamma A)^{-1}$. For nonempty closed convex $C$, $P_C$ is the
nearest-point projection (SYNOPSIS15, item 38). Minty's theorem, maximality
of convex subdifferentials, and the characterization of resolvents as firmly
nonexpansive maps are SYNOPSIS35, items 32, 38, and 33.

* **27. Fermat rule.** For convex $f$, $x$ is a global minimizer exactly when
  $0\in\partial f(x)$. Minimization over $C$ is minimization of $f+\delta_C$;
  a sum-rule qualification is needed to replace its subdifferential by
  $\partial f+N_C$.
* **28. Direct method for convex functions.** On a reflexive Banach space,
  a proper norm-lower-semicontinuous convex coercive function attains its minimum.
  Convex norm-closed sublevel sets are weakly closed, and bounded ones are
  weakly compact.
* **29. Strong convexity.** If $f$ is proper and lower semicontinuous on $H$
  and $f-\frac\mu2\|\cdot\|^2$ is convex, $\mu>0$, then a minimizer $x_*$
  exists, is unique, and satisfies
  $f(x)-f(x_*)\geq\frac\mu2\|x-x_*\|^2$. Its subdifferential is
  $\mu$-strongly monotone.
* **31. Normal-cone resolvent.** $J_{\gamma N_C}=P_C$ for $\gamma>0$;
  equivalently, $u\in N_C(x)$ exactly when $P_C(x+\gamma u)=x$.
* **66. Tangent and intersection rules.** For closed convex $C$ and $x\in C$,
  the tangent cone is $T_C(x)=\overline{\operatorname{cone}(C-x)}$ and
  $N_C(x)=T_C(x)^\circ$, using the nonpositive polar convention for cones.
  If $\operatorname{ri}C\cap\operatorname{ri}D\ne\varnothing$ in finite
  dimensions, $N_{C\cap D}(x)=N_C(x)+N_D(x)$.
* **32. Proximal mapping.** For proper lower semicontinuous convex $f$,
  $$\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma f}(x)=
    \operatorname*{argmin}_y\left(f(y)+\frac{\|x-y\|^2}{2\gamma}\right)$$
  exists uniquely and equals $J_{\gamma\partial f}x$. It is firmly nonexpansive.
* **33. Moreau envelope.** The infimum in result 32 defines a finite convex
  differentiable function $e_\gamma f$, with
  $\nabla e_\gamma f(x)=\gamma^{-1}(x-\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma f}x)$
  and gradient Lipschitz constant $1/\gamma$. As $\gamma\downarrow0$,
  $e_\gamma f$ increases pointwise to $f$, and $e_\gamma f$ has the same
  infimum and minimizers as $f$.
* **34. Moreau decomposition.**
  $\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma f}(x)+
  \gamma\operatorname{prox}_{f^*/\gamma}(x/\gamma)=x$.
  For indicators of closed convex cones, this becomes orthogonal decomposition
  into the cone and its polar.
* **38. Baillon–Haddad theorem.** If a convex differentiable function has
  $L$-Lipschitz gradient, $L>0$, then
  $$\langle\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle
    \geq L^{-1}\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|^2.$$

## Duality and optimality conditions

* **39. Fenchel–Rockafellar duality.** For proper closed convex $f,g$ on
  Banach spaces and bounded linear $A$, assume $f(x_0)<\infty$ and $g$ is
  continuous at $Ax_0$. If the primal value is finite, then
  $$\inf_x\{f(x)+g(Ax)\}
    =\max_u\{-f^*(-A^*u)-g^*(u)\}.$$
  Dual attainment is asserted; primal attainment is a separate question.
* **40. Slater and KKT conditions.** For finite convex functions in
  $\mathbb R^n$, constraints $g_i\leq0$, and affine equalities $Ax=b$, a
  strictly feasible point implies that every minimizer has multipliers
  $\lambda_i\geq0,y$ with
  $$0\in\partial f(x_*)+\sum_i\lambda_i\partial g_i(x_*)+A^*y,
    \qquad\lambda_ig_i(x_*)=0.$$
  These conditions are sufficient for optimality even without strict feasibility.
* **41. Farkas alternative.** Exactly one of the following is possible:
  $Ax=b$ with $x\geq0$; or $A^*y\geq0$ with $\langle b,y\rangle<0$.
* **42. Linear-programming strong duality.** A feasible finite-dimensional
  linear program with finite optimum and its standard dual have equal values,
  both attained. Complementary slackness characterizes optimal primal–dual pairs.
* **43. Conic duality.** For a closed convex cone $K$ with nonempty interior,
  the program $\inf\{\langle c,x\rangle:Ax=b,x\in K\}$ has dual
  $\sup\{\langle b,y\rangle:c-A^*y\in K^*\}$.
  Primal strict feasibility and finite primal value imply equality and dual
  attainment. Strict dual feasibility gives the corresponding primal conclusion.
* **44. Convex minimax theorem.** If $X$ is compact convex, $Y$ convex, and
  $F$ is continuous, convex in $x$, and concave in $y$, then
  $\inf_{x\in X}\sup_{y\in Y}F(x,y)=\sup_{y\in Y}\inf_{x\in X}F(x,y)$.
  Semicontinuous quasiconvex–quasiconcave versions are provided by Sion's theorem.

## Variational convergence, nonsmooth calculus, and regularity

**Epi-convergence** $f_n\to f$ means the lower bound
$f(x)\leq\liminf f_n(x_n)$ for every $x_n\to x$, together with a recovery
sequence at each $x$ giving the reverse upper bound. **Mosco convergence** on
a Hilbert space uses weakly convergent sequences for the lower bound and
strongly convergent recovery sequences. For a locally Lipschitz function,
$f^\circ(x;d)=\limsup_{y\to x,t\downarrow0}(f(y+td)-f(y))/t$ and
$\partial_Cf(x)=\{u:\langle u,d\rangle\leq f^\circ(x;d)\ \forall d\}$.

* **45. Ekeland variational principle.** On a complete metric space, let $f$
  be proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below. If
  $f(x_0)\leq\inf f+\varepsilon$, then for every $\lambda>0$ some $x_\varepsilon$
  has $d(x_\varepsilon,x_0)\leq\lambda$, $f(x_\varepsilon)\leq f(x_0)$, and
  $f(y)>f(x_\varepsilon)-(\varepsilon/\lambda)d(y,x_\varepsilon)$ for $y\ne x_\varepsilon$.
  For differentiable functionals this produces minimizing Palais–Smale
  sequences.
* **68. Brøndsted–Rockafellar approximation.** For a proper closed convex
  function on a Banach space, let $u$ be an **$\varepsilon$-subgradient** at
  $x$, $u\in\partial_\varepsilon f(x)$, meaning
  $f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle u,y-x\rangle-\varepsilon$ for all $y$. Then every
  $\lambda>0$ admits an exact subgradient $u_\lambda\in\partial f(x_\lambda)$
  with
  $\|x_\lambda-x\|\leq\lambda$ and
  $\|u_\lambda-u\|\leq\varepsilon/\lambda$. In particular
  $\operatorname{dom}\partial f$ is dense in $\operatorname{dom}f$.
* **46. Berge maximum theorem.** A continuous objective over a nonempty
  compact-valued continuous feasible correspondence has continuous optimal
  value and an upper semicontinuous compact-valued optimizer correspondence.
* **47. Fundamental theorem of epi-convergence.** For proper closed functions
  on $\mathbb R^n$, epi-convergence to a proper function and equicoercivity
  imply convergence of the infima, which are finite. Sequences of
  $\varepsilon_n$-minimizers with $\varepsilon_n\to0$ have convergent
  subsequences, and every such limit minimizes the limit function.
* **48. Attouch's theorem.** For proper closed convex functions on $H$,
  Mosco convergence $f_n\to f$ is equivalent to pointwise convergence
  $e_\gamma f_n\to e_\gamma f$ of Moreau envelopes for every $\gamma>0$.
  It is also equivalent to strong pointwise convergence of the proximal
  mappings $\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma f_n}$ together with a normalization
  fixing additive constants, such as convergence
  $f_n(x_n)\to f(x)$ along one sequence $(x_n,u_n)\in\operatorname{gph}\partial f_n$
  converging to some $(x,u)\in\operatorname{gph}\partial f$.
* **49. Convex-set convergence and projection.** Nonempty closed convex sets
  converge in the Mosco sense exactly when their projections converge strongly
  at every point. In finite dimensions this is the usual inner-and-outer
  Painlevé–Kuratowski convergence of sets.
* **50. Clarke gradient representation.** In finite dimensions, $\partial_Cf(x)$
  is nonempty compact convex and equals the closed convex hull of limits of
  ordinary gradients at differentiability points tending to $x$.
  Its support function is $f^\circ(x;\cdot)$.
* **51. Nonsmooth Fermat rule.** A local minimizer of a locally Lipschitz
  function satisfies $0\in\partial_Cf(x)$. This is necessary, rather than
  sufficient, outside convexity.
* **52. Clarke chain rule.** For $F$ of class $C^1$ and locally Lipschitz $g$,
  $\partial_C(g\circ F)(x)\subseteq DF(x)^*\partial_Cg(F(x))$, with equality
  when $g$ is Clarke regular (for example convex) or $DF(x)$ is surjective.
  Similarly $\partial_C(f+g)\subseteq\partial_Cf+\partial_Cg$, with equality
  for regular functions.
* **53. Generic differentiability of convex functions.** On a separable
  Banach space, a continuous convex function on an open convex set is Gâteaux
  differentiable on a dense $G_\delta$. On an Asplund space, the corresponding
  generic conclusion is Fréchet differentiability.
  A Banach space is Asplund when every separable subspace has separable dual;
  Hilbert spaces and reflexive Banach spaces are standard examples.
* **54. Lyusternik–Graves theorem.** If a $C^1$ map between Banach spaces has
  surjective derivative at $x_0$, then locally
  $\operatorname{dist}(x,F^{-1}(y))\leq C\|F(x)-y\|$ for $x$ near $x_0$
  and $y$ near $F(x_0)$. Surjectivity gives metric regularity even when a
  single-valued inverse is unavailable.
* **55. Hoffman's error bound.** For a nonempty polyhedron
  $C=\{x:Ax\leq b,Ex=d\}$, there is $\kappa<\infty$ such that
  $\operatorname{dist}(x,C)\leq\kappa(\|(Ax-b)_+\|+\|Ex-d\|)$ for all $x$.

## Limiting subdifferentials and nonlinear programming

Here sets are closed in $\mathbb R^n$ and functions are proper and lower
semicontinuous. The **regular normal cone** $\widehat N_C(x)$ consists of
$v$ with $\langle v,y-x\rangle\leq o(|y-x|)$ for $y\in C$; the **limiting
(Mordukhovich) normal cone** $N^L_C(x)$ consists of limits of
$v_k\in\widehat N_C(x_k)$ with $C\ni x_k\to x$. Similarly
$v\in\widehat\partial f(x)$ if $f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle v,y-x\rangle+o(|y-x|)$,
and the **limiting subdifferential** $\partial_Lf(x)$ consists of limits of
$v_k\in\widehat\partial f(x_k)$ with $x_k\to x$ and $f(x_k)\to f(x)$. For a
closed-graph set-valued map $F:\mathbb R^n\rightrightarrows\mathbb R^m$ and
$\bar y\in F(\bar x)$, the **coderivative** is
$D^*F(\bar x|\bar y)(y^*)=\{x^*:(x^*,-y^*)\in N^L_{\operatorname{gph}F}(\bar x,\bar y)\}$.
$F$ has the **Aubin property** at $\bar x$ for $\bar y$ if, for neighbourhoods
$U\ni\bar x$, $V\ni\bar y$ and some $\kappa$,
$F(x')\cap V\subseteq F(x)+\kappa|x'-x|B$ for $x,x'\in U$.

* **70. Limiting subdifferential.** $\widehat\partial f(x)\subseteq\partial_Lf(x)$,
  and a local minimizer satisfies $0\in\widehat\partial f(x)$. Both sets equal
  the convex subdifferential for convex $f$ and equal $\{\nabla f(x)\}$ for
  $C^1$ $f$; $N^L_C=\partial_L\delta_C$. For locally Lipschitz $f$,
  $\partial_Lf(x)$ is nonempty and compact, and its convex hull is the Clarke
  subdifferential $\partial_Cf(x)$; likewise the Clarke normal cone is the
  closed convex hull of $N^L_C(x)$. Thus $\partial_L$ is generally smaller
  and nonconvex, as for $f(x)=-|x|$, where $\partial_Lf(0)=\{-1,1\}$.
* **71. Limiting calculus rules.** If $f_1$ is lower semicontinuous and $f_2$
  is locally Lipschitz near $x$, then
  $\partial_L(f_1+f_2)(x)\subseteq\partial_Lf_1(x)+\partial_Lf_2(x)$. If
  $N^L_C(x)\cap(-N^L_D(x))=\{0\}$, then
  $N^L_{C\cap D}(x)\subseteq N^L_C(x)+N^L_D(x)$. For $C=\{x:G(x)\in D\}$ with
  $G$ of class $C^1$, if the only $y\in N^L_D(G(x))$ with $\nabla G(x)^*y=0$
  is $y=0$, then $N^L_C(x)\subseteq\nabla G(x)^*N^L_D(G(x))$, with equality
  when $D$ is convex (Rockafellar–Wets, Mordukhovich).
* **72. Extremal principle.** Let $\bar x$ be a local extremal point of closed
  sets $\Omega_1,\Omega_2\subseteq\mathbb R^n$: there are a neighbourhood $U$
  and $a_k\to0$ with $(\Omega_1-a_k)\cap\Omega_2\cap U=\varnothing$. Then
  some $v\neq0$ has $v\in N^L_{\Omega_1}(\bar x)$ and
  $-v\in N^L_{\Omega_2}(\bar x)$ (Mordukhovich). This nonconvex substitute for
  separation yields the rules in result 71; in Banach spaces an approximate
  version with regular normals at nearby points holds exactly for Asplund
  spaces.
* **73. Mordukhovich criterion.** A closed-graph $F$ has the Aubin property
  at $\bar x$ for $\bar y$ exactly when $D^*F(\bar x|\bar y)(0)=\{0\}$; the
  infimum of admissible constants $\kappa$ is then
  $\sup\{|x^*|:x^*\in D^*F(\bar x|\bar y)(y^*),\ |y^*|\leq1\}$. Since $F$ is
  metrically regular at $\bar x$ for $\bar y$ exactly when $F^{-1}$ has the
  Aubin property at $\bar y$ for $\bar x$, metric regularity is equivalent to
  $0\in D^*F(\bar x|\bar y)(y^*)\Rightarrow y^*=0$. For $C^1$ single-valued
  $F$ this is surjectivity of $\nabla F(\bar x)$, as in result 54.
* **74. KKT conditions under constraint qualifications.** Let $x_*$ be a
  local minimizer of $f$ subject to $g_i(x)\leq0$, $i\in I$, and $h_j(x)=0$,
  $j\in J$, with $C^1$ data, and let $I_0$ be the active inequality indices.
  Without qualification there are $\lambda_0,\lambda_i\geq0$ and $\mu_j$, not
  all zero, with $\lambda_ig_i(x_*)=0$ and
  $\lambda_0\nabla f+\sum_i\lambda_i\nabla g_i+\sum_j\mu_j\nabla h_j=0$ at
  $x_*$ (Fritz John). Under the **Mangasarian–Fromovitz condition**
  (the $\nabla h_j(x_*)$ are linearly independent and some $d$ has
  $\nabla h_j(x_*)d=0$, $\nabla g_i(x_*)d<0$ for $i\in I_0$) one can take
  $\lambda_0=1$, and the KKT multiplier set is nonempty and compact; MFCQ is
  equivalent to this compactness (Gauvin). Under the **linear independence
  condition** (the active gradients $\nabla g_i(x_*)$, $i\in I_0$, and
  $\nabla h_j(x_*)$ are linearly independent) the multipliers are unique.
* **75. Second-order optimality conditions.** With $C^2$ data, Lagrangian
  $L(x,\lambda,\mu)=f+\sum_i\lambda_ig_i+\sum_j\mu_jh_j$, and critical cone
  $K=\{d:\nabla h_j(x_*)d=0,\ \nabla g_i(x_*)d\leq0\ (i\in I_0),\
  \nabla f(x_*)d\leq0\}$: if LICQ holds at a local minimizer with multipliers
  $(\lambda,\mu)$, then $d^{\mathsf T}\nabla_x^2L\,d\geq0$ for all $d\in K$.
  Conversely, if $(x_*,\lambda,\mu)$ satisfies the KKT conditions and
  $d^{\mathsf T}\nabla_x^2L\,d>0$ for all nonzero $d\in K$, then $x_*$ is a
  strict local minimizer with quadratic growth
  $f(x)\geq f(x_*)+c|x-x_*|^2$ near $x_*$ on the feasible set. Under MFCQ
  alone the necessary condition holds with a maximum over the multiplier set.

## Basic convergence theorems for convex algorithms

The Browder–Göhde–Kirk fixed-point theorem and Krasnosel'skiĭ–Mann iteration
are SYNOPSIS35, items 21 and 55; the methods below are instances of the latter
applied to averaged operators.

* **57. Proximal point method.** If $\operatorname{argmin}f\ne\varnothing$
  and $\gamma>0$, $x_{n+1}=\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma f}(x_n)$ converges
  weakly in $H$ to a minimizer, with
  $f(x_n)-\min f\leq\|x_0-x_*\|^2/(2\gamma n)$ for $n\geq1$. Strong
  convergence can fail (Güler). More generally, the iteration
  $x_{n+1}=J_{\gamma A}x_n$ converges weakly to a zero of a maximal monotone
  $A$ whenever $A^{-1}(0)\ne\varnothing$ (Rockafellar).
* **58. Projected gradient.** For convex $f$ with $L$-Lipschitz gradient and
  nonempty minimizer set on closed convex $C$, the iteration
  $x_{n+1}=P_C(x_n-\gamma\nabla f(x_n))$ converges weakly for $0<\gamma<2/L$.
  For $\gamma\leq1/L$ and $x_0\in C$, objective errors are $O(1/n)$.
* **59. Forward–backward splitting.** For convex $L$-smooth $f$ and proper
  closed convex $g$, assume $\operatorname{argmin}(f+g)\ne\varnothing$.
  The iteration $x_{n+1}=\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma g}
  (x_n-\gamma\nabla f(x_n))$ converges weakly to a minimizer for
  $0<\gamma<2/L$, with the standard $O(1/n)$ objective bound for $\gamma\leq1/L$.
* **60. Accelerated gradient bound.** For a convex $L$-smooth function on
  $\mathbb R^n$, set $t_0=1$, $y_0=x_0$ and
  $$x_{k+1}=y_k-L^{-1}\nabla f(y_k),\quad
    t_{k+1}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4t_k^2}}2,\quad
    y_{k+1}=x_{k+1}+\frac{t_k-1}{t_{k+1}}(x_{k+1}-x_k).$$
  If a minimizer $x_*$ exists,
  $f(x_k)-f(x_*)\leq2L\|x_0-x_*\|^2/(k+1)^2$ for $k\geq1$. The rate is
  optimal (Nesterov): for $1\leq k\leq(n-1)/2$ there is a convex $L$-smooth
  quadratic on $\mathbb R^n$ such that every method with
  $x_k\in x_0+\operatorname{span}\{\nabla f(x_0),\ldots,\nabla f(x_{k-1})\}$
  has $f(x_k)-f(x_*)\geq3L\|x_0-x_*\|^2/(32(k+1)^2)$.
* **61. Subgradient complexity bound.** For a convex globally $G$-Lipschitz
  function, projected subgradient steps on $C$ with
  $x_0\in C$, $\|x_0-x_*\|\leq R$, $G,R>0$, and constant step
  $R/(G\sqrt N)$ give a best-iterate,
  and an averaged-iterate, objective gap at most $RG/\sqrt N$ after $N$ steps.
* **62. Alternating projections.** For closed convex $C,D\subset H$ with
  nonempty intersection, $(P_CP_D)^nx_0$ converges weakly to a point of the
  intersection. For closed subspaces it converges strongly to
  $P_{C\cap D}x_0$; the nearest-point conclusion is special to that setting.
* **63. Douglas–Rachford splitting.** In finite dimensions, for proper closed
  convex $f,g$ with $\operatorname{zer}(\partial f+\partial g)\ne\varnothing$,
  $$z_{n+1}=z_n+\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma f}
     (2\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma g}z_n-z_n)
       -\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma g}z_n$$
  converges to a fixed point, and $\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma g}z_n$ converges
  to a minimizer of $f+g$. In a Hilbert space both convergences hold weakly.

## Further conjugacy and subgradient structure

A relation is **cyclically monotone** if every finite cycle in
its graph satisfies
$\sum_i\langle u_i,x_{i+1}-x_i\rangle\leq0$, with $x_{m+1}=x_1$.
Ordinary monotonicity is the two-point case. This notion explains the
additional integrability property that makes a monotone relation the
derivative of a convex potential.

* **64. Strong convexity and smooth conjugacy.** For proper closed convex
  functions on $H$, $\mu$-strong convexity of $f$, $\mu>0$, is equivalent to
  $f^*$ being everywhere Fréchet differentiable with $1/\mu$-Lipschitz gradient.
  In particular, strong convexity turns the inverse subdifferential into the
  single-valued map $\nabla f^*$. Dually, an everywhere differentiable
  $L$-smooth closed convex function has $1/L$-strongly convex conjugate.
* **65. Closed perspectives.** For proper closed convex $f$ on $\mathbb R^n$,
  its perspective $p(x,t)=tf(x/t)$, $t>0$, is convex. Its closed extension has
  $p(x,0)=f^\infty(x)$ and value infinity for $t<0$.
* **69. Rockafellar's cyclic-monotonicity theorem.** Maximally cyclically
  monotone relations on a Hilbert space are exactly subdifferentials of proper
  lower semicontinuous convex functions, the potential being unique up to an
  additive constant. General maximal monotone operators, such as a nonzero
  skew-symmetric linear map, need not have a convex potential.

## Reference guide

Rockafellar, Hiriart-Urruty–Lemaréchal, and Borwein–Lewis give overlapping
finite-dimensional developments of separation, conjugacy, subgradients, and
duality. Bauschke–Combettes, Chapters 12–20 and the splitting chapters, provides
the Hilbert-space proximal and monotone-operator framework. Rockafellar–Wets
develops epigraphical limits, normal cones, and variational stability; Clarke,
Penot, Borwein–Zhu, and Dontchev–Rockafellar develop the nonsmooth and
metric-regularity endpoint. Mordukhovich, *Variational Analysis and
Generalized Differentiation*, Rockafellar–Wets, Chapters 6, 8, and 9, and
Borwein–Zhu supply limiting normals and subdifferentials, the extremal
principle, and coderivative criteria; Güler, *Foundations of Optimization*,
and Borwein–Lewis treat the Fritz John, KKT, and second-order conditions of
smooth nonlinear programming.

The compact-convex representation material is treated by Simon and Phelps;
the geometric theorems by Webster, Hug–Weil, Gruber, and Barvinok. Boyd–Vandenberghe
and Borwein–Lewis connect duality to linear and conic optimization. The final
algorithmic results recur in Bauschke–Combettes, Beck, Nesterov, and Peypouquet;
their inclusion records mathematical convergence principles, rather than an
inventory of optimization implementations.
