## What is present nearby

The power-series exponential of a complete normed algebra over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\) satisfies \(\exp((s+t)a)=\exp(sa)\exp(ta)\), because \(sa\) and \(ta\) commute. The orbit \(t\mapsto\exp(ta)\) is therefore a norm-continuous one-parameter group inside the group of units (`Analysis/Normed/Algebra/Exponential.lean`). Section 20 states the exponential. This identity is not a generation theorem for strongly continuous semigroups.

The continuous functional calculus of a normal or self-adjoint element of a C⋆-algebra is the star-isometric equivalence of sections 21 and 23, from continuous functions on the spectrum onto the closed star-subalgebra generated by the element (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Basic.lean`).

Ordinary differential equations, including the Picard–Lindelöf theorem, are section 31 (`Analysis/ODE/PicardLindelof.lean`). Algebraic semigroups lie outside this note.

## Additional coverage in TauCeti

The following additions were checked in TauCeti revision `6e53de0d3ce9`; see the [revision, build evidence, and comparison scope](TauCetiCoverage.md). Source links below are pinned to that revision.

TauCeti contains a developed theory of strongly continuous semigroups on Banach spaces. It defines their partially defined generators and proves density of the generator domain, closedness, orbit differentiation, and resolvent estimates. The Hille–Yosida theorem is an actual generation equivalence: for a real Banach space, growth bound \(M e^{\omega t}\) is characterized by dense domain, resolvent existence for every real \(\lambda>\omega\), and the bounds \(\|R(\lambda,A)^n\|\le M/(\lambda-\omega)^n\), with \(M\ge1\) ([Hille–Yosida](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6e53de0d3ce9ea24d7d487e656ac4590b0c45b3a/TauCeti/Analysis/Semigroups/Generation/HilleYosida/Generation.lean)). Dense maximal dissipativity characterizes generators of contraction semigroups, in the Lumer–Phillips theorem ([source](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6e53de0d3ce9ea24d7d487e656ac4590b0c45b3a/TauCeti/Analysis/Semigroups/Generation/LumerPhillips.lean)).

Semigroup orbits are unique mild solutions of \(u'=Au\), and unique classical solutions for initial data in the generator domain ([solutions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6e53de0d3ce9ea24d7d487e656ac4590b0c45b3a/TauCeti/Analysis/Semigroups/CauchyProblem/Basic.lean), [uniqueness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6e53de0d3ce9ea24d7d487e656ac4590b0c45b3a/TauCeti/Analysis/Semigroups/CauchyProblem/Uniqueness.lean)). Bounded perturbations \(A+B\) generate semigroups on the same domain, with the corresponding exponential growth estimate ([perturbation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6e53de0d3ce9ea24d7d487e656ac4590b0c45b3a/TauCeti/Analysis/Semigroups/Generation/BoundedPerturbation.lean)). Stone's theorem for unitary groups is recorded in Section 22. This fills the original semigroup and autonomous abstract-evolution gaps, but does not establish sectorial-operator or holomorphic-semigroup theory.
