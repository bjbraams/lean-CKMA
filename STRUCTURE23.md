## C*-algebras, positivity, and representations

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS23.md#c-algebras-positivity-and-representations).

### Mathlib

**The C⋆-identity.**

A normed star group has an isometric star. A C⋆-ring is a normed star ring satisfying \(\lVert x\rVert^2\le\lVert x^\star x\rVert\). The inequality is an equality: \(\lVert x^\star x\rVert = \lVert x\rVert^2 = \lVert x^\star\rVert\,\lVert x\rVert\) (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Basic.lean`).

A non-unital complex C⋆-algebra is a complete normed star algebra over \(\mathbb{C}\), with conjugate-linear star, satisfying the C⋆-identity. The unital, commutative, and non-unital commutative variants are the corresponding classes. A closed star subalgebra inherits the class. On a complete complex inner product space, the bounded endomorphisms form a unital C⋆-algebra (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Classes.lean`, `Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousLinearMap.lean`). Square matrices with entries in a C⋆-algebra, normed as operators on the Hilbert module \(A^n\), form a C⋆-algebra (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/CStarMatrix.lean`).

**Spectrum.**

In a complete C⋆-ring over a normed field, the spectrum of a unitary lies on the unit circle. In a unital complex C⋆-algebra, a self-adjoint element has real spectrum, the complement of that spectrum is connected, and the spectral radius equals the norm. A normal element likewise has spectral radius equal to its norm. The square root of the spectral radius of \(a^\star a\), and of \(a a^\star\), equals \(\lVert a\rVert\) (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Spectrum.lean`).

Spectral permanence: if \(S\) is a closed unital C⋆-subalgebra of a unital complex C⋆-algebra and \(x\in S\), the spectrum of \(x\) in \(S\) equals its spectrum in the ambient algebra. The argument uses the connectedness of the complement of the spectrum of a self-adjoint element. The file asks for the same permanence for star homomorphisms and for the quasispectrum; those variants are unproved (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Spectrum.lean`). A star homomorphism between non-unital complex C⋆-algebras is contractive (`NonUnitalStarAlgHom.norm_apply_le`), a star isomorphism is isometric (`StarAlgEquiv.isometry`), which gives uniqueness of the C⋆-norm, and an injective star homomorphism is isometric (`NonUnitalStarAlgHom.norm_map`, `Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Hom.lean`). Every element \(x\) of a unital C⋆-algebra is a linear combination of four unitaries with coefficients of norm at most \(\lVert x\rVert/2\), so the unitaries span the algebra (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Unitary/Span.lean`).

**Continuous functional calculus, in this setting.**

Section 21 records the star-algebra equivalence \(C(\sigma(a),\mathbb{C})\simeq\) the C⋆-subalgebra generated by a normal element \(a\), its isometry, and its uniqueness. What is special to the C⋆-setting is the source of that equivalence: the Gelfand transform of the commutative C⋆-algebra generated by \(a\), transported along the homeomorphism that evaluates a character at \(a\). The same calculus, restricted along the real part, is the calculus for self-adjoint elements (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Basic.lean`). The calculus of \(a\) lies in every closed star subalgebra containing \(a\), and its range is the elemental subalgebra when the calculus is a closed embedding (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Range.lean`); it commutes with Bochner integrals of continuous families of symbols (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Integral.lean`).

**Positivity and order.**

In a star-ordered ring, \(x\le y\) if and only if \(y-x\) lies in the additive submonoid generated by elements of the form \(s^\star s\) (`Algebra/Order/Star/Basic.lean`).

A C⋆-algebra also carries the spectral order, defined by declaring \(x\le y\) when \(y-x\) is self-adjoint and its quasispectrum lies in the nonnegative reals. This order is a partial order and a star-order. It is not registered as the ambient order, because another order may already be present (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Basic.lean`).

Once a C⋆-algebra is star-ordered, the conditions \(0\le a\), \(a=b^\star b\) for some \(b\), \(a=bb^\star\), \(a=c^2\) with \(c\ge0\), and \(a=c^2\) with \(c\) self-adjoint are equivalent (`CStarAlgebra.nonneg_iff_eq_star_mul_self`, `CStarAlgebra.nonneg_iff_exists_nonneg_and_eq_mul_self`, `CStarAlgebra.nonneg_iff_eq_sqrt_mul_sqrt`, `Analysis/SpecialFunctions/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Rpow/Basic.lean`), and the positive square root is unique (`CFC.sqrt_unique`). A nonnegative element has nonnegative quasispectrum, and an element is nonnegative if and only if it is self-adjoint with quasispectrum in the nonnegative reals. Commuting nonnegative elements have nonnegative product. A self-adjoint element \(a\) satisfies \(-\lVert a\rVert\le a\le\lVert a\rVert\) (`IsSelfAdjoint.le_algebraMap_norm_self`, `IsSelfAdjoint.neg_algebraMap_norm_le_self`), and conversely \(a\le b\le c\) with \(b\) self-adjoint gives \(\lVert b\rVert\le\max(\lVert a\rVert,\lVert c\rVert)\) (`IsSelfAdjoint.norm_le_max_of_le_of_le`), so \(\lVert a\rVert\le r\) exactly when \(-r\le a\le r\). The positive cone is closed (`CStarAlgebra.isClosed_nonneg`), and every self-adjoint element is \(a^+-a^-\) (`CFC.posPart_sub_negPart`). On the positive cone the norm is monotone. In the unital case, inversion reverses the order of positive invertible elements (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Instances.lean`, `Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Order.lean`).

A positive linear map between non-unital star-ordered C⋆-algebras is bounded (`PositiveLinearMap.exists_norm_apply_le`). A continuous linear functional on such an algebra is monotone if and only if it tends to its operator norm along any increasing approximate unit. On a unital algebra, a positive functional has \(\lVert f\rVert = f(1)\) (`PositiveContinuousLinearMap.ofReal_opNorm_eq_map_one`), and a continuous functional is monotone exactly when \(\lVert f\rVert=f(1)\) (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/PositiveLinearMap.lean`, `Analysis/CStarAlgebra/PositiveLinearFunctional.lean`).

**The Gelfand–Naimark–Segal construction.**

Let \(f\) be a positive linear functional on a non-unital star-ordered complex C⋆-algebra. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for such a functional reads
\[
\lVert f(x^\star y)\rVert \le \sqrt{\lVert f(x^\star x)\rVert}\,\sqrt{\lVert f(y^\star y)\rVert}.
\]
The pre-GNS space is the algebra itself, with inner product \(\langle a,b\rangle = f(a^\star b)\). The GNS space is the Hilbert completion of that pre-inner-product space. Left multiplication by \(a\) extends to a bounded operator on the pre-GNS space of norm at most \(\lVert a\rVert\), and passes to the completion. The resulting map is a non-unital star homomorphism into the bounded operators on the GNS space. When the algebra and the functional are unital, the homomorphism is unital (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/GelfandNaimarkSegal.lean`, `PositiveLinearMap.GNS`, `gnsNonUnitalStarAlgHom`, `gnsStarAlgHom`). The module lists the unit cyclic vector realizing \(f\) as a vector state as future work.

**Gelfand duality.**

Section 20 records characters, the Gelfand transform of a topological algebra, and spectrum preservation in a commutative complex Banach algebra. When the algebra is a commutative unital complex C⋆-algebra, the Gelfand transform is a star homomorphism, an isometry, and bijective onto the continuous complex functions on the character space. The resulting star-algebra equivalence is natural in star homomorphisms of commutative unital C⋆-algebras (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/GelfandDuality.lean`).

In the other direction, a compact Hausdorff space \(X\) is homeomorphic to the character space of \(C(X,\mathbb{k})\) for \(\mathbb{k}=\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), by evaluation, and this homeomorphism is natural in continuous maps (`Topology/ContinuousMap/Ideals.lean`). The file records both naturality squares and describes them as the data of a contravariant equivalence of categories. The categories themselves, and a bundled equivalence, are left unconstructed.

For a non-unital commutative complex C⋆-algebra, if \(ab=0\) then \(\lVert a+b\rVert = \max(\lVert a\rVert,\lVert b\rVert)\). This is deduced from the unital duality by unitization. A full non-unital duality with \(C_0(X)\) is not stated (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/GelfandDuality.lean`).

**Approximate units, projections, unitization, multipliers.**

In a non-unital star-ordered C⋆-algebra, the positive elements of norm strictly less than one are directed. The filter generated by the sections \(\{x: a\le x\}\) inside the closed unit ball, as \(a\) runs through those positive strict contractions, is an increasing approximate unit (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ApproximateUnit.lean`).

In the presence of the continuous functional calculus, a star projection is an idempotent normal element, equivalently a self-adjoint idempotent whose spectrum, or quasispectrum, lies in \(\{0,1\}\). For star projections \(p\) and \(q\) in a non-unital star-ordered C⋆-algebra, \(p\le q\) if and only if \(qp=p\), if and only if \(pq=p\), if and only if \(q-p\) is a star projection, if and only if \(q-p\) is idempotent (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Projection.lean`).

Every C⋆-algebra has regular norm, so the minimal unitization of section 20 is available. That unitization over \(\mathbb{C}\) is a C⋆-algebra. When \(A\) is already unital, the unitization is star-isomorphic to \(\mathbb{C}\times A\). If the star is isometric, right multiplication is an isometry as well (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Unitization.lean`).

The multiplier algebra \(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{k},A)\) is the algebra of double centralizers: pairs of continuous linear maps \(L,R\) on \(A\) with \(R(x)y = x\,L(y)\). The algebra embeds by left and right multiplication. The norm is the operator norm of either component. When \(A\) is a non-unital complex C⋆-algebra, the multiplier algebra is a C⋆-algebra (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Multiplier.lean`).

**Fuglede, the exponential, and the unitary group.**

In a unital C⋆-algebra, if \(a\) and \(b\) are normal and \(xa=bx\), then \(x a^\star = b^\star x\). This is the Fuglede–Putnam–Rosenblum theorem. Separately, \(a\) is normal if and only if \(\exp(xa)\exp(-x a^\star)\) is unitary for every real scalar \(x\) (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Fuglede.lean`).

On a complete normed complex algebra with continuous star, \(\exp(i a)\) is unitary whenever \(a\) is self-adjoint. Commuting self-adjoint elements give a homomorphism from their sum to the product of the exponentials (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Exponential.lean`).

The star projections are exactly the extreme points of the nonnegative part of the closed unit ball, in a non-unital star-ordered C⋆-algebra (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Extreme.lean`).

The unitary group of a unital C⋆-algebra is locally path-connected. Any ball of radius \(\delta<2\) is path-connected. If \(\lVert u-1\rVert<2\), the spectrum of \(u\) lies in the slit plane, the continuous functional calculus supplies a self-adjoint logarithm. For a self-adjoint \(x\) with \(\|x\|\le\pi\),
\[
\lVert \exp(ix)-1\rVert^2 = 2\bigl(1-\cos\lVert x\rVert\bigr).
\]
These two maps form a partial homeomorphism between the ball of radius \(2\) about \(1\) in the unitary group and the ball of radius \(\pi\) about \(0\) in the self-adjoint part. The path component of \(1\) consists of the finite products of exponentials of self-adjoint elements (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Unitary/Connected.lean`).

## Von Neumann algebras and projection structure

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS23.md#von-neumann-algebras-and-projection-structure).

### Mathlib

**Von Neumann algebras.**

An abstract von Neumann algebra, under the name of a W⋆-algebra, is a C⋆-algebra for which some complex Banach space has strong dual isometrically and conjugate-linearly isomorphic to the algebra. The predual is asserted to exist and is not chosen (`Analysis/VonNeumannAlgebra/Basic.lean`).

A concrete von Neumann algebra on a complex Hilbert space \(H\) is a star-subalgebra of the bounded operators on \(H\) equal to its double commutant. The commutant is again a concrete von Neumann algebra, and the double commutant equals the algebra because that equality is the defining field. An idempotent lies in the algebra if and only if its range and kernel are invariant under the commutant. A star projection lies in the algebra if and only if its range is invariant under the commutant.

The file says that the equivalence of the abstract and concrete definitions, and the double commutant theorem identifying the weak closure with the double commutant, are still ahead. It also says that the bounded operators on \(H\) will later be shown to satisfy the abstract definition. The bounded operators are a C⋆-algebra by the result of section 21.

### TauCeti

For a bounded operator on a Hilbert space, the polar decomposition \(T=U\lvert T\rvert\) with a canonical partial isometry \(U\), its initial and final projections, and its uniqueness are proved ([polar decomposition](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Polar/Bounded.lean); Section 21). This is the case \(M=\mathcal B(H)\); membership of \(U\) in a general von Neumann algebra is not stated.

## Modular theory, equilibrium, and subfactors

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS23.md#modular-theory-equilibrium-and-subfactors).

### Mathlib

**Standard subspaces.**

A standard subspace of a complex inner product space is a closed real subspace \(S\) with \(S\cap iS = 0\) and \(S+iS\) dense in the whole space. The join used in the definition is the join of closed subspaces, hence the closure of the algebraic sum. Multiplication by \(i\) is an involution on these subspaces; on a complete space the symplectic complement is also an involution. The file describes these subspaces as the geometric setting for an analogue of Tomita–Takesaki theory, and it leaves the Tomita conjugation, Tomita’s theorem, and the KMS condition unproved (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/StandardSubspace.lean`).

## K-theory, extensions, and crossed products

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS23.md#k-theory-extensions-and-crossed-products).

### Mathlib

**Hilbert C⋆-modules.**

A Hilbert C⋆-module over a star ring \(A\) is a complex vector space with a right \(A\)-action and an \(A\)-valued inner product. The inner product is additive and \(A\)-linear in the right argument, conjugate-symmetric, positive definite, and complex-linear in the right argument. The norm is \(\lVert x\rVert = \sqrt{\lVert\langle x,x\rangle\rVert}\). The Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities are \(\lVert\langle x,y\rangle\rVert\le\lVert x\rVert\,\lVert y\rVert\) and \(\langle x,y\rangle\langle y,x\rangle\le\lVert x\rVert^2\,\langle y,y\rangle\). A normed-space core is provided so that a normed-space structure can be registered downstream. The class itself does not register a normed additive group (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Module/Defs.lean`).

This is the natural starting point for imprimitivity bimodules and Morita equivalence, which are not formalized. Finite products and pi types of Hilbert C⋆-modules, the algebra as a module over itself, and complex Hilbert spaces as modules over \(\mathbb C\) are constructed (`Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Module/Constructions.lean`).

### TauCeti

C⋆-dynamical systems are introduced: a continuous action of a topological group on a possibly nonunital C⋆-algebra by star automorphisms with norm-continuous orbits, with pullback along group homomorphisms and transport along star isomorphisms ([C⋆-actions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Action/Basic.lean)). The generator of a point-norm continuous action of \(\mathbb R\) is a closed, densely defined derivation on a star subalgebra, commuting with the star and with the action ([generators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Action/Generator.lean)).

The algebraic Bott periodicity of Clifford algebras ([Clifford periodicity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/CliffordAlgebra/BottPeriodicity.lean)) is supporting algebra without C⋆-algebra K-theory, and the Fredholm index of Section 21 is not connected to a boundary map.
