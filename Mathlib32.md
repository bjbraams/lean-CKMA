## The divergence theorem on a box

The Henstock–Kurzweil form uses a non-standard gauge integral on a closed rectangular box \(I\) in \(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\), identified with \(\mathrm{Fin}(n+1)\to\mathbb{R}\). Tags must lie in their boxes, the partition must be subordinate to a gauge, and every box is required to have bounded eccentricity: the ratios of its side lengths are at most a prescribed constant. In dimension one that eccentricity restriction is automatic for constants at least \(1\), so the integral reduces to the ordinary Henstock–Kurzweil integral (`Analysis/BoxIntegral/DivergenceTheorem.lean`).

If \(f\) takes values in \(E^{n+1}\), with \(E\) a real normed space, and \(f\) is continuous on \(I\) and Fréchet differentiable within \(I\) off a countable set, then the divergence \(\sum_i \partial_i f_i\), formed by applying the derivative to the \(i\)-th basis vector and reading the \(i\)-th component, is integrable in this gauge sense. Its integral equals the sum, over the \(n+1\) coordinate directions, of the integrals of the corresponding component of \(f\) over the upper face minus the integral over the lower face. The file describes this identity as a divergence theorem and tags it as Stokes’ theorem, but the statement is the rectangular divergence theorem, not Stokes’ theorem for differential forms.

The Bochner form assumes \(a\le b\) in \(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\) and a map \(f\) into \(E^{n+1}\) that is continuous on the closed box \([a,b]\), Fréchet differentiable on the interior off a countable set, and whose divergence is Bochner-integrable on the box (`MeasureTheory/Integral/DivergenceTheorem.lean`). The integral of the divergence over \([a,b]\) then equals the same alternating sum of face integrals. If some pair of opposite faces coincides, both sides are zero. An equivalent statement is given for a family of scalar-valued component functions. The same identity is rewritten for a linearly ordered normed space affinely identified with \(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\) by a volume-preserving equivalence, and there are two-dimensional specializations along rectangles in the plane, with and without a countable exceptional set. A version that assumes only partial derivatives, rather than the full Fréchet derivative, is marked as not yet proved.

No divergence theorem on a manifold, on a domain with smooth boundary, or for a differential form was found. Partitions of unity are available for manifolds, and alternating multilinear algebra is available, but neither is an integral Stokes theorem.

## Lax–Milgram

Let \(V\) be a real Hilbert space and let \(B:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}\) be a continuous bilinear form. The form is coercive when some \(C>0\) satisfies \(C\|u\|^2\le B(u,u)\) for every \(u\). Coercivity implies that the bounded operator \(B^\sharp:V\to V\) defined by \(\langle B^\sharp v,w\rangle=B(v,w)\) is bounded below, hence injective, has closed range, and is surjective. It is therefore a continuous linear automorphism of \(V\): for every \(\ell\in V^*\) there is a unique \(u\in V\) with \(B(u,w)=\ell(w)\) for every \(w\), and \(u\) depends continuously and linearly on \(\ell\). This follows by applying its inverse to the Riesz representative of \(\ell\) (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LaxMilgram.lean`). The scalar field is \(\mathbb{R}\). This is the nearest existence theorem for a linear elliptic variational equation; harmonic functions, including the Poisson integral formula on disks in the complex plane, are recorded in [Mathlib33.md](Mathlib33.md), where the same statement is repeated.

## Additional coverage in TauCeti

The following additions were checked in TauCeti revision `6ebb29f84b4d`; see the [revision, build evidence, and comparison scope](TauCetiCoverage.md). Source links below are pinned to that revision.

TauCeti defines weak divergence-form Dirichlet equations on \(H^1_0(\Omega)\). For bounded measurable coefficients and a coercive energy form, every \(L^2\) forcing has a unique weak solution, with an energy estimate. Concrete sufficient ellipticity, drift, and mass conditions imply coercivity; in particular the zero-boundary Poisson problem is solved on bounded open domains, without a smooth-boundary hypothesis. Nonzero boundary values \(g\in H^1(\Omega)\) are imposed as \(u-g\in H^1_0(\Omega)\), and the resulting problem again has a unique solution (`TauCeti.PDE.existsUnique_sub_mem_w1p0Submodule_forall_energyFormH1_eq`; [Dirichlet problem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/DirichletProblem.lean)). A function of class \(C^2\) on a bounded open \(\Omega\), continuous on the closure, zero on the boundary, with \(\Delta w\in L^2\), lies in \(H^1_0(\Omega)\) and is the weak solution for its own Laplacian; Green's first identity \(\int_\Omega\nabla v\cdot\nabla w=-\int_\Omega v\Delta w\) holds for \(v\in H^1_0(\Omega)\), with no boundary term and no regularity of \(\partial\Omega\) ([classical solutions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/ClassicalSolution.lean)). Weak maximum and comparison principles hold for subsolutions whose positive part lies in \(H^1_0\), under uniform ellipticity, \(c\ge0\), and a drift smallness condition ([weak maximum principle](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/MaximumPrinciple/Weak.lean)).

On bounded domains the solution operator is compact on \(L^2\), and a scalar mass shift satisfies the Fredholm alternative ([alternative](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/FredholmAlternative.lean)). With symmetric coercive energy form, Dirichlet eigenfunctions form an orthonormal basis of \(L^2\), with finite-dimensional eigenspaces and a Rayleigh characterization of the first eigenvalue ([spectrum](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Spectrum/Basic.lean)).

Interior regularity: if the principal coefficients are uniformly elliptic and locally Lipschitz on \(\Omega\), \(b,c\in L^\infty\), \(f\in L^2\), and \(u\in H^1(\Omega)\) is a weak solution with no boundary condition, then \(u\in H^2(V)\) for every \(V\Subset\Omega\) ([interior \(H^2\)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Regularity/Interior.lean)). For smooth principal coefficients and \(f\in W^{k,2}(\Omega)\), \(u\in H^{k+2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\), and smooth data give a solution a.e. equal to a smooth function ([interior \(H^k\)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Regularity/HigherOrder.lean), [smoothness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Regularity/Smooth.lean)). Up to the boundary, only the constant-coefficient half-space model is proved: for a constant uniformly elliptic matrix, the \(H^1_0\) solution of the Dirichlet problem on a half-space with \(f\in L^2\) lies in \(H^2\) of the half-space ([half-space \(H^2\)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Regularity/HalfSpace.lean)). Further elliptic regularity is recorded in [Mathlib33.md](Mathlib33.md).

For the Laplacian, TauCeti constructs the Poisson kernel of the unit ball, proves it has mass one, and shows that the Poisson integral of continuous boundary data is harmonic in the ball and attains the data continuously, so that the Dirichlet problem is solvable on every ball ([ball Poisson integral](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/PoissonIntegral/Ball.lean)); the half-space Poisson kernel \(2t/(n\omega_n|y-x|^n)\) has mass one and solves the Dirichlet problem for bounded continuous data ([half-space Poisson integral](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/PoissonIntegral/HalfSpace.lean)). Green kernels of the ball and the half-space are built by the method of images ([ball Green kernel](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/GreenFunction/Ball.lean), [half-space Green kernel](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/GreenFunction/HalfSpace.lean)). Perron's method makes the Perron solution harmonic on any bounded open set, and boundary values are attained at regular (barrier) points; the exterior sphere condition gives barriers, so the Dirichlet problem for continuous data is solvable on bounded open sets satisfying it at each boundary point (`TauCeti.exists_harmonicOnNhd_continuousOn_closure_eqOn_frontier_of_exterior_sphere`; [Perron solution](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Perron/Basic.lean), [barriers](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Perron/Barrier.lean)).

Gauss–Green on a half-space holds for \(H^1\) functions with the Sobolev trace as boundary term ([half-space integration by parts](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/Trace/IntegrationByParts.lean)). On Lipschitz domains, local traces in boundary charts are bounded operators, and \(u\in H^1(\Omega)\) lies in \(H^1_0(\Omega)\) exactly when all chart traces vanish ([chart traces](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/LipschitzDomain/Trace/Basic.lean), [zero trace](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/LipschitzDomain/Trace/Kernel.lean)). These traces are measured in chart coordinates; no trace space \(H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)\) or right inverse of the trace is constructed.

The Gaussian heat kernel \(K_t(x)=(4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^2/4t}\) is positive with mass one, satisfies \(\partial_tK=\Delta K\) and \(K_t*K_s=K_{t+s}\) ([heat kernel](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Basic.lean)); for bounded measurable \(g\), \(K_t*g\) is smooth for \(t>0\), solves the heat equation, and tends to \(g(x_0)\) at points of continuity ([heat equation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/HeatEquation.lean)). Convolution with \(K_t\) is a strongly continuous contraction semigroup on \(L^p\), \(1\le p<\infty\), whose generator agrees with \(\Delta\) on compactly supported \(C^2\) functions ([heat semigroup](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Semigroup.lean), [generator](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Generator.lean)). The weak maximum principle for \(\partial_tu-\Delta u-b\cdot\nabla u\) on a compact space-time cylinder bounds a subsolution by its values on the parabolic boundary ([parabolic maximum principle](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/MaximumPrinciple/Parabolic.lean)). The parabolic Dirichlet problem \(\partial_tu+L(t)u=f\), \(u=0\) on the lateral boundary, \(u(t_0)=u_0\), with jointly measurable bounded uniformly elliptic coefficients \(a(t,x)\), bounded drift and potential, \(f\in L^2(H^{-1})\), and \(u_0\in L^2\), has a unique weak solution \(u\in L^2(H^1_0)\) with \(u'\in L^2(H^{-1})\) and a continuous \(L^2\)-valued representative (`exists_hasWeakLineDerivOn_gelfandDual_energyFormH1`, `eqOn_of_hasWeakLineDerivOn_gelfandDual_energyFormH1`; [parabolic Dirichlet problem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Parabolic/DirichletProblem.lean)).

The Laplacian has implemented distributional fundamental solutions (Section 38), while semigroup generation and the autonomous abstract Cauchy problem are covered in Section 24. These are concrete second-order results beyond the abstract Lax–Milgram theorem; they do not constitute a general linear PDE or hypoellipticity theory.

## Topics of Section 32 not found in either inspected library

- The Cauchy–Kowalevski theorem, Lewy's nonsolvability example, and a general weak/distributional theory for arbitrary linear PDE, beyond the second-order elliptic, parabolic, and semigroup results above.
- Stokes’ theorem for differential forms, the divergence theorem on manifolds or on curved domains, and Green's identities with boundary integrals on general smooth domains. Divergence theorems exist on boxes (Mathlib), on a half-space for \(H^1\) functions, and for planar regions (TauCeti).
- Among the model equations of Evans's first part: the transport equation, the wave equation with d'Alembert's and Kirchhoff's formulas, energy methods and finite propagation speed for waves, the method of characteristics for first-order nonlinear equations, and Hamilton–Jacobi equations with the Hopf–Lax formula. The Laplacian (Poisson integrals, Green kernels) and the heat equation (Gaussian kernel, heat semigroup, parabolic maximum principle) are present in TauCeti.
- The trace space \(H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)\) and surjectivity of the trace, the Neumann problem, the passage from a smooth boundary to the exterior sphere condition, regularity up to the boundary for variable coefficients or curved boundaries (Lions–Magenes, Grisvard), and complementing (Lopatinskii–Shapiro) boundary conditions for higher-order systems. TauCeti imposes nonzero data \(g\in H^1(\Omega)\) through \(u-g\in H^1_0\), has local chart traces on Lipschitz domains, solves the classical Dirichlet problem under the exterior sphere condition, and proves half-space \(H^2\) regularity for constant coefficients.
- General higher-order variable-coefficient PDE and hypoellipticity. Coercive second-order divergence-form equations with measurable coefficients, and fundamental solutions of the Laplacian, are present in TauCeti.
