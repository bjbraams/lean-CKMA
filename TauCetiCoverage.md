# TauCeti coverage of Sections 04–53

This file summarises, section by section, what TauCeti adds to Mathlib for the
mathematical content described in [`SYNOPSIS04.md`](SYNOPSIS04.md)–[`SYNOPSIS53.md`](SYNOPSIS53.md).
It is a digest of the TauCeti parts of the coverage inventories
[`STRUCTURE04.md`](STRUCTURE04.md)–[`STRUCTURE53.md`](STRUCTURE53.md), which give the
declarations, source links, and hypotheses, and classify every numbered synopsis
entry as covered, partly covered, or not covered by the two libraries together.
The companion file [MathlibCoverage.md](MathlibCoverage.md) summarises the Mathlib
coverage in the same way, and [GAPS.md](GAPS.md) selects and assesses the principal
missing results. Results that TauCeti imports from Mathlib are not attributed to
TauCeti here; where a TauCeti result specialises or repackages Mathlib material,
only the addition is described.

describing future work, and declaration names alone are not counted. No local
build or axiom-dependency check was run. Statements here are summaries: the full

their use.

Each section below gives a verdict for this library alone, judged against the
content of the corresponding synopsis:

- **Substantial:** a large part of the graduate core of the section is proved.
- **Partial:** a real cluster of the section's own definitions and theorems is
  proved, but the section as a whole is not.
- **Thin:** a few of the section's results, or only neighbouring ingredients.
- **None:** nothing of the section's own content beyond general infrastructure.

## 04. Real analysis, measure, and integration

**Partial.** TauCeti proves Lusin's theorem in closed-set form for weakly regular measures (04.20) and Vitali's convergence theorem for pseudometric targets. It adds the atomic–diffuse decomposition of s-finite measures. In Lᵖ it proves the norming bound behind isometry of Lq → (Lᵖ)* (04.39, surjectivity open), Minkowski's integral inequality (04.36), strict convexity of Lᵖ, the limit of Lᵖ norms as p → ∞, continuity of translation (04.41), approximate identities by smooth bump mollifiers (04.42), Young's inequality for L¹ kernels and an integral-operator form with Schur's test (04.43), and a sufficient Fréchet–Kolmogorov compactness criterion. It proves polar integration for integrable functions (04.48), universal measurability of analytic sets, the Kuratowski–Ryll-Nardzewski and Jankov–von Neumann selection theorems, the atomless measure isomorphism theorem and Pettis' theorem for separable inner product targets; Riesz–Thorin and diagonal Marcinkiewicz interpolation are supporting results. Nothing is added on reflexivity of Lᵖ, the dual of C₀(X), Daniell–Stone or de la Vallée Poussin. [Details in STRUCTURE04.md](STRUCTURE04.md).

## 05. Differentiation and the fine structure of real functions

**Partial.** TauCeti proves the Morse–Sard theorem for Cᵏ maps with the non-sharp threshold k ≥ (dim E)² + 1, sharp when the source dimension does not exceed the target dimension, hence for C^∞ maps (05.5, partly). For absolutely continuous curves it proves the length formula through the metric derivative, the Banach indicatrix formula for Lipschitz curves (05.58, partly), chain rule, integration by parts and monotone substitution (05.15, 05.20), and lower semicontinuity of variation, the half of Helly's theorem without selection (05.19, partly). It defines the centred Hardy–Littlewood maximal function on finite-dimensional spaces with weak (1,1) and strong (p,p) bounds (05.25, 05.26) and dyadic Lebesgue differentiation. It localizes Rademacher's theorem, proves Alexandrov's theorem for convex functions (05.38, partly), weak differentiability of Lipschitz functions and local Lipschitz continuity of W^{1,∞} functions (05.37, partly), and the one-dimensional absolute continuity of W^{1,1} (05.36). Nothing is added on Dini derivatives, Baire classes, Whitney, Kirszbraun, BV fine properties or nonabsolute integrals. [Details in STRUCTURE05.md](STRUCTURE05.md).

## 06. Probability: foundations and limit theory

**Partial.** TauCeti proves Kolmogorov's extension theorem for countable index sets with standard Borel coordinates (06.1, partly), the Hewitt–Savage zero–one law (06.6), and Laplace-transform determinacy of measures on [0, ∞). It adds inverse transform sampling, Pinsker's inequality, the weak law in Chebyshev's form and a strong law on the product space. It proves Bochner's theorem in finite dimensions and moment determinacy under exponential moments or compact support (06.32, partly). Lévy's downward theorem is proved (06.46). For Gaussian laws it proves conditioning with Schur-complement covariance (06.49); the Cauchy law is treated as the α = 1 stable case (06.52, partly). The de Finetti–Ryll-Nardzewski theorem and the separately and jointly exchangeable Aldous–Hoover representations (06.55) are proved, together with a large catalogue of distributions including Dirichlet and Wishart laws. The Donsker–Varadhan formula gives lower semicontinuity of relative entropy, but no large-deviation principle is stated. Nothing is added for random series, Lindeberg, functional limits, random measures, branching, percolation or Gibbs measures. [Details in STRUCTURE06.md](STRUCTURE06.md).

## 07. Concentration, high-dimensional probability, and random matrices

**Thin.** TauCeti proves McDiarmid's bounded-differences theorem on finite products of probability spaces, giving the sub-Gaussian moment-generating bound with parameter Σcᵢ²/4 and the usual tail estimate (07.13). It proves the Prékopa–Leindler inequality on finite-dimensional real inner product spaces and the Brunn–Minkowski inequality in additive, multiplicative and weighted forms, but derives no concentration inequality from them. As supporting material it has the Hermite basis of L² of the standard Gaussian, empirical measures of finite samples without uniform deviation bounds, polynomial (Chebyshev and union-bound) concentration for entries, eigenvalues and operator norms of random matrices, and the exact moment-generating function of a Gaussian quadratic form. Nothing is added on Bernstein inequalities, Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities, hypercontractivity, chaining, empirical-process limit theorems, Hanson–Wright, matrix Bernstein or Johnson–Lindenstrauss, or on random matrix theory (07B). [Details in STRUCTURE07.md](STRUCTURE07.md).

## 08. Stochastic processes, stochastic calculus, and control

**Thin.** TauCeti proves Kolmogorov's extension theorem for countable index sets with standard Borel coordinates, which yields Brownian motion at dyadic times but not a process indexed by all real times. It packages the path law of a time-homogeneous Markov chain with its Markov property and cylinder formula (08.45, partly), proves recurrence of stationary processes on countable state spaces through Poincaré recurrence, decomposes a chain started at a recurrent state into i.i.d. excursions, and proves the Diaconis–Freedman characterization of recurrent Markov-exchangeable processes as mixtures of Markov chains. Strongly continuous semigroups with the Hille–Yosida and Lumer–Phillips theorems are developed analytically but are not connected to transition kernels or Feller processes. Nothing is added on Brownian path properties, continuous-time martingales, stochastic integration, SDEs, local time, Lévy processes, ergodicity of chains, diffusions, particle systems or stochastic control. [Details in STRUCTURE08.md](STRUCTURE08.md).

## 09. Malliavin calculus, rough paths, and stochastic PDE

**Thin.** TauCeti proves the Hermite orthogonality relation for the standard Gaussian on the line and shows that normalized Hermite polynomials, and their tensor products over finitely many coordinates, form Hilbert bases of the Gaussian L² space, with coefficient formulas, Parseval identities and L²-convergent expansions. This is the Wiener chaos decomposition for a finite-dimensional isonormal process (09.1, 09.2, partly); multiple Wiener integrals and the infinite-dimensional case are absent. The remaining material is deterministic and supporting: mild and classical solutions of linear evolution equations generated by C₀-semigroups with uniqueness, Hilbert–Schmidt norms, Gelfand triples with existence of variational solutions for linear parabolic problems under Gårding's inequality, and the heat kernel and heat semigroup. Nothing is added on Malliavin derivatives, Ornstein–Uhlenbeck analysis, Cameron–Martin theory, Poisson space, rough paths, singular SPDE, or stochastic integration in Hilbert spaces and random-field SPDE. [Details in STRUCTURE09.md](STRUCTURE09.md).

## 10. Ergodic theory and measurable dynamics

**Thin.** TauCeti applies the mean ergodic theorem to the Koopman operator and identifies the limit projection with conditional expectation on the invariant σ-algebra, giving L² convergence of Birkhoff averages to E[f | I] for square-integrable f (10.5, partly); almost-everywhere and L¹ convergence for integrable f are not proved. It proves ergodicity of Bernoulli shifts and of shift-invariant laws with independent coordinates, and extends the constancy of invariant functions and the extreme-point characterization of ergodic measures to countable group actions. The only ergodic decomposition is de Finetti's, for exchangeable laws on a standard Borel space (10.8, partly). Herglotz's theorem and diagonal spectral measures of normal operators are proved but not applied to Koopman operators (10.11, partly). Nothing is added on the pointwise ergodic theorem, Kac, Kingman, mixing, entropy and classification, thermodynamic formalism, smooth ergodic theory, multiple recurrence, infinite measures or amenable actions. [Details in STRUCTURE10.md](STRUCTURE10.md).

## 11. Classical inequalities, means, majorization, and rearrangement

**Partial.** TauCeti adds the operator AM–GM inequality for the geometric mean of positive elements and superadditivity of weighted geometric means. In majorization it proves Karamata's inequality under the hypothesis x = Dy with D doubly stochastic (11.22, 11.24, partly), the resulting ℓᵖ contraction (11.26, partly), monotonicity of symmetric gauges under weak majorization of antitone nonnegative tuples (11.71, partly), and the forward Schur–Horn theorem; the converse construction from partial-sum inequalities is absent. It proves Pinsker's inequality (11.30), the integral Grönwall inequality with constant rate (11.40, partly), and the sharp Wirtinger and interval Poincaré inequalities with the periodic equality case (11.43). It defines Schwarz rearrangement with equimeasurability and Lᵖ-norm preservation, and Steiner symmetrization of sets with volume preservation (11.48, partly). Schur's test and Young's inequality for integral kernels (11.56, partly) and Riesz–Thorin are also proved. Nothing is added on Hardy, Hilbert or Carleman inequalities, Hardy–Littlewood and Riesz rearrangement inequalities, convex order or Clarkson's inequalities. [Details in STRUCTURE11.md](STRUCTURE11.md).

## 12. Convexity, convex analysis, optimization, and variational analysis

**Partial.** TauCeti develops conjugate duality on real dual pairings: Fenchel–Young (12.17), the Fenchel–Moreau theorem in locally convex spaces (12.18), subdifferentials with conjugate-subgradient reciprocity (12.19), nonempty subdifferentials on the interior of the domain in finite dimension (12.13, partly), and the absolute bipolar theorem. It proves almost-everywhere differentiability and Alexandrov's theorem for convex functions, the Hessian criterion (12.15, partly), and C¹ conjugates of strictly convex superlinear functions (12.20, 12.64, partly). It proves the Fermat rule and monotonicity of the subdifferential (12.27), the tangent-paraboloid bound for strongly convex functions, Moreau–Yosida approximation with existence of resolvents on proper metric spaces (12.32, 12.33, partly), and the Milman–Pettis theorem. It adds the Gordan, Stiemke and Tucker alternatives, sequential Γ-convergence with convergence of minima (12.47, 12.48, partly), the homogeneous Hoffman bound and Rockafellar's cyclic-monotonicity theorem (12.69). Nothing is added on Minkowski–Weyl, Choquet, John, subdifferential calculus rules, Fenchel–Rockafellar and KKT duality, nonsmooth and limiting subdifferentials, or algorithm convergence. [Details in STRUCTURE12.md](STRUCTURE12.md).

## 13. Matrix analysis and noncommutative inequalities

**Partial.** TauCeti proves the spectral theorem for normal matrices (13.1), the singular value decomposition in rank-one form (13.2) and polar decomposition for bounded Hilbert-space operators (13.3). It proves Courant–Fischer (13.5), Ky Fan's maximum principle (13.6, partly), Weyl's perturbation bound (13.8, partly), forward Schur–Horn (13.9, partly) and Hoffman–Wielandt for symmetric operators (13.10, partly). For singular values it proves Fan dominance and the gauge representation of unitarily invariant seminorms (13.14), constructs Schatten norms (13.17, partly), and proves the von Neumann trace inequality for symmetric operators (13.13, partly), operator-norm Eckart–Young (13.18, partly), the index inequality for products (13.19, partly) and Minkowski's determinant inequality for real matrices (13.22, partly). It adds approximation numbers of bounded operators, the geometric mean a#b with the operator AM–GM inequality (13.39, 13.40, partly), Pick–Nevanlinna representations without the link to Loewner's theorem, and the Sylvester equation and Davis–Kahan sin Θ theorems (13.66, 13.67, partly). Nothing is added on operator convexity, trace inequalities, quantum entropy, completely positive maps or regularized determinants. [Details in STRUCTURE13.md](STRUCTURE13.md).

## 14. Functional, isoperimetric, and geometric inequalities

**Partial.** TauCeti extends the Sobolev inequality to \(W^{1,p}_0(\Omega)\) for \(1\le p<n\) and adds Morrey's embedding of \(W^{1,p}(\mathbb R^n)\), \(p>n\) (14.2); Poincaré inequalities on slabs and balls, the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality on bounded convex domains with explicit non-sharp constant (14.6), Sobolev inequalities on sets of finite measure, and Trudinger's inequality with a non-sharp exponent (14.8). The Aubin–Talenti bubbles are shown to solve the critical Yamabe equation (14.3 in part). It proves De Giorgi's functional isoperimetric inequality on convex domains, defines perimeter with lower semicontinuity, and defines the Schwarz rearrangement with equimeasurability and \(L^p\) invariance (14.19 in part). It proves Prékopa–Leindler and Brunn–Minkowski for measurable sets without the equality case (14.28 in part), the isodiametric inequality, Wirtinger's inequality and the planar isoperimetric inequality with equality cases (14.29), a derivative form of Minkowski's first inequality (14.78 in part), and the Donsker–Varadhan formula (14.66); Pinsker's inequality and \(CD(K,\infty)\) are present. Capacity, Pólya–Szegő, Faber–Krahn, Gaussian isoperimetry, mixed volumes, Markov-semigroup functional inequalities and transport–entropy inequalities are absent. [Details in STRUCTURE14.md](STRUCTURE14.md).

## 15. Core Banach and Hilbert space theory

**Partial.** TauCeti adds the converse half of Arzelà–Ascoli (15.69), the isometric duality of subspaces and quotients with annihilators, and Goldstine's theorem (15.14, 15.16). It proves equivalence of norm and weak boundedness, extension of pointwise convergence from a dense set, and closed-range estimates (15.21, 15.22 in part). It characterizes reflexivity by weak compactness of the unit ball, proves its permanence properties, and proves Mazur's lemma and the Milman–Pettis theorem (15.26, 15.27, 15.33). It has Parseval's identity and Lions' representation theorem. For operators it gives Banach adjoints and the closed range theorem (15.46, 15.47 in part), weakly compact operators with Gantmacher's theorem, approximation numbers and compactness of Hilbert-space operators via \(a_n(T)\to0\), Riesz–Schauder theory with index zero for \(1-K\) (15.52, 15.53), an eigenvector Hilbert basis for compact self-adjoint operators (`ContinuousLinearMap.exists_hilbertBasis_forall_hasEigenvector`), polar decomposition, and scalar spectral measures. TauCeti has nothing on duality of \(\ell^p\), uniform convexity of \(L^p\), the Davis–Figiel–Johnson–Pełczyński factorization, the infinite-dimensional singular value decomposition, or the projection-valued measure. [Details in STRUCTURE15.md](STRUCTURE15.md).

## 16. Locally convex spaces, duality, and distributions

**Partial.** TauCeti proves the bipolar theorem in real or complex locally convex spaces, with double annihilators and weak-star continuous functionals on weak duals (16.13; 16.19 in part). It constructs the projective tensor topology for finite families of seminormed spaces, its completion, and the universal property for continuous multilinear maps (16.29, `TauCeti.ProjectiveTensorProduct.liftEquiv`). It proves the du Bois-Reymond lemma on an interval (16.45 in part). For Schwartz functions it proves density of compactly supported smooth functions, determination of \(L^p\) functions by Schwartz pairings, Fourier multipliers with Schwartz symbols as convolutions, and a derivative characterization of Bessel-potential spaces. It gives fundamental solutions: the Newtonian kernel in dimension \(n\ge3\) and the logarithmic kernel in the plane with Newtonian potentials of compactly supported \(C^2\) data, the Cauchy–Pompeiu formula for \(\partial/\partial\bar z\), and the heat kernel without the space-time distributional identity (16.64, 16.65 in part). Nuclear spaces, injective tensor products, structure theorems, convolution of distributions, and the Malgrange–Ehrenpreis theorem are absent. [Details in STRUCTURE16.md](STRUCTURE16.md).

## 17. Banach space geometry, bases, and operator ideals

**Partial.** TauCeti adds to Mathlib's bases that subsequences of a basis are bases, that bases transport along isomorphisms, monotone renorming, the small-perturbation principle (17.5 in part), and unconditional unit-vector bases of \(\ell^p\) and \(c_0\). It proves the shrinking criterion and the duality between shrinking and boundedly complete bases (17.9, 17.11 in part), and James's theorem that a Banach space with a basis is reflexive exactly when the basis is shrinking and boundedly complete (17.10, `SchauderBasis.surjective_inclusionInDoubleDual_iff`). It defines the approximation, bounded approximation, and metric approximation properties, with spaces with a basis, \(\ell^p\), and \(c_0\) as examples (17.13). It proves the Milman–Pettis theorem (17.45 in part). It develops approximation numbers with their ideal inequalities, symmetric-gauge operator ideals on Hilbert spaces, and the nuclear gauge as a trace-class analogue (17.59, 17.61, 17.64 in part). Classical subspaces and complemented copies, Rademacher sums, type and cotype, uniform smoothness, the Radon–Nikodým property, \(p\)-summing operators, Pietsch factorization, and tensor norms on Banach spaces are absent. [Details in STRUCTURE17.md](STRUCTURE17.md).

## 18. Vector-valued analysis and analysis in Banach spaces

**Partial.** TauCeti proves Pettis's weak-measurability criterion for separable Hilbert targets (`TauCeti.aestronglyMeasurable_iff_forall_inner`; 18.1 in part). It proves the norming half of \(L^{q'}\hookrightarrow(L^q)^*\) for scalar and Hilbert-valued functions (18.5 in part), and Young's inequality with an \(L^1\) kernel together with Banach-valued approximate identities on \(L^p\), \(1\le p<\infty\) (18.9 in part). Its main contribution is Calderón–Zygmund theory: weak type \((1,1)\) and \(L^p\) bounds for \(1<p<2\) for Banach-valued operators with Hörmander kernels, extended to all \(1<p<\infty\) for Hilbert-valued operators (18.35 in part). It also proves the Mihlin–Hörmander multiplier theorem for scalar symbols acting on Hilbert-valued functions (18.37, 18.38 in part) and Calderón's identification \(H^{k,p}=W^{k,p}\) for real scalar functions (18.40 in part). Evolution spaces over a Gelfand triple carry continuous representatives and the energy identity. Vector measures, the Radon–Nikodým property, Banach-valued martingales, UMD spaces, \(R\)-boundedness, operator-valued multipliers, \(\gamma\)-radonifying operators, and maximal regularity are absent. [Details in STRUCTURE18.md](STRUCTURE18.md).

## 19. Banach lattices, positive operators, and ordered structures

**Thin.** TauCeti defines Hilbert's projective metric on the positive orthant of \(\mathbb R^n\) and proves its metric laws and zero set (`TauCeti.hilbertProjectiveDist`; 19.71 in part). It proves Birkhoff's contraction \(d_H(Kx,Ky)\le\tanh(\Delta(K)/4)\,d_H(x,y)\) for entrywise positive matrices (19.72 in part), applied to the Sinkhorn iteration; the eigenvector existence and convergence consequences are not derived. Further results are isolated: the Koopman operator of a measure-preserving map is a positive, multiplicative operator on classes of real functions, and complex-scalar Riesz–Thorin interpolation holds on simple functions (19.60 in part). TauCeti has nothing on Riesz spaces, ideals and bands, Banach-lattice norms and order continuity, order duality and regular operators, AM/AL spaces, domination, linear Perron–Frobenius theory, or positive semigroups. [Details in STRUCTURE19.md](STRUCTURE19.md).

## 20. Banach algebras

**Thin.** TauCeti develops the Banach-algebra logarithm series near \(1\) with the local inverse identities \(\log\exp x=x\) and \(\exp\log x=x\), square roots near the identity, a local Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff map, and the absence of small subgroups of units near \(1\). For unital commutative C⋆-algebras, positive functionals are integrals against measures on the character space. It proves automatic continuity only for homomorphisms from noetherian nonarchimedean Banach algebras into finite-dimensional algebras. On the convolution side it gives integrated forms of group representations on \(L^1(G)\), the cyclic SNAG theorem, Bochner's theorem on locally compact abelian groups (`ContRepresentation.exists_pontryaginMeasureTransform_eq_inner`), and approximate identities on \(L^p(\mathbb R^n)\) and on compact groups (20.37 in part). TauCeti has no general theory of the spectrum, Gelfand representation, uniform algebras, spectral synthesis, the Banach algebra \(L^1(G)\) and its character space, amenability, or Arens regularity. [Details in STRUCTURE20.md](STRUCTURE20.md).

## 21. Bounded operators, spectra, and model theory

**Partial.** TauCeti constructs the scalar spectral measures of a normal operator and proves the elementary direction of Douglas factorization, \(T=VA\) from \(\lVert Tx\rVert\le C\lVert Ax\rVert\) (21.3 in part). It extends Fredholm theory: kernel and cokernel finiteness suffice between Banach spaces, Hilbert adjoints negate the index, and compact perturbations preserve Fredholmness and index (`ContinuousLinearMap.IsFredholm.add_of_isCompactOperator`; 21.12; 21.11, 21.13, 21.16 in part). Self-adjoint Fredholm operators have index zero, and resolvents are analytic. It develops approximation numbers with index laws, the ideal inequality, compactness of Hilbert-space operators via \(a_n\to0\), Ky Fan inequalities, the basis-free Hilbert–Schmidt norm, symmetric-gauge ideals including the Schatten classes, and the nuclear gauge (21.22 in part). ## 23. Operator algebras, operator spaces, free probability, and noncommutative geometry

## 45. Several complex variables, complex manifolds, pluripotential theory, and CR analysis

**Thin.** TauCeti proves the iterated Cauchy integral formula on polydiscs (`DifferentiableOn.torusIntegral_prod_sub_inv_smul`), so that on open subsets of finite-dimensional complex spaces complex differentiability and analyticity coincide (part of 45.2); Cauchy estimates and normal convergence are not stated. It proves the Cauchy–Pompeiu formula and solvability of \(2\bar\partial v=f\) for compactly supported \(C^1\) data in one variable (part of 45.30). As supporting material it develops the pointwise Levi form of \(C^2\) functions, with strict plurisubharmonicity and strong \(q\)-pseudoconvexity and their stability properties, the sheaf of holomorphic germs on the plane, and nonarchimedean Weierstrass preparation and division. ## 46. Complex dynamics

**Thin.** TauCeti does not define the Fatou or Julia set, and no synopsis entry of the section is covered. It supplies neighbouring ingredients only. For normal families it proves Montel's selection theorem for locally bounded families of holomorphic maps on plane domains (`TauCeti.montel`), Vitali's convergence theorem, and Hurwitz's theorem in both directions; normality in the spherical metric and Montel's theorem for families omitting two values (used in 46.5) are absent. For self-maps of the disc, Schwarz–Pick rigidity yields that a holomorphic self-map other than the identity has at most one interior fixed point, an ingredient of Denjoy–Wolff (46.70) without convergence of iterates or Wolff's lemma. The Néron–Tate canonical height of an elliptic curve is constructed by Tate averaging, not the canonical height of a morphism of projective space. TauCeti has nothing on Julia sets, local normal forms, Fatou components, Green functions and rays, parameter spaces, thermodynamic formalism, transcendental or several-variable dynamics. [Details in STRUCTURE46.md](STRUCTURE46.md).

## 47. Approximation theory

**Thin.** TauCeti adds averaging operators by normalized smooth bumps that converge strongly to the identity in \(L^p\), \(1\le p<\infty\), including Banach-valued functions, and cutoff approximation in the Schwartz space. Normalized Chebyshev polynomials form a Hilbert basis of the Chebyshev-weighted \(L^2\) space on \([-1,1]\), with Parseval identities, but without coefficient decay or rates. Bochner's theorem characterizes continuous positive-definite functions on finite-dimensional real inner product spaces as Fourier transforms of finite measures (`TauCeti.bochner`, part of 47.56), with Kolmogorov decompositions and universal kernels as supporting material. Approximation numbers of bounded operators are developed; they equal singular values in finite dimension (Eckart–Young) and the coefficient magnitudes for decreasing diagonal operators on \(\ell^p\) (part of 47.64), and characterize compactness of operators into Hilbert spaces; widths of sets and function classes are not defined. TauCeti has nothing on best uniform approximation, direct and inverse theorems, rational approximation, splines, kernel interpolation, nonlinear approximation, or discrepancy. [Details in STRUCTURE47.md](STRUCTURE47.md).

## 48. Orthogonal polynomials, moment problems, and spectral recurrences

**Partial.** TauCeti adds the Hermite recurrence and lowering identity, Hermite orthogonality \(\int He_mHe_ne^{-x^2/2}\,dx=\delta_{mn}n!\sqrt{2\pi}\) by integration by parts, and Hilbert bases of \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) by Hermite functions and of the Gaussian measure by \(He_n/\sqrt{n!}\) (parts of 48.3, 48.11, 48.12); the Chebyshev \(T_n\) form a Hilbert basis of their weighted \(L^2\) space. ## 50. Asymptotic analysis, resurgence, and perturbation

**Thin.** TauCeti proves the Morse–Palais lemma on Banach spaces, the change of variables behind Laplace-type expansions (50.11, 50.12, 50.14), without the integral asymptotics themselves. On the summation side it proves the Wiener–Ikehara theorem and a transfer of averages to power weights, Tauberian results outside the synopsis entries. For perturbation of differential equations it proves \(C^{n+1}\) dependence of ODE solutions on parameters and initial data (part of 50.57, `ODE.contDiff_globalSolution`), exponential dichotomies, Lyapunov–Perron local stable sets, and \(C^1\) local stable and unstable manifolds of gradient flows at nondegenerate critical points; these concern hyperbolic equilibria, not slow manifolds. Operator perturbation results adjacent to 50.68 include bounded perturbation of semigroup generators, stability of the Fredholm index, and the Kato–Rellich theorem. TauCeti has nothing on asymptotic expansions, oscillatory integrals, Euler–Maclaurin and Karamata theorems, WKB and irregular singularities, Borel summation and resurgence, Tikhonov–Fenichel theory, or Rayleigh–Schrödinger series. ## 51. Integrable systems and Riemann–Hilbert methods

## 52. Geometric analysis on manifolds

**Partial.** TauCeti defines the curvature of a connection, proves the first Bianchi identity, the symmetries of the Riemann and Ricci tensors, and smoothness of the Levi-Civita connection, and defines sectional, Ricci and scalar curvature and the Schouten and Weyl tensors (part of 52.1; no second Bianchi identity). ## 53. Elliptic operators on manifolds, index theory, and spectral geometry

**Thin.** TauCeti adds abstract Fredholm theory: the index is invariant under compact perturbations (`ContinuousLinearMap.index_add_of_isCompactOperator`) and constant along continuous families over preconnected parameter spaces (the abstract parts of 53.6), adjoints have opposite index, and self-adjoint Fredholm operators have index zero; no elliptic operator on a manifold is shown to be Fredholm. Its remaining material is neighbouring. On bounded Euclidean domains it proves a Fredholm alternative for coercive divergence-form operators and a Dirichlet spectral theorem with compact resolvent, a Hilbert basis of eigenfunctions and the Rayleigh-quotient characterization of the first eigenvalue, together with the explicit Dirichlet spectrum of an interval and finite-dimensional Courant–Fischer. It also has the Euclidean heat kernel, counts of Morse critical points with a deformation lemma, Euler classes of vector bundles, and polygonal hyperbolic area formulas. TauCeti has nothing on elliptic operators on manifolds, Hodge theory, heat-trace expansions, Weyl laws, Dirac operators and index theorems, spectral flow, nodal geometry, or trace formulas.
