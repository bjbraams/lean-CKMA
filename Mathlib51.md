# 51. Integrable Systems and Riemann–Hilbert Methods

Checked against Mathlib commit `f039e1b8a616d45782a5c7fc5e7909249c36b8ea` (2026-10-18, Lean `v4.36.0-rc1`). The checkout was only read. The survey below concerns this Mathlib revision; the TauCeti supplement and final gap list also use the [TauCeti source audit](TauCetiCoverage.md).

**Imports.** There is no aggregate module. A file `Mathlib/A/B/C.lean` is imported as `import Mathlib.A.B.C`. There is no root for integrable systems or for Riemann–Hilbert problems. The Lie-algebra file that the name \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) suggests is `Mathlib.Algebra.Lie.Sl2`.

**Namespaces.** `IsSl2Triple` is the predicate that three elements of a Lie algebra satisfy the \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) relations. It is not a namespace of integrable systems.

A search for Riemann–Hilbert problems, integrable systems, the Korteweg–de Vries equation, solitons, Lax pairs, and isomonodromy returns nothing in that sense.

`Algebra/Lie/Sl2.lean` defines an \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) triple \((h,e,f)\) in a Lie algebra by \(h\neq 0\), \([e,f]=h\), \([h,e]=2e\), and \([h,f]=-2f\), and studies primitive vectors in finite-dimensional representations. That is the representation theory of \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), not an integrable system.

Two nearby pieces of analysis are present. For a measure \(\mu\) on a normed field \(\Bbbk\) and an element \(a\) of a normed \(\Bbbk\)-algebra, the resolvent transform \(\int(x-a)^{-1}\,d\mu(x)\) specializes to the Stieltjes transform (\(\Bbbk=\mathbb R\), values in \(\mathbb C\)) and to the Cauchy transform of a measure on \(\mathbb C\); it is differentiable off the support of \(\mu\), and holomorphic there when the algebra is \(\mathbb C\) (`MeasureTheory/Measure/ResolventTransform.lean`). These are transforms of measures, not Cauchy operators on contours, and no Plemelj–Sokhotski boundary-value formula is proved. The symplectic group of matrices preserving the standard form \(J\) is defined in `LinearAlgebra/SymplecticGroup.lean`; it is linear algebra, not Hamiltonian dynamics.

## Additional coverage in TauCeti

The following additions were checked in TauCeti revision `6ebb29f84b4d`; see the [revision, build evidence, and comparison scope](TauCetiCoverage.md). Source links below are pinned to that revision.

TauCeti contains geometric prerequisites for Hamiltonian analysis. On the linear cotangent space \(V\times V'\), with continuous dual \(V'\), it constructs the Liouville one-form \(\lambda_{(q,p)}(\delta q,\delta p)=p(\delta q)\) and proves that the canonical symplectic form is \(-d\lambda\), without a finite-dimensional restriction ([linear cotangent model](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Geometry/Symplectic/Cotangent/Liouville.lean)). This is an exact symplectic construction, not an integrable Hamiltonian system. On a finite-dimensional real vector space with a constant symplectic form \(\omega\), the Hamiltonian vector field \(X_H\) of a differentiable \(H\) is the unique field with \(\omega(X_H(x),v)=dH(x)v\); for \(\omega=dq\wedge dp\) these are Hamilton’s equations, and for \(C^2\) \(H\) the linearization of \(X_H\) is infinitesimally symplectic ([Hamiltonian vector fields](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Geometry/Symplectic/Hamiltonian/Basic.lean)). If \(X_H\) is globally Lipschitz, its global flow conserves \(H\) along each orbit, and for \(C^2\) \(H\) every time-\(t\) map preserves \(\omega\) on tangent vectors ([Hamiltonian flows](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Geometry/Symplectic/Hamiltonian/Flow.lean)). Smooth two-forms on manifolds and their closedness are defined as the layer beneath symplectic manifolds ([two-forms](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Geometry/Manifold/TwoForm/Basic.lean)), but Hamiltonian dynamics on symplectic manifolds, Poisson brackets, and first integrals in involution are not developed. The linear symplectic algebra around these files (Lagrangian subspaces, compatible almost complex structures) and constant-structure \(J\)-holomorphic maps with their energy identity are also developed, as geometric rather than integrable-systems content.

On the operator side, the Toeplitz operator \(T_a=PM_a|_{H^2}\) of an essentially bounded symbol \(a\) on the circle is a bounded linear function of \(a\), with matrix entries \(\hat a(i-j)\) in the Hardy basis, adjoint \(T_{\bar a}\), norm at most \(\|a\|_\infty\), and injective symbol map ([Toeplitz operators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Toeplitz.lean)). The semicommutator \(T_{ab}-T_aT_b\) is a product of Hankel operators, and vanishes when \(b\) is analytic or \(a\) coanalytic ([Hankel operators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Hankel.lean)). These are the scalar ingredients of Wiener–Hopf theory, but no factorization theorem or matrix partial indices are proved. No Riemann–Hilbert boundary-value problem, isomonodromy, Lax-pair, or soliton theorem was located.

## Topics of Section 51 not found in either inspected library

- Riemann–Hilbert boundary-value problems: Cauchy operators on contours and the Plemelj–Sokhotski formulas, Birkhoff (Wiener–Hopf) factorization and partial indices, the small-norm theorem, and parametrix error estimates.
- The Korteweg–de Vries equation, solitons, Lax pairs, and the Toda lattice; Jost solutions, inverse scattering, and Marchenko reconstruction; theta-function (finite-gap) solutions.
- Integrable Hamiltonian systems: Hamiltonian dynamics on symplectic manifolds, Poisson brackets, first integrals in involution, the Liouville–Arnold theorem, and action–angle variables. Hamiltonian vector fields and flows on symplectic vector spaces are present in TauCeti.
- Isomonodromy and the Schlesinger equations, Painlevé transcendents and the Painlevé property, the Hastings–McLeod solution, and the nonlinear steepest-descent method of Deift–Zhou.
- The Fokas–Its–Kitaev characterization of orthogonal polynomials, bulk (sine-kernel) and soft-edge (Airy-kernel) universality, Tracy–Widom distributions, and the Baik–Deift–Johansson theorem on longest increasing subsequences.
