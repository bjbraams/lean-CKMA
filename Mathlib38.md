# 38. Potential Theory and Capacity

Checked against Mathlib commit `f039e1b8a616d45782a5c7fc5e7909249c36b8ea` (2026-10-18, Lean `v4.36.0-rc1`). The checkout was only read. The survey below concerns this Mathlib revision; the TauCeti supplement and final gap list also use the [TauCeti source audit](TauCetiCoverage.md).

**Imports.** There is no aggregate module. A file `Mathlib/A/B/C.lean` is imported as `import Mathlib.A.B.C`. There is no import root for this subject. The nearest constructions are the Poisson integral of section 33, in `Mathlib.Analysis.Complex.Poisson` and `Mathlib.Analysis.Complex.Harmonic.Poisson`, and the Bessel potential spaces of section 26, in `Mathlib.Analysis.FunctionalSpaces.BesselPotentialSpace`.

**Namespaces.** None for capacity or potential theory. The Poisson kernel is defined in the root namespace of its file, and the harmonic Poisson formula is `InnerProductSpace`.

The Poisson integral on a disk in the complex plane is the formula of section 33: circle averages against the Poisson kernel recover holomorphic functions and real-valued harmonic functions inside the disk. It is not developed as potential theory. Harmonic functions on a finite-dimensional real inner product space are defined through the Laplacian (`InnerProductSpace.HarmonicOnNhd`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/Basic.lean`). In the plane, harmonic functions with values in a Banach space have the circle mean-value property (`InnerProductSpace.HarmonicOnNhd.circleAverage_eq`, `Analysis/Complex/Harmonic/MeanValue.lean`), and a bounded harmonic function on \(\mathbb C\) is constant (`Analysis/Complex/Harmonic/Liouville.lean`).

Bessel potential spaces are the Fourier-theoretic Sobolev spaces of section 26. No capacity is defined from them.

One computation of logarithmic potential theory is present: the circle average of \(z\mapsto\log|z-a|\) over the unit circle equals \(\log^+|a|\), that is, the logarithmic potential of normalized arc length on the circle (`circleAverage_log_norm_sub_const_eq_posLog` in `Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Integrals/PosLog.lean`). Jensen's formula, which expresses the circle average of \(\log|f|\) for a meromorphic \(f\) through its zeros and poles, is proved (`Analysis/Complex/JensenFormula.lean`) and underlies the Nevanlinna theory recorded in Section 41. In the language of Ransford, these are statements about the logarithmic potentials of specific measures and about the Riesz measure of \(\log|f|\); the general theory is absent.

No capacity, Newtonian potential, subharmonic function (as a class defined by the sub-mean-value property), equilibrium measure, fine topology, or thinness in the potential-theoretic sense was found.

TauCeti constructs the Euclidean Newtonian kernel and proves its fundamental-solution identity for \(-\Delta\) in dimension \(n\ge3\): for compactly supported \(C^2\) test functions, \(\int G_n\Delta\varphi=-\varphi(0)\), including translated poles ([distributional identity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/FundamentalSolution/Euclidean/DistributionalLaplacian.lean)). There is a separate logarithmic planar kernel ([planar kernel](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/FundamentalSolution/Planar/Basic.lean)). The Newtonian potential \(G_n\star f\) of a compactly supported \(C^2\) function solves \(-\Delta u=f\), is harmonic off the support of \(f\), decays like \(|x|^{2-n}\) for \(n\ge3\), and is the unique \(C^2\) solution vanishing at infinity; in the plane the logarithmic potential is the unique solution with \(u-G\int f\to0\) at infinity ([Newtonian potential](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/FundamentalSolution/Euclidean/Potential.lean), [planar potential](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/FundamentalSolution/Planar/Potential.lean)). Green kernels for balls/discs are constructed, with boundary vanishing, the distributional identity \(-\Delta G(\cdot,y)=\delta_y\) in the ball ([source](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/GreenFunction/DistributionalLaplacian.lean)), and the normal-derivative relation to the Poisson kernel ([Euclidean ball](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/GreenFunction/Ball.lean), [planar disk](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/GreenFunction/Disk.lean), [planar Poisson relation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/GreenFunction/Poisson.lean)). The half-space Green kernel is obtained by reflection ([half-space](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/GreenFunction/HalfSpace.lean)). The Poisson integral of continuous data on the sphere solves the Dirichlet problem in a ball ([ball Poisson integral](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/PoissonIntegral/Ball.lean); Section 32).

Nonnegative harmonic functions satisfy Harnack comparison on compact subsets of a connected open set in any finite dimension ([Harnack theorem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Harnack/Basic.lean)). The mean-value property on Euclidean balls, weak and strong maximum principles for \(C^2\) subharmonic functions (functions with \(\Delta u\ge0\)), and Hopf's boundary-point lemma are also proved (Section 33; [mean value](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/MeanValue/Basic.lean), [strong maximum principle](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Laplacian/StrongMaximumPrinciple.lean)). Conversely, a continuous function equal to its ball averages at every point for arbitrarily small radii is harmonic (`TauCeti.harmonicOnNhd_iff_continuousOn_setAverage_ball_eq`; [source](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/MeanValue/Converse.lean)).

Continuous subharmonic functions are defined by the local sub-mean-value inequality, without differentiability (`TauCeti.SubharmonicOn`; [source](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/Subharmonic.lean)). The class contains harmonic functions and is closed under maxima, nonnegative multiples and subtraction of harmonic functions, and the comparison principle holds on bounded open sets: a subharmonic \(u\) and a superharmonic \(w\), both continuous on \(\overline U\), with \(u\le w\) on \(\partial U\) satisfy \(u\le w\) on \(\overline U\). Perron's method is carried out on an arbitrary bounded open set \(\Omega\) ([source](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Perron/Basic.lean)): for boundary data bounded on \(\partial\Omega\), the supremum of the Perron family (functions continuous on \(\overline\Omega\), subharmonic in \(\Omega\), below \(g\) on \(\partial\Omega\)) is harmonic in \(\Omega\), with no regularity of \(\partial\Omega\) or continuity of \(g\) (`TauCeti.harmonicOnNhd_perronSolution`). At a boundary point with a barrier where \(g\) is continuous along \(\partial\Omega\), the Perron solution attains \(g\) continuously; if every boundary point has a barrier, the Dirichlet problem is solvable for continuous data, and the exterior sphere condition supplies Newtonian (\(n\ne2\)) or logarithmic (\(n=2\)) barriers (`TauCeti.tendsto_perronSolution_of_isBarrier`, `TauCeti.exists_harmonicOnNhd_continuousOn_closure_eqOn_frontier_of_exterior_sphere`; [source](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Perron/Barrier.lean)). The upper Perron solution is the symmetric construction; Wiener's resolutivity theorem (equality of the upper and lower solutions for continuous data on every bounded open set) and the Wiener criterion for regular points are not proved.

A capacity of Choquet type for measures appears in the proof that analytic sets are universally measurable ([source](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Analytic.lean)); it is not Newtonian capacity. No Newtonian, Sobolev or logarithmic capacity, equilibrium measure, fine topology, or general Riesz-potential mapping theory was located.

## Topics of Section 38 not found in either inspected library

- Capacities, including Newtonian, Bessel, Sobolev and logarithmic capacity, and the Choquet capacitability theorem for Newtonian capacity.
- A general Newtonian/Riesz potential-operator theory with mapping and regularity estimates, Green potentials on general domains, and Wolff potentials. The Newtonian potential of compactly supported \(C^2\) data and the Green kernels of the ball, disk and half-space are present in TauCeti.
- Equilibrium measures, Frostman's theorem for logarithmic energy, the transfinite diameter, and the fine topology and thin sets.
- Subharmonic and superharmonic functions in the upper-semicontinuous sense, the Riesz measure and its distributional characterization, the Riesz decomposition, and polar sets. TauCeti has continuous subharmonic functions defined by the sub-mean-value property.
- Wiener's resolutivity theorem for Perron solutions with continuous data, the Wiener criterion, harmonic measure on general domains, and the Martin boundary. TauCeti has Perron's theorem, barriers, and solvability of the Dirichlet problem under the exterior sphere condition.
- Logarithmic potentials with external fields in the sense of Saff–Totik; nonlinear potential theory, \(p\)-harmonic functions and the nonlinear Wiener criterion; the probabilistic potential theory of Brownian motion and Dirichlet forms.
