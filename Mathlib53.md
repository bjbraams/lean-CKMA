# 53. Elliptic Operators on Manifolds, Index Theory, and Spectral Geometry

Checked against Mathlib commit `f039e1b8a616d45782a5c7fc5e7909249c36b8ea` (2026-10-18, Lean `v4.36.0-rc1`). The checkout was only read. The survey below concerns this Mathlib revision; the TauCeti supplement and final gap list also use the [TauCeti source audit](TauCetiCoverage.md).

**Imports.** There is no aggregate module. A file `Mathlib/A/B/C.lean` is imported as `import Mathlib.A.B.C`. Some roots below are directory prefixes; the individual `.lean` files cited are the importable modules. There is no root for elliptic operators on manifolds, the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, Hodge theory, or spectral geometry. The neighbouring theories named below are `Mathlib.Analysis.Normed.Operator.Fredholm`, `Mathlib.Analysis.Distribution.DerivNotation`, `Mathlib.Analysis.InnerProductSpace.Spectrum`, and `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.ExteriorPower.HodgeStar`.

**Namespaces.** There is no namespace for the Laplace–Beltrami operator, the Dirac operator, or an analytic index. `ContinuousLinearMap.IsFredholm` is the Fredholm condition of Section 21. `Laplacian` is the notation typeclass whose Euclidean instance is a sum of second derivatives.

Elliptic theory on manifolds was not found. A search through `Geometry` for an elliptic operator, the Atiyah–Singer theorem, Hodge theory, a Weyl law, spectral geometry, a Dirac operator, and the Laplace–Beltrami operator returns nothing in that sense.

Fredholm operators, as in Section 21, are continuous linear maps that are strict, with closed finite-codimension range and finite-dimensional topologically complemented kernel (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Fredholm/Basic.lean`). When the scalar field and the domain are complete and the codomain is normed, the set of Fredholm operators is open in the operator norm, and the index is locally constant (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Fredholm/Open.lean`). That is the operator-theoretic index, not the index of an elliptic complex on a manifold.

The Euclidean Laplacian is the notation \(\Delta\) of `Analysis/Distribution/DerivNotation.lean`. On an inner-product space it is realised as the sum of second directional derivatives along an orthonormal basis, and the sum does not depend on the basis. It is not the Laplace–Beltrami operator of a Riemannian metric.

The spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators on an inner-product space is proved: the eigenspaces span a dense subspace, since their joint orthogonal complement is zero, and each eigenspace at a nonzero eigenvalue is finite-dimensional (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Spectrum.lean`). It is the abstract input to any discrete-spectrum theorem for an elliptic operator with compact resolvent. The Hodge star on exterior powers is the linear-algebraic isomorphism \(\bigwedge^k\simeq\bigwedge^{n-k}\) attached to a nondegenerate bilinear form and a volume element (`LinearAlgebra/ExteriorPower/HodgeStar.lean`); it is not stated on a manifold.

## Additional coverage in TauCeti

The following additions were checked in TauCeti revision `6ebb29f84b4d`; see the [revision, build evidence, and comparison scope](TauCetiCoverage.md). Source links below are pinned to that revision.

The abstract index-theory foundations are appreciably stronger: TauCeti proves Fredholm stability and index invariance under compact perturbations, the adjoint index identity on Hilbert spaces, and index zero for self-adjoint Fredholm operators ([compact perturbations](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/CompactPerturbation.lean), [adjoints](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/Adjoint.lean), [self-adjoint operators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/SelfAdjoint.lean)). Local constancy of the index is already in Mathlib, as recorded above; TauCeti restates it in \(\varepsilon\)-form ([small perturbations](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/SmallPerturbation.lean)) and deduces that the index of a continuous family of Fredholm operators is constant on a preconnected parameter space ([continuous families](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/ContinuousFamily.lean)). This is an integer-valued invariant, not an index class in \(K\)-theory. The nonlinear Fredholm theory of Section 35, including the Sard–Smale theorem, is the functional-analytic input for index computations of moduli spaces, not an index theorem.

For a divergence-form operator \(Lu=-\partial_j(a^{ij}\partial_iu)+b^i\partial_iu+cu\) on a bounded open set \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n\) with a symmetric coercive energy form on \(H^1_0(\Omega)\), TauCeti shows that the \(L^2\) solution operator is compact (by Rellich–Kondrachov) and symmetric, that its nonzero eigenvalues are the reciprocals of the Dirichlet eigenvalues, that the Dirichlet eigenfunctions contain a Hilbert basis of \(L^2(\Omega)\), and that the first eigenvalue is the minimum of the Rayleigh quotient ([Dirichlet spectrum](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Spectrum/Basic.lean)). For the shifted problem \(B(u,v)-\kappa\langle u,v\rangle=\langle f,v\rangle\) with bounded measurable coefficients, either the homogeneous problem has a nonzero solution, in a finite-dimensional solution space, or every \(L^2\) forcing has a unique weak solution ([Fredholm alternative](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/FredholmAlternative.lean)). On an interval \((a,b)\), the Dirichlet eigenvalues of \(-d^2/dx^2\) are exactly \((k\pi/(b-a))^2\), \(k\ge1\), with sine eigenfunctions, which gives the sharp Poincaré constant ([interval spectrum](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Spectrum/Interval.lean)). In finite dimensions, the Courant–Fischer min–max characterization of the eigenvalues of a symmetric operator, their monotonicity under form domination, and Weyl’s perturbation inequality are proved ([Courant–Fischer](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/CourantFischer.lean)). The Euclidean heat kernel \((4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-\|x\|^2/(4t)}\) and its semigroup are developed on \(\mathbb R^n\) ([heat kernel](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Basic.lean)). This is elliptic spectral theory on domains, without a smooth-boundary assumption, but not a Weyl asymptotic law, a heat-trace expansion, or an elliptic-operator theory on general manifolds.

The manifold-side ingredients described in Section 52 are present: the de Rham complex and its cohomology, Stokes’ theorem on oriented manifolds with boundary, and the Laplace–Beltrami operator \(\operatorname{div}\circ\operatorname{grad}\) of a Riemannian metric (see Mathlib52.md). Critical points of a Morse function on a compact manifold are finite in number and counted by index, with the counts for \(-f\) obtained by exchanging \(j\) and \(\dim M-j\) ([critical points](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Geometry/Manifold/Morse/CriticalPoints.lean)); the Morse inequalities are not proved. The Euler class of a real vector bundle is defined from a Thom class and is natural under pullback ([Euler class](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Topology/VectorBundle/EulerClass.lean)); the Thom isomorphism is not formalized. A compact convex hyperbolic polygon with \(n\) vertices and interior angles \(\alpha_i\) has area \((n-2)\pi-\sum\alpha_i\) ([hyperbolic Gauss–Bonnet](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/UpperHalfPlane/Polygon/GaussBonnet.lean)), which is not the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet theorem. Clifford algebras, spin groups, and spin representations are developed algebraically, without spinor bundles or Dirac operators. TauCeti's files under `Geometry/Hodge` concern algebraic Hodge structures; they do not establish harmonic representatives or the Hodge theorem on compact Riemannian manifolds.

## Topics of Section 53 not found in either inspected library

- Elliptic differential operators between bundles on closed manifolds, their symbols, and their Fredholm realization; elliptic operators on manifolds with boundary and Atiyah–Patodi–Singer boundary conditions.
- Hodge theory on a compact Riemannian manifold, with and without boundary; the Kähler Hodge decomposition and the hard Lefschetz theorem. TauCeti has de Rham cohomology but no harmonic representatives.
- The Witten deformation and the Morse inequalities; TauCeti counts critical points by index.
- Heat-kernel methods on manifolds: the local heat expansion, the McKean–Singer identity, the local index theorem, and the heat-kernel proof of the index theorem. Only the Euclidean heat kernel is present.
- The Atiyah–Singer index theorem and its special cases (spin index formula, Chern–Gauss–Bonnet, Hirzebruch signature, Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch), the families index theorem, the Atiyah–Patodi–Singer formula and the eta invariant, spectral flow as an index, and Atiyah’s \(L^2\) index theorem.
- The Dirac operator, its essential self-adjointness on complete manifolds, the Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula, and the positive-scalar-curvature obstruction. The spin Laplacian. Clifford algebras and spin representations are present only algebraically.
- A Weyl law (including the two-term boundary version), spectral zeta functions, analytic torsion and the Cheeger–Müller theorem, and Bergman kernel asymptotics. TauCeti proves the Dirichlet spectral theorem for symmetric coercive forms on bounded Euclidean domains and computes the interval spectrum explicitly.
- Eigenvalue inequalities and nodal theory: Szegő–Weinberger, Cheeger’s inequality, eigenvalue comparison theorems, the Steklov spectrum, Courant’s nodal theorem, nodal-volume bounds, and Sogge’s eigenfunction bounds.
- Geodesic spectral geometry: quantum ergodicity, the wave trace and length spectrum, the Selberg trace formula, hyperbolic resonances, and isospectrality.
