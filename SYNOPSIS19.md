# Synopsis: Banach lattices, positive operators, and ordered structures

Part of the [mathematical synopsis series](SYNOPSIS.md).

This synopsis selects the recurring core of Section 19 of
[CKMA-bib.md](CKMA-bib.md): vector
lattices, order and norm completeness, duality, representation, compactness,
and the spectral consequences of positivity. Ordered locally convex spaces
provide a wider setting, but the principal analytic statements below concern
Banach lattices. The order theory is real; spectral statements use the
complexification of a real Banach lattice.

A **vector lattice**, or **Riesz space**, is an ordered vector space in
which each pair has a supremum $x\vee y$ and infimum $x\wedge y$.
Write $x^+=x\vee0$, $x^-=(-x)\vee0$, and $|x|=x\vee(-x)$.
Vectors are **disjoint**, $x\perp y$, if $|x|\wedge|y|=0$.
A **Banach lattice** is a vector lattice with a complete norm satisfying
$|x|\leq|y|\Rightarrow\|x\|\leq\|y\|$. Its positive cone $E_+$ is
closed and generates $E$.

## Lattice structure, ideals, and bands

An **ideal** is a linear subspace $I$ such that $|x|\leq|y|$, $y\in I$,
implies $x\in I$. A **band** is an ideal closed under suprema of its
positive subsets whenever those suprema exist in the ambient space.
The disjoint complement is $A^d=\{x:x\perp a\text{ for all }a\in A\}$.
The space is **Archimedean** if $nx\leq y$ for all positive integers $n$
implies $x\leq0$, and **Dedekind complete** if every nonempty order-bounded
above subset has a supremum.

* **1. Lattice identities.** $x=x^+-x^-$, $|x|=x^++x^-$, and
  $x^+\perp x^-$. Moreover
  $x\vee y=(x+y+|x-y|)/2$ and $x\wedge y=(x+y-|x-y|)/2$.
  Every vector lattice is a distributive lattice, and
  $|x+y|\leq|x|+|y|$, $\bigl||x|-|y|\bigr|\leq|x-y|$.
* **2. Riesz decomposition.** If $0\leq x\leq y+z$ with $y,z\geq0$,
  then $x=x_1+x_2$ with $0\leq x_1\leq y$ and $0\leq x_2\leq z$.
  Finite families admit the analogous decomposition; this is the algebraic
  basis for positive-operator lattice formulas.
* **3. Order continuity of lattice operations.** If $x_\alpha\uparrow x$,
  then $x_\alpha\wedge y\uparrow x\wedge y$ and $x_\alpha\vee y\uparrow x\vee y$;
  more generally $y\wedge\sup_\alpha x_\alpha=\sup_\alpha(y\wedge x_\alpha)$
  whenever $\sup_\alpha x_\alpha$ exists.
* **4. Disjoint complements and generated bands.** In every vector lattice
  $A^d$ is a band and $A\cap A^d\subset\{0\}$. In an Archimedean vector
  lattice $A^{dd}$ is the smallest band containing $A$.
* **5. Band projections.** In a Dedekind complete vector lattice,
  every band $B$ gives $E=B\oplus B^d$ (Riesz). Its projection satisfies
  $0\leq P_B\leq I$ and
  $P_Bx=\sup\{y\in B:0\leq y\leq x\}$ for $x\geq0$. In a Dedekind
  $\sigma$-complete space this holds for principal bands.
* **6. Boolean algebra of bands.** The bands of an Archimedean vector lattice
  form a complete Boolean algebra under inclusion, with complement $B\mapsto B^d$.
  In the Dedekind complete case band projections commute, and products
  correspond to intersections and complements to $I-P$.
* **7. Dedekind completion.** Every Archimedean vector lattice embeds
  order densely and majorizingly in a Dedekind complete vector lattice,
  uniquely up to the order isomorphism fixing the original space.
* **8. Freudenthal spectral theorem.** In a vector lattice with the principal
  projection property (in particular a Dedekind $\sigma$-complete one), an
  element of the principal ideal generated by $u\geq0$ can be approximated
  uniformly in the order-unit norm by finite linear combinations of pairwise
  disjoint components of $u$.

## Norms, order continuity, and monotone completeness

The **principal ideal** $E_u=\{x:|x|\leq cu\text{ for some }c\}$ has
order-unit norm $\|x\|_u=\inf\{c:|x|\leq cu\}$. A positive $u$ is a
**strong unit** if $E_u=E$, and a **quasi-interior point** if $E_u$ is
norm dense. The norm is **order continuous** if
$x_\alpha\downarrow0\Rightarrow\|x_\alpha\|\to0$, for nets.
A **KB space** is a Banach lattice in which every increasing norm-bounded
positive sequence converges in norm.

* **9. Norm continuity of lattice operations.**
  $\||x|-|y|\|\leq\|x-y\|$ and
  $\|x^+-y^+\|\leq\|x-y\|$. Consequently the closure of a sublattice or
  ideal is again one, and closed ideals and closed sublattices are Banach
  lattices.
* **10. Ideal quotients.** If $I$ is a norm-closed ideal of $E$, then
  $E/I$ is a Banach lattice with its quotient norm, and the quotient map
  is a lattice homomorphism.
* **11. Order continuity criteria.** For a Banach lattice the following are
  equivalent: the norm is order continuous; every order-bounded disjoint
  sequence is norm null; every increasing order-bounded sequence in $E_+$
  is norm Cauchy; $E$ is Dedekind $\sigma$-complete and
  $x_n\downarrow0$ implies $\|x_n\|\to0$. An order continuous Banach
  lattice is Dedekind complete.
* **12. Order continuity and weak compactness.** $E$ has order continuous
  norm exactly when every order interval $[-u,u]$ is weakly compact.
  Thus $c_0$ has weakly compact order intervals even though its unit ball
  is not weakly compact.
* **13. Order continuity and the bidual.** Under the canonical lattice
  embedding $J:E\to E^{**}$, $J(E)$ is an ideal exactly when the norm
  of $E$ is order continuous.
* **14. KB characterizations.** For a Banach lattice $E$ the following are
  equivalent: $E$ is a KB space; $J(E)$ is a band in $E^{**}$; $E$ is
  weakly sequentially complete; $E$ has no closed subspace isomorphic to
  $c_0$; $E$ has no closed sublattice lattice isomorphic to $c_0$
  (Lozanovsky, Meyer-Nieberg). Every KB space has order continuous norm;
  the converse fails for $c_0$.
* **15. Reflexivity criterion.** $E$ is reflexive exactly when both $E$
  and $E^*$ are KB spaces, and exactly when $E$ contains no closed subspace
  isomorphic to $c_0$ or to $\ell^1$ (Lozanovsky); it suffices to exclude
  lattice copies.
* **16. Classical examples.** $L^p(\mu)$ has order continuous norm for
  $1\leq p<\infty$ and is a KB space. For $1<p<\infty$ it is reflexive.
  The supremum norms of $\ell^\infty$ and of $C(K)$, $K$ infinite, are not
  order continuous.
* **17. Compact order intervals.** Every order interval of $E$ is norm
  compact exactly when $E$ is atomic and has order continuous norm. Here
  atomic means that the band generated by its atoms is all of $E$; an
  atom $a>0$ has one-dimensional principal ideal.

## Order duality and positive operators

A linear operator is **positive** if $T(E_+)\subset F_+$, **regular** if
it is a difference of positive operators, and **order bounded** if it maps
order-bounded sets to order-bounded sets. The **order dual** $E^\sim$
consists of order-bounded scalar functionals. An operator is **order
continuous** if it preserves decreasing nets with infimum zero.

* **18. Automatic boundedness of positive maps.** Every everywhere-defined
  positive linear map from a Banach lattice into a normed lattice is bounded.
  Thus every regular map between Banach lattices is continuous.
* **19. Order dual equals continuous dual.** For a Banach lattice,
  $E^\sim=E^*$. The dual is a Dedekind complete Banach lattice, and
  $$|f|(x)=\sup_{|y|\leq x}|f(y)|,\qquad
    f^+(x)=\sup_{0\leq y\leq x}f(y)\quad(x\geq0)$$
  in the real case. Its lattice norm agrees with the operator norm.
* **20. Riesz–Kantorovich theorem.** If $F$ is Dedekind complete, the
  order-bounded maps $E\to F$ form a Dedekind complete vector lattice
  and are exactly the regular maps. For $x\geq0$,
  $$(S\vee T)x=\sup_{0\leq y\leq x}\{Sy+T(x-y)\},
    \qquad |T|x=\sup_{|y|\leq x}|Ty|.$$
* **21. Regular operator norm.** If $E,F$ are Banach lattices and $F$ is
  Dedekind complete, the regular maps form a Banach lattice for
  $\|T\|_r=\||T|\|$. One has $\|T\|\leq\|T\|_r$, with equality
  for positive $T$; equality need not hold for all regular maps.
* **22. Positive adjoints and norming.** The adjoint of a positive bounded
  map is positive. For $x\geq0$,
  $\|x\|=\sup\{f(x):f\in E_+^*,\|f\|\leq1\}$, so positive
  functionals suffice to recover the norm on the cone.
* **23. Order-continuous dual band.** Order-continuous functionals form
  a band $E_n^\sim$ in $E^\sim$. If the norm of $E$ is order continuous,
  then $E_n^\sim=E^*$.
* **24. Ideal–band duality.** If $I$ is an ideal in a Banach lattice,
  its annihilator $I^\perp\subset E^*$ is a weak-star closed band.
  This identifies $(E/I)^*$ with that band when $I$ is norm closed.
* **25. Kantorovich extension theorem.** Let $G$ be a majorizing vector
  subspace of an ordered vector space $E$ (each $x\in E$ satisfies
  $x\leq g$ for some $g\in G$) and $F$ a Dedekind complete vector lattice.
  Every positive operator $G\to F$ extends to a positive operator $E\to F$.
  For $F=\mathbb R$ this is the positive Hahn–Banach theorem.
* **26. Norm domination.** If $0\leq S\leq T$ between Banach lattices,
  then $|Sx|\leq S|x|\leq T|x|$ and $\|S\|\leq\|T\|$.
  In the complexification, a positive operator likewise satisfies
  $|Tz|\leq T|z|$.

## Representation and lattice functional calculus

An **AM space** satisfies $\|x\vee y\|=\max(\|x\|,\|y\|)$ for
$x,y\geq0$; an **AL space** satisfies
$\|x+y\|=\|x\|+\|y\|$ for $x,y\geq0$. An AM space **with unit**
has an element $e$ whose order interval $[-e,e]$ is its unit ball.
A **lattice homomorphism** is a linear map with $T(x\vee y)=Tx\vee Ty$,
equivalently $|Tx|=T|x|$.

* **27. Kakutani representation of AM spaces with unit.** Every AM space
  with unit is lattice isometric to $C(K)$ for a compact Hausdorff $K$,
  taking its unit to the constant one. Conversely every such $C(K)$ is
  an AM space with unit.
* **28. General AM representation.** Every AM space is lattice isometric
  to a closed sublattice of some $C(K)$; in general it need not be an
  ideal of, or isomorphic to, a $C(K)$ space.
* **29. Kakutani representation of AL spaces.** Every AL space is lattice
  isometric to an $L^1$ space for a suitable measure space. AL spaces
  are KB spaces and have order continuous norm.
* **30. Abstract $L^p$ spaces.** If $1\leq p<\infty$ and
  $\|x+y\|^p=\|x\|^p+\|y\|^p$ for all disjoint $x,y$ in a Banach lattice
  $E$, then $E$ is lattice isometric to $L^p(\mu)$ for some measure $\mu$
  (Bohnenblust–Nakano–Kakutani).
* **31. AM–AL duality.** The dual of an AL space is an AM space with
  unit, and the dual of an AM space is an AL space. The familiar pair
  $L^1$–$L^\infty$ and the measure dual of $C(K)$ are the basic models.
* **32. Principal-ideal representation.** For $u>0$ in a Banach lattice,
  $(E_u,\|\cdot\|_u)$ is an AM space with unit $u$, hence is $C(K)$
  for some compact $K$. Its inclusion into $E$ is continuous.
* **33. Krivine functional calculus.** For $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in E$, every
  continuous positively homogeneous real function $F$ on $\mathbb R^n$
  has a well-defined value $F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in E$, compatibly with
  lattice polynomials. Pointwise inequalities between such functions give
  order inequalities after substitution. In particular
  $(\sum|x_i|^p)^{1/p}$ is defined for $1\leq p<\infty$, and scalar
  inequalities such as Hölder's and Minkowski's hold in $E$.
* **34. Convexity, concavity, and square functions.** $E$ is
  **$p$-convex** if $\|(\sum|x_i|^p)^{1/p}\|\leq C(\sum\|x_i\|^p)^{1/p}$
  and **$q$-concave** if $(\sum\|x_i\|^q)^{1/q}\leq C\|(\sum|x_i|^q)^{1/q}\|$
  for all finite families. $E$ has finite cotype exactly when it is
  $q$-concave for some $q<\infty$ (Maurey). In every Banach lattice
  $\|(\sum|x_i|^2)^{1/2}\|\leq C\,\mathbb E\|\sum\varepsilon_ix_i\|$
  for independent signs $\varepsilon_i$; if $E$ has finite cotype the
  reverse inequality also holds, so Rademacher averages are equivalent
  to square functions.
* **35. Lattice homomorphisms.** A positive operator $T$ is a lattice
  homomorphism exactly when $T^*$ is interval preserving,
  $T^*[0,f]=[0,T^*f]$ for $f\geq0$. The lattice homomorphisms
  $C(K)\to C(L)$ are the weighted compositions $Tf=w\cdot(f\circ\varphi)$
  with $0\leq w\in C(L)$ and $\varphi$ continuous on $\{w>0\}$. Hence
  $C(K)$ and $C(L)$ are lattice isomorphic exactly when $K$ and $L$ are
  homeomorphic (Kaplansky).
* **36. Ideals of continuous-function spaces.** Closed ideals of $C(K)$
  are exactly $\{f:f|_F=0\}$ for closed $F\subset K$. In $L^p$ over a
  $\sigma$-finite measure space, $p<\infty$, closed ideals are exactly
  functions supported in a measurable subset, modulo null sets.
* **37. Conditional expectations as positive projections.** On a
  probability space, conditional expectation is a positive contractive
  projection on each scalar $L^p$, $1\leq p\leq\infty$, fixes constants,
  and preserves integrals. Its range is the corresponding sub-$\sigma$-algebra's
  $L^p$ space.

## Weak compactness and domination

A bounded set $A\subset E$ is **almost order bounded** if for every
$\varepsilon>0$ it lies in $[-u,u]+\varepsilon B_E$ for some $u\geq0$.
It is **L-weakly compact** if every disjoint sequence in its solid hull
is norm null. An operator into a Banach lattice is **L-weakly compact** if
it maps the unit ball onto an L-weakly compact set; an operator from a
Banach lattice is **M-weakly compact** if it sends bounded disjoint
sequences to norm-null sequences. An operator is **AM-compact** if it maps
order intervals to relatively compact sets.

* **38. Almost order boundedness.** In a Banach lattice with order
  continuous norm, almost order-bounded sets are relatively weakly compact.
  On a finite measure space, almost order-bounded subsets of $L^1$ are
  exactly the bounded uniformly integrable subsets.
* **39. L-weak compactness.** Every L-weakly compact set is relatively
  weakly compact. In scalar $L^1$ on a finite measure space, relative weak
  compactness, L-weak compactness, and uniform integrability are equivalent.
* **40. L–M duality.** A bounded operator from a Banach space into a
  Banach lattice is L-weakly compact exactly when its adjoint is
  M-weakly compact. A bounded operator from a Banach lattice is M-weakly
  compact exactly when its adjoint is L-weakly compact.
* **41. Weak compactness domination.** If $0\leq S\leq T:E\to F$,
  $T$ is weakly compact, and either $E^*$ or $F$ has order continuous
  norm, then $S$ is weakly compact; this condition on $(E,F)$ is also
  necessary for the domination property (Wickstead). For endomorphisms of
  an arbitrary Banach lattice, $0\leq S\leq T$ with $T$ weakly compact
  implies that $S^2$ is weakly compact (Aliprantis–Burkinshaw).
* **42. Compactness domination.** If $0\leq S\leq T:E\to F$, $T$ is
  compact, and both $E^*$ and $F$ have order continuous norms, then
  $S$ is compact (Dodds–Fremlin). Without the order hypotheses this
  conclusion can fail, even when $T$ has rank one.
* **43. Cube theorem.** If $0\leq S\leq T$ are endomorphisms of any
  Banach lattice and $T$ is compact, then $S^3$ is compact
  (Aliprantis–Burkinshaw). In general $S^2$ need not be compact.
* **44. Product domination theorem.** If two successive positive maps
  $S_1:E\to F$, $S_2:F\to G$ are each dominated by compact positive
  maps, then $S_2S_1$ is compact whenever $E^*$ or $G$ has order
  continuous norm. A product of three such maps is compact without
  these additional assumptions.
* **45. AM-compact operators form a band.** If $F$ is Dedekind complete
  with order continuous norm, the AM-compact order-bounded operators
  $E\to F$ form a band in the regular operators (Dodds–Fremlin). In
  particular $|T|$ is AM-compact when $T$ is, and $0\leq S\leq T$ with
  $T$ AM-compact implies $S$ AM-compact.

## Positive spectra and Perron–Frobenius theory

A positive operator is **ideal irreducible** if it leaves no nonzero proper
closed ideal invariant. On an ordered Banach space whose closed cone $K$
has nonempty interior, $T$ is **strongly positive** if it maps
$K\setminus\{0\}$ into the interior of $K$.
For these statements $E$ is nonzero and spectra are taken in its
complexification.

* **46. Positive spectral radius.** For a bounded positive operator $T$
  on a Banach lattice, $r(T)\in\sigma(T)$. For real $\lambda>r(T)$,
  $(\lambda I-T)^{-1}=\sum_{n\geq0}\lambda^{-n-1}T^n\geq0$.
* **47. Spectral radius domination.** If $0\leq S\leq T$, then
  $r(S)\leq r(T)$. More generally $|Sx|\leq T|x|$ implies the same
  spectral radius inequality.
* **48. Finite-dimensional Perron–Frobenius theorem.** An irreducible
  nonnegative $n\times n$ matrix, $n\geq2$, has $r>0$ as an algebraically
  simple eigenvalue with strictly positive right and left eigenvectors, and
  no other eigenvalue has a nonnegative eigenvector. If $h$ is its period,
  the peripheral eigenvalues are exactly $r e^{2\pi ik/h}$, $0\leq k<h$,
  each simple.
* **49. Primitive matrices.** If a nonnegative matrix has a strictly
  positive power, $r$ is its only peripheral eigenvalue and
  $r^{-n}T^n$ converges to the rank-one positive spectral projection.
  An irreducible matrix has this property exactly when its period is one.
* **50. Krein–Rutman existence theorem.** If $T$ is a compact positive
  operator on a Banach lattice (more generally on an ordered Banach space
  with closed total cone) and $r(T)>0$, then $r(T)$ is an eigenvalue of
  both $T$ and $T^*$ with nonzero positive eigenvectors. Compactness alone
  does not guarantee $r(T)>0$.
* **51. Strong Krein–Rutman theorem.** If $T$ is compact and strongly
  positive on an ordered Banach space whose cone has nonempty interior,
  then $r(T)>0$ is an algebraically simple eigenvalue with an interior
  eigenvector, it is the only eigenvalue with a positive eigenvector, and
  every other spectral value has modulus strictly less than $r(T)$. Typical
  spaces are $C(\overline\Omega)$ and $C^1_0(\overline\Omega)$, the latter
  for elliptic resolvents with the strong maximum principle.
* **52. Irreducible compact operators.** If $T$ is compact, positive, and
  ideal irreducible on a Banach lattice of dimension greater than one, then
  $r(T)>0$ (de Pagter). Its positive eigenvector is quasi-interior, its
  dual eigenfunctional is strictly positive, the eigenvalue $r(T)$ is
  algebraically simple, and the peripheral spectrum is $r(T)$ times the
  group of $h$-th roots of unity for some $h\geq1$.
* **53. Asymptotic rank-one convergence.** Under result 51,
  $r(T)^{-n}T^n\to P$ in operator norm, where
  $Px=f(x)u/f(u)$ for principal positive eigenvectors $u$ of $T$ and
  $f$ of $T^*$. If $\rho$ is the remaining spectral radius, then the
  error is $O(q^n)$ for every $\rho/r(T)<q<1$.

## Positive semigroups and approximation

A **positive $C_0$-semigroup** has $T(t)E_+\subset E_+$ for every
$t\geq0$. Its generator here is $A$, so $T(t)=e^{tA}$ symbolically.
The **growth bound** $\omega_0$ is the infimum of $\omega$ admitting
$\|T(t)\|\leq Me^{\omega t}$; the **spectral bound** is
$s(A)=\sup\{\operatorname{Re}\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma(A)\}$.

* **54. Resolvent criterion for positivity.** A $C_0$-semigroup is positive
  exactly when $R(\lambda,A)\geq0$ for every sufficiently large real
  $\lambda$, by the Laplace formula in one direction and the Euler formula
  $T(t)=\lim_n(\tfrac nt R(\tfrac nt,A))^n$ in the other.
* **55. Bounded positive perturbations.** If $A$ generates a positive
  $C_0$-semigroup and $B\geq0$ is bounded, then $A+B$ also generates a
  positive $C_0$-semigroup, and its semigroup dominates that of $A$.
* **56. Resolvent comparison.** If two positive semigroups satisfy
  $0\leq T(t)\leq S(t)$ for all $t\geq0$, then
  $0\leq R(\lambda,A_T)\leq R(\lambda,A_S)$ for sufficiently large
  real $\lambda$. Conversely such comparison of all sufficiently large
  real resolvents implies semigroup comparison.
* **57. Spectral bound of positive semigroups.** For a positive
  $C_0$-semigroup on a Banach lattice with $s(A)>-\infty$, one has
  $s(A)\in\sigma(A)$, and for $\operatorname{Re}\lambda>s(A)$ the resolvent
  is the (improper) Laplace transform $R(\lambda,A)x=\lim_{t\to\infty}
  \int_0^te^{-\lambda s}T(s)x\,ds$. Always $s(A)\leq\omega_0$; equality
  holds on AL spaces and $C_0(\Omega)$ (Derndinger), on Hilbert lattices,
  and on every $L^p$, $1\leq p<\infty$ (Weis), but can fail on other
  Banach lattices.
* **58. Beurling–Deny criteria.** Let $a$ be a closed nonnegative symmetric
  form on real $L^2(\mu)$ with associated semigroup $e^{-tA}$. The
  semigroup is positive exactly when $u\in D(a)$ implies $|u|\in D(a)$ and
  $a(|u|,|u|)\leq a(u,u)$. It is moreover $L^\infty$-contractive
  (sub-Markovian) exactly when $u\in D(a)$ implies $0\vee u\wedge1\in D(a)$
  and $a(0\vee u\wedge1,0\vee u\wedge1)\leq a(u,u)$.
* **59. Matrix generators.** A real matrix generates a positive semigroup
  exactly when its off-diagonal entries are nonnegative. It generates a
  stochastic semigroup on column probability vectors exactly when, in
  addition, each column sum is zero.
* **60. Positive contraction extension.** A positive map acting consistently
  as a contraction on scalar $L^1$ and $L^\infty$ is a contraction on
  every intermediate $L^p$, $1\leq p\leq\infty$.
* **61. Order-unit norm test.** For a positive operator on $C(K)$,
  $\|T\|=\|T1\|_\infty$. Thus a positive unital operator is contractive,
  and uniform bounds on $T_n1$ control the operator norms of positive
  approximation schemes.
* **62. Korovkin theorem.** If positive linear maps
  $T_n:C[0,1]\to C[0,1]$ satisfy
  $T_n1\to1$, $T_nt\to t$, and $T_nt^2\to t^2$ uniformly, then
  $T_nf\to f$ uniformly for every $f\in C[0,1]$. The Bernstein operators
  $B_nf(x)=\sum_{k=0}^nf(k/n)\binom nkx^k(1-x)^{n-k}$ satisfy
  $B_n1=1$, $B_nt=t$, $B_nt^2=t^2+t(1-t)/n$, which proves the Weierstrass
  theorem.
* **63. Positive mean ergodic projection.** If a positive operator $T$ is
  power bounded on a reflexive Banach lattice, its Cesàro means converge
  strongly to a positive projection onto $\ker(I-T)$. The same conclusion
  holds for time averages of bounded positive $C_0$-semigroups on reflexive
  Banach lattices.

## Reference guide

Aliprantis–Burkinshaw supplies the vector-lattice foundations, duality,
Banach lattice structure, and compactness domination theorems. Schaefer
independently develops representation, positive spectra, irreducibility,
and Korovkin approximation, beginning with finite-dimensional
Perron–Frobenius theory. Luxemburg–Zaanen and Meyer-Nieberg provide the
broader structural tradition underlying these common topics; Meyer-Nieberg
and Lindenstrauss–Tzafriri II treat convexity, concavity, and square
functions. Bátkai–Kramar Fijavž–Rhandi connects them with modern positive
semigroup theory, as do the Engel–Nagel and Nagel references in the
bibliography; Ouhabaz is the standard source for the Beurling–Deny criteria.
General nonlinear cone spectral theory and its projective metrics are beyond
this selection.
