## Chebyshev polynomials

Over any commutative ring \(R\), the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind \(T_n\in R[X]\), indexed by \(n\in\mathbb{Z}\), are defined by \(T_0=1\), \(T_1=X\), and \(T_{n+2}=2XT_{n+1}-T_n\), with the same recurrence run backwards for negative indices. They are even in the index: \(T_{-n}=T_n\). The second kind starts with \(U_0=1\), \(U_1=2X\), and the same recurrence; \(U_{-1}=0\). Rescalings satisfy \(C_n(2X)=2T_n(X)\) and \(S_n(2X)=U_n(X)\). The product identity is \(2T_m T_k=T_{m+k}+T_{m-k}\), and composition is \(T_{mn}=T_m\circ T_n\). The formal derivative is \((T_n)'=n\,U_{n-1}\), and the iterated derivatives at \(1\) are given explicitly, in particular \(T_n'(1)=n^2\) (`RingTheory/Polynomial/Chebyshev.lean`, `derivative_T_eval_one`, `iterate_derivative_T_eval_one`).

Over an integral domain in which \(2\neq 0\), \(T_n\) has degree \(\lvert n\rvert\) and leading coefficient \(2^{\lvert n\rvert-1}\), the case \(n=0\) being the constant \(1\).

On the complex and real trigonometric functions, \(T_n(\cos\theta)=\cos(n\theta)\) and \(U_n(\cos\theta)\sin\theta=\sin((n+1)\theta)\), with the analogous identities for \(\cosh\) and \(\sinh\) (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Trigonometric/Chebyshev/Basic.lean`).

The orthogonality measure `measureT` is Lebesgue measure on \((-1,1]\) with density \((1-x^2)^{-1/2}\). For a real function,
\[
\int f\,d\mu_T=\int_{-1}^{1} f(x)\,(1-x^2)^{-1/2}\,dx=\int_0^\pi f(\cos\theta)\,d\theta,
\]
and every function continuous on \([-1,1]\) is integrable. The polynomials of the first kind are orthogonal for this measure: \(\int T_0\,d\mu_T=\pi\) and \(\int T_n\,d\mu_T=0\) for \(n\neq 0\); \(\int T_n T_m\,d\mu_T=0\) for distinct natural numbers \(n,m\); \(\int T_0^2\,d\mu_T=\pi\); and \(\int T_n^2\,d\mu_T=\pi/2\) for \(n\neq 0\) (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Trigonometric/Chebyshev/Orthogonality.lean`). Orthogonality of \(U_n\) against the weight \(\sqrt{1-x^2}\), and the statement that the \(T_n\) form a Hilbert basis of \(L^2(\mu_T)\), are marked as future work in that file.

For \(n\neq 0\), \(\lvert T_n(x)\rvert\leq 1\) if and only if \(\lvert x\rvert\leq 1\). The roots of \(T_n\) are the simple points \(\cos\bigl((2k+1)\pi/(2n)\bigr)\) for \(k=0,\ldots,n-1\). The roots of \(U_n\) are the simple points \(\cos\bigl((k+1)\pi/(n+1)\bigr)\) for \(k=0,\ldots,n-1\). The local extrema of \(T_n\) on \((-1,1)\) occur at \(\cos(k\pi/n)\). A nonzero root of \(T_n\) is irrational. On \([-1,1]\), the \(k\)-th derivative of \(T_n\) is bounded by its value at \(1\) (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Trigonometric/Chebyshev/RootsExtrema.lean`).

Chebyshev–Gauss quadrature is the equal-weight rule at the roots of \(T_n\). For \(n\neq 0\) and a real polynomial \(P\) of degree less than \(2n\),
\[
\int P\,d\mu_T=\frac{\pi}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} P\Bigl(\cos\frac{(2i+1)\pi}{2n}\Bigr)
\]
(`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Trigonometric/Chebyshev/ChebyshevGauss.lean`). This is quadrature for the single weight \((1-x^2)^{-1/2}\), not a general Gauss quadrature theorem.

The extremal comparison used in approximation theory is in the same directory: if \(n\ge1\), \(\deg P\leq n\), and \(\lvert P\rvert\leq 1\) on \([-1,1]\), then the leading coefficient of \(P\) is at most \(2^{n-1}\), with equality for \(n\geq 2\) if and only if \(P=T_n\); under the same hypotheses \(P^{(k)}(x)\le T_n^{(k)}(x)\) for \(x\ge1\) (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Trigonometric/Chebyshev/Extremal.lean`; see Section 47).

## Hermite and shifted Legendre

The probabilists’ Hermite polynomials are the integer polynomials defined by \(He_0=1\) and \(He_{n+1}=X\,He_n-(He_n)'\). Each \(He_n\) is monic of degree \(n\), with an explicit coefficient formula, and the coefficient of \(X^k\) vanishes when \(n+k\) is odd (`RingTheory/Polynomial/Hermite/Basic.lean`). Analytically, the only identity proved is the Gaussian derivative formula: the \(n\)-th derivative of \(\exp(-y^2/2)\) at \(x\) equals \((-1)^n He_n(x)\exp(-x^2/2)\) (`RingTheory/Polynomial/Hermite/Gaussian.lean`). No integral orthogonality for the Gaussian weight is proved.

The shifted Legendre polynomials are
\[
P_n(X)=\sum_{k=0}^n (-1)^k\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{n} X^k\in\mathbb{Z}[X],
\]
of degree \(n\). Rodrigues’ formula is \(n!\,P_n=\frac{d^n}{dX^n}\bigl(X^n(1-X)^n\bigr)\), and evaluation satisfies \(P_n(x)=(-1)^n P_n(1-x)\) (`RingTheory/Polynomial/ShiftedLegendre.lean`). The file remarks that these polynomials appear in the theory of orthogonal polynomials. No integral orthogonality on \([0,1]\) is proved. The classical (unshifted) Legendre polynomials were not found; the Legendre symbol of number theory is a different object.

## Moment problems and recurrences

The three-term recurrence is present as the defining relation of \(T_n\) and \(U_n\), and the file records that relating the definition to the general `LinearRecurrence` API is future work. That recurrence is not given a spectral theorem.

Hermite polynomial/function orthogonality and completeness are implemented. The normalized Hermite functions form a Hilbert basis of \(L^2(\mathbb R)\), with creation/annihilation identities and the harmonic-oscillator differential equation ([orthogonality](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Hermite/Orthogonality.lean), [basis](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Hermite/Function/HilbertBasis.lean), [oscillator](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Hermite/Function/Oscillator.lean)). With the weight in the measure, \(He_n/\sqrt{n!}\) is an orthonormal basis of \(L^2\) of the standard Gaussian measure ([Gaussian Hermite basis](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Probability/Distributions/Gaussian/Hermite/Basis.lean)). The normalized Chebyshev \(T_n\) form a Hilbert basis for the weighted \(L^2\) space with weight \((1-x^2)^{-1/2}\) on \([-1,1]\), with Parseval identities ([Chebyshev basis](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Trigonometric/Chebyshev/HilbertBasis.lean), [Parseval](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Trigonometric/Chebyshev/Parseval.lean)); cosine and unweighted forms of this basis are described in Section 47.

Finite measures on \(\mathbb R\) are determined by their polynomial moments under a finite exponential-moment hypothesis ([determinacy](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Probability/Moments/Determinacy.lean)), and correspondingly a function in \(L^2(\nu)\) orthogonal to all monomials vanishes when \(e^{a\lvert x\rvert}\in L^1(\nu)\) for some \(a>0\) ([vanishing moments](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Probability/Moments/VanishingMoments.lean)). Finite measures on \([0,\infty)\) are determined by their Laplace transform, even its values at the nonnegative integers ([Laplace uniqueness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Probability/Moments/LaplaceDeterminacy.lean)). Two finite measures on a compact subset of \(\mathbb R^n\) with the same mixed monomial moments are equal ([compact determinacy](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Probability/Moments/CompactDeterminacy.lean)); this is the uniqueness half of the Hausdorff moment problem, in several variables. These are determinacy theorems; exponential integrability is strictly stronger than the Carleman condition, and none of them solves the Hamburger, Stieltjes or Hausdorff existence problem for a prescribed moment sequence.

A general completeness criterion connects the two: if a weighted measure on \(\mathbb{R}\) has a finite exponential moment, any orthogonal family of polynomials of exact degrees is a Hilbert basis of \(L^2\) of that measure, because a function orthogonal to every monomial vanishes by moment determinacy ([polynomial completeness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/PolynomialCompleteness.lean), [weighted bases](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/WeightedOrthogonalBasis.lean)).

On the existence side, Herglotz's theorem is proved: a positive-definite function \(\varphi\) on \(\mathbb Z\) is the moment sequence \(\varphi(n)=\int z^n\,d\mu(z)\) of a finite positive measure on the unit circle (`TauCeti.exists_isFiniteMeasure_integral_zpow_eq`, via Fejér densities; [Herglotz](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Bochner/Herglotz.lean)). This is the trigonometric moment problem, not the Verblunsky or Szegő theory of the associated orthogonal polynomials. The Hausdorff–Bernstein–Widder theorem characterizes the functions continuous on \([0,\infty)\) and completely monotone on \((0,\infty)\) as Laplace transforms of finite measures on \([0,\infty)\), with uniqueness ([Bernstein's theorem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/CompletelyMonotone/Bernstein/HausdorffBernsteinWidder.lean)); the sequence analogue (Hausdorff's theorem for completely monotone sequences) is not derived. A positive-semidefinite Hankel sequence \((a_{m+n})\) that is bounded above satisfies \(a_1\le a_0\) (`TauCeti.sub_nonneg_of_posSemidef_hankel`; [Hankel estimate](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Matrix/PosSemidef.lean)), a Cauchy–Schwarz consequence rather than a moment existence theorem. Stieltjes functions determine their representing data uniquely ([Stieltjes uniqueness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/CompletelyMonotone/Stieltjes/Uniqueness.lean)), and the boundary Cayley map transports Herglotz representations to Nevanlinna representations on the real line ([Nevanlinna transport](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Pick/Nevanlinna.lean)); no indeterminate moment problem or Nevanlinna parametrization is built on these.

## Topics of Section 48 not found in either inspected library

- Existence criteria for prescribed Hamburger, Stieltjes or Hausdorff moment sequences (positivity of Hankel forms, completely monotone sequences), the correspondence between determinacy and essential self-adjointness, the Carleman criterion, and the Nevanlinna parametrization of indeterminate problems. TauCeti proves moment determinacy under exponential integrability, uniqueness on compact sets and from Laplace transforms, Herglotz's theorem on the circle, and Bernstein's theorem for completely monotone functions.
- Jacobi operators, the spectral correspondence for bounded Jacobi matrices, and a spectral theorem for three-term recurrences.
- Integral orthogonality of the shifted Legendre polynomials and of \(U_n\). Hermite and Chebyshev \(T_n\) orthogonality and completeness are proved in TauCeti.
- The classical Legendre polynomials on \([-1,1]\), as opposed to the shifted family, and the Laguerre, Jacobi, and Gegenbauer families.
- General orthogonal polynomials with respect to a measure: Favard's theorem, the Christoffel–Darboux formula, reality, simplicity and interlacing of zeros, Gauss quadrature for a general weight, and asymptotic zero distribution (equilibrium measures). Gauss quadrature is proved only for the Chebyshev weight.
- Orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle: the Szegő recursion and Verblunsky's theorem, the CMV representation, and Szegő's theorems (including the strong Szegő limit theorem for Toeplitz determinants).
