## The Laplacian and classical potential methods

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS32.md#the-laplacian-and-classical-potential-methods).

### Mathlib

**The Laplacian and harmonic functions.**

On a finite-dimensional real inner product space \(E\), with values in a real normed space \(F\), the Laplacian of \(f:E\to F\) is the second derivative contracted against the canonical covariant tensor of \(E\). Equivalently, for any orthonormal basis \((e_i)\),
\[
\Delta f(x)=\sum_i D^2f(x)(e_i,e_i),
\]
and on \(\mathbb{R}\) this is the ordinary second derivative. The same contraction within a set of differentiability defines \(\Delta[s]\), and it agrees with \(\Delta\) when the set is everything. On functions of class \(C^2\), the Laplacian is real-linear (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Laplacian.lean`).

A function is harmonic at a point when it is of class \(C^2\) there and its Laplacian vanishes on a neighborhood of the point. It is harmonic on a neighborhood of a set when it is harmonic at every point of the set. Harmonicity is local, and it is preserved by addition, scalar multiplication, and post-composition with a continuous real-linear map. Constant functions are harmonic. The set of points of harmonicity is open (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/Basic.lean`).

A function is said to be harmonic on a set and continuous on its closure when both of those hold. On a closed set the second clause follows from harmonicity on the set. The predicate is stable under addition, subtraction, real scalar multiplication, and continuous real-linear post-composition, and it passes to subsets (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/HarmonicContOnCl.lean`).

On the complex plane, a map that is of class \(C^2\) over \(\mathbb{C}\) at a point is harmonic there over \(\mathbb{R}\). A map into a complete complex normed space that is complex-analytic at a point is harmonic there. The real part, the imaginary part, and the complex conjugate of a function \(\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}\) are harmonic at a point of complex analyticity, and so is \(\log\|f\|\) at any point where an analytic \(f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}\) does not vanish (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/Constructions.lean`).

A real-valued function harmonic on a ball in \(\mathbb{C}\) is the real part of a holomorphic function on that ball, and a real-valued function harmonic on the whole plane is the real part of an entire function. A real-valued function harmonic at a point of \(\mathbb{C}\) is real-analytic there (`Analysis/Complex/Harmonic/Analytic.lean`). Real analyticity of harmonic functions in higher dimensions is not proved.

**Mean value, Liouville, and the Poisson integral in the plane.**

The Mathlib mean-value property is a theorem about circle averages on disks in \(\mathbb{C}\), not about ball averages in a general Euclidean space. Let \(F\) be a complete real normed space. If \(f:\mathbb{C}\to F\) is harmonic on a neighborhood of the closed disk of radius \(|R|\) about \(c\), the circle average of \(f\) on the circle of radius \(R\) about \(c\) equals \(f(c)\). If instead \(f\) is harmonic on the open disk and continuous up to the closure, the same equality holds (`Analysis/Complex/Harmonic/MeanValue.lean`). Completeness of \(F\) is required because the circle average is a Bochner integral. The proof reduces to the real-valued case by continuous linear functionals.

Liouville’s theorem: a harmonic map \(f:\mathbb{C}\to E\) into a real normed space, defined and harmonic on the whole plane, with bounded image, is constant (`InnerProductSpace.bounded_harmonic_on_complex_plane_is_constant`, `Analysis/Complex/Harmonic/Liouville.lean`).

The Poisson kernel of a disk of center \(c\), at an interior point \(w\) and a boundary point \(z\), is
\[
P_c(w,z)=\frac{\|z-c\|^2-\|w-c\|^2}{\|(z-c)-(w-c)\|^2}.
\]
On the circle it agrees with the real part of the Herglotz–Riesz kernel \((z-c+(w-c))/(z-c-(w-c))\) (`Analysis/Complex/Poisson.lean`). For a complex-differentiable map from a disk into a complete complex normed space, continuous up to the closure, the circle average of the Poisson kernel against the map recovers the value at any interior point. The same formula holds with the real part of the Herglotz–Riesz kernel in place of the Poisson kernel.

For real-valued harmonic functions the same identities are theorems, not only for holomorphic functions. If \(f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}\) is harmonic on a neighborhood of the closed disk of radius \(R>0\) about \(c\), or harmonic on the open disk and continuous on its closure, and if \(w\) lies in the open disk, then the circle average of \(z\mapsto P_c(w,z)\,f(z)\) equals \(f(w)\), and likewise with the real part of the Herglotz–Riesz kernel (`Analysis/Complex/Harmonic/Poisson.lean`). This is a representation formula; the solution of the Dirichlet problem for given boundary data on a disk is not stated in Mathlib.

**The divergence theorem on a box.**

The Henstock–Kurzweil form uses a non-standard gauge integral on a closed rectangular box \(I\) in \(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\), identified with \(\mathrm{Fin}(n+1)\to\mathbb{R}\). Tags must lie in their boxes, the partition must be subordinate to a gauge, and every box is required to have bounded eccentricity: the ratios of its side lengths are at most a prescribed constant. In dimension one that eccentricity restriction is automatic for constants at least \(1\), so the integral reduces to the ordinary Henstock–Kurzweil integral (`Analysis/BoxIntegral/DivergenceTheorem.lean`).

If \(f\) takes values in \(E^{n+1}\), with \(E\) a real normed space, and \(f\) is continuous on \(I\) and Fréchet differentiable within \(I\) off a countable set, then the divergence \(\sum_i \partial_i f_i\), formed by applying the derivative to the \(i\)-th basis vector and reading the \(i\)-th component, is integrable in this gauge sense. Its integral equals the sum, over the \(n+1\) coordinate directions, of the integrals of the corresponding component of \(f\) over the upper face minus the integral over the lower face. The file describes this identity as a divergence theorem and tags it as Stokes’ theorem, but the statement is the rectangular divergence theorem, not Stokes’ theorem for differential forms.

The Bochner form assumes \(a\le b\) in \(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\) and a map \(f\) into \(E^{n+1}\) that is continuous on the closed box \([a,b]\), Fréchet differentiable on the interior off a countable set, and whose divergence is Bochner-integrable on the box (`MeasureTheory/Integral/DivergenceTheorem.lean`). The integral of the divergence over \([a,b]\) then equals the same alternating sum of face integrals. If some pair of opposite faces coincides, both sides are zero. An equivalent statement is given for a family of scalar-valued component functions. The same identity is rewritten for a linearly ordered normed space affinely identified with \(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\) by a volume-preserving equivalence, and there are two-dimensional specializations along rectangles in the plane, with and without a countable exceptional set. These box theorems are the only Mathlib form of the Gauss–Green formula; Green's identities on curved domains are not stated.

## Weak elliptic problems and boundary regularity

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS32.md#weak-elliptic-problems-and-boundary-regularity).

### Mathlib

**Lax–Milgram.**

Let \(V\) be a real Hilbert space and let \(B:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}\) be a continuous bilinear form. The form is coercive when some \(C>0\) satisfies \(C\|u\|^2\le B(u,u)\) for every \(u\). Coercivity implies that the bounded operator \(B^\sharp:V\to V\) defined by \(\langle B^\sharp v,w\rangle=B(v,w)\) is bounded below, hence injective, has closed range, and is surjective. It is therefore a continuous linear automorphism of \(V\): for every \(\ell\in V^*\) there is a unique \(u\in V\) with \(B(u,w)=\ell(w)\) for every \(w\), and \(u\) depends continuously and linearly on \(\ell\). This follows by applying its inverse to the Riesz representative of \(\ell\) (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LaxMilgram.lean`). The scalar field is \(\mathbb{R}\). Mathlib does not specialize it to a differential operator.

### TauCeti

TauCeti defines weak divergence-form Dirichlet equations \(-\partial_j(a^{ij}\partial_iu)+b^i\partial_iu+cu=f\) on \(H^1_0(\Omega)\) for real-valued functions. For bounded measurable coefficients and a coercive energy form, every \(L^2\) forcing has a unique weak solution, with an energy estimate (`TauCeti.PDE.existsUnique_isWeakSolutionDirichlet`); a general functional on \(H^1_0(\Omega)\) is reached only through the abstract Lax–Milgram theorem applied to the bundled energy form. Concrete sufficient ellipticity, drift, and mass conditions imply coercivity: on a domain inside a ball or slab with \(c\ge0\) and small drift, or on any open set with a mass floor dominating the drift; in particular the zero-boundary Poisson problem is solved on bounded open domains, without a smooth-boundary hypothesis. Nonzero boundary values \(g\in H^1(\Omega)\) are imposed as \(u-g\in H^1_0(\Omega)\), and the resulting problem again has a unique solution (`TauCeti.PDE.existsUnique_sub_mem_w1p0Submodule_forall_energyFormH1_eq`; [Dirichlet problem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/DirichletProblem.lean)). A function of class \(C^2\) on a bounded open \(\Omega\), continuous on the closure, zero on the boundary, with \(\Delta w\in L^2\), lies in \(H^1_0(\Omega)\) and is the weak solution for its own Laplacian ([classical solutions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/ClassicalSolution.lean)).

**Gårding's inequality.** For uniformly elliptic bounded measurable coefficients with \(|b|\le\beta\) and \(c\ge\delta\), \(\delta\) of either sign, every \(u\in H^1(\Omega)\) satisfies \((\lambda-\varepsilon)\|\nabla u\|_2^2+(\delta-\beta^2/4\varepsilon)\|u\|_2^2\le a(u,u)\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\) (`TauCeti.PDE.UniformlyEllipticOn.garding_energyFormH1_self_of_mass_lower_bound_with_parameter`; [energy form and Gårding](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/EnergyForm/Sobolev.lean)).

**Maximum principles.** Weak maximum and comparison principles hold for subsolutions whose positive part lies in \(H^1_0\), under uniform ellipticity, \(c\ge0\), and a drift smallness condition ([weak maximum principle](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/MaximumPrinciple/Weak.lean)), and on sets of finite measure without the smallness condition ([bounded drift](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/MaximumPrinciple/Drift.lean)). For classical \(C^2\) functions on a compact set, the weak maximum principle, the comparison principle and Dirichlet uniqueness hold for the nondivergence operator \(a^{ij}\partial_{ij}+b^i\partial_i\) with \(c\ge0\), variable elliptic \(a\) and bounded drift ([non-divergence form](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/MaximumPrinciple/NonDivergence.lean)). Hopf's boundary-point lemma and the strong maximum principle are proved for \(-\Delta-b\cdot\nabla+c\) with bounded drift and \(0\le c\) bounded, that is, with the Laplacian as principal part (`TauCeti.fderiv_pos_of_mul_le_laplacian_add_fderiv_of_lt_ball_of_le_sphere`; [Hopf lemma](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Laplacian/HopfLemma.lean), [strong maximum principle](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Laplacian/StrongMaximumPrinciple.lean)); for weak subsolutions of divergence-form equations with measurable coefficients a strong maximum principle for the essential supremum is proved ([weak strong maximum principle](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/MaximumPrinciple/Strong.lean)).

**Fredholm alternative and spectrum.** On bounded domains the solution operator is compact on \(L^2\), and for a scalar mass shift \(c-\kappa\) either the homogeneous problem has a nonzero solution or every \(L^2\) forcing has a unique weak solution; the homogeneous solution space is finite dimensional (`TauCeti.PDE.fredholmAlternative_isWeakSolutionDirichlet_sub_const`; [alternative](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/FredholmAlternative.lean)). The solvability condition in terms of the adjoint kernel is not stated. With a symmetric coercive energy form on a bounded domain, Dirichlet eigenfunctions form an orthonormal basis of \(L^2\), with positive eigenvalues and finite-dimensional eigenspaces, and the first eigenvalue is the minimum of the Rayleigh quotient (`TauCeti.PDE.exists_hilbertBasis_forall_isDirichletEigenvalue`, `TauCeti.PDE.isLeast_rayleighQuotient_firstDirichletEigenvalue`; [spectrum](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Spectrum/Basic.lean)). The Dirichlet spectrum of \(-\Delta\) on an interval is computed explicitly ([interval](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Spectrum/Interval.lean)). Courant–Fischer min–max for higher eigenvalues, simplicity and positivity of the first eigenfunction, and boundary smoothness of eigenfunctions are not proved.

**Regularity.** Interior regularity: if the principal coefficients are uniformly elliptic and locally Lipschitz on \(\Omega\), \(b,c\in L^\infty\), \(f\in L^2(\Omega)\), and \(u\in H^1(\Omega)\) is a weak solution with no boundary condition, then \(u\in H^2(V)\) for every \(V\Subset\Omega\) (`TauCeti.PDE.UniformlyEllipticOn.exists_lowerOrder_eq_restrictL_of_locallyLipschitzOn`; [interior \(H^2\)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Regularity/Interior.lean)); the local statement carries no norm estimate. For smooth principal coefficients, no lower-order terms, and \(f\in W^{k,2}(\Omega)\), \(u\in H^{k+2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\), and smooth data give a solution a.e. equal to a smooth function ([interior \(H^k\)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Regularity/HigherOrder.lean), [smoothness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Regularity/Smooth.lean)). Up to the boundary, only the constant-coefficient half-space model is proved: for a constant uniformly elliptic matrix, the \(H^1_0\) solution of the Dirichlet problem on a half-space with \(f\in L^2\) lies in \(H^2\) of the half-space ([half-space \(H^2\)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Regularity/HalfSpace.lean)). The \(L^p\) theory is the whole-space Calderón–Zygmund estimate \(\|D^2u\|_p\le C\|\Delta u\|_p\), \(1<p<\infty\), and its constant-coefficient \(W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)\) form ([Calderón–Zygmund](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Regularity/Hessian/Lp.lean), [strong solutions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Regularity/Hessian/Sobolev.lean)); further elliptic regularity is recorded in [STRUCTURE33.md](STRUCTURE33.md).

**Traces.** On Lipschitz domains, local traces in boundary charts are bounded operators, and \(u\in H^1(\Omega)\) lies in \(H^1_0(\Omega)\) exactly when all chart traces vanish ([chart traces](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/LipschitzDomain/Trace/Basic.lean), [zero trace](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/LipschitzDomain/Trace/Kernel.lean)). These traces are measured in chart coordinates; no trace space \(H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)\) or right inverse of the trace is constructed.

**Synopsis entries.** Covered: 32.27 (TauCeti, real-valued functions). Partly covered: 32.24 (\(L^2\) forcing, drift and signed mass allowed under coercivity; \(H^{-1}\) data only through abstract Lax–Milgram), 32.25 (boundary data \(g\in H^1(\Omega)\) via \(u-g\in H^1_0\); no \(H^{1/2}\) lifting), 32.28 (Fredholm dichotomy for a scalar mass shift; no adjoint-kernel condition), 32.30 (membership in \(H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}\) without the local estimate), 32.31 (integer orders, divergence form without lower-order terms, \(H^1\) weak solutions), 32.32 (constant coefficients on a half-space), 32.33 (whole-space constant-coefficient \(W^{2,p}\) estimate), 32.35 (comparison for nondivergence operators; Hopf and strong principle with Laplacian principal part; weak divergence-form strong principle), 32.36 (orthonormal eigenbasis and finite multiplicities; no boundary smoothness), 32.37 (Rayleigh characterization of the first eigenvalue only). Not covered: 32.26, 32.38.

## Heat equations and parabolic energy methods

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS32.md#heat-equations-and-parabolic-energy-methods).

### TauCeti

The Gaussian heat kernel \(K_t(x)=(4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^2/4t}\) is positive with mass one, satisfies \(\partial_tK=\Delta K\) and \(K_t*K_s=K_{t+s}\) ([heat kernel](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Basic.lean)), and its derivatives of every order have Gaussian bounds ([bounds](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Bounds.lean)). For bounded measurable \(g\), \(K_t*g\) is smooth for \(t>0\), solves the heat equation, and tends to \(g(x_0)\) at points of continuity ([heat equation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/HeatEquation.lean), [approximate identity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/ApproximateIdentity.lean)). Convolution with \(K_t\) is a strongly continuous contraction semigroup on \(L^p\), \(1\le p<\infty\), whose generator agrees with \(\Delta\) on compactly supported \(C^2\) functions (`TauCeti.heatSemigroup`; [heat semigroup](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Semigroup.lean), [generator](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Generator.lean)). The ultracontractive bound \(\|K_t*f\|_\infty\le(4\pi t)^{-n/(2p)}\|f\|_p\) holds, and every \(L^p\) datum, \(p<\infty\), has a smooth representative of its evolution at positive times ([smoothing](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Smoothing.lean)). Uniqueness in \(C([0,T];L^p)\), uniqueness under Gaussian growth, and derivative smoothing estimates between \(L^p\) and \(L^q\) are not proved.

The weak maximum principle for \(\partial_tu-\Delta u-b\cdot\nabla u\) with time-dependent drift on a compact space-time cylinder bounds a subsolution by its values on the parabolic boundary; the minimum principle, the comparison principle, and uniqueness for the initial-boundary value problem follow (`TauCeti.le_of_deriv_le_laplacian_add_fderiv_le_parabolicBoundary`; [parabolic maximum principle](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/MaximumPrinciple/Parabolic.lean)).

For an abstract Gelfand triple, functions \(u\in L^2(a,b;V)\) with \(u'\in L^2(a,b;V^*)\) form a Banach space with a bounded map to a continuous \(H\)-valued representative, and \(\|u(t)\|_H^2-\|u(s)\|_H^2=2\int_s^t\langle u',u\rangle\) (`TauCeti.EvolutionSpace.norm_sq_sub_norm_sq_eq_integral`; [evolution space](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/EvolutionSpace/Basic.lean), [integration by parts](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/EvolutionSpace/IntegrationByParts.lean)). For \(u'+A(t)u=f\) with \(A(t)\) satisfying Gårding's inequality, an energy estimate and uniqueness are proved ([energy estimate](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Parabolic/EnergyEstimate.lean)), and existence follows from Lions' representation theorem ([existence](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Parabolic/Existence.lean)). The parabolic Dirichlet problem \(\partial_tu+L(t)u=f\), \(u=0\) on the lateral boundary, \(u(t_0)=u_0\), with jointly measurable bounded uniformly elliptic coefficients \(a(t,x)\), bounded drift and potential, \(f\in L^2(H^{-1})\), and \(u_0\in L^2\), has a unique weak solution \(u\in L^2(H^1_0)\) with \(u'\in L^2(H^{-1})\) and a continuous \(L^2\)-valued representative (`TauCeti.PDE.exists_hasWeakLineDerivOn_gelfandDual_energyFormH1`, `TauCeti.PDE.eqOn_of_hasWeakLineDerivOn_gelfandDual_energyFormH1`; [parabolic Dirichlet problem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Parabolic/DirichletProblem.lean)).

The Duhamel formula is proved only in the form \(K_t*\varphi-\varphi=\int_0^tK_s*\Delta\varphi\,ds\) for compactly supported \(C^2\) data; the inhomogeneous formula for a Dirichlet realization, the heat eigenfunction expansion, and the backward-heat instability are not stated.

Partly covered: 32.40 (\(L^p\) semigroup, smoothing and initial convergence; no uniqueness class or Tychonoff example), 32.41 (\(L^p\)–\(L^\infty\) bound without derivatives), 32.43 (abstract energy identity and estimate; the heat identity is not stated separately). Not covered: 32.46–32.49.

## General methods and structural conclusions

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS32.md#general-methods-and-structural-conclusions).

### TauCeti

For a Gelfand triple and an increasing family of finite-dimensional subspaces, the Galerkin approximations of the linear parabolic equation \(u'+A(t)u=f\) exist and are unique: for continuous forcing as global \(C^1\) solutions (`TauCeti.PDE.existsUnique_galerkinSolution`), and for measurable coefficients as Carathéodory solutions ([Galerkin](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Parabolic/Galerkin.lean)). The existence theorem for the weak parabolic problem is proved by Lions' theorem rather than by passing to the limit in these approximations.

**Synopsis entries.** Partly covered: 32.63 (existence of parabolic Galerkin approximations; no compactness passage to the limit). Not covered: 32.64–32.66, 32.68, 32.69.
