## Distributions

A test function of order \(n\) on an open set \(\Omega\) in a real normed space is a \(C^n\) map of compact support contained in \(\Omega\). Functions supported in a fixed compact carry the locally convex topology of uniform convergence of derivatives. The full test-function space carries the finest locally convex topology making every inclusion of such a subspace continuous, and a linear map into a locally convex space is continuous exactly when each of those restrictions is continuous (`Analysis/Distribution/TestFunction.lean`, `Analysis/Distribution/ContDiffMapSupportedIn.lean`).

An \(F\)-valued distribution on \(\Omega\) is a continuous real-linear map from the real test functions into a real topological vector space \(F\), with the topology of compact convergence. The text of the file specializes to finite-dimensional domains and locally convex values; those hypotheses are not part of the type. They are used where stated below. Distributions of order at most \(n\) form a separate space. The file's comparison of this topology with the strong dual topology assumes that the test functions are a Montel space and is not a theorem here (`Analysis/Distribution/Distribution.lean`).

The Dirac mass evaluates at a point, and vanishes if the point lies outside \(\Omega\). The directional derivative satisfies \(\langle\partial_v T,\varphi\rangle=\langle T,-\partial_v\varphi\rangle\), and the operator from order \(k\) to order \(n\) is zero unless \(k+1\le n\). A locally integrable function induces a distribution by integration against test functions, the zero distribution if it is not locally integrable. On a finite-dimensional Borel domain, with complete normed values, the distribution determines the function almost everywhere on \(\Omega\). The same almost-everywhere conclusion holds for a locally integrable function on a finite-dimensional real manifold. The support is the smallest closed set outside which the distribution vanishes; differentiation does not enlarge it; and in finite dimensions the support of a Dirac mass in \(\Omega\) is the point (`Analysis/Distribution/Distribution.lean`, `Analysis/Distribution/AEEqOfIntegralContDiff.lean`, `Analysis/Distribution/Support.lean`).

There is no space of distributions on a manifold, and the Schwartz kernel theorem is not proved. Both are named, the second as future work, in `Analysis/Distribution/Distribution.lean`.

## Schwartz space and tempered distributions

A Schwartz function is a smooth function between real normed spaces whose derivatives decay faster than every power of the norm. The countable family of seminorms expressing those bounds makes the space a first countable locally convex space. Completeness, the Fréchet property, and the Montel property are not proved. Smooth compactly supported functions are Schwartz. Against a measure of temperate growth — some negative power of \(1+\lVert x\rVert\) is integrable — Schwartz functions are integrable, the integral is continuous and linear, and for \(p\ge 1\) they map continuously into \(L^p\). On a finite-dimensional domain, with the measure finite on compact sets, they are dense in \(L^p\) for finite \(p\) (`Analysis/Distribution/SchwartzSpace/Basic.lean`, `Analysis/Distribution/TemperateGrowth.lean`).

On a finite-dimensional real inner product space the Fourier transform is a continuous linear endomorphism of Schwartz space. If the values are complete, inversion holds and the transform is a continuous linear equivalence. Derivatives pass through the transform as multiplication by \(\pm 2\pi i\langle\cdot,m\rangle\), and integration by parts holds for directional derivatives and the Laplacian (`Analysis/Distribution/SchwartzSpace/Deriv.lean`). Plancherel for Schwartz functions with values in a complete complex inner product space preserves the integrated inner product, and the \(L^2\) norms. The pointwise bound is the \(L^1\) norm (`Analysis/Distribution/SchwartzSpace/Fourier.lean`). The Fourier transform extends to a linear isometric equivalence of \(L^2\), compatible with the transforms on Schwartz functions and on tempered distributions (`Lp.fourierTransformₗᵢ`, `Analysis/Fourier/LpSpace.lean`). Convolution of Schwartz functions is a Schwartz function whose transform is the product of the transforms (`SchwartzMap.convolution`, `Analysis/Fourier/Convolution.lean`); convolution of distributions is not defined.

A tempered distribution is a continuous complex-linear map on complex Schwartz space, topologized by pointwise convergence rather than by the strong dual topology. Temperate measures, temperate functions, Schwartz functions, and \(L^p\) functions for \(p\ge 1\) embed into tempered distributions; the \(L^p\) embedding is injective in finite dimensions when the measure is locally finite. The Fourier transform, defined by transposition, is a continuous linear equivalence of tempered distributions and agrees on Schwartz functions with the function transform. The transform of the Dirac mass at the origin is Lebesgue measure. Multiplication by a temperate function, differentiation, and the Laplacian are continuous, and the first two do not enlarge support (`Analysis/Distribution/TemperedDistribution.lean`, `Analysis/Distribution/Support.lean`).

A Fourier multiplier by a scalar function \(g\) is \(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\circ(g\cdot)\circ\mathcal{F}\), on Schwartz functions and on tempered distributions. The multiplication is zero unless \(g\) has temperate growth. Multipliers compose by multiplication of symbols. The directional derivative and the Laplacian are multipliers, by \(2\pi i\langle\cdot,m\rangle\) and by \(-(2\pi)^2\lVert x\rVert^2\) respectively (`Analysis/Distribution/FourierMultiplier.lean`). Fourier multipliers with suitable symbol estimates are translation-invariant pseudodifferential operators; polynomial symbols already give differential operators of positive order. Bessel-potential Sobolev spaces are built from multipliers (`Analysis/Distribution/Sobolev.lean`). The Bessel potential of order \(s\) is the multiplier with symbol \((1+\lVert\xi\rVert^2)^{s/2}\), and a tempered distribution lies in \(H^{s,p}\) when its Bessel potential of order \(s\) is an \(L^p\) function. Schwartz functions lie in every \(H^{s,p}\); \(H^{s,2}\) is characterized by a weighted \(L^2\) condition on the Fourier transform; for \(2s>\dim E\) the transform of an \(H^{s,2}\) element is integrable; multipliers with bounded symbols preserve \(H^{s,2}\) (`MemSobolev.fourierMultiplierCLM_of_bounded`); \(H^{s,2}\subseteq H^{s',2}\) for \(s'\le s\); and directional derivatives and the Laplacian lower the order by one and two (`MemSobolev.lineDerivOp`, `MemSobolev.laplacian`). These are constant-coefficient statements.

What is missing here is the general theory of symbol classes, quantization of symbols depending on both position and frequency, and the associated composition formula.

## Additional coverage in TauCeti

The following additions were checked in TauCeti revision `6ebb29f84b4d`; see the [revision, build evidence, and comparison scope](TauCetiCoverage.md). Source links below are pinned to that revision.

TauCeti proves convergence of compactly supported smooth cutoffs in the Schwartz topology, including explicit estimates in each Schwartz seminorm, and hence density of smooth compactly supported functions in Schwartz space on finite-dimensional spaces ([cutoff approximation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Distribution/SchwartzSpace/Cutoff.lean)). It also develops weak Sobolev spaces and proves distributional fundamental-solution identities for the Euclidean Laplacian; see Sections 26 and 38.

TauCeti carries the constant-coefficient multiplier theory to \(L^p\). For an essentially bounded symbol \(m\), the \(L^2\) Fourier multiplier \(T_m=\mathcal F^{-1}(m\,\mathcal F\cdot)\) is bounded with \(\|T_m\|\le\|m\|_\infty\) ([\(L^2\) multipliers](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/L2Multiplier.lean)). The Mihlin–Hörmander multiplier theorem is proved: on a real inner product space of dimension \(n\ge1\), if \(m\) is smooth away from the origin with \(\|D^km(\xi)\|\le A\|\xi\|^{-k}\) for \(k\le N\) and \(2N>n\), then \(\|T_mf\|_p\le CA\|f\|_p\) for \(1<p<\infty\) with \(C\) independent of \(m\) and \(A\) (`TauCeti.exists_eLpNorm_fourierMultiplierCLM_le_of_norm_iteratedFDeriv_le`), and for symbols of temperate growth the tempered-distribution multiplier maps \(L^p\) to \(L^p\) with the same bound ([Mihlin–Hörmander](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/MihlinHormander.lean)). The proof uses Littlewood–Paley pieces of the symbol, the identification of multipliers with Schwartz symbols as convolutions, weighted Plancherel bounds on their kernels, and the Calderón–Zygmund theorem under Hörmander's condition ([Littlewood–Paley pieces](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Distribution/SchwartzSpace/LittlewoodPaley.lean), [band-limited kernels](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Distribution/SchwartzSpace/BandLimited.lean), [convolution kernels](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Distribution/SchwartzSpace/Convolution.lean), [weighted Plancherel](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Distribution/SchwartzSpace/WeightedPlancherel.lean)). The operator \(\partial_v\partial_w\Delta^{-1}\), with symbol \(\langle\xi,v\rangle\langle\xi,w\rangle/\|\xi\|^2\), gives \(\|\partial_v\partial_wu\|_{L^2}\le\|v\|\|w\|\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}\) for Schwartz \(u\) ([Hessian multiplier](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/HessianMultiplier.lean)).

On Mathlib's Bessel-potential spaces, TauCeti proves for \(1<p<\infty\) that Mihlin multipliers and real symbols of order \(0\) preserve \(H^{s,p}\), that \(H^{s,p}\subseteq H^{s',p}\) for \(s'\le s\), that \(\partial_m\) maps \(H^{s,p}\) to \(H^{s-1,p}\), and that \(f\in H^{s+1,p}\) exactly when \(f\) and all its first directional derivatives (equivalently, those along an orthonormal basis) lie in \(H^{s,p}\) (`TemperedDistribution.memSobolev_add_one_iff`); the Bessel potential of order \(2\) is \(1-(2\pi)^{-2}\Delta\) ([Bessel-potential regularity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Distribution/Sobolev.lean)). Smaller tools include the du Bois-Reymond lemma (a locally integrable function on an interval with vanishing distributional derivative is a.e. constant; [du Bois-Reymond](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Distribution/DuBoisReymond.lean)), the determination of tempered distributions by real compactly supported test functions ([test functions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Distribution/TemperedDistribution/TestFunction.lean)), and the a.e. determination of \(L^p\) functions by their pairings with Schwartz functions ([\(L^p\) embedding](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Distribution/TemperedDistribution/Lp.lean)).

All of these are Fourier multipliers or constant-coefficient operators; no symbol classes, pseudodifferential quantization, wavefront sets, or Fourier integral operator calculus were located.
