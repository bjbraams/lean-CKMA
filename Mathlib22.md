This note records partially defined operators and their adjoints, the Euclidean Laplacian as a pointwise differential operator, and the Lax–Milgram theorem.

## Partially defined operators

A partially defined linear map carries a domain. It is closed when its graph is closed, and closable when the closure of the graph is still a graph. A closable map has a closure, the smallest closed extension, whose graph is the closure of the graph; a subspace is a core when the closure of the restriction is the closure of the map (`LinearPMap.HasCore`), and the original domain is a core of the closure. For injective maps, closedness and closability pass to the inverse, and the closure of the inverse is the inverse of the closure (`Topology/Algebra/Module/LinearPMap.lean`).

On inner product spaces, \(T\) is a formal adjoint of \(S\) when \(\langle Tx, y\rangle = \langle x, Sy\rangle\) for \(x\) in the domain of \(T\) and \(y\) in the domain of \(S\). The adjoint \(T^\dagger\) is defined for every partially defined map. Its domain consists of those vectors \(y\) for which \(z\mapsto\langle y, Tz\rangle\) is continuous on the domain of \(T\). If the domain of \(T\) is dense and the source is a Hilbert space, the adjoint is the Riesz representative of that functional: it is a formal adjoint, it contains every formal adjoint, and its graph is closed. If the domain is not dense, the adjoint is defined to be the zero map on its domain. A self-adjoint partially defined operator is closed (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LinearPMap.lean`).

The bounded adjoint and this adjoint agree when the bounded operator is viewed as a partially defined operator with dense domain.

## The Laplacian

For a function from a finite-dimensional real inner product space into a real normed space, the Laplacian is the second derivative contracted against the canonical covariant tensor of the domain. It equals the sum of the second directional derivatives along any orthonormal basis. On \(\mathbb{R}\) it is the ordinary second derivative. A constant function has Laplacian zero. Functions that agree on a neighborhood have Laplacians that agree at the point. On twice continuously differentiable functions the Laplacian is \(\mathbb{R}\)-linear: it preserves addition, subtraction, negation, and scalar multiplication, and it commutes with postcomposition by a continuous linear map (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Laplacian.lean`).

On Schwartz functions and on tempered distributions the Laplacian is the Fourier multiplier with symbol \(-4\pi^2\lVert\xi\rVert^2\) in Mathlib's normalization (`Analysis/Distribution/FourierMultiplier.lean`, `SchwartzMap.laplacian_eq_fourierMultiplierCLM`, `TemperedDistribution.laplacian_eq_fourierMultiplierCLM`).

This is a differential operator on smooth functions and distributions. Mathlib does not realize the Laplacian as an unbounded operator on \(L^2\), and it does not prove self-adjointness or a spectral theorem.

## Lax–Milgram

Let \(V\) be a real Hilbert space, and let \(B\) be a bounded bilinear form \(V\times V\to\mathbb{R}\). The form is coercive when some constant \(C>0\) satisfies \(C\lVert u\rVert^2\le B(u,u)\) for every \(u\). After the Riesz identification \(V^*\cong V\), the operator \(B^\sharp\) defined by \(\langle B^\sharp v,w\rangle=B(v,w)\) is a continuous linear automorphism of \(V\). Thus, for every continuous linear functional \(\ell\in V^*\), there is a unique \(u\in V\) satisfying \(B(u,w)=\ell(w)\) for every \(w\), and the solution depends continuously and linearly on \(\ell\) (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LaxMilgram.lean`, `Analysis/Normed/Operator/NormedSpace.lean`).

Stone's theorem is proved: a self-adjoint partially defined operator on a complex Hilbert space gives a unique strongly continuous unitary group whose complex generator is \(iA\); conversely the unitary-group generator is skew-self-adjoint ([unbounded Stone theorem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Semigroups/Group/Stone/Unbounded.lean), [unitary generators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Semigroups/Group/Stone/Basic.lean)). The general closed-generator, resolvent, and generation results are described in Section 24.

For unbounded operators TauCeti also supplies the resolvent calculus that Mathlib's Banach-algebra resolvent cannot express. A bounded resolvent of a partially defined operator at \(\lambda\) is defined, with the resolvent set and the resolvent; the resolvent set is open and the resolvent is analytic in operator norm, by the Neumann series ([resolvent set](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/Resolvent/Unbounded.lean), [analyticity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/Resolvent/Analytic.lean)). For a self-adjoint operator every nonreal \(c\) is in the resolvent set: \(c-A\) is bijective with bounded inverse, by the identity \(\|(c-A)x\|^2=(\operatorname{Im}c)^2\|x\|^2+\|(\operatorname{Re}c-A)x\|^2\) and the absence of nonreal eigenvalues ([self-adjoint shifts](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LinearPMap/SelfAdjoint.lean)). More precisely, a shift \(z-A\) bounded below by \(c\lVert x\rVert\) gives a resolvent point with \(\lVert R(z)\rVert\le c^{-1}\), so \(\lVert R(z)\rVert\le|\operatorname{Im}z|^{-1}\), the spectrum is real, and \(R(z)^\ast=R(\bar z)\); conversely, if \(\sigma(A)\) avoids \((c-s,c+s)\) then \(\lVert R(c)\rVert\le s^{-1}\) ([resolvent bounds](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LinearPMap/Resolvent.lean), [spectral gaps](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LinearPMap/SpectralGap.lean)). The Cayley transform \((A-i)(A+i)^{-1}\) of a symmetric operator with \(-i\) in the resolvent set is an isometry, and for self-adjoint \(A\) it is unitary (`IsSelfAdjoint.cayley_mem_unitary`), so the continuous functional calculus of the unitary applies ([Cayley transform](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LinearPMap/Cayley.lean)). A subspace whose orthogonal projection preserves the domain and which is invariant together with its complement reduces \(A\), and the reduced part inherits density of the domain, closedness, symmetry and self-adjointness ([reducing subspaces](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LinearPMap/Reducing.lean)). These are the basic tools behind the deficiency operators \(\pm i-A\), but deficiency indices and the classification of self-adjoint extensions are not formalized.

Relative boundedness \(\lVert Vx\rVert\le a\lVert x\rVert+b\lVert Ax\rVert\) on \(D(A)\subseteq D(V)\) is set up with its calculus (`LinearPMap.IsRelativelyBounded`) ([relative bounds](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/LinearPMap/RelativeBound.lean)). The Kato–Rellich theorem is proved over any `RCLike` field: if \(A\) is self-adjoint, \(V\) symmetric and \(A\)-bounded with \(b<1\), then \(A+V\) is self-adjoint on \(D(A)\) (`IsSelfAdjoint.add_of_isRelativelyBounded`) ([Kato–Rellich](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LinearPMap/KatoRellich.lean)). A resolvent point \(\lambda\) of \(A\) survives a bounded perturbation \(B\) with \(\lVert B\rVert\lVert R(\lambda,A)\rVert<1\), with \(R(\lambda,A+B)=R(\lambda,A)(I-BR(\lambda,A))^{-1}\) ([resolvent perturbation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/Resolvent/Perturbation.lean)). For a nonnegative measurable multiplier \(m\), multiplication by \(-m\) on \(L^p(\mu)\), \(1\le p<\infty\), with its exact maximal domain, generates \(e^{-tm}\), and its resolvent is multiplication by \((\lambda+m)^{-1}\) ([multiplication generators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Semigroups/Multiplication/MeasureLp/Generator.lean)).

For a strongly continuous unitary representation of a locally compact abelian group, each diagonal matrix coefficient is the Fourier–Stieltjes transform of a finite measure on the dual group, the cyclic form of the SNAG theorem ([cyclic SNAG theorem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Pontryagin/StronglyContinuous.lean); see Section 20). With the scalar spectral measures of bounded normal operators (Section 21) and the Cayley transform, these are the ingredients of the unbounded spectral theorem, which itself is not formalized.

The Dirichlet eigenvalue problem for a divergence-form elliptic operator \(-\partial_j(a^{ij}\partial_i u)+b^i\partial_iu+cu\) on an open set \(\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n\) is developed in weak form through the solution operator \(S:L^2(\Omega)\to L^2(\Omega)\), with no boundary regularity. Under coercivity the eigenvalues are positive and bounded below by the coercivity constant; on a bounded domain \(S\) is compact by Rellich–Kondrachov; and when the energy form is symmetric, \(L^2(\Omega)\) has an orthonormal basis of Dirichlet eigenfunctions, with finite-dimensional eigenspaces ([Dirichlet spectrum](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Spectrum/Basic.lean); the abstract form for coercive variational problems is [variational spectrum](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Variational/Spectrum.lean)). This is the spectral theorem for the Dirichlet Laplacian and its divergence-form relatives on bounded domains, proved through the compact inverse; the operator is not realized as a self-adjoint `LinearPMap`, and the Rayleigh principle (the first eigenvalue is the minimum of the Rayleigh quotient, `IsCoercive.isLeast_rayleighQuotient`) is stated for the abstract variational problem ([Rayleigh](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Variational/Rayleigh.lean)). On a bounded interval \((a,b)\) the Dirichlet eigenvalues of \(-d^2/dx^2\) are exactly \((k\pi/(b-a))^2\), \(k\ge1\), with sine eigenfunctions ([interval spectrum](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Spectrum/Interval.lean)). In finite dimension the Courant–Fischer min–max characterization of the eigenvalues of a symmetric operator and Weyl's perturbation inequality are proved ([Courant–Fischer](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/CourantFischer.lean)). Lions' representation theorem, a Lax–Milgram variant with test space only mapped into the Hilbert space and no uniqueness, is also proved ([Lions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Lions.lean)).

The normalized Hermite functions form a Hilbert basis of \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) and satisfy the pointwise harmonic-oscillator equation \(-\psi_n''+x^2\psi_n=(2n+1)\psi_n\) ([basis](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Hermite/Function/HilbertBasis.lean), [eigen-equation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Hermite/Function/Oscillator.lean)). On the real Schwartz space, the creation and annihilation operators satisfy \([a,a^\dagger]=I\), with \(a\psi_n=\sqrt n\,\psi_{n-1}\), \(a^\dagger\psi_n=\sqrt{n+1}\,\psi_{n+1}\) and \(H\psi_n=(n+\tfrac12)\psi_n\) for \(H=a^\dagger a+\tfrac12\) ([ladder operators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Hermite/Function/Operator.lean)). These files do not identify a self-adjoint realization of the oscillator differential expression or prove a general Schrödinger spectral theorem.

## Topics of Section 22 not found in either inspected library

- A spectral theorem for unbounded self-adjoint operators, with a projection-valued measure and the characterization of the domain. TauCeti has the unitary Cayley transform and the cyclic SNAG theorem.
- Deficiency indices, von Neumann's classification of self-adjoint extensions, and general criteria for essential self-adjointness. In the Kato–Rellich setting, essential self-adjointness of \(A+V\) on every core of \(A\) follows by a short argument from TauCeti's Kato–Rellich theorem.
- Closed semibounded forms with the first representation theorem, the Friedrichs extension, and the KLMN theorem. TauCeti has Lax–Milgram–Lions solution operators for bounded coercive forms.
- The min–max principle for self-adjoint operators relative to \(\inf\sigma_{\rm ess}\), and the Birman–Schwinger principle. TauCeti has finite-dimensional Courant–Fischer and the Rayleigh formula for the first variational eigenvalue.
- Scattering theory.
- The Schrödinger operator as a self-adjoint operator.
- Self-adjointness of the Euclidean Laplacian defined above as an operator on \(L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)\), and its spectrum. TauCeti's Dirichlet spectral theorem on bounded domains is recorded above.
- Analytic perturbation theory in the sense of Kato: holomorphic families of type (A) or (B), Riesz projections, and perturbation of isolated eigenvalues. Relative boundedness, the Kato–Rellich theorem and resolvent analyticity are in TauCeti.
- Sturm–Liouville theory beyond the explicit Dirichlet spectrum of \(-d^2/dx^2\) on an interval, Weyl's limit-point/limit-circle alternative, and eigenfunction expansions for ordinary differential operators.
- The RAGE theorem and the Kato–Rosenblum theorem.
- Trace formulas, spectral shift functions, and pseudospectra of nonnormal operators. Operator-ideal gauges are recorded in Section 21.
