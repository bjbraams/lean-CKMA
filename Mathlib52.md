## Charts, smoothness, and the manifold derivative

A charted space carries an atlas of open partial homeomorphisms into the model space, covering the manifold. A model with corners is a map from that model space into a normed space, restricting to a well-behaved embedding of the model. The \(C^n\) groupoid consists of those partial homeomorphisms that are \(C^n\) when read in the normed space, for \(n\) a finite extended natural number, \(\infty\), or the analytic regularity \(\omega\). A space is a \(C^n\) manifold for that model when its atlas changes of chart lie in the groupoid (`Geometry/Manifold/IsManifold/Basic.lean`). The real models, defined in `Geometry/Manifold/Instances/Real.lean` and named in that header, are Euclidean space (no boundary), the Euclidean half-space (boundary), and the Euclidean quadrant (corners). The library assumes neither the Hausdorff property nor second countability; those hypotheses are added where a theorem needs them. The empty type, a discrete space, a product, and a disjoint union of \(C^n\) manifolds are again \(C^n\) manifolds.

A map is \(C^n\) when it is \(C^n\) in charts (`Geometry/Manifold/ContMDiff/Defs.lean`). The manifold Fréchet derivative `mfderiv` at a point is a continuous linear map between tangent spaces. If the map is not differentiable there, the derivative is the zero map. Derivatives within a set require a uniqueness-of-derivatives predicate on that set before the chain rule is available (`Geometry/Manifold/MFDeriv/Defs.lean`).

An immersion at a point is a map which, in some charts, has the normal form \(u\mapsto(u,0)\) for a chosen complement of the model. A submersion has the normal form \((u,v)\mapsto u\). Differentiability follows from the normal form. These definitions are the ones appropriate to infinite-dimensional models. The comparison with injectivity or surjectivity of `mfderiv`, and the equivalence of the two in finite dimension, is described as future work (`Geometry/Manifold/Immersion.lean`, `Geometry/Manifold/Submersion.lean`).

A local diffeomorphism is a map that restricts to a diffeomorphism between neighbourhoods of a point and of its image. A local diffeomorphism is a local homeomorphism, its image is open, and a bijective local diffeomorphism is a diffeomorphism. For regularity other than the zero regularity, the derivative of a diffeomorphism, and of a local diffeomorphism, is a continuous linear equivalence (`Geometry/Manifold/LocalDiffeomorph.lean`). A smooth embedding is a \(C^n\) immersion that is a topological embedding. The identity, an open inclusion, a model with corners, and the two inclusions of a disjoint union are smooth embeddings. Composition of smooth embeddings and their relation to diffeomorphisms are TODOs. The comment’s proposed implication from a local diffeomorphism to a global smooth embedding needs an injectivity hypothesis: a local diffeomorphism need not be globally injective. A homeomorphism that happens to be smooth need not be a smooth embedding (`Geometry/Manifold/SmoothEmbedding.lean`).

## Bump functions, partitions of unity, and smooth approximation

A smooth bump centred at a point is the data of radii \(0<r_{\mathrm{in}}<r_{\mathrm{out}}\), small enough that the closed ball of radius \(r_{\mathrm{out}}\) in the extended chart meets the range of the model inside the chart target. The associated function equals \(1\) on the closed ball of radius \(r_{\mathrm{in}}\), equals \(0\) outside the ball of radius \(r_{\mathrm{out}}\), and takes values in \([0,1]\). When the manifold is \(C^\infty\), this function is a \(C^\infty\) map to \(\mathbb{R}\), and the supports of such bumps form a neighbourhood basis (`Geometry/Manifold/BumpFunction.lean`).

On a Hausdorff \(\sigma\)-compact charted space modelled on a finite-dimensional real normed space, a closed set admits a locally finite covering by such bumps subordinate to any neighbourhood assignment (`Geometry/Manifold/PartitionOfUnity.lean`, `SmoothBumpCovering.exists_isSubordinate`). The file introduces that construction as the covering used on a smooth manifold. Once the space is a \(C^\infty\) manifold, the same hypotheses yield a smooth partition of unity subordinate to an open cover of a closed set, and the smooth Urysohn lemma: for disjoint closed sets there is a \(C^n\) function, any regularity \(n\), with values in \([0,1]\), equal to \(0\) on the first set and to \(1\) on the second. On a compact manifold the index set of a bump covering is finite.

Smooth approximation uses that partition. On a \(\sigma\)-compact Hausdorff \(C^\infty\) manifold modelled on a finite-dimensional real space, a continuous map into a real normed space may be approximated by a \(C^n\) map to within a prescribed continuous positive tolerance, with support contained in the support of the original map. If the original map is already \(C^n\) on a neighbourhood of a closed set, the approximant may be taken equal to it on that set (`Geometry/Manifold/SmoothApprox.lean`). The same statement on a finite-dimensional real normed space is the input to the \(L^p\) density of Section 47.

## Whitney embedding

For a compact Hausdorff \(C^\infty\) manifold modelled on a finite-dimensional real space, some Euclidean space \(\mathbb{R}^n\) receives a \(C^\infty\) closed embedding of the manifold whose differential is injective at every point (`Geometry/Manifold/WhitneyEmbedding.lean`, `exists_embedding_euclidean_of_compact`). The integer \(n\) is large enough for the finite bump covering used in the construction; it is not pinned to \(2m+1\). If a finite smooth bump covering exists, an immersion into some Euclidean space exists as well.

The weak Whitney theorem is a TODO in that file, in these words: any \(\sigma\)-compact smooth \(m\)-dimensional manifold embeds into \(\mathbb{R}^{2m+1}\), and the comment proposes a dimension/measure estimate for a locally Lipschitz map \(\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}^n\) with \(m<n\), whose image then has Hausdorff dimension at most \(m\) and hence measure zero. The general Hausdorff-dimension bounds for Lipschitz and differentiable maps are already available (Section 37), so that comment should not be read as saying that all such dimension estimates are absent. The \(\sigma\)-compact embedding is not a theorem of this checkout.

## Integral curves

On a \(C^1\) real manifold whose model is a complete normed space, a vector field that is \(C^1\) at an interior point admits a local integral curve through that point. On a Hausdorff manifold, two integral curves of a \(C^1\) field that agree at one time of an open interval, and that stay in the interior, agree throughout the interval. On a boundaryless manifold the interior hypothesis is free, and two global integral curves that agree at one time are equal. A global integral curve on a boundaryless manifold is periodic or injective, and not both (`Geometry/Manifold/IntegralCurve/ExistUnique.lean`).

The uniform-time lemma: on a boundaryless Hausdorff \(C^1\) manifold, if a single \(\varepsilon>0\) is such that every point has an integral curve of a \(C^1\) field defined on \((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\), then every point lies on a global integral curve (`Geometry/Manifold/IntegralCurve/UniformTime.lean`). Integral curves that meet the boundary are a TODO.

## The tangent bundle, brackets, and the Levi-Civita connection

The tangent space at a point is a type synonym of the model normed space. If \(M\) is \(C^{n+1}\), the tangent bundle carries the structure of a \(C^n\) vector bundle, with transition maps given by the derivatives of the changes of chart, and is then itself a \(C^n\) manifold (`Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/Tangent.lean`).

The Lie bracket of two vector fields is the pullback, through the extended chart, of the Lie bracket in the model space. The pullback of a bracket is the bracket of the pullbacks, and the Jacobi identity \([U,[V,W]]=[[U,V],W]+[V,[U,W]]\) holds (`Geometry/Manifold/VectorField/LieBracket.lean`).

A covariant derivative on a vector bundle is an operator on differentiable sections that is additive and satisfies the Leibniz rule. The difference of two connections is a one-form with values in endomorphisms, and adding such a one-form produces another connection. Metric compatibility of a connection on the tangent bundle means that the derivative of the metric tensor along any vector field, corrected by the two terms in which the connection hits one argument of the metric, vanishes. The torsion vanishes if and only if \(\nabla_X Y-\nabla_Y X=[X,Y]\) for differentiable vector fields (`Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/CovariantDerivative/`).

On a finite-dimensional real \(C^2\) manifold whose tangent spaces carry a Riemannian bundle metric of class \(C^1\), a Levi-Civita connection is a covariant derivative that is metric-compatible and torsion-free. The Koszul formula expresses \(\langle\nabla_X Y,Z\rangle\) in terms of derivatives of the metric coefficients and Lie brackets, for vector fields differentiable at the point in question. Two Levi-Civita connections therefore agree on every differentiable vector field, in every tangent direction; the value on a vector field that fails to be differentiable at the point is not constrained by the definition. A Levi-Civita connection is constructed and proved to be metric-compatible and torsion-free (`Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/CovariantDerivative/LeviCivita.lean`). Smoothness of this connection, when the manifold and the metric have higher regularity, is left to future work. Parallel transport is not given an isometry theorem.

## Riemannian length

Where each tangent space carries an extended norm, the length of a path \(\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M\) from time \(a\) to time \(b\) is the extended integral over \([a,b]\) of the extended norm of `mfderiv` \(\gamma\) applied to \(1\). The length is invariant under monotone reparametrisation. The Riemannian extended distance is the infimum of the lengths of \(C^1\) paths joining the two points. The file notes that the same quantity would be a Finsler distance, because the norm need not come from an inner product. The triangle inequality is proved (`Geometry/Manifold/Riemannian/PathELength.lean`).

`IsRiemannianManifold` is the proposition that an already given extended distance coincides with this infimum. An inner-product space, with the constant metric given by its inner product on each tangent space, satisfies the proposition. From a Riemannian metric one can build the extended distance: on a regular space the construction is a pseudo-extended-metric, and on a Hausdorff \(T_3\) space it is an extended metric, and the predicate then holds (`Geometry/Manifold/Riemannian/Basic.lean`).

A \(\sigma\)-compact Hausdorff charted space modelled, through a model with corners, on a finite-dimensional real normed space is metrizable (`Geometry/Manifold/Metrizable.lean`). The theorem uses the charted-space structure, not a \(C^n\) hypothesis.

## Lie groups

A Lie group of class \(C^n\) is a group and a charted space whose multiplication and inversion are \(C^n\). Second countability is not assumed, and the model need not be finite-dimensional. A normed space is an additive Lie group, and the units of a complete normed algebra form a multiplicative Lie group (`Geometry/Manifold/Algebra/LieGroup.lean`).

The Lie algebra of such a group, of regularity at least \(C^3\) over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\) and analytic over other fields, is the tangent space at the identity. The bracket of two tangent vectors is the value at the identity of the Lie bracket of the corresponding left-invariant vector fields, and this bracket makes the tangent space a Lie algebra (`Geometry/Manifold/GroupLieAlgebra.lean`). Left-invariant derivations of the smooth functions form a Lie algebra as well. For finite-dimensional \(C^\infty\) real groups the two descriptions should agree; that identification is a TODO.

## Algebraic spin and Hodge, and the divergence theorem on a box

The spin group of a quadratic form is the even part of the pin group inside the Clifford algebra (`LinearAlgebra/CliffordAlgebra/SpinGroup.lean`). The Hodge star on an exterior power is the algebraic isomorphism \(\bigwedge^k\simeq\bigwedge^{n-k}\) associated with a bijective bilinear form and a volume element (`LinearAlgebra/ExteriorPower/HodgeStar.lean`). Neither construction is stated on a manifold.

Differential forms are defined on normed spaces, as continuous alternating maps. The exterior derivative is linear, and the second exterior derivative of a form of sufficient smoothness vanishes. The Cartan formula evaluating \(d\omega\) on differentiable vector fields, with correction terms given by their Lie brackets, is proved on the normed space (`Analysis/Calculus/DifferentialForm/Basic.lean`, `Analysis/Calculus/DifferentialForm/VectorField.lean`). The file records that forms on manifolds are not defined yet.

The divergence theorem that is proved is Euclidean. For a Bochner integral, if a map from a rectangular box in \(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\) into a product of copies of a complete normed space is continuous on the box, differentiable off a countable subset of the interior, and has integrable divergence, then the integral of the divergence equals the signed sum of the integrals of the map over the faces (`MeasureTheory/Integral/DivergenceTheorem.lean`). A Henstock–Kurzweil form of the same statement, for a differentiable map on a closed rectangular box, is in `Analysis/BoxIntegral/DivergenceTheorem.lean`. This is the divergence theorem of Section 32, not Stokes’ theorem for a form on a manifold.

## The Poincaré conjecture, as a statement only

`Geometry/Manifold/PoincareConjecture.lean` defines the proposition that a homotopy equivalence from a manifold to a sphere is realised by a diffeomorphism, and records the classical status: true in dimensions \(1,2,3,5,6,12,56,61\), open in dimension \(4\). It does not prove the proposition. The `proof_wanted` declarations live in `Wanted/Geometry/Manifold/PoincareConjecture.lean`: the generalised topological conjecture, the three-dimensional topological and smooth conjectures, the existence of an exotic \(7\)-sphere, and the existence of a small exotic \(\mathbb{R}^4\). None of these is a theorem.
