This note records convex sets and functions in real vector spaces, the combinatorial theorems on hulls, extreme points, cones, and the separation and derivative theorems that support them. `Geometry/Convex` is the part of the theory that does not use a norm or an inner product. `Analysis/Convex` is the part that does.

## Convex sets, hulls, and convex functions

A set is convex when it is star-convex at each of its points, equivalently when it contains the segment between any two of its points. The convex hull is the closure operator generated by that predicate (`Analysis/Convex/Basic.lean`, `Analysis/Convex/Hull.lean`).

A real-valued function on a convex set is convex when
\[
f(a x+b y)\le a\,f(x)+b\,f(y)
\]
for \(a,b\ge 0\) with \(a+b=1\). It is strictly convex when the inequality is strict for \(a,b>0\) and \(x\neq y\). Concavity and strict concavity reverse the inequality (`Analysis/Convex/Function.lean`).

On a nonempty open convex set in a real normed space, local Lipschitz continuity, continuity, continuity at a single point, and local boundedness above are equivalent for a convex function, and likewise for local boundedness of the absolute value. In finite dimension a real-valued convex function is automatically locally Lipschitz, hence continuous, on the interior of its domain; continuity also holds on the relative interior. The same holds for concave functions. In infinite dimension the equivalences require one of the continuity or boundedness conditions; convexity alone does not imply continuity (`Analysis/Convex/Continuous.lean`).

A local minimum of a convex function on a convex set is a global minimum (`IsMinOn.of_isLocalMinOn_of_convexOn`, `Analysis/Convex/Extrema.lean`). The set of functions convex on a given set is closed in the topology of pointwise convergence (`Analysis/Convex/FunctionTopology.lean`). In a real locally convex space, a real function that is convex and lower semicontinuous on a closed convex set is the supremum of the restrictions of continuous affine functions below it, and of a countable such family when the space is hereditarily Lindelöf (`sSup_affine_eq`, `sSup_of_countable_affine_eq`, `Analysis/Convex/Approximation.lean`). This is the real-valued affine-minorant half of Fenchel–Moreau; Mathlib defines no Legendre–Fenchel conjugate.

## Jensen

The finite and integral forms of Jensen’s inequality, including the equality cases for strictly convex and strictly concave functions and the maximum principle on a convex hull, are stated in Section 11. The finite form lives in `Analysis/Convex/Jensen.lean`: nonnegative weights summing to \(1\), points in the convex domain, and \(f(\sum w_i p_i)\le\sum w_i f(p_i)\). The integral form, in `Analysis/Convex/Integral.lean`, needs a complete normed real space, a closed convex set, a continuous convex integrand, a finite nonzero measure, and integrability of \(f\) and of \(g\circ f\).

## The Minkowski functional

The gauge of a set \(s\) in a real vector space is
\[
\operatorname{gauge}_s(x)=\inf\{r>0:x\in r\cdot s\}.
\]
If \(s\) is convex and absorbent, the gauge is subadditive. If it is also balanced, the gauge is a seminorm. If \(s\) is a convex neighborhood of the origin in a topological real vector space, the gauge is continuous, the open sublevel set \(\{\operatorname{gauge}_s<1\}\) is the interior of \(s\), the closed sublevel set \(\{\operatorname{gauge}_s\le 1\}\) is the closure, and the level set \(\{\operatorname{gauge}_s=1\}\) is the frontier. In a normed space the gauge of a convex neighborhood of the origin is Lipschitz, and the gauge of the open unit ball is the norm (`Analysis/Convex/Gauge.lean`).

## Carathéodory, Radon, and Helly

Over a field that is a strict ordered ring, the convex hull of a set is the union of the convex hulls of its affine-independent finite subsets. A point of the hull is a convex combination, with strictly positive weights summing to \(1\), of an affine-independent family taken from the set (`Analysis/Convex/Caratheodory.lean`).

Radon’s theorem: an affinely dependent family can be split into a set and its complement whose convex hulls meet. In finite dimension this applies to any \(d+2\) points (`Analysis/Convex/Radon.lean`).

Helly’s theorem, for a finite family of convex sets in a finite-dimensional space of dimension \(d\): if every subfamily of size at most \(d+1\) has nonempty intersection, the whole family does. The classical finite form assumes at least \(d+1\) sets and assumes the intersections only for subfamilies of size exactly \(d+1\). For a possibly infinite family of compact convex sets in a Hausdorff finite-dimensional space, the same numerical hypotheses imply that the whole family meets. The file gives both the indexed form and the form for a set of sets; the classical compact form uses cardinality, or encard, at least \(d+1\).

## Extreme points, exposed sets, and Krein–Milman

A subset \(B\subseteq A\) is extreme when a point of \(B\) that lies on an open segment with ends in \(A\) forces the left endpoint, and hence the right endpoint, into \(B\). An extreme point of \(A\) lies on an open segment in \(A\) only in the trivial case (`Analysis/Convex/Extreme.lean`).

A set \(B\) is exposed in \(A\) when, as soon as \(B\) is nonempty, it is exactly the set of maximizers in \(A\) of some continuous linear functional. The empty set is exposed. An exposed set is extreme, and exposed points are extreme points (`Analysis/Convex/Exposed.lean`).

In a real locally convex Hausdorff topological vector space, a nonempty compact set has an extreme point, and a compact convex set is the closure of the convex hull of its extreme points. Every extreme point of the image of a compact set under a continuous affine map has an extreme-point preimage. In symbols, \(\operatorname{ext}(f(K))\subseteq f(\operatorname{ext}K)\). This inclusion can be strict: an extreme point of \(K\) need not map to an extreme point of its image (`Analysis/Convex/KreinMilman.lean`). The interior of a set in a nontrivial topological vector space contains no extreme point, and the extreme points of a strictly convex set in a nontrivial normed space are exactly its points outside the interior (`Analysis/Convex/Strict/Extreme.lean`). The finite-dimensional form without the closure, the Minkowski–Carathéodory theorem, is stated there to be absent from the library.

## Strict, uniform, and strong convexity

A set is strictly convex when the open segment between any two distinct points lies in its interior. An open convex set is strictly convex. A real normed space is strictly convex when the closed balls of positive radius about the origin are strictly convex sets; every closed ball is then strictly convex. In such a space, \(\|x+y\|<\|x\|+\|y\|\) if and only if \(x\) and \(y\) do not lie on the same ray (`Analysis/Convex/Strict.lean`, `Analysis/Convex/StrictConvexSpace.lean`).

A seminormed space is uniformly convex when, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), some \(\delta>0\) has the property that unit vectors with \(\|x-y\|\ge\varepsilon\) satisfy \(\|x+y\|\le 2-\delta\). The same modulus controls pairs of vectors in the closed unit ball, and a scaled form controls pairs of norm at most \(r\) (`Analysis/Convex/Uniform.lean`). Milman–Pettis and Hanner’s inequalities are marked as future work.

A function on a convex set in a real normed space is uniformly convex with modulus \(\varphi\) when
\[
f(ax+by)\le a f(x)+b f(y)-ab\,\varphi(\|x-y\|)
\]
for \(a,b\ge 0\) summing to \(1\). It is \(m\)-strongly convex when the modulus is \((m/2)r^2\). On a real inner product space this is equivalent to convexity of \(x\mapsto f(x)-(m/2)\|x\|^2\). A positive strong-convexity constant implies strict convexity (`Analysis/Convex/Strong.lean`). Derivative criteria for strongly convex functions are a TODO.

## Cones, Farkas, and Stone separation

A pointed cone in a module over an ordered ring is a submodule for the nonnegative scalars (`Geometry/Convex/Cone/Pointed.lean`). A proper cone in the analytic development is a closed submodule over \(\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), that is, a closed pointed cone in this terminology (`Analysis/Convex/Cone/Basic.lean`). Here “pointed” means that the cone contains the origin; it does not impose the usual additional condition that the cone contain no nontrivial line.

Farkas’ lemma, in a real locally convex space: if a proper cone is disjoint from a compact convex set, some continuous linear functional is nonnegative on the cone and strictly negative on the set. A point outside the cone is separated in the same way. For a continuous perfect pairing, the double dual of a proper cone is the cone (`Analysis/Convex/Cone/Dual.lean`). The algebraic dual, consisting of the vectors nonnegative on a set for a bilinear pairing, is developed without topology in `Geometry/Convex/Cone/Dual.lean`. In a complete real inner product space the inner dual of a closed convex cone has the cone as its own double dual, and a point outside such a cone is separated from it by a vector of the inner dual (`Analysis/Convex/Cone/InnerDual.lean`). The M. Riesz extension theorem for linear functionals nonnegative on a convex cone, and the algebraic Hahn–Banach theorem with a sublinear bound, are in `Analysis/Convex/Cone/Extension.lean`. Primal and dual cone programs, weak and strong conic duality, and linear-programming duality are listed as the next steps in `Analysis/Convex/Cone/Basic.lean` and are not theorems.

Stone’s separation theorem, for a module over an ordered field: two disjoint convex sets are contained, one in a convex set and the other in its complement, with that complement itself convex (`Analysis/Convex/StoneSeparation.lean`). The hyperplane separations used for Krein–Milman and Farkas are the locally convex Hahn–Banach theorems.

## Derivatives of convex functions of one variable

If \(f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\) is continuous on a convex set, differentiable on the interior, and the derivative is monotone on the interior, then \(f\) is convex. A strictly monotone derivative yields strict convexity; continuity on the domain is assumed, and strict monotonicity of the derivative supplies differentiability except at most at one point. A nonnegative second derivative on the interior, with the function and its first derivative differentiable there, yields convexity. A strictly positive second derivative yields strict convexity, again without a separate twice-differentiability hypothesis beyond what the sign of the second derivative gives. Antitone derivatives and nonpositive second derivatives give concavity (`Analysis/Convex/Deriv.lean`).

Conversely, a convex function on a real interval has left and right derivatives at each interior point: the right derivative is the infimum of the right-hand slopes, the left derivative is the supremum of the left-hand slopes, the left derivative is at most the right derivative, and both are monotone on the interior. If the function is differentiable on a subset of the domain, the derivative is monotone there. A sign change of the one-sided derivatives at an interior point, left derivative nonpositive and right derivative nonnegative, is a minimum on the domain. Where the function is differentiable, slopes compare with the derivative in the expected way, and strict convexity makes those comparisons strict. The concave statements are dual.

## Simplicial complexes

A simplicial complex in a module over the scalar field is a down-closed family of nonempty finite sets of vertices, each affinely independent, such that the convex hulls of two faces meet inside the convex hull of the intersection of their vertex sets. For faces \(s\) and \(t\),
\[
\operatorname{conv}(s)\cap\operatorname{conv}(t)=\operatorname{conv}(s\cap t).
\]
The textbook alternative, that two faces are disjoint or meet in a face, is deduced from that identity (`Analysis/Convex/SimplicialComplex/Basic.lean`).

## `Geometry/Convex`

`Geometry/Convex` builds convexity from convex combinations alone. The standard simplex on a type \(X\) is the set of finitely supported nonnegative weights on \(X\) summing to \(1\). A convex space is a type equipped with an operation taking such a weight system to a point, equal to the point itself on a Dirac weight, and associative in the monadic sense. A set is convex when it contains every convex combination of its points, and the folder has its own convex hull. Star-convex sets are included. Cones, faces, simplicial cones, and duals along a bilinear pairing stay algebraic (`Geometry/Convex/README.md`, `Geometry/Convex/ConvexSpace/Defs.lean`, `Geometry/Convex/Set.lean`).

`Analysis/Convex` adds the structure that needs a topology, a norm, or an inner product: continuity and Lipschitz estimates, gauges, strict and uniform convexity of spaces, strong convexity, integral Jensen, and the locally convex separation used for Krein–Milman and Farkas (`Analysis/Convex/README.md`).

Lagrange multiplier theorems for smooth equality-constrained extrema are also present in `Analysis/Calculus/LagrangeMultipliers.lean`; Section 36 states the hypotheses and the possibly abnormal multiplier conclusion. They do not constitute a subdifferential or KKT theory.

## Sion's minimax theorem

Sion's theorem is proved in `Topology/Sion.lean`, with quasiconvexity from `Analysis/Convex/Quasiconvex.lean`. For nonempty convex sets \(X,Y\) in real topological vector spaces, with \(X\) compact, lower-semicontinuous quasiconvex sections in \(x\) and upper-semicontinuous quasiconcave sections in \(y\) give the minimax equality, formulated using order bounds and complete-order versions. When both sets are compact, the corresponding saddle-point existence theorem is available. This is a convex minimax theorem, not the mountain-pass theorem for critical points of a nonlinear functional.

## Additional coverage in TauCeti

The following additions were checked in TauCeti revision `6ebb29f84b4d`; see the [revision, build evidence, and comparison scope](TauCetiCoverage.md). Source links below are pinned to that revision.

The extended-real Legendre–Fenchel conjugate is defined for a real bilinear dual pairing. TauCeti proves Fenchel–Young, the order-reversing conjugacy relation, \(f^{**}\le f\), and \(f^{***}=f^*\), as well as convexity and lower semicontinuity of the conjugate when the pairing functionals are continuous ([conjugacy](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Convex/Conjugate.lean)).

The Fenchel–Moreau theorem is proved. Let \(E\) be a real locally convex topological vector space and \(f:E\to[-\infty,\infty]\) convex (convex real epigraph), lower semicontinuous, and never \(-\infty\). For every \(x_0\) and real \(t<f(x_0)\) there is a continuous affine minorant of \(f\) taking the value \(t\) at \(x_0\) (`TauCeti.exists_affine_le_of_lt`); hence, for a pairing \(B\) that represents every continuous linear functional on \(E\), \(f^{**}=f\) (`TauCeti.fenchelConjugate_flip_fenchelConjugate_eq`). If in addition the functionals \(B(\cdot,y)\) are continuous, \(f^{**}=f\) holds exactly when \(f\) is convex, lower semicontinuous, and either never \(-\infty\) or identically \(-\infty\). No Hausdorff hypothesis is needed ([Fenchel–Moreau](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Convex/FenchelMoreau.lean)). For a superlinear continuous strictly convex function on a finite-dimensional real inner product space, the gradient of the conjugate is the unique maximizer in the conjugate supremum ([superlinear conjugates](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Convex/Superlinear.lean)).

The subdifferential is defined by supporting affine functions at points where the function is finite. Its values are closed and convex under the stated continuity hypotheses; equality in Fenchel–Young characterizes subgradients, and conjugate subgradient reciprocity is proved, with the reverse implication using equality with the biconjugate at the point ([subdifferentials](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Convex/Subdifferential.lean)). The subdifferential is monotone; the only calculus rule is that adding an affine function translates it (`mem_subdifferential_add_affine_iff`). In a finite-dimensional real inner product space, the subgradients of a convex function are bounded on compact subsets of the interior of the effective domain and the subgradient image of a compact subset of that interior is compact ([subgradient images](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Convex/SubgradientImage.lean)); the Hessian of a convex function, where it exists, is a positive operator, and a function on an open convex set with positive semidefinite Hessian everywhere is convex ([Hessians](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Convex/Hessian.lean)).

Alexandrov's theorem is proved. Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real inner product space with an additive Haar measure. For a real function \(u\) convex on a set \(\Omega\), at almost every \(x\in\Omega\) the function \(u\) is differentiable and there is a unique positive operator \(A\) with
\[
u(x')=u(x)+\langle\nabla u(x),x'-x\rangle+\tfrac12\langle A(x'-x),x'-x\rangle+o(\|x'-x\|^2)
\]
(`ConvexOn.ae_existsUnique_isPositive_isLittleO`); the same holds for an extended-real convex function at almost every point of its effective domain (`TauCeti.ae_existsUnique_isPositive_isLittleO`), and the subdifferential itself is differentiable there with derivative \(A\) ([Alexandrov](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Convex/Alexandrov.lean)). On the way, the resolvent \((I+\partial f)^{-1}\) of the subdifferential of a convex function on a real inner product space is shown to be \(1\)-Lipschitz on its range: \(y_i\in\partial f(x_i)\) implies \(\|x_1-x_2\|\le\|(x_1+y_1)-(x_2+y_2)\|\) (`norm_sub_le_norm_add_sub_add_of_mem_subdifferential`).

Proximal steps exist under compactness hypotheses. On an extended metric space, for an energy \(\varphi\) and a time step \(\tau>0\), the Moreau–Yosida resolvent, the set of minimizers of \(y\mapsto\varphi(y)+d(x,y)^2/(2\tau)\), is nonempty under the Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré hypotheses (lower semicontinuity for an auxiliary convergence, relative compactness of bounded sublevels, and coercivity), in particular for a lower-semicontinuous energy on a proper metric space (`moreauYosidaResolvent_nonempty`, `moreauYosidaResolvent_nonempty_of_lowerSemicontinuous`, [Moreau–Yosida existence](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/MoreauYosida/Existence.lean); gradient flows are in Section 36). The single-valued, firmly nonexpansive proximal map of a convex function on a general Hilbert space and maximal-monotone operator theory are not developed.

The Milman–Pettis theorem is proved: a uniformly convex real Banach space is reflexive, its canonical image in the bidual being norm dense by Goldstine's theorem and closed by completeness; uniform convexity passes to subspaces (`TauCeti.surjective_inclusionInDoubleDual_of_uniformConvexSpace`, [Milman–Pettis](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Convex/Uniform.lean)).

Among theorems of the alternative, Stiemke's lemma is proved: a subspace of \(\mathbb R^\iota\) meets the nonnegative orthant only in \(0\) exactly when some strictly positive vector is orthogonal to it, with a lattice version for subgroups of \(\mathbb Z^\iota\) ([Stiemke](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Convex/Stiemke.lean)). In a locally convex ordered space with closed positive cone, the monotone continuous linear functionals determine the order ([positive dual](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Convex/Cone/PositiveDual.lean)).

Bernstein's theorem on completely monotone functions, in the Hausdorff–Bernstein–Widder form, is proved: a function continuous on \([0,\infty)\) and completely monotone on \((0,\infty)\) is the Laplace transform of a unique finite positive measure ([Hausdorff–Bernstein–Widder](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/CompletelyMonotone/Bernstein/HausdorffBernsteinWidder.lean); details in Section 49).
