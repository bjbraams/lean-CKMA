# Mathlib coverage of Sections 04–53

This file summarises, section by section, what Mathlib contains of the
mathematical content described in [`SYNOPSIS04.md`](SYNOPSIS04.md)–[`SYNOPSIS53.md`](SYNOPSIS53.md).
It is a digest of the Mathlib parts of the coverage inventories
[`STRUCTURE04.md`](STRUCTURE04.md)–[`STRUCTURE53.md`](STRUCTURE53.md), which give the
declarations, source files, and hypotheses, and classify every numbered synopsis
entry as covered, partly covered, or not covered by the two libraries together.
The companion file [TauCetiCoverage.md](TauCetiCoverage.md) summarises the TauCeti
additions in the same way, and [GAPS.md](GAPS.md) selects and assesses the principal
missing results. This file replaces the earlier `Coverage.md` and the per-section
`MathlibNN.md` notes.

[source conventions](STRUCTURE.md#source-baseline-and-conventions) of the series.
Statements here are summaries: the full hypotheses of the cited declarations, as
recorded in the STRUCTURE files, govern their use.

Each section below gives a verdict for this library alone, judged against the
content of the corresponding synopsis:

- **Substantial:** a large part of the graduate core of the section is proved.
- **Partial:** a real cluster of the section's own definitions and theorems is
  proved, but the section as a whole is not.
- **Thin:** a few of the section's results, or only neighbouring ingredients.
- **None:** nothing of the section's own content beyond general infrastructure.

## 04. Real analysis, measure, and integration

**Substantial.** Mathlib proves the Carathéodory construction, extension of σ-subadditive contents, the π–λ uniqueness theorems and completion (04.1–04.4), Lebesgue and Stieltjes measures with Haar uniqueness and determinant scaling (04.5, 04.6, partly). It has simple approximation, monotone and dominated convergence, Fatou, the Bochner integral, layer-cake formulas, Jensen for closed convex domains, and Riemann integrability via the box integral (04.7–04.15). Markov, Egorov, convergence in measure and Vitali-type uniform integrability are covered (04.16–04.21), as are product measures, Tonelli and Fubini for s-finite measures, image measures, kernels and differentiation under the integral (04.23–04.28). Hahn, Jordan, Lebesgue and Radon–Nikodym decompositions are proved for positive and signed measures (04.29–04.34). The \(L^p\) theory gives Hölder, Minkowski, completeness, density of simple and continuous functions, and the bounded pairing of conjugate \(L^p\) spaces (04.35–04.39, the last two partly). Change of variables, regularity on Polish spaces, real Riesz–Markov–Kakutani, weak convergence and analytic sets are also present. Absent: Lusin's theorem, Scheffé/Brezis–Lieb, surjectivity of the \(L^p\) duality, reflexivity, the dual of \(C_0(X)\), Daniell–Stone and de la Vallée Poussin. [Details in STRUCTURE04.md](STRUCTURE04.md).

## 05. Differentiation and the fine structure of real functions

**Partial.** Mathlib proves Taylor's theorem in one variable with Peano, Lagrange, Cauchy and integral remainders, and in several variables with integral remainder (05.2, partly), and the easy dimension cases of Sard's theorem. It proves Darboux's theorem (05.6) and a Dini-type comparison principle. On the line it covers bounded variation and Jordan's theorem (05.12), almost-everywhere differentiability of monotone and BV functions with the vector Stieltjes measure (05.13, 05.14, partly), absolute continuity with the fundamental theorem and integration by parts (05.15, 05.20). The Vitali and Besicovitch covering theorems, the Lebesgue differentiation and density theorems, and differentiation of measures along Vitali families are proved (05.21–05.23, 05.27–05.29). McShane extension (05.32) and Rademacher's theorem are proved, as are Cousin's lemma, gauge integrability of Lebesgue-integrable functions and integration of derivatives on boxes (05.51–05.53). Nothing is present on Dini derivatives and Denjoy–Young–Saks, Baire classes, Banach–Zarecki, Helly selection, maximal functions, Whitney decomposition and extension, Kirszbraun, fine properties of BV in several variables, or the Denjoy–Perron theory. [Details in STRUCTURE05.md](STRUCTURE05.md).

## 06. Probability: foundations and limit theory

**Partial.** For 06A Mathlib provides laws, densities, distribution functions and standard distributions; independence with infinite product measures, Kolmogorov's zero–one law and the first Borel–Cantelli lemma (06.2, 06.3, 06.5). It has kernels, Ionescu–Tulcea, disintegration on standard Borel spaces and conditional expectation with its pull-out, tower and conditional Jensen properties (06.7–06.10). It proves the implications between modes of convergence (06.12, partly), convergence in distribution, Etemadi's strong law (06.24, 06.25), uniqueness of characteristic functions, Lévy's continuity theorem in finite dimensions, Cramér–Wold, the one-dimensional i.i.d. central limit theorem (06.31, 06.33) and the Poisson limit. Discrete-time martingales receive Doob decomposition, upcrossings, a.e. and \(L^1\) convergence, optional sampling for bounded times and the maximal inequality (06.40–06.47). Gaussian measures on Banach spaces are defined (06.48), and the FKG inequality is proved (06.74). Absent are the three-series theorem, Lindeberg's theorem, Doob's \(L^p\) inequality, functional limit theorems, infinite divisibility, and all of 06B–06D: large deviations, random measures, random fields, branching, percolation and Gibbs measures. [Details in STRUCTURE06.md](STRUCTURE06.md).

## 07. Concentration, high-dimensional probability, and random matrices

**Partial.** Mathlib covers the scalar sub-Gaussian cluster of 07A. It proves Chernoff's bound through the cumulant-generating function (07.1), Hoeffding's lemma for bounded variables (07.2), the sub-Gaussian tail bound and Hoeffding's inequality for independent sums (07.3, 07.7), with conditional sub-Gaussian bounds on standard Borel spaces. Sub-Gaussianity is formalized only through the moment-generating bound, which is shown to give the tail bound and finiteness of all moments (07.6, partly); Orlicz norms and the converses are absent. The Azuma–Hoeffding estimate is proved for conditionally sub-Gaussian increments (07.12, partly), Fernique's theorem gives square-exponential integrability for Gaussian measures, covering and packing numbers with their standard comparisons are defined, and the Sauer–Shelah bound is proved (07.35). Absent are Bernstein-type inequalities, Gaussian Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities, Lipschitz concentration, chaining and Gaussian comparison, symmetrization and uniform laws, matrix concentration, and all of the random matrix theory of 07B. [Details in STRUCTURE07.md](STRUCTURE07.md).

## 08. Stochastic processes, stochastic calculus, and control

**Thin.** Mathlib has the vocabulary of process laws, filtrations, adapted, progressive and predictable processes, stopping times, hitting times and localization. It defines Kolmogorov's moment condition and characterizes pre-Brownian and Brownian motion by their finite-dimensional laws, proving uniqueness of the law on the product σ-algebra and invariance under negation, scaling, shift and (for pre-Brownian motion) time inversion (08.1, 08.2, partly); no Brownian motion is constructed. Martingales are defined for a general time index, and optional sampling is proved for bounded stopping times with countable range (08.10, partly). Transition kernels give Chapman–Kolmogorov composition, invariant and reversible measures, \(\varphi\)-irreducibility and discrete-time chain laws (08.45, partly). Absent are the Kolmogorov continuity theorem, path properties of Brownian motion, continuous-time martingale theory, stochastic integration and Itô calculus, Girsanov, SDEs, local time, Lévy processes, Markov semigroups and recurrence theory, diffusion generators, and all of stochastic control (08C). [Details in STRUCTURE08.md](STRUCTURE08.md).

## 09. Malliavin calculus, rough paths, and stochastic PDE

**Thin.** Mathlib's only result of the section's own content is Fernique's theorem: a Gaussian measure on a complete real normed space with its Borel σ-algebra has \(\exp(C\lVert x\rVert^2)\) integrable for some \(C>0\), hence moments of all orders (09.61, partly; not with the sharp constant or the seminorm version). Gaussian measures on topological vector spaces are defined through their one-dimensional projections. Supporting material consists of the probabilists' Hermite polynomials with their Gaussian-derivative formula (without orthogonality), the Gaussian-process and Brownian-motion predicates, and the scalar Poisson law. Mathlib has nothing on Wiener chaos and Malliavin derivatives, Ornstein–Uhlenbeck analysis and density criteria, the Cameron–Martin space and Feldman–Hájek, Poisson-space analysis, Young and rough integration, regularity structures, or stochastic PDE (09B, 09C). [Details in STRUCTURE09.md](STRUCTURE09.md).

## 10. Ergodic theory and measurable dynamics

**Thin.** Mathlib defines measure-preserving, ergodic and conservative maps and proves Poincaré recurrence (10.1), almost-everywhere constancy of invariant functions (10.3, partly), invariance of the Lebesgue decomposition of invariant measures, and the von Neumann mean ergodic theorem for Hilbert-space contractions (10.6), with a normed-space version given the fixed-space splitting (10.59, partly). Birkhoff sums and averages are defined, but the pointwise ergodic theorem is not proved. In topological dynamics it proves Krylov–Bogolyubov and the characterization of ergodic measures as extreme invariant measures (10.29, partly), defines minimal actions, flows, periodic points and Bowen–Dinaburg topological entropy, proves ergodicity of multiplication maps on the circle (10.32, partly) and the theory of translation numbers and sets up shift spaces. Van der Waerden's theorem and finitary Roth (10.65, 10.50, partly) have combinatorial proofs; Følner families and ergodic group actions are supporting. Absent are Kac, the maximal and pointwise theorems, Kingman, mixing and spectral theory, measure-theoretic entropy, thermodynamic formalism, hyperbolicity, Oseledets, joinings and Furstenberg recurrence, infinite-measure and amenable ergodic theorems. [Details in STRUCTURE10.md](STRUCTURE10.md).

## 11. Classical inequalities, means, majorization, and rearrangement

**Partial.** Mathlib proves Young's inequality with its equality case, weighted AM–GM with equality and HM–GM (11.1, 11.2), the power-mean comparisons with smaller exponent 1 (11.3, partly), and Jensen's inequality for finite sums and integrals with the strict case (11.4, 11.5). It proves Cauchy–Schwarz with equality (11.12), Hölder and Minkowski for finite sums, series and integrals without equality cases (11.13–11.15, partly), Lyapunov's inequality (11.16), Chebyshev's sum inequality with uniform weights, the rearrangement inequality (11.18) and Abel's inequality (11.73). Birkhoff's theorem on doubly stochastic matrices (11.23), nonnegativity of relative entropy (11.30), the layer-cake formula (11.44), the differential Grönwall inequality (11.40, partly) and Hadamard's three-lines theorem (11.69) are also proved. Absent are Hermite–Hadamard, Newton–Maclaurin and Muirhead, the partial-sum characterization of majorization and Karamata, convex order and Strassen, the Hardy, Hilbert, Carleman and Opial inequalities, rearrangement inequalities and symmetrization, sharp Young, Prékopa and Clarkson. [Details in STRUCTURE11.md](STRUCTURE11.md).

## 12. Convexity, convex analysis, optimization, and variational analysis

**Partial.** Mathlib's strength is convex geometry. It proves geometric Hahn–Banach separation in locally convex spaces (12.1), supporting functionals for convex sets with nonempty interior (12.2, partly), the Carathéodory, Radon and Helly theorems (12.3–12.5), Krein–Milman (12.6, partly), Farkas' lemma for proper cones with the bipolar identity for closed convex cones, and Stone separation; gauges, extreme and exposed sets, cones and simplicial complexes are defined. For convex functions it proves continuity and local Lipschitz continuity on open sets (12.12), local-to-global minima, one-variable derivative criteria, and the definitions of strict, uniform and strong convexity. It has the Hilbert projection theorem (12.31, partly), weak closedness of closed convex sets, Sion's minimax theorem (12.44), Graves's local surjectivity (12.54, partly) and Lagrange multipliers for equality constraints. Absent are Minkowski–Weyl, Choquet theory, John's ellipsoid, conjugacy and subdifferential calculus, proximal maps, Fenchel–Rockafellar and Lagrangian duality, LP duality, Ekeland, epi-convergence, Clarke and limiting subdifferentials, and convergence of convex algorithms. [Details in STRUCTURE12.md](STRUCTURE12.md).

## 13. Matrix analysis and noncommutative inequalities

**Partial.** Mathlib proves the spectral theorem for Hermitian matrices with real, ordered eigenvalues and the associated functional calculus (13.1; 13.28, partly), Gershgorin's theorem with the definitions of irreducible and primitive nonnegative matrices (13.11, partly), and the variational characterization of extreme eigenvalues through the Rayleigh quotient. It defines singular values, positive operators, positive-semidefinite matrices and the Loewner order, and proves the Schur-complement criterion for positivity (13.23), the Schur product theorem (13.25), positivity of Kronecker products (13.26, partly) and the \(LDL^*\) factorization. In C⋆-algebras it proves operator monotonicity and concavity of \(t\mapsto t^p\) for \(0\le p\le 1\), antitonicity of the inverse and monotonicity and concavity of the logarithm (13.29–13.31). Totally nonnegative matrices are defined. Absent are Courant–Fischer, interlacing, SVD and polar decomposition, Schatten norms and determinant inequalities, Loewner's theorem and operator convexity, matrix means, trace inequalities, completely positive maps and quantum entropy, completions and regularized determinants. [Details in STRUCTURE13.md](STRUCTURE13.md).

## 14. Functional, isoperimetric, and geometric inequalities

**Thin.** Mathlib proves the Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality for compactly supported \(C^1\) functions on a finite-dimensional real space of dimension \(n\ge2\): the endpoint \(L^{n/(n-1)}\) bound by the \(L^1\) norm of the derivative (`MeasureTheory.lintegral_pow_le_pow_lintegral_fderiv`), the Sobolev-conjugate \(L^{p'}\) bound for \(1\le p<n\) with inner-product or finite-dimensional codomain, and a Poincaré inequality for functions supported in a fixed bounded set (14.2, and 14.1 in part); the constants are not sharp or identified, and there is no isoperimetric form or equality case. Otherwise it has only neighbouring ingredients: the finite-sum rearrangement inequality of Section 11, and `ConvexBody` with the Hausdorff metric together with compactness of the nonempty compact subsets of a compact set (14.74 in part; convexity of Hausdorff limits is not proved). Mathlib has nothing on symmetrization, capacity, perimeter, Brunn–Minkowski, mixed volumes, Markov semigroups and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities, curvature–dimension, or transport–entropy inequalities. [Details in STRUCTURE14.md](STRUCTURE14.md).

## 15. Core Banach and Hilbert space theory

**Substantial.** Mathlib proves completion, quotients by closed subspaces, Riesz's lemma, finite-dimensional theory, completeness of \(\ell^p\) and \(C_0\), Hölder pairings on \(\ell^p\), Arzelà–Ascoli, and complemented subspaces (15.1–15.5; 15.9 in part). It has the analytic and geometric Hahn–Banach theorems, separation in locally convex spaces, and the isometric embedding into the bidual (15.10–15.12); Baire category, uniform boundedness, open mapping, closed graph, and closed range for injective maps (15.17–15.20). Banach–Alaoglu, weak-star metrizability of dual balls for separable spaces, weak closedness of closed convex sets, and uniform convexity of inner product spaces are proved (15.23; 15.24, 15.25 in part). Hilbert-space theory includes Cauchy–Schwarz, Riesz representation, orthogonal projections, Hilbert bases and the isometry with \(\ell^2\), Gram–Schmidt, and real Lax–Milgram (15.36–15.43). Hilbert adjoints, compact operators, the Fredholm alternative for compact operators (`IsCompactOperator.hasEigenvalue_or_mem_resolventSet`), Fredholm operators with index, the spectrum, Gelfand's formula, and the continuous functional calculus (15.60–15.65) are also proved. Duality of \(\ell^p\) and \(L^p\), Banach adjoints and the closed range theorem, reflexivity, and the Borel functional calculus are absent. [Details in STRUCTURE15.md](STRUCTURE15.md).

## 16. Locally convex spaces, duality, and distributions

**Partial.** Mathlib develops seminorm topologies, absolutely convex sets, local convexity, polynormability via gauges, boundedness, and metrizability of countably seminormed Hausdorff spaces (16.1, 16.2, 16.10; 16.3 in part). It proves the geometric and analytic Hahn–Banach theorems in locally convex spaces, defines polars and weak topologies compatible with a dual pair, and proves the Krein–Milman theorem in real Hausdorff locally convex spaces (16.11, 16.12; 16.20 in part). Barrelled spaces, defined through lower semicontinuous seminorms, satisfy Banach–Steinhaus, and Baire spaces are barrelled (16.21). Test functions with the inductive-limit topology, distributions, regular distributions with almost-everywhere uniqueness, support, and distributional derivatives are defined (16.36, 16.37, 16.44 in part). Schwartz space, tempered distributions, and the Fourier transform as a continuous linear equivalence on both, with derivative intertwining, \(\widehat{\delta_0}=1\), and Fourier multipliers, are proved (16.53; 16.52, 16.54 in part). There is nothing on the bipolar theorem, Mackey topologies, bornological spaces, Fréchet open mapping, nuclear spaces, injective tensor norms, structure theorems for distributions, convolution of distributions, or fundamental solutions. [Details in STRUCTURE16.md](STRUCTURE16.md).

## 17. Banach space geometry, bases, and operator ideals

**Thin.** Mathlib defines generalized, classical, and unconditional Schauder bases, proves uniform boundedness of the basis projections on a Banach space, and builds a classical basis from nested finite-rank projections with rank-one differences (17.1, 17.8 in part); Hilbert bases are unconditional Schauder bases. Its notion of summation is unconditional convergence of finite partial sums, with the Cauchy criterion and permutation invariance (17.15 in part). It proves the Mazur–Ulam theorem (17.65, `IsometryEquiv.toRealAffineIsometryEquiv`) and Rademacher's theorem for finite-dimensional targets (17.67 in part), and shows that a Fréchet derivative of the norm at \(x\) is a norm-one functional attaining \(\|x\|\) (17.47 in part). Neighbouring ingredients are complemented subspaces, L- and M-projections, uniform convexity of inner product spaces, and the uncompleted projective tensor seminorm. Mathlib has nothing on block sequences, weak sequential theorems, classical subspaces of \(\ell^p\), \(c_0\), and \(L^p\), type and cotype, finite representability, smoothness, the Radon–Nikodým property, summing operators, tensor norms, the approximation property, or nonlinear classification. [Details in STRUCTURE17.md](STRUCTURE17.md).

## 18. Vector-valued analysis and analysis in Banach spaces

**Partial.** Mathlib has the Bochner integral with dominated convergence, commutation with continuous linear maps (18.2), and Fubini for Banach-valued integrands (18.4 in part). Pettis's measurability theorem is proved in its Borel form (18.1 in part). The Lebesgue–Bochner spaces \(L^p(\mu;E)\) are complete with dense simple functions, and the pairing \(L^{p'}(E^*)\to L^p(E)^*\) is bounded (18.3, 18.5 in part). It has Banach-valued conditional expectation (`ContinuousLinearMap.comp_condExp_comm`; 18.6), Lebesgue differentiation (18.7), and the almost-everywhere derivative of the indefinite integral (18.8 in part). Vector measures come with variation, semivariation, and bounded range (18.68 in part), integration through bilinear pairings, products, and Stieltjes measures. Radon–Nikodým, Rademacher, and martingale convergence are proved only for real or finite-dimensional values (18.10, 18.12–18.14 in part). The Hilbert-valued Plancherel theorem holds on \(L^2\) (18.32 in part), and the Banach-valued Cauchy formula and Liouville theorem hold (18.53 in part). There is nothing on the Banach-valued Radon–Nikodým property, UMD spaces, \(R\)-boundedness, operator-valued multipliers, \(\gamma\)-radonifying operators and stochastic integration, or sectorial operators and maximal regularity. [Details in STRUCTURE18.md](STRUCTURE18.md).

## 19. Banach lattices, positive operators, and ordered structures

**Thin.** Mathlib proves the elementary Riesz-space identities for lattice-ordered additive groups (19.1) and defines solid sets. Solid norms (`HasSolidNorm`) give the inequality \(\||a|-|b|\|\le\|a-b\|\), Lipschitz lattice operations, and a closed positive cone (19.9 in part; 19.69 for solid norms only); \(C_b\) and \(L^p(\mu;E)\) are instances, though no bundled Banach-lattice class exists. Positive linear maps are bundled, and the M. Riesz extension theorem is proved for real functionals (19.25 in part). Conditional expectation is positive, contractive, and idempotent (19.37). The closed ring ideals of \(C(K)\) are classified (19.36 in part). Positive maps between \(C^\star\)-algebras are bounded, and a positive functional on a unital \(C^\star\)-algebra has norm \(f(1)\) (19.66, 19.61 in part). Irreducible and primitive nonnegative matrices are defined as supporting material only. Mathlib has nothing on ideals and bands, order continuity and KB spaces, order duals and regular operators, AM/AL spaces and Kakutani representation, domination and weak compactness, Perron–Frobenius theory, or positive semigroups. [Details in STRUCTURE19.md](STRUCTURE19.md).

## 20. Banach algebras

**Partial.** Mathlib proves the Neumann series and openness of the units, compactness and nonemptiness of the spectrum, Gelfand's spectral radius formula, upper semicontinuity of the spectrum, and the exponential series (20.1–20.4, 20.9), with polynomial spectral mapping over \(\mathbb C\) (20.5 in part). It proves the Gelfand–Mazur theorem over \(\mathbb C\) (20.6 in part). The Jacobson radical and the density theorem are treated algebraically only (20.10, 20.65 in part). For commutative algebras, characters are continuous and the character space is compact (20.11); in the unital case, the Gelfand transform describes the spectrum as the range of characters (`Ideal.toCharacterSpace`; 20.12–20.14 in part). Stone–Weierstrass is proved (20.22), and the closed ideals of \(C(K)\) are classified (20.31 in part). Convolution of measures and functions, finite abelian Pontryagin duality, and the convolution theorem on \(\mathbb R^n\) are present (20.38 in part). Mathlib has nothing on numerical range, non-unital Gelfand theory, uniform algebras, spectral synthesis, the Banach algebra \(L^1(G)\), derivations, amenable Banach algebras, or Arens products. [Details in STRUCTURE20.md](STRUCTURE20.md).

## 21. Bounded operators, spectra, and model theory

**Partial.** Mathlib has the spectrum and spectral radius formula for bounded operators (21.5 in part). It proves Banach–Steinhaus and defines the weak operator topology (21.4 in part). The Chebyshev–Gauss rule is exact for polynomials of degree below \(2n\) for that single weight (part of 48.14). Probabilists' Hermite and shifted Legendre polynomials are defined algebraically, with Rodrigues formulas (part of 48.12) but without orthogonality. The Chebyshev sup-norm extremal property is supporting material for asymptotics. Mathlib has nothing on general quadrature and Cauchy transforms, Jacobi operators and spectral measures, moment problems, orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle, asymptotics and potential theory, or multivariate and modified measures. [Details in STRUCTURE48.md](STRUCTURE48.md).

## 49. Special functions, integral transforms, and classical formulae

**Partial.** The strongest coverage is the Gamma–zeta cluster: Euler's integral and the meromorphic Gamma function with \(1/\Gamma\) entire, Bohr–Mollerup, Euler's limit, Beta, reflection and duplication, digamma identities, and Stirling's formula for \(n!\) (49.1, 49.4, 49.5, 49.7; parts of 49.2, 49.3, 49.6, 49.8, 49.9); the Riemann zeta function with Euler product, functional equation and Bernoulli values (49.11) and the Hurwitz zeta function (part of 49.10). Euler's sine product is proved. Hypergeometric series come with their radii of convergence (part of 49.12), \(J_\nu\) is defined only by its series (part of 49.25),, and the Gaussian integral and its Fourier transform are evaluated (part of 49.38). Chebyshev orthogonality and Rodrigues formulas give parts of 49.40 and 49.41. The \(\wp\)-function with its differential equation (part of 49.51), Jacobi theta transformations (49.53, 49.54), the pentagonal number theorem (part of 49.59), and Mellin inversion (49.68) are present. It has nothing on hypergeometric identities, Airy functions, Jacobi or Laguerre families, elliptic integrals, q-series, Laplace inversion, or fractional calculus. [Details in STRUCTURE49.md](STRUCTURE49.md).

## 50. Asymptotic analysis, resurgence, and perturbation

**Thin.** Mathlib supplies the Landau relations \(O\), \(\Theta\), \(o\) and asymptotic equivalence along arbitrary filters, linear and exponential growth rates, superpolynomial decay, and Taylor's theorem with little-o and explicit remainders, but no notion of an asymptotic expansion with respect to a scale. The real flat function \(e^{-1/x}\) is smooth and not analytic at \(0\) (part of 50.7), and composition and inversion of convergent power series are proved (part of 50.8). The model integral \(\int_0^\infty x^qe^{-bx^p}\,dx\) is evaluated for real parameters (part of 50.15), and the Cauchy coefficient estimate gives the saddle-point bound (part of 50.73). Abel summation, Bernoulli polynomials, Mellin inversion and the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma are supporting material, as are the multiseries lemmas behind an asymptotics procedure. Mathlib has nothing on Laplace and stationary-phase asymptotics, Euler–Maclaurin and Tauberian theorems, irregular singularities and WKB, Gevrey series, Borel summation and resurgence, or singular perturbation. [Details in STRUCTURE50.md](STRUCTURE50.md).

## 51. Integrable systems and Riemann–Hilbert methods

**None.** Mathlib has no development of Riemann–Hilbert problems, integrable systems, or inverse scattering, and no synopsis entry of the section is covered. What it offers is general infrastructure. The resolvent transform \(\int(x-a)^{-1}\,d\mu(x)\) of a measure (`resolventTransform`), which specializes to the Stieltjes and Cauchy transforms of measures, is holomorphic off the support, but there are no Cauchy operators on contours and no Plemelj–Sokhotski formulas; Liouville's theorem is available for uniqueness arguments. The symplectic matrix group and \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) triples in Lie algebras are linear-algebraic and representation-theoretic, not Hamiltonian dynamics. Mathlib has nothing on Cauchy operators and Wiener–Hopf factorization, nonlinear steepest descent, direct and inverse scattering, KdV and Lax pairs, Hamiltonian integrability and finite-gap solutions, isomonodromy and Painlevé equations, or Christoffel–Darboux kernels and random-matrix limits. [Details in STRUCTURE51.md](STRUCTURE51.md).

## 52. Geometric analysis on manifolds

**Thin.** Mathlib's contribution is mostly manifold infrastructure: charted spaces and \(C^n\) manifolds, tangent bundles, Lie brackets, immersions and embeddings, bump functions and partitions of unity, Whitney embedding of compact manifolds into some \(\mathbb R^n\), integral curves, Lie groups, and differential forms on normed spaces. Of the section's own content it has covariant derivatives with metric compatibility and torsion, and on finite-dimensional manifolds with a \(C^1\) Riemannian metric the construction of a Levi-Civita connection and its uniqueness on differentiable vector fields via the Koszul formula (`CovariantDerivative.leviCivitaConnection`, part of 52.1); curvature is not defined. The Riemannian length of paths and the induced extended distance are defined, with the triangle inequality. The divergence theorem is Euclidean only, and the Poincaré conjectures appear only as definitions and unproved statements. Mathlib has nothing on geodesics and comparison theorems, functional inequalities and heat kernels on manifolds, conformal geometry, harmonic maps, minimal surfaces, mean curvature or Ricci flow, or symplectic and gauge analysis. [Details in STRUCTURE52.md](STRUCTURE52.md).

## 53. Elliptic operators on manifolds, index theory, and spectral geometry

**None.** Mathlib has no elliptic differential operators between bundles, no Hodge theory on manifolds, and no index theorem or spectral geometry; what it offers is general operator-theoretic and algebraic infrastructure. Fredholm operators are defined, the index is additive under composition (`ContinuousLinearMap.IsFredholm.index_comp`), and on complete spaces the Fredholm operators form an open set on which the index is locally constant; these are the abstract parts of 53.6, with no elliptic operator shown to be Fredholm. The spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators is the abstract input to discrete-spectrum theorems. The Laplacian is the Euclidean one, not the Laplace–Beltrami operator, and the Hodge star and spin group are purely algebraic. Mathlib has nothing on elliptic regularity on manifolds, elliptic complexes and Hodge decomposition, heat-trace expansions and Weyl laws, Dirac operators and index theorems, spectral flow, eigenvalue inequalities and nodal domains, or trace formulas and scattering.
