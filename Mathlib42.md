Reproducing-kernel Hilbert spaces, in the abstract sense of a Hilbert space of functions whose kernel functions are dense and whose kernel is a positive semidefinite matrix, are Section 29 (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Reproducing.lean`).

The one construction that uses the name of a Blaschke product is the finite canonical decomposition of a meromorphic function on a disc. The factor \((R^2-\overline{w}\,z)/(R(z-w))\) is meromorphic and, for \(R>0\) and \(|w|<R\), has a single pole at \(w\) and modulus one on the circle of radius \(R\). A finite product of such factors rewrites a meromorphic function on a disc, up to an analytic factor without zeros. The file calls this a finite Blaschke product and uses it for the counting formulae of Section 41. It does not define an infinite Blaschke product, an inner function, or a factorization of a function in a Hardy space (`Analysis/Complex/CanonicalDecomposition.lean`).

## Additional coverage in TauCeti

The following additions were checked in TauCeti revision `6ebb29f84b4d`; see the [revision, build evidence, and comparison scope](TauCetiCoverage.md). Source links below are pinned to that revision.

The Herglotz representation is proved: if \(F\) is holomorphic on the unit disc with nonnegative real part, then
\[
 F(w)=\int_{|z|=1}\frac{z+w}{z-w}\,d\mu(z)+i\operatorname{Im}F(0)
\]
for a finite positive measure \(\mu\), and conversely such transforms have those properties ([Herglotz](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Herglotz.lean)). This is relevant boundary-measure theory for analytic functions. Its half-plane counterpart, the Nevanlinna representation of Pick functions, is recorded in Section 13 ([Pick functions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Pick/Basic.lean)). The planar Poisson integral of integrable real boundary data on a circle is harmonic in the disc, and for continuous data it tends to the boundary value at each boundary point, which solves the Dirichlet problem on a disc ([Poisson integral](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Poisson/Integral.lean)).

TauCeti begins the Hilbert-space Hardy theory on the circle. The boundary Hardy space \(H^2\) is the closed subspace of \(L^2(\mathbb T)\), with normalized Haar measure, spanned by the nonnegative Fourier modes; equivalently its elements are the functions with vanishing negative Fourier coefficients. The nonnegative modes form a Hilbert basis, identifying \(H^2\) isometrically with \(\ell^2\), and the orthogonal projection onto \(H^2\) is available ([boundary Hardy space](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Basic.lean)). For \(x\in H^2\) with coefficients \(\hat x(n)\), the disc function \(F_x(w)=\sum_n\hat x(n)w^n\) is holomorphic on the unit disc, satisfies \(|F_x(w)|\le\|x\|/\sqrt{1-|w|^2}\), and its circle restrictions \(t\mapsto F_x(re(t))\) converge to \(x\) in \(L^2\) as \(r\to1^-\) (`HardySpace.tendsto_eLpNorm_diskFunction_sub`, [disc function](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/DiskFunction.lean)). Almost-everywhere nontangential limits, the norm identity for the holomorphic \(H^p\), and every \(p\neq2\) are not treated.

For an essentially bounded symbol \(a\), the Toeplitz operator \(T_a=PM_a|_{H^2}\) is a bounded linear function of \(a\) with \(\|T_a\|\le\|a\|_\infty\); its matrix in the basis \(e_0,e_1,\dots\) has entries \(\hat a(i-j)\), its adjoint is \(T_{\bar a}\), and the symbol is determined by the operator ([Toeplitz operators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Toeplitz.lean)). The Hankel operator of \(a\) is multiplication followed by projection onto the orthogonal complement of \(H^2\), with norm at most \(\|a\|_\infty\). The semicommutator \(T_{ab}-T_aT_b\) is a product of Hankel operators (`toeplitz_comp_sub_toeplitz`), and the Fourier characterization of vanishing Hankel operators (`hankel_eq_zero_iff`) gives \(T_{ab}=T_aT_b\) when \(b\) is analytic or \(a\) is coanalytic ([Hankel operators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Hankel.lean)). Fredholm theory and the index of Toeplitz operators, Nehari's distance formula, and Beurling's invariant-subspace theorem are not proved.

TauCeti also develops disc automorphisms and Schwarz–Pick contraction (Section 43), but no Bergman spaces, infinite Blaschke products or inner functions were located.

## Topics of Section 42 not found in either inspected library

- The Hardy spaces \(H^p\) of holomorphic functions for general \(p\), the Bergman spaces with their kernel and projection, the Dirichlet space, and the model spaces of Sz.-Nagy–Foiaș. In Mathlib the name Hardy belongs to real-variable inequalities; TauCeti has only the boundary space \(H^2\) and its disc functions described above.
- Inner functions, infinite Blaschke products, and singular inner functions. Only the finite canonical factors above are present.
- Fredholmness and the index theorem for Toeplitz operators, Nehari's theorem, and Beurling’s theorem on invariant subspaces; TauCeti has the Toeplitz and Hankel operators on \(H^2\) with the norm bounds, adjoints and semicommutator identity above. The Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem, that a symmetric everywhere-defined operator on a Hilbert space is continuous, is a theorem of unbounded-operator theory and is not an operator on a space of holomorphic functions.
- Boundary behaviour: Fatou's theorem on nontangential limits, the norm identity \(\|f\|_{H^p}=\|f^*\|_{L^p}\), the Poisson integral of an \(L^p\) boundary function beyond the harmonicity and continuous boundary values proved in TauCeti, and the F. and M. Riesz theorem. TauCeti proves only the \(L^2\) radial convergence of disc functions of boundary \(H^2\) elements.
- Carleson measures, the corona theorem, BMOA, interpolating sequences, and Nevanlinna–Pick interpolation.
- Composition operators (Shapiro's compactness criterion), Stegenga's Dirichlet-space embedding theorem, the disc algebra, and Fock spaces with their sampling and interpolation theory.
- The shift, the compressed shift, and reproducing kernels specialized to the disc or the half-plane. The abstract reproducing-kernel Hilbert space is Section 29, and the geometry of the disc and the half-plane is Section 40.
