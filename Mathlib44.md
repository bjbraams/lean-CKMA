# 44. Riemann Surfaces, Quasiconformal Mappings, and Teichmüller Theory

Checked against Mathlib commit `f039e1b8a616d45782a5c7fc5e7909249c36b8ea` (2026-10-18, Lean `v4.36.0-rc1`). The checkout was only read. The survey below concerns this Mathlib revision; the TauCeti supplement and final gap list also use the [TauCeti source audit](TauCetiCoverage.md).

**Imports.** The relevant foundations are `Mathlib.Geometry.Manifold.IsManifold.Basic`, `Mathlib.Geometry.Manifold.ContMDiff.Defs`, `Mathlib.Geometry.Manifold.Complex`, `Mathlib.Analysis.Complex.UpperHalfPlane.Manifold`, `Mathlib.Analysis.Complex.CoveringMap`, and `Mathlib.Analysis.Complex.RiemannMapping`.

The general manifold framework supports complex atlases and holomorphic maps. A boundaryless manifold modeled on \(\mathbb C\), with complex-smooth transition maps, provides the atlas structure underlying a Riemann surface. Hausdorffness and second countability are separate hypotheses, as in the general manifold library. It would therefore be too strong to say that holomorphic atlases are absent. What was not found is a dedicated global theory of Riemann surfaces, quasiconformal mappings, or their moduli.

## Complex-manifold foundations

The upper half-plane is a one-dimensional complex manifold via its inclusion in \(\mathbb C\). Möbius transformations of positive determinant act holomorphically (`Analysis/Complex/UpperHalfPlane/Manifold.lean`). Its hyperbolic metric and invariant measure are described in Section 40; infinitesimal conformality is Section 43.

The maximum principle is proved on boundaryless complex manifolds, including constancy of holomorphic functions into a complex normed space on a connected compact complex manifold (`Geometry/Manifold/Complex.lean`; see Section 45). These are genuine complex-manifold results, though they do not provide uniformization or a classification of surfaces. On the upper half-plane itself, a holomorphic function vanishing frequently near a point vanishes identically (`UpperHalfPlane.eq_zero_of_frequently`).

## Plane coverings and steps toward the Riemann mapping theorem

`Analysis/Complex/CoveringMap.lean` proves that \(\exp:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\) is a covering map, a quotient by \(2\pi i\mathbb Z\). Over a proper nontrivially normed field such as \(\mathbb C\), the power maps \(z\mapsto z^n\) with \(n\neq0\) in the field are covering maps of the punctured field, and a polynomial is a covering map over the complement of its critical values (`Polynomial.isCoveringMapOn_eval`).

`Analysis/Complex/RiemannMapping.lean` contains only the first two steps of the Riemann mapping theorem: an open simply connected \(U\subsetneq\mathbb C\) admits a map that is injective on \(U\), sends \(U\) into the open unit disc, and has nonvanishing derivative there (`Complex.exists_mapsTo_unitBall_injOn_deriv_ne_zero`). The theorem itself is not yet in Mathlib; TauCeti proves it (below).

## Additional coverage in TauCeti

The following additions were checked in TauCeti revision `6ebb29f84b4d`; see the [revision, build evidence, and comparison scope](TauCetiCoverage.md). Source links below are pinned to that revision.

TauCeti develops concrete Riemann surfaces from discrete subgroups of \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)\): coarse upper-half-plane quotients and their cusp extensions have complex-manifold atlases, including the cusp charts ([cusp-extended quotient](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Fuchsian/Compactification/Manifold.lean)). Despite the type name `CompactifiedQuotient`, compactness is a further theorem under a compact-truncation condition and finiteness of cusp orbits; it should not be asserted for every discrete subgroup ([compactness criterion](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Fuchsian/Compactification/Compactness.lean)). For a finite-index inclusion \(\Delta\le\Gamma\), the induced map of compactified quotients has degree \([\Gamma:\Delta]\), with cusp multiplicities given by stabilizer indices ([degree](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Fuchsian/Compactification/Degree.lean)); for the modular group, the normalized \(j\)-invariant is a biholomorphism of the compactified level-one quotient onto the Riemann sphere ([level one](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Fuchsian/LevelOne/Sphere.lean)).

For holomorphic maps between Riemann surfaces (complex manifolds modeled on \(\mathbb C\)), local multiplicity is defined independently of charts and proved multiplicative under composition; multiplicity one characterizes local injectivity ([multiplicity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/RiemannSurface/LocalMultiplicity.lean)). A map holomorphic and nonconstant near a point has the local form \(u\mapsto u^m\) in suitable centered charts, with \(m\) the local multiplicity ([local normal form](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/RiemannSurface/LocalNormalForm.lean)). The identity and open mapping theorems hold on Riemann surfaces. For a map with compact source that is constant near no point, the number of preimages counted with multiplicity is locally constant, so over a connected target it defines the degree; the degree is multiplicative under composition, and such a map from a nonempty compact surface onto a connected surface is surjective ([degree](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/RiemannSurface/Degree.lean)). Divisor pullback multiplies degrees by the degree of the map ([pullback](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/RiemannSurface/Divisor.lean)). The ramification divisor \(R_f=\sum_x(e_x-1)[x]\) of a finite holomorphic map is defined, it is finite on a compact source, the fibre over \(y\) has \(\deg f-\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}(e_x-1)\) points, and the chain rule \(R_{g\circ f}=R_f+f^*R_g\) holds ([ramification](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/RiemannSurface/Ramification.lean)). With compact source the map is a covering exactly over the complement of the finite set of branch values, with every unbranched fibre of cardinality the degree ([branch values](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/RiemannSurface/BranchValues.lean)). The file docstrings state that the Euler-characteristic step of Riemann–Hurwitz is not part of this development. Meromorphic orders on a Riemann surface are chart independent and multiply by the local multiplicity under pullback ([meromorphic orders](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/RiemannSurface/Meromorphic.lean)).

Analytic Belyi pairs (compact surfaces with a map to the sphere branched only over \(0,1,\infty\)) are coverings over the thrice-punctured sphere with well-defined monodromy triples ([Belyi pairs](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/RiemannSurface/BelyiPair.lean)), and connected covers of \(\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}\) are classified by connected permutation triples ([classification](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/AlgebraicTopology/ThricePuncturedSphere/Classification.lean)). The genus formula for the associated filled covers is combinatorial, not the genus of a compact Riemann surface ([filled cover](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/AlgebraicTopology/ThricePuncturedSphere/FilledCover/Ramification.lean)).

The Riemann mapping theorem is proved: every simply connected open proper subset of \(\mathbb C\) is biholomorphic to the unit disc, via an extremal problem (Montel and Hurwitz) and the Koebe square-root step ([existence](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/RiemannMapping/Existence.lean), [Koebe step](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/Koebe.lean)). TauCeti also characterizes holomorphic functions with equal pre-Schwarzian derivative \(f''/f'\) as differing by an affine postcomposition ([pre-Schwarzian](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/PreSchwarzian.lean)). The holomorphic-germ sheaf on the plane and monodromy are also implemented (Sections 40 and 45).

On the algebraic side, TauCeti proves the Hurwitz genus formula \(2g'-2=n\,(2g-2)+\deg\operatorname{Diff}(F'/F)\) for finite separable extensions of algebraic function fields of one variable with exact constant fields ([Hurwitz formula](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Different/Hurwitz.lean)), the Riemann–Roch theorem for such function fields in the form that the divisor of any nonzero Weil differential is a Riemann–Roch divisor for the genus ([Riemann–Roch](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Differential/CanonicalDivisor.lean)), and, for line bundles \(L\) on proper curves over a field (integral, separated, Noetherian, with exact constants), Serre duality \(H^1(X,L)^\vee\simeq H^0(X,\omega\otimes L^{-1})\) as the existence of an isomorphism, together with \(h^0(L)-h^0(\omega\otimes L^{-1})=\deg L+1-g\) ([Serre duality](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/LineBundle/SerreDuality.lean), [Euler-characteristic form](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/WeilDivisor/Scheme/RiemannRoch/Basic.lean)). Its Abel–Jacobi map is a formal divisor-class map into an abstract \(\mathrm{Pic}^0\), without periods or a Jacobian variety ([Abel–Jacobi](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/WeilDivisor/AbelJacobi/Basic.lean)), and its Riemann forms are linear-algebraic polarizations of lattices ([Riemann forms](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6ebb29f84b4d6e2019233da0718776a6e32e1526/TauCeti/Geometry/Hodge/WeightOne/RiemannForm.lean)). No comparison between compact Riemann surfaces and algebraic curves, general uniformization theorem, Beltrami-equation theory, or Teichmüller theory was located.

## Topics of Section 44 not found in either inspected library

- Uniformization of abstract simply connected Riemann surfaces and the representation of surfaces as quotients by deck groups, beyond the Riemann mapping theorem for plane domains and the Fuchsian-quotient constructions described above.
- The analytic theory of compact Riemann surfaces: the analytic genus, harmonic and holomorphic differentials and their periods, the Riemann bilinear relations, Abel's theorem, Jacobi inversion and the analytic Jacobian. The Riemann–Hurwitz formula, the Riemann–Roch theorem and Serre duality are proved only for algebraic function fields and curves; their analytic versions on compact Riemann surfaces, or a comparison theorem (Riemann existence, GAGA), were not found. TauCeti supplies the degree, ramification divisor and branched-covering structure on the analytic side.
- Quasiconformal mappings, the Beltrami equation and the measurable Riemann mapping theorem, area distortion, extremal length, and holomorphic motions.
- Teichmüller space, the Teichmüller metric, quadratic differentials, Teichmüller's extremal theorems, the Bers embedding, and Mumford compactness. The order-theoretic Teichmüller–Tukey lemma and the Teichmüller representatives in Witt vectors are unrelated to this subject.
