## Stone–Weierstrass

Let \(X\) be a compact topological space. The lattice form is the engine of the proof. If \(L\subseteq C(X,\mathbb{R})\) is nonempty, closed under pointwise minimum and pointwise maximum, and separates points strongly — for every continuous real \(f\) and every pair of points there is an element of \(L\) taking the same values as \(f\) at those two points — then the uniform closure of \(L\) is all of \(C(X,\mathbb{R})\) (`Topology/ContinuousMap/StoneWeierstrass.lean`, `sublattice_closure_eq_top`).

The real Stone–Weierstrass theorem follows. A subalgebra \(A\subseteq C(X,\mathbb{R})\) that separates points has uniform closure equal to \(C(X,\mathbb{R})\). The closure of a subalgebra is a sublattice, because the absolute value of each of its elements lies in the closure, by polynomial approximation of the absolute value on a compact interval of \(\mathbb{R}\). Epsilon forms are recorded: every continuous real function is within any prescribed \(\varepsilon>0\), in the uniform norm, of an element of \(A\).

If \(X\) is not assumed compact, the same separation hypothesis gives uniform approximation on compact subsets: for every compact \(K\subseteq X\), every continuous real \(f\), and every \(\varepsilon>0\), some \(g\in A\) satisfies \(\|g(x)-f(x)\|<\varepsilon\) for all \(x\in K\).

For \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), the scalar field being `RCLike`, a star subalgebra of \(C(X,\mathbb{k})\) that separates points is uniformly dense. The reduction passes to the real subalgebra of modulus-squared elements \(\lvert F\rvert^2\), and then applies the real theorem. On a compact \(s\subseteq\mathbb{R}\), polynomial functions are uniformly dense in \(C(s,\mathbb{R})\). On a compact \(s\subseteq\mathbb{k}\), the star closure of the polynomial functions is uniformly dense in \(C(s,\mathbb{k})\), and the closed star subalgebra generated by the identity function is all of \(C(s,\mathbb{k})\).

## Bernstein polynomials and Weierstrass on an interval

On the unit interval \(I=[0,1]\), the Bernstein basis polynomials are
\[
b_{n,\nu}(x)=\binom{n}{\nu}x^\nu(1-x)^{n-\nu}.
\]
They are nonnegative and sum to \(1\). For a continuous \(f:I\to E\) with values in a locally convex real topological vector space, the Bernstein approximation is
\[
B_n f=\sum_{k=0}^n b_{n,k}\cdot f(k/n).
\]
As \(n\to\infty\), \(B_n f\) converges to \(f\) in the topology of uniform convergence (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Bernstein.lean`, `bernsteinApproximation_uniform`). The argument is the usual second-moment estimate: the probability that \(k/n\) differs from \(x\) by more than \(\delta\) is at most \((4n\delta^2)^{-1}\).

This is the Weierstrass theorem on \([0,1]\): the uniform closure of the polynomial functions is all of \(C(I,\mathbb{R})\). Transport by an affine homeomorphism gives the same statement on every compact interval \([a,b]\subseteq\mathbb{R}\) (`Topology/ContinuousMap/Weierstrass.lean`). The Stone–Weierstrass proof uses this interval case, because it approximates the absolute value by polynomials. Epsilon forms give a polynomial uniformly within \(\varepsilon\) of a given continuous real function on \([a,b]\), and the corresponding statement for a function merely continuous on the interval.

## Smooth functions in \(L^p\), and convolution

Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real normed space with its Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra, let \(F\) be a real normed space, and let \(\mu\) be a measure on \(E\) that is finite on compact sets. Every continuous compactly supported \(F\)-valued function can be approximated in the \(L^p(\mu)\) norm, for every \(p\in[0,\infty]\), by a smooth compactly supported function: the support of the approximant is contained in the support of the original function (`Analysis/Normed/Lp/SmoothApprox.lean`). The same approximation holds for an arbitrary class in \(L^p\) when \(p\neq\infty\) and \(1\leq p\). Consequently, when \(p\neq\infty\) and \(1\leq p\), the smooth compactly supported functions are dense in \(L^p(E,F;\mu)\). The argument calls the manifold approximation theorem of Section 52, specialised to the normed space.

Convolution is developed for a bilinear multiplication against a Haar measure (`Analysis/Convolution.lean`). What is proved about approximation is pointwise, not a named density theorem. On a finite-dimensional real normed space with a Haar measure, if \(\varphi_i\) is a family of smooth bumps at the origin whose outer radii tend to \(0\) along a filter, and \(g\) is continuous with values in a complete real normed space, then the normalised convolution \((\varphi_i\star g)(x_0)\) tends to \(g(x_0)\) (`Analysis/Calculus/BumpFunction/Convolution.lean`). If \(g\) is only locally integrable, and the ratio of outer radius to inner radius stays bounded, the same convergence holds for almost every \(x_0\). There is no theorem in these files asserting density of mollifications in \(L^p\).

## A pointer to Chebyshev comparison

For \(n\ge1\), among real polynomials of degree at most \(n\) that are bounded by \(1\) on \([-1,1]\), the leading coefficient is at most \(2^{n-1}\), and for \(n\geq 2\) equality holds precisely for the Chebyshev polynomial \(T_n\) (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Trigonometric/Chebyshev/Extremal.lean`). The polynomials themselves, their weight, roots, and quadrature, are in Section 48. This comparison is the extremal property of \(T_n\), not a general equioscillation theorem.

Arzelà–Ascoli supplies compactness of equicontinuous families with pointwise relatively compact ranges in the uniform topology on a compact domain (`Topology/UniformSpace/Ascoli.lean`; see Section 15). This is another part of the function-space approximation infrastructure.

## Additional coverage in TauCeti

The following additions were checked in TauCeti revision `6e53de0d3ce9`; see the [revision, build evidence, and comparison scope](TauCetiCoverage.md). Source links below are pinned to that revision.

The \(L^p\) approximation by mollifiers missing from the Mathlib survey is present in TauCeti. Normalized smooth bumps define continuous linear averaging operators of norm at most one, converging strongly to the identity as their support radii shrink, for \(1\le p<\infty\), including Banach-valued functions ([approximate identities](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6e53de0d3ce9ea24d7d487e656ac4590b0c45b3a/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/Lp/ApproximateIdentity.lean)). Dilated smooth compact cutoffs also converge to a Schwartz function in every Schwartz seminorm ([Schwartz approximation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6e53de0d3ce9ea24d7d487e656ac4590b0c45b3a/TauCeti/Analysis/Distribution/SchwartzSpace/Cutoff.lean)).

Chebyshev and Hermite Hilbert-basis results add \(L^2\) approximation by concrete orthogonal systems (Section 48). No Jackson rate, spline approximation theory, or full equioscillation theorem was located.
