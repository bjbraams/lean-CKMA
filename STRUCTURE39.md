## 39A. Metric Measure Spaces and Fractal Geometry

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS39.md#synopsis-39a).

Metric measure structures, dimension, Gromov–Hausdorff geometry, and relaxed metric Sobolev energy are grouped here.

### Upper gradients and metric Sobolev spaces

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS39.md#upper-gradients-and-metric-sobolev-spaces).

#### Mathlib

**Doubling measures.** A measure on a pseudo-metric space is uniformly locally doubling when some constant \(C\) controls all sufficiently small scales: for every center, the measure of the closed ball of radius \(2\varepsilon\) is at most \(C\) times the measure of the closed ball of radius \(\varepsilon\), once \(\varepsilon>0\) is small enough (`IsUnifLocDoublingMeasure`, `MeasureTheory/Measure/Doubling.lean`). The small-scale restriction is essential; the definition is written so that exponential volume growth, as in hyperbolic space, is not forbidden. From one doubling constant one obtains constants that absorb a fixed bound on the ratio of radii (`IsUnifLocDoublingMeasure.scalingConstantOf`). Binary and finite products of uniformly locally doubling measures are uniformly locally doubling (`IsUnifLocDoublingMeasure.prod` in `MeasureTheory/Measure/Prod.lean`, `IsUnifLocDoublingMeasure.pi` in `MeasureTheory/Constructions/Pi.lean`).

Every additive Haar measure on a finite-dimensional real normed space is uniformly locally doubling, with constant \(2\) raised to the dimension, because the measure of a closed ball scales by that power of the radius (`isUnifLocDoublingMeasureOfIsAddHaarMeasure`, `MeasureTheory/Measure/Lebesgue/EqHaar.lean`). Haar measure on the circle \(\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z}\), for \(T>0\), is uniformly locally doubling as well (`MeasureTheory/Integral/IntervalIntegral/Periodic.lean`). No doubling metric space, and no theorem that a doubling measure makes the space metrically doubling, is formalized.

**Lebesgue differentiation.** For a uniformly locally doubling, locally finite measure on a second-countable pseudo-metric Borel space, a locally integrable function has almost every point as a Lebesgue point, along closed balls shrinking to the point with comparable, not necessarily fixed, centers (`IsUnifLocDoublingMeasure.ae_tendsto_average_norm_sub`, `IsUnifLocDoublingMeasure.ae_tendsto_average`), and the density theorem for measurable sets holds in the same form (`IsUnifLocDoublingMeasure.ae_tendsto_measure_inter_div`, `MeasureTheory/Covering/DensityTheorem.lean`). These come from the Vitali family of a doubling measure. No maximal-function estimate is proved for general doubling measures.

#### TauCeti

The centred Hardy–Littlewood maximal function is weak type \((1,1)\) and bounded on \(L^p\), \(1<p<\infty\), only for an additive Haar measure on a finite-dimensional real normed space ([maximal function](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Integral/MaximalFunction.lean)). The precise representative, the limit of averages over shrinking closed balls, is defined on a metric measure space and agrees almost everywhere with a locally integrable function when the measure is doubling ([precise representative](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/PreciseRepresentative.lean)).

The Cheeger energy of \(f\in L^2(X,m)\) is defined by relaxation of the local Lipschitz constants of Lipschitz functions; it is convex, \(2\)-homogeneous and lower semicontinuous, and its finiteness domain is the linear subspace \(W^{1,2}(X,d,m)\) (`TauCeti.cheegerEnergy`, `TauCeti.cheegerEnergyDomain`, [Cheeger energy](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/CheegerEnergy.lean)). Strong upper gradients in the gradient-flow sense, along absolutely continuous curves, are defined, with the chain-rule inequality and the theorem that the descending slope of a locally Lipschitz function is a strong upper gradient (`TauCeti.IsStrongUpperGradient`, [upper gradients](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/UpperGradient.lean)). These are energies of gradient-flow type; curve modulus, \(p\)-weak upper gradients, Newtonian spaces, and Poincaré inequalities on metric measure spaces are not defined.

**Synopsis entries.** Partly covered: 39.2 (Lebesgue differentiation for uniformly locally doubling measures in Mathlib; the maximal inequality only for Haar measure on finite-dimensional spaces in TauCeti). Not covered: 39.1, 39.3–39.14.

### Embeddings, quasisymmetry, and Gromov–Hausdorff convergence

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS39.md#embeddings-quasisymmetry-and-gromovhausdorff-convergence).

#### Mathlib

**Gromov–Hausdorff distance.** The Gromov–Hausdorff space is the space of nonempty compact metric spaces up to isometry. It is realized as the quotient of the nonempty compact subsets of \(\ell^\infty(\mathbb{R})\) by isometry, using the Kuratowski embedding of separable metric spaces (`GromovHausdorff.GHSpace`, `Topology/MetricSpace/GromovHausdorff.lean`). The distance between two classes is the infimum of Hausdorff distances between isometric copies in \(\ell^\infty(\mathbb{R})\), and the infimum is attained (`GromovHausdorff.hausdorffDist_optimal`, `GromovHausdorff.ghDist_eq_hausdorffDist`). The resulting function is a metric; two nonempty compact metric spaces determine the same point if and only if they are isometric (`GromovHausdorff.toGHSpace_eq_toGHSpace_iff_isometryEquiv`). The distance is at most the Hausdorff distance of any pair of isometric embeddings into a common metric space (`GromovHausdorff.ghDist_le_hausdorffDist`). The Gromov–Hausdorff space is complete and second countable. A set of classes is totally bounded if the representatives have uniformly bounded diameter and, along some sequence of radii tending to zero, there is a uniform bound on the number of balls of that radius needed to cover each representative (`GromovHausdorff.totallyBounded`). This is the sufficiency half of Gromov's precompactness criterion; the converse and the Riemannian and doubling examples are not formalized.

**Snowflakes.** The \(\alpha\)-snowflake \((X,d^\alpha)\), \(0<\alpha\le1\), of a (pseudo, extended) metric space is defined as a type wrapper with the snowflaked metric (`Metric.Snowflaking`, `Topology/MetricSpace/Snowflaking.lean`). This is supporting material: Assouad’s embedding theorem is not proved.

**Delone sets.** A Delone set in a metric space is a set bundled with two positive radii: a packing radius, strictly separating distinct points, and a covering radius, such that every point of the space lies at most that far from the set (`Delone.DeloneSet`, `Analysis/AperiodicOrder/Delone/Basic.lean`). Any ball of radius at most half the packing radius contains at most one point of the set. A bilipschitz equivalence transports a Delone set to a Delone set, scaling the packing radius by the inverse of the anti-Lipschitz constant and the covering radius by the Lipschitz constant (`Delone.DeloneSet.mapBilipschitz`); an isometry preserves both radii (`Delone.DeloneSet.mapIsometry`). This is supporting metric geometry.

#### TauCeti

Sturm’s transportation distance \(\mathbb D\) between metric measure spaces, an infimum over metric couplings and measure couplings, is defined (`TauCeti.sturmEDist`, [transportation distance](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/MetricMeasure/TransportationDistance.lean)), and Gromov–Wasserstein distances are developed ([Gromov–Wasserstein](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/GromovWasserstein/Basic.lean)). These are measured analogues of the Gromov–Hausdorff distance, not precompactness theorems.

**Synopsis entries.** Partly covered: 39.17 (sufficiency of the covering criterion for total boundedness, with completeness of the Gromov–Hausdorff space, in Mathlib). Not covered: 39.15, 39.16, 39.85.

### Self-similar sets and dimensions

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS39.md#self-similar-sets-and-dimensions).

#### Mathlib

**Hausdorff dimension.** Hausdorff measure and Hausdorff dimension are those of section 37 (`MeasureTheory/Measure/Hausdorff.lean`, `Topology/MetricSpace/HausdorffDimension.lean`): the dimension of a countable union, Hölder and Lipschitz image bounds, invariance under bi-Lipschitz and linear equivalences, and the dimension of open subsets of \(\mathbb{R}^n\) are stated there. Box, packing and Assouad dimensions, the mass distribution principle, and product inequalities are not defined or proved.

**Cantor set and the Hutchinson ingredients.** The ternary Cantor set is constructed by repeated removal of middle thirds. It is compact, and its points are characterized by ternary expansions using only the digits \(0\) and \(2\); it is homeomorphic to the Cantor space \(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\) (`cantorSet`, `isCompact_cantorSet`, `cantorSetHomeomorphNatToBool`, `Topology/Instances/CantorSet.lean`). Its Hausdorff dimension is not computed. The ingredients of the Hutchinson construction are present separately: the nonempty compact subsets of a complete metric space form a complete space for the Hausdorff distance (`TopologicalSpace.NonemptyCompacts`, `Topology/UniformSpace/Closeds.lean`), and the Banach fixed-point theorem is available. No iterated function system, attractor, or self-similar measure is defined.

**Synopsis entries.** Not covered: 39.81–39.84, 39.73, 39.74, 39.86, 39.87.

## 39B. Dirichlet Forms, Heat Kernels, and Analysis on Graphs and Fractals

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS39.md#synopsis-39b).

The only heat-kernel model in either library is the Euclidean Gaussian kernel. Hilbert-space quadratic-form and coercive-operator tools are described in [Section 22](STRUCTURE22.md) and [Section 32](STRUCTURE32.md); these retain their stated settings.

### Dirichlet forms and symmetric processes

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS39.md#dirichlet-forms-and-symmetric-processes).

#### TauCeti

On a finite-dimensional real inner product space, convolution with the Gaussian heat kernel is a strongly continuous contraction semigroup on \(L^p\), \(1\le p<\infty\), with values in a real Banach space ([heat semigroup](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Semigroup.lean)). This is one concrete symmetric Markov semigroup. Dirichlet forms, the form–operator correspondence, capacities, and associated Hunt processes are not defined in either library.

**Synopsis entries.** Partly covered: 39.20 (only the Euclidean heat semigroup, as a strongly continuous \(L^p\) contraction semigroup for \(1\le p<\infty\), in TauCeti). Not covered: 39.18, 39.19, 39.21–39.26.

### Heat kernels, inequalities, and curvature

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS39.md#heat-kernels-inequalities-and-curvature).

#### TauCeti

The Gaussian heat kernel \(K_t(x)=(4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^2/4t}\) on a finite-dimensional real inner product space is positive with mass one, solves the heat equation, and satisfies the Chapman–Kolmogorov identity \(K_s*K_t=K_{s+t}\) ([heat kernel](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Basic.lean), [heat semigroup](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Semigroup.lean)); Gaussian bounds for the kernel and its derivatives are recorded for differentiation under the integral ([bounds](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/HeatKernel/Bounds.lean)). See [Section 32](STRUCTURE32.md) for the Euclidean PDE theory. No heat kernel of a Dirichlet form, manifold, or metric measure space is defined.

**Synopsis entries.** Partly covered: 39.27 (the Euclidean Gaussian kernel only: symmetric, Chapman–Kolmogorov, mass one). Not covered: 39.28–39.33, 39.35.

### Networks, resistance, and fractal diffusion

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS39.md#networks-resistance-and-fractal-diffusion).

#### Mathlib

As supporting material, the Laplacian matrix \(D-A\) of a finite simple graph is defined, with the quadratic-form identity \(x^{\mathsf T}Lx=\frac12\sum_{i\sim j}(x_i-x_j)^2\), positive semidefiniteness, and the dimension of its kernel equal to the number of connected components (`SimpleGraph.lapMatrix`, `SimpleGraph.lapMatrix_toLinearMap₂'`, `SimpleGraph.posSemidef_lapMatrix`, `SimpleGraph.card_connectedComponent_eq_finrank_ker_toLin'_lapMatrix`, `Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/LapMatrix.lean`). These are unweighted finite graphs without conductances, masses, or boundary data.

**Synopsis entries.** Not covered: 39.66–39.72, 39.75–39.80.

## 39C. Optimal Transport, Wasserstein Geometry, and Metric Gradient Flows

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS39.md#synopsis-39c).

Couplings, optimal plans and maps, Wasserstein geometry, metric minimizing movements, and conditional properties of EVI solutions belong here.

### Optimal transportation and Wasserstein geometry

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS39.md#optimal-transportation-and-wasserstein-geometry).

#### Mathlib

**Metrics on probability measures.** The Lévy–Prokhorov metric on probability measures is constructed, and on a separable pseudo-metric Borel space it induces the topology of weak convergence (`LevyProkhorov.probabilityMeasureHomeomorph`, `MeasureTheory/Measure/LevyProkhorovMetric.lean`). Prokhorov's theorem is proved in the form that the closure of a tight set of probability measures is compact, with the converse on Polish spaces; the probability measures on a compact space form a compact space (`isCompact_closure_of_isTightMeasureSet`, `isTightMeasureSet_of_isCompact_closure`, `MeasureTheory/Measure/Prokhorov.lean`). This is supporting infrastructure; Wasserstein distances and optimal transport are not in Mathlib.

#### TauCeti

**Kantorovich problem.** TauCeti contains an extensive Kantorovich optimal-transport development: couplings, gluing, cost minimization, and existence of an optimal plan for a lower-semicontinuous \([0,\infty]\)-valued cost and Polish (more generally tight) marginals, the optimal value being allowed to be infinite (`TauCeti.exists_isOptimalCoupling`, [existence](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Existence/Basic.lean)). For an extended-real cost bounded below by integrable marginal terms \(a(x)+b(y)\), the signed transport value is attained between equal-mass finite measures on Polish spaces when the nonnegative residual \(c-a-b\) is lower semicontinuous, for instance when \(c\) is lower semicontinuous and \(a,b\) are upper semicontinuous (`TauCeti.exists_isCoupling_planCostBddBelow_eq_transportCostBddBelow_of_lowerSemicontinuous_residual`, [signed costs](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Existence/Finite.lean)).

**Duality.** Strong duality for a lower-semicontinuous cost \(c:X\times Y\to[0,\infty]\) is proved first on compact metrizable spaces, where continuous feasible pairs suffice, and then on Polish spaces, where the transport cost equals the supremum of \(\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\psi\,d\nu\) over integrable pairs with \(\varphi(x)+\psi(y)\le c(x,y)\), the common value being allowed to be infinite (`TauCeti.isLUB_kantorovichDualValue_continuous`, [compact duality](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Duality/Compact.lean); `TauCeti.isLUB_ofReal_kantorovichDualValue_integrable_of_lowerSemicontinuous`, [lower-semicontinuous duality](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Duality/LowerSemicontinuous.lean)). For a bounded continuous nonnegative cost on Polish spaces the dual problem is attained by a pair of \(c\)-conjugate integrable potentials, and every optimal plan is certified by such a pair (`TauCeti.isGreatest_kantorovichDualValue_integrable`, [dual attainment](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Duality/Attainment.lean)); on compact spaces the \(c\)-transforms of continuous functions for a continuous cost are continuous ([compact \(c\)-transforms](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/CTransform/Compact.lean)). A differentiable twisted cost and a dual contact certificate yield a measurable optimal transport map and its almost-everywhere uniqueness ([twist mechanism](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Twist/Map.lean)).

**Cyclical monotonicity.** An optimal plan of finite cost for a lower-semicontinuous cost on Polish spaces is concentrated on a measurable \(c\)-cyclically monotone set (`TauCeti.IsOptimalCoupling.exists_isCyclicallyMonotone_of_lowerSemicontinuous`, [necessity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Duality/CyclicalMonotonicity.lean)); conversely, for a finite-valued cost, concentration on such a set forces optimality, the Schachermayer–Teichmann theorem (`TauCeti.IsCoupling.isOptimalCoupling_iff_exists_isCyclicallyMonotone`, [sufficiency](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Duality/SchachermayerTeichmann.lean)). The Rockafellar–Rüschendorf theorem places a \(c\)-cyclically monotone set in the \(c\)-superdifferential of a \(c\)-concave potential ([cyclical monotonicity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/CTransform/CyclicalMonotonicity.lean)), Rockafellar's theorem places a cyclically monotone set in the subdifferential of a convex conjugate ([Rockafellar](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/CTransform/Rockafellar.lean)), and complementary slackness serves as an optimality certificate ([certificates](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Duality/Certificate.lean)).

**Wasserstein spaces.** The \(p\)-Wasserstein spaces of probability measures with finite \(p\)-moment are constructed, with the triangle inequality from gluing. For \(1\le p<\infty\) they are complete over a complete separable ground metric space (`TauCeti.WassersteinSpace.completeSpace`, [completeness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/Complete.lean)) and separable, hence Polish over a Polish space (`TauCeti.WassersteinSpace.separableSpace`, [separability](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/Separable.lean)). Wasserstein convergence implies weak convergence ([comparison](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/WeakConvergence.lean)), and for \(1\le p<\infty\) \(W_p\) convergence is equivalent, on a separable space, to weak convergence together with convergence of \(p\)-th moments, with uniformly small moment tails along convergent families (`TauCeti.tendsto_wassersteinEDist_of_tendsto_probabilityMeasure_of_tendsto_lintegral`, [moments](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/Moment.lean)). Empirical measures of an i.i.d. sample converge almost surely in \(W_p\) ([empirical measures](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/Empirical.lean)). Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality is proved for finite-first-moment probability measures on second-countable pseudo-metric spaces with standard Borel measurable structure, in particular Polish spaces: \(W_1\) is the supremum of differences of integrals against real 1-Lipschitz functions (`TauCeti.wassersteinEDist_one_eq_iSup`, [duality](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/KantorovichRubinstein.lean)).

**One-dimensional transport.** On the line the monotone (quantile) coupling is optimal for \(|x-y|^p\) whenever \(1\le p\), and \(W_p\) is the \(L^p(0,1)\) distance of quantile functions for every \(1\le p\le\infty\) (`MeasureTheory.Measure.isOptimalCoupling_quantileCoupling`, `TauCeti.wassersteinEDist_eq_eLpNorm_quantile_sub`, [monotone rearrangement](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/Rearrangement.lean)); \(W_1\) is the \(L^1\) distance of the distribution functions ([one-dimensional formula](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/OneDimensional.lean)). Representation of the quantile coupling by a monotone map when \(\mu\) has no atoms was not found; on a Polish space, \(W_p^p\) from an atomless law with finite \(p\)-moment is the Monge infimum over transport maps (`TauCeti.wassersteinEDist_rpow_eq_mongeCost`, [Monge infimum](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/Monge.lean)). The \(W_\infty\) distance, tensorization over products, multi-marginal couplings with finite duality, and Wasserstein barycenters are also developed.

**Entropic transport and Sinkhorn.** The static Schrödinger problem and entropically regularized transport \(\int c\,d\pi+\varepsilon\operatorname{KL}(\pi\,|\,\mu\otimes\nu)\) are defined and identified through the Gibbs measure; a finite Schrödinger value is attained by exactly one coupling ([entropic transport](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Entropic/Basic.lean)). For a bounded measurable cost and \(\varepsilon>0\), the Schrödinger system has bounded measurable solutions, found as the fixed point of a contracting Sinkhorn sweep, and the entropic optimal plan has density \(e^{(\varphi(x)+\psi(y)-c(x,y))/\varepsilon}\) against \(\mu\otimes\nu\) (`TauCeti.exists_isCoupling_withDensity_exp_add`, `TauCeti.exists_isCoupling_lintegral_add_mul_klDiv_eq_entropicTransportCost`, [Schrödinger system](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Entropic/SchroedingerSystem.lean)). For matrices with positive entries, the Sinkhorn–Knopp iteration converges linearly in Hilbert's projective metric, at the Birkhoff–Franklin–Lorenz rate \(\tanh(\Delta(K)/4)^{2n}\) ([Sinkhorn rates](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Data/Matrix/Sinkhorn/Rate.lean)). As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) the regularized values converge to the transport cost for discretizable costs, in particular continuous costs on compact spaces, and weak limits of entropic optimizers are optimal plans for lower-semicontinuous costs (`TauCeti.tendsto_entropicTransportCost_transportCost_of_continuous`, `TauCeti.isOptimalCoupling_of_tendsto_of_entropicTransportCost`, [zero-temperature limit](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Entropic/ZeroTemperature.lean)).

**Brenier's theorem and the Monge–Ampère equation.** On a finite-dimensional real inner product space, for the cost \(\|x-y\|^2/2\), a source law absolutely continuous with respect to Haar measure, and finite optimal cost, the optimal plan is unique and is the graph of \(\nabla u\) for a proper lower-semicontinuous convex \(u\), and the optimal map is unique \(\mu\)-almost everywhere (`TauCeti.exists_isKantorovichOptimalTransportMap_gradient`, [Brenier](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Brenier.lean)). Finite second moments give finite quadratic cost (`TauCeti.transportCost_norm_sub_sq_div_two_ne_top`, [Wasserstein space](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/Space.lean)). When both laws are absolutely continuous with densities \(g_0,g_1\), the Brenier potential is a weak (Monge–Ampère measure) solution of its transport equation and solves \(g_0(x)=g_1(\nabla u(x))\det D^2u(x)\) almost everywhere with the Alexandrov Hessian; with a target density on a closed convex set carrying it, \(u\) is an Aleksandrov solution (`TauCeti.exists_isKantorovichOptimalTransportMap_gradient_and_rnDeriv_ae_eq`, `TauCeti.exists_isKantorovichOptimalTransportMap_gradient_and_mongeAmpereMeasure_eq_withDensity`, same file). Regularity of the Brenier potential (Caffarelli) and transport on Riemannian manifolds (McCann) are not formalized.

**Geodesics and the dynamic formulation.** On a Polish geodesic space, two measures at finite \(W_p\) distance are joined by a dynamic plan concentrated on geodesics whose time marginals form a constant-speed geodesic, so \(\mathcal P_p(X)\) is a geodesic space for \(1\le p\le\infty\); on a separable Banach space, the laws of \((1-t)x+ty\) under an optimal coupling form a geodesic segment (`TauCeti.WassersteinSpace.instIsGeodesicSpace`, `TauCeti.WassersteinSpace.exists_isGeodesicSegment_map_smul_add_smul`, [geodesics](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/Geodesic.lean)). On a finite-dimensional real normed space and for \(1\le p<\infty\), the displacement interpolation of an optimal coupling at finite \(W_p\) distance solves the continuity equation with \(p\)-action at most \(W_p(\mu,\nu)^p\), with equality and velocity equal to the displacement on a strictly convex space for \(1<p<\infty\) (`TauCeti.exists_isContinuityEquation_map_smul_fst_add_smul_snd_of_isOptimalCoupling`, [plan interpolation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/ContinuityEquation/PlanInterpolation.lean)). This is the upper bound in the Benamou–Brenier formula; the lower bound for arbitrary distributional solutions is not proved. For \(1<p<\infty\), Lisini's superposition principle represents an absolutely continuous curve in \(\mathcal P_p(X)\) with finite \(p\)-action, over a complete separable metric space, as the time marginals of a law on absolutely continuous paths with matching action ([superposition](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/Superposition.lean)), and every such representation averages the path speeds to at least the metric speed of the curve ([curves of laws](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/AbsolutelyContinuous.lean)). The tangent space \(\overline{\{\nabla\varphi:\varphi\in C_c^\infty\}}^{L^2(\mu)}\) is defined on a finite-dimensional space, with the orthogonal projection as the least-norm field of given divergence ([tangent space](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/TangentSpace.lean)); the minimal velocity field of an absolutely continuous \(W_2\) curve is not constructed.

**Energies and curvature.** Potential, interaction and internal energies, the Boltzmann entropy and the Rényi entropies are defined ([internal energy](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/InternalEnergy.lean), [Rényi entropy](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/RenyiEntropy.lean)). The potential energy of an \(m\)-strongly convex potential is \(m\)-geodesically convex on \(\mathcal P_2\) of a separable Banach space and \(m\)-convex along generalized geodesics (`StrongConvexOn.geodesicallyConvex_potentialEnergy`, `StrongConvexOn.generalizedGeodesicallyConvex_potentialEnergy`, [potential energy](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/PotentialEnergy.lean)), and the interaction energy of a convex difference kernel is geodesically convex (`ConvexOn.geodesicallyConvex_interactionEnergy`, [interaction energy](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/InteractionEnergy.lean)). ### Metric gradient flows and synthetic diffusion

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS39.md#metric-gradient-flows-and-synthetic-diffusion).

#### TauCeti

**Metric derivatives and slopes.** For a curve absolutely continuous on \([a,b]\) in an extended pseudo-metric space, the metric derivative \(|\gamma'|(t)\) exists as a limit almost everywhere, is integrable, bounds the distances \(d(\gamma_s,\gamma_u)\le\int_s^u|\gamma'|\), and is the smallest such density; conversely an integrable density bounding the distances gives absolute continuity (`TauCeti.metricDerivative`, `absolutelyContinuousOnInterval_iff_exists_edist_le_lintegral`, [metric derivative](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/MetricDerivative/Basic.lean)). The total variation of such a curve equals the integral of its metric derivative (`TauCeti.eVariationOn_eq_lintegral_metricDerivative`, [variation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/MetricDerivative/Variation.lean)). The descending slope is defined, with its Lipschitz bound and identification with the norm of the derivative on normed spaces ([slope](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/Slope.lean)); for a geodesically convex energy it is the global slope and is lower semicontinuous ([geodesic convexity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/GeodesicConvex.lean)).

**Minimizing movements.** The Moreau–Yosida approximation and resolvent are defined, with the one-step energy and slope estimates and the De Giorgi energy identity; resolvents are nonempty for a coercive energy that is lower semicontinuous for an auxiliary convergence with compact bounded sublevels ([Moreau–Yosida](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/MoreauYosida/Basic.lean), [energy identity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/MoreauYosida/Derivative.lean), [existence](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/MoreauYosida/Existence.lean)). Under these hypotheses of Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré, generalized minimizing movements exist; they are \(1/2\)-Hölder on bounded time intervals and, on a pseudo-metric space, absolutely continuous with finite \(2\)-action and the limiting energy estimate (`TauCeti.IsGeneralizedMinimizingMovement`, [minimizing movements](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/MinimizingMovement.lean)). Curves of maximal slope satisfy the energy identity when the upper gradient is strong ([maximal slope](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/MaximalSlope/Basic.lean)), and the Sandier–Serfaty argument passes the energy-dissipation inequality to limits of curves of maximal slope under separate convergence hypotheses ([limits](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/MaximalSlope/Convergence.lean)).

**EVI solutions.** For a lower-semicontinuous energy, \(EVI_\lambda\) solutions satisfy \(d(u_t,v_t)\le e^{-\lambda t}d(u_0,v_0)\), hence uniqueness (`TauCeti.IsEVISolution.dist_le`, `TauCeti.IsEVISolution.eqOn`, [EVI](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/EVI/Basic.lean)). They are locally Lipschitz at positive times, their speed equals the descending slope almost everywhere, and they are curves of maximal slope satisfying the energy identity \(F(u_s)-F(u_t)=\frac12\int_s^t(|\dot u_r|^2+|\partial F|^2(u_r))\,dr\) (`TauCeti.IsEVISolution.ae_descendingSlope_eq_metricDerivative`, `TauCeti.IsEVISolution.apply_eq_add_dissipation`, [maximal slope](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/EVI/MaximalSlope.lean)). They form a jointly continuous \(\lambda\)-contractive semiflow on the set of initial points from which a solution exists (`TauCeti.eviFlow_add`, `TauCeti.eviSemiflow`, [semigroup](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/GradientFlow/EVI/Semigroup.lean)). These conclusions assume an \(EVI\) solution exists; generation of \(EVI\) flows is not proved. CAT(0) spaces are defined by the Bruhat–Tits CN inequality, with strong convexity of squared distance along geodesics ([CAT(0)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Topology/MetricSpace/CATZero.lean)); this is supporting material, as no gradient flow in a Hadamard space is constructed.

**Wasserstein gradient flows.** The Jordan–Kinderlehrer–Otto step exists for a narrowly lower-semicontinuous coercive energy whose bounded sublevel sequences are tight (`TauCeti.WassersteinSpace.moreauYosidaResolvent_nonempty`, [JKO step](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/Resolvent.lean)), and is unique when the energy is \(m\)-convex along generalized geodesics and \(1+m\tau>0\) ([uniqueness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/GeneralizedGeodesic/Convex.lean)). On \(\mathcal P_2\) of a separable Hilbert space, a JKO minimizer satisfies the Euler–Lagrange variational inequality along vector-field perturbations ([Euler–Lagrange](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/EulerLagrange.lean)). The Boltzmann entropy is lower semicontinuous and coercive on \(\mathcal P_p(X)\) under an integrable confining weight, the compactness input for the heat and Fokker–Planck flows ([Boltzmann entropy](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/Wasserstein/BoltzmannEntropy.lean)). Convergence of the JKO scheme, the identification of the heat, Fokker–Planck and porous-medium equations as Wasserstein gradient flows, and \(RCD\) theory are not formalized.

**Cheeger energy.** The Cheeger energy recorded under 39A is convex, \(2\)-homogeneous and lower semicontinuous on \(L^2(X,m)\) ([Cheeger energy](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/CheegerEnergy.lean)); its \(L^2\) gradient-flow semigroup and the characterization of linearity by quadraticity are not proved.
