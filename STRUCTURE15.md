## Completeness, quotient spaces, and classical examples

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS15.md#completeness-quotient-spaces-and-classical-examples).

### Mathlib

**Completion and quotients.** The uniform completion of a seminormed space is a normed space, and the canonical map into it is a linear isometry (`UniformSpace.Completion.toComplₗᵢ`, `Analysis/Normed/Module/Completion.lean`) with dense range; any two completions are identified by `AbstractCompletion.compareEquiv`. A bounded linear map defined on a dense subspace, with values in a Banach space, extends uniquely to a bounded linear map, with control of the operator norm (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Extend.lean`). The space of continuous linear maps into a complete space is complete (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Completeness.lean`).

The quotient by a subspace carries the quotient seminorm \(\|x+M\|=\inf_{m\in M}\|x+m\|\). When the subspace is closed this is a norm, compatible with the quotient topology. The canonical projection is continuous with norm at most one, and continuous linear maps vanishing on the subspace descend to the quotient. A quotient of a Banach space by a closed subspace is complete (`Analysis/Normed/Group/Quotient.lean`, instance `Submodule.Quotient.completeSpace`).

**Finite dimension and Riesz's lemma.** Riesz's lemma: for a closed proper subspace \(F\) of a normed space and any \(r<1\), some nonzero \(x\) satisfies \(\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,F)\ge r\|x\|\); over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\) one may take \(\|x\|=1\) (`riesz_lemma`, `riesz_lemma_of_lt_one`, `Analysis/Normed/Module/RieszLemma.lean`). A normed space over a complete nontrivially normed field that is locally compact, or whose closed unit ball is compact, is finite-dimensional (`FiniteDimensional.of_isCompact_closedBall`). A finite-dimensional normed space over a complete field is complete, all norms on it are equivalent, every linear map out of it is continuous, and finite-dimensional subspaces are closed (`LinearMap.continuous_of_finiteDimensional`, `Submodule.closed_of_finiteDimensional`; `Analysis/Normed/Module/FiniteDimension.lean`, `Topology/Algebra/Module/FiniteDimension.lean`).

**Classical spaces.** A normed group is complete exactly when every absolutely summable series converges (`NormedAddCommGroup.completeSpace_of_summable_imp_tendsto`, `NormedAddCommGroup.summable_imp_tendsto_of_complete`, `Analysis/Normed/Group/Completeness.lean`). For a family of normed groups, \(\ell^p\) is the subgroup of the product consisting of families with finitely many nonzero terms when \(p = 0\), with summable \(p\)th powers of the norms when \(0 < p < \infty\), and with bounded norms when \(p = \infty\). For \(1 \le p \le \infty\) this is a normed additive group, and a normed space when each factor is a normed space over a normed field. If \(1 \le p\) and each factor is complete, \(\ell^p\) is complete. For \(1 \le p < \infty\), the finitely supported families are dense: the canonical single-coordinate series sums to the family. Membership at an exponent \(q\) implies membership at every larger exponent, so \(\ell^p \subset \ell^q\) whenever \(p \le q\). A pointwise limit of a norm-bounded family in \(\ell^p\) remains in \(\ell^p\) (`Analysis/Normed/Lp/lpSpace.lean`). The space \(C_0(X,\beta)\) of continuous functions vanishing at infinity is complete when \(\beta\) is (`Topology/ContinuousMap/ZeroAtInfty.lean`); \(c_0\) is the case of discrete \(\mathbb N\). Completeness of the measure-theoretic spaces \(L^p\) is recorded in [Section 04](STRUCTURE04.md).

Scalar Hölder on \(\ell^p\): if the real numbers \(p.\mathrm{toReal}\) and \(q.\mathrm{toReal}\) are Hölder conjugate, then \(\sum_i \|f_i\|\,\|g_i\| \le \|f\|\,\|g\|\) (`lp.tsum_mul_le_mul_norm`). Since \(\infty.\mathrm{toReal}=0\), this formulation excludes the endpoint \(p = 1\), \(q = \infty\), which the file lists as future work. A uniformly bounded family of continuous bilinear maps, over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), induces a map \(\ell^p \times \ell^q \to \ell^r\) for a Hölder triple, including the extreme exponents, and the map is continuous of operator norm at most the uniform bound when \(1 \le p,q,r\). For conjugate exponents and a complete target, the same data give a continuous pairing by summation (`lp.dualPairing`), of norm at most the uniform bound; in particular there is a contractive map from \(\ell^{p}\) of duals into the dual of \(\ell^{q}\). A uniformly bounded family of continuous linear maps induces a continuous linear map on \(\ell^p\) for \(1 \le p\) (`Analysis/Normed/Lp/lpHolder.lean`). Neither the isometric identification of \((\ell^p)^*\), \(c_0^*\), \((\ell^1)^*\) nor that of the dual of measure-theoretic \(L^p\) is proved.

**Arzelà–Ascoli.** On a compact domain, an equicontinuous family of continuous maps into a metric space whose values at each point lie in a compact set has compact closure in the uniform topology (`BoundedContinuousFunction.arzela_ascoli`, `Topology/ContinuousMap/Bounded/ArzelaAscoli.lean`). There is also a general version for uniform convergence on families of compact subsets (`ArzelaAscoli.isCompact_of_equicontinuous`, `Topology/UniformSpace/Ascoli.lean`). On an equicontinuous family over a compact domain, pointwise and uniform convergence induce the same topology.

**Complemented subspaces.** `Submodule.ClosedComplemented` asks for a continuous projection onto the subspace. For a closed subspace of a Banach space this is equivalent to the existence of a closed algebraic complement (`Submodule.closedComplemented_iff_isClosed_exists_isClosed_isCompl`, `Analysis/Normed/Module/Complemented.lean`). Finite-dimensional subspaces of a polynormable space over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\), in particular of a real or complex normed space, are complemented (`Submodule.ClosedComplemented.of_finiteDimensional`, `Analysis/LocallyConvex/HahnBanach.lean`), and so are closed subspaces of finite codimension (`Submodule.ClosedComplemented.of_finiteDimensional_quotient`). No example of an uncomplemented closed subspace is formalized.

*Supporting material: Schauder bases.* A generalized Schauder basis of a normed space, over a nontrivially normed field, is a family of vectors, a family of coordinate functionals in the continuous dual, a biorthogonality relation, and an expansion of every vector as a sum along a specified summation filter. The classical Schauder basis is the case indexed by \(\mathbb{N}\), with conditional sums of partial sums. An unconditional Schauder basis is indexed by an arbitrary type, with sums over finite subsets. The vectors are linearly independent. The projection onto a finite set of basis vectors has range equal to their span and rank equal to the cardinality of the set; these projections converge pointwise to the identity, and compose as the projection onto the intersection. If the space is complete, the partial-sum projections of a classical basis are uniformly bounded, by Banach–Steinhaus, and so are all finite projections of an unconditional basis. A classical basis can be built from nested finite-rank projections \(P_n\) with \(P_0=0\), \(\dim\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}P_n=n\), \(P_nP_m=P_{\min(n,m)}\), and pointwise convergence to the identity (`Analysis/Normed/Module/Bases.lean`). A Hilbert basis is an unconditional Schauder basis, and a Hilbert basis indexed by \(\mathbb{N}\) is a classical Schauder basis. Bases are treated in [Section 17](STRUCTURE17.md).

### TauCeti

The converse half of Arzelà–Ascoli is proved: on a compact space, every compact subset of \(C(X,Y)\) is equicontinuous (`IsCompact.equicontinuous`); the file also restates the forward direction directly in \(C(X,Y)\) and gives a version for complete targets with total boundedness on a dense set ([Ascoli in \(C(X,Y)\)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Topology/ContinuousMap/Ascoli.lean)). Descending a continuous linear map through a submodule contained in its kernel preserves the operator norm ([quotient operator norms](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Quotient.lean)).

Partly covered: 15.9 (definition, characterization by closed complements, and complementation of finite-dimensional and finite-codimensional subspaces; no uncomplemented example). Not covered: 15.6.

## Hahn–Banach, separation, and duality

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS15.md#hahnbanach-separation-and-duality).

### Mathlib

**Hahn–Banach.** A real linear functional on a subspace dominated by a sublinear function extends with the same domination (`exists_extension_of_le_sublinear`, `Analysis/Convex/Cone/Extension.lean`). On a seminormed space over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), a continuous linear functional on a subspace extends to the whole space with the same operator norm (`exists_extension_norm_eq`). The space need not be complete. If \(x\) is nonzero, some continuous linear functional of norm \(1\) takes the value \(\|x\|\) at \(x\), with the norm read in the scalar field. For an arbitrary vector the same conclusion holds with norm at most \(1\). If the space is a nontrivial normed space, a functional of norm exactly \(1\) exists for every vector, the zero vector included (`exists_dual_vector`, `exists_dual_vector'`, `exists_dual_vector''`, `Analysis/Normed/Module/HahnBanach.lean`). For a real or complex normed space the canonical map \(J:E\to E^{**}\) is a linear isometry (`NormedSpace.inclusionInDoubleDualLi`, `Analysis/Normed/Module/DoubleDual.lean`).

**Separation.** The geometric forms are proved for a real topological vector space, before local convexity is assumed. Disjoint convex sets, one of them open, are weakly separated by a continuous real-linear functional. If both are open, the separation is semistrict: the two images lie on opposite sides of a common value, both inequalities strict. If one convex set has nonempty interior and is disjoint from another convex set, the separation is nonstrict, and the functional may be taken nonzero when the second set is nonempty. For a convex set \(A\) with nonempty interior and \(x\notin\mathop{\mathrm{int}}A\), a nonzero continuous linear functional satisfies \(f(y)\le f(x)\) for all \(y\in A\) (`Analysis/LocallyConvex/Separation.lean`).

Local convexity is the hypothesis for strict separation. In a real locally convex space, a compact convex set and a disjoint closed convex set are strictly separated, with a gap between the two values. A point outside a closed convex set is strictly separated from it. In a \(T_1\) space, distinct points are strictly separated, so the dual separates points. A closed convex set is the intersection of the closed half-spaces containing it. The same statements are repeated for a space over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), with continuous linear functionals over that field and with real parts in the inequalities. A real or complex normed space is locally convex, so these forms apply to it (`Analysis/LocallyConvex/Separation.lean`).

The analytic theorem for polynormable spaces, which Section 16 develops, extends a continuous linear functional on a subspace without a norm-preservation claim in the bare extension statement, and extends a functional dominated by a continuous seminorm with the same domination. A continuous linear map from a subspace into a Hausdorff topological vector space, with finite-dimensional range, extends (`Analysis/LocallyConvex/HahnBanach.lean`).

### TauCeti

**Duality of subspaces and quotients.** The dual of \(E/S\) is identified isometrically with the annihilator of \(S\), with no closedness assumption (`Submodule.quotientDualEquivAnnihilator`, [dual of a quotient](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Dual/Quotient.lean)). Restriction to a submodule \(S\) of a real or complex seminormed space induces an isometric identification \(E^*/S^\perp\cong S^*\) (`Submodule.quotientAnnihilatorEquivDual`); in the same file, a vector outside a closed submodule is separated from it by a continuous functional vanishing on the submodule (`Submodule.exists_dual_forall_mem_eq_zero_ne_zero`, [dual of a submodule](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Dual/Submodule.lean)). The exact distance formula \(\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,M)=\sup\{|f(x)|:f\in M^\perp,\ \|f\|\le1\}\) is not stated.

**Goldstine's theorem.** For a seminormed space over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\), the weak-star closure of \(J(\bar B(x,r))\) is the closed ball \(\bar B(Jx,r)\) of the bidual; in particular \(J(B_E)\) is weak-star dense in \(B_{E^{**}}\) and \(J(E)\) is weak-star dense in \(E^{**}\) (`NormedSpace.closure_image_inclusionInDoubleDualWeak_closedBall_zero_one`, `NormedSpace.denseRange_inclusionInDoubleDualWeak`, [Goldstine](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Dual/Goldstine.lean)).

## Baire category and the fundamental mapping principles

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS15.md#baire-category-and-the-fundamental-mapping-principles).

### Mathlib

**Baire category.** A completely pseudometrizable space is a Baire space (`BaireSpace.of_completelyPseudoMetrizable`, `Topology/Baire/CompleteMetrizable.lean`). In a Baire space a countable intersection of dense open sets is dense, and a countable union of closed sets with empty interior has empty interior (`dense_iInter_of_isOpen`, `nonempty_interior_of_iUnion_of_closed`, `Topology/Baire/Lemmas.lean`).

**Uniform boundedness.** A family of continuous semilinear maps from a complete seminormed space into a seminormed space, over nontrivially normed fields and along an isometric scalar homomorphism, is uniformly bounded in operator norm as soon as it is pointwise bounded. The file describes this as the theorem for maps from a Banach space into a normed space, and deduces it from the barrelled form. The same bound is stated with suprema in the extended nonnegative reals (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/BanachSteinhaus.lean`).

A space is barrelled when every lower-semicontinuous seminorm is continuous. The definition is stated for an arbitrary nontrivially normed scalar field. A Baire topological vector space over such a field is barrelled, and the file records that this includes Banach spaces and Fréchet spaces. A pointwise seminorm-bounded family of continuous semilinear maps from a barrelled space into a space with a seminorm topology is uniformly equicontinuous. If the codomain is polynormable, von Neumann boundedness of each orbit gives the same conclusion. A pointwise limit of continuous semilinear maps from a barrelled space into a Hausdorff polynormable space, along a countably generated nontrivial filter, is continuous (`continuousLinearMapOfTendsto`); the argument reduces the filter to a sequence (`Analysis/LocallyConvex/Barrelled.lean`). Section 16 records the barrelled theory at more length. A pointwise limit of a norm-bounded family of continuous semilinear maps is continuous (`ContinuousLinearMap.ofTendstoOfBoundedRange`, `Analysis/Normed/Operator/Completeness.lean`).

**Open mapping.** The maps in the open-mapping file are continuous semilinear maps between normed spaces over nontrivially normed fields, and the scalar homomorphisms are inverse to each other and isometric (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Banach.lean`).

Completeness is introduced in two steps. While only the codomain is complete, Baire’s theorem gives an approximate preimage: a surjective continuous semilinear map admits a constant \(C \ge 0\) such that every \(y\) is within distance \(\|y\|/2\) of the image of some \(x\) with \(\|x\| \le C\|y\|\). Once the domain is complete as well, the iterative series of these approximations converges, and there is a constant \(C > 0\) such that every \(y\) has an exact preimage \(x\) with \(\|x\| \le C\|y\|\). The same pair of completeness assumptions makes a surjective continuous semilinear map an open map, and a quotient map. A surjective map admits a right inverse, possibly nonlinear, with \(\|x\| \le C\|y\|\). The file records that a continuous linear right inverse need not exist: the quotient by a closed subspace with no closed complement is the example given there.

A continuous surjective affine map between normed affine spaces modelled on Banach spaces is an open map. A bijective continuous semilinear map between Banach spaces, still with inverse isometric scalar homomorphisms, upgrades to a continuous semilinear equivalence. A continuous linear endomorphism of a Banach space is a unit if and only if it is bijective, and its spectrum coincides with its spectrum as an algebraic endomorphism.

**Closed range.** For continuous linear maps between Banach spaces over the same scalar field, an injective map has closed range if and only if it is antilipschitz, and then it has a continuous left inverse defined on the range. If the range is algebraically complemented by a closed submodule and the kernel is trivial, the range is closed; the file marks removal of the trivial-kernel hypothesis as future work. An antilipschitz continuous semilinear map on a Banach domain has closed range, with no completeness hypothesis on the codomain at that line. If both spaces are Banach spaces and the scalar homomorphisms are inverse and isometric, bijectivity is equivalent to dense range together with an antilipschitz bound (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Banach.lean`).

**Closed graph.** The closed graph theorem is for linear maps between two Banach spaces over one common scalar field, not for semilinear maps. A linear map with closed graph is continuous (`LinearMap.continuous_of_isClosed_graph`). Sequentially: if \(u_n \to x\) and \(g(u_n) \to y\) imply \(y = g(x)\), then \(g\) is continuous. Either hypothesis upgrades the linear map to a continuous linear map (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Banach.lean`).

A different open-mapping theorem concerns group homomorphisms. A continuous surjective homomorphism from a σ-compact topological group to a Hausdorff topological group which is a Baire space is an open map, and the orbit map of a continuous transitive action of a σ-compact group on a Hausdorff Baire space is open. The file records that σ-compactness is essential (`Topology/Algebra/Group/OpenMapping.lean`).

### TauCeti

A set in a real or complex seminormed space is norm bounded if and only if it is weakly bounded, so weakly compact sets and weakly convergent sequences are bounded ([weak boundedness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/WeakSpace/Bounded.lean)). A uniformly bounded family of continuous linear maps between seminormed spaces that converges pointwise to a given continuous linear map on a dense subset converges to it pointwise everywhere, and uniform Cauchy convergence on a dense subset likewise spreads to every vector (`ContinuousLinearMap.tendsto_apply_of_dense`, `ContinuousLinearMap.uniformCauchySeqOn_apply_of_dense`, [dense convergence](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/Dense.lean)). A closed-range operator between Banach spaces whose kernel has a continuous projection \(P\) satisfies the a priori estimate \(\|x\|\le C\|Tx\|+\|Px\|\) ([a priori estimate](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/ClosedRange.lean)); Peetre's estimate \(\|x\|\le C\|Tx\|+\|Kx\|\) with \(K\) compact gives finite-dimensional kernel and, with a complemented kernel, closed range ([compact-error estimate](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/Compact/Estimate.lean)).

## Weak topologies, compactness, and reflexivity

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS15.md#weak-topologies-compactness-and-reflexivity).

### Mathlib

**Banach–Alaoglu.** Banach–Alaoglu has two forms. In the operator space, if the codomain is a proper metric space, the set of continuous semilinear maps at distance at most \(r\) from a given map is compact in the topology of pointwise convergence (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Completeness.lean`). In the dual of a normed space, not necessarily complete, closed balls are compact in the weak-star topology when the scalar field is proper, and so is the polar of a neighbourhood of the origin. The scalar fields \(\mathbb{R}\) and \(\mathbb{C}\) are proper. If the normed space is separable, those balls and polars are sequentially compact, and every weak-star compact subset of the dual is metrizable (`WeakDual.metrizable_of_isCompact`). On the weak dual of a Banach space, norm-boundedness and von Neumann boundedness coincide, by the uniform boundedness principle (`WeakDual.isBounded_iff_isVonNBounded`, `Analysis/Normed/Module/WeakDual.lean`).

**Weak closures and the bidual.** In a locally convex space over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\), the weak closure of a real-convex set equals its norm closure (`Convex.toWeakSpace_closure`, `toWeakSpace_closedConvexHull_eq`, `Analysis/LocallyConvex/WeakSpace.lean`); hence norm-closed convex sets are weakly closed. With the weak topology on \(E\) and the weak-star topology on \(E^{**}\), the canonical map \(J\) is a topological embedding, and a bounded set \(S\subset E\) has compact weak closure if the weak-star closure of \(J(S)\) is contained in \(J(E)\) (`isCompact_closure_of_isBounded`, `Analysis/Normed/Module/DoubleDual.lean`).

**Uniform convexity.** A real normed space is uniformly convex (`UniformConvexSpace`) when for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there is \(\delta>0\) such that \(\|x\|,\|y\|\le1\) and \(\|x-y\|\ge\varepsilon\) imply \(\|x+y\|\le2-\delta\). Uniformly convex spaces are strictly convex, the same bound holds on balls of any radius, and every real inner product space is uniformly convex (`Analysis/Convex/Uniform.lean`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Convex.lean`). The file lists the Milman–Pettis theorem and Hanner's inequalities as future work, and no instance for \(L^p\) or \(\ell^p\) is provided.

### TauCeti

**Reflexivity and weak compactness.** A real or complex normed space is reflexive (\(J\) surjective) if and only if its closed unit ball is weakly compact; in a reflexive space bounded sets have weakly compact closure and norm-closed bounded convex sets are weakly compact (`surjective_inclusionInDoubleDual_iff_isCompact_toWeakSpace_closedBall`, [weak compactness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Dual/Reflexive/WeakCompact.lean)). Reflexivity passes to the dual, a Banach space is reflexive if its dual is, and reflexivity is preserved by bounded linear equivalences ([reflexivity and duals](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Dual/Reflexive/Basic.lean)); closed subspaces and quotients of reflexive spaces are reflexive ([subspaces and quotients](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Dual/Reflexive/Submodule.lean)). James's basis criterion characterizes reflexivity of a space with a Schauder basis by the basis being shrinking and boundedly complete ([bases and reflexivity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Bases/Reflexive.lean)); this is supporting material, not James's compactness theorem.

**Metrics on dual balls.** When the predual is separable, an explicit metric \(\sum_n\min(2^{-n},\|f(u_n)-g(u_n)\|)\), built from a dense sequence \((u_n)\) of the predual unit ball, induces the weak-star topology on every weak-star compact set and on every dual closed ball ([weak-star metrics](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/WeakDual/Metric.lean)).

**Mazur and Milman–Pettis.** Mazur's lemma: a weak limit lies in the closed convex hull of the sequence, and finite convex combinations from successive tails converge to it in norm (`exists_tendsto_sum_smul_of_tendsto_weak`, [Mazur](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Mazur.lean)). The Milman–Pettis theorem holds: a real uniformly convex Banach space is reflexive, and without completeness \(J(E)\) is norm dense in \(E^{**}\); subspaces of uniformly convex spaces are uniformly convex (`TauCeti.surjective_inclusionInDoubleDual_of_uniformConvexSpace`, [Milman–Pettis](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Convex/Uniform.lean)). ## Hilbert space geometry and variational problems

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS15.md#hilbert-space-geometry-and-variational-problems).

### Mathlib

Throughout this section the scalar field is \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\).

**Inner product identities.** The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality holds, with equality for nonzero vectors exactly when they are proportional (`norm_inner_le_norm`, `norm_inner_eq_norm_iff`), and the inner product is recovered from the norm by polarization (`inner_eq_sum_norm_sq_div_four`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Basic.lean`). Conversely, a norm satisfying the parallelogram law comes from an inner product (`InnerProductSpace.ofNorm`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/OfNorm.lean`).

**Riesz representation.** An inner product induces a conjugate-linear isometric embedding of a seminormed inner product space into its continuous dual, by \(x \mapsto (y \mapsto \langle x, y \rangle)\). If the space is complete, this embedding is a conjugate-linear isometric equivalence onto the continuous dual (`InnerProductSpace.toDual`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Dual.lean`).

**Projections.** In a real inner product space, a nonempty complete convex set has, for every point, a point of the set at minimal distance (`exists_norm_eq_iInf_of_complete_convex`). The comment calls that point unique; the theorem that is stated is existence. The minimizer is characterized, without completeness, by the variational inequality \(\langle u - v, w - v \rangle_{\mathbb{R}} \le 0\) for every \(w\) in the set (`norm_eq_iInf_iff_real_inner_le_zero`). No nearest-point map onto a convex set, and no nonexpansiveness statement, is provided. For a complete subspace of a real or complex inner product space, a distance minimizer exists, and it is characterized by orthogonality of the residual to the subspace. Complete subspaces have an orthogonal projection, which is continuous and linear; the space is then the topological direct sum of the subspace and its orthogonal. The adjoint of the inclusion of a complete subspace is the orthogonal projection onto that subspace (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Projection/Minimal.lean`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Projection/Basic.lean`). An operator is a self-adjoint idempotent exactly when it is the orthogonal projection onto its range (`LinearMap.isSymmetricProjection_iff_eq_coe_starProjection_range`, `ContinuousLinearMap.isStarProjection_iff_eq_starProjection_range`).

The orthogonal of a submodule is closed, meets the submodule only at zero, and contains the submodule in its double orthogonal. The triple orthogonal equals the orthogonal. In a Hilbert space the double orthogonal equals the closure; the file records that completeness is essential for that equality. In a Hilbert space, a submodule is dense if and only if its orthogonal is zero (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Orthogonal.lean`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Projection/Submodule.lean`).

**Orthonormal systems.** An orthonormal family has norm-one vectors and vanishing inner products off the diagonal, and satisfies Bessel’s inequality for finite sums and for the series of squared moduli of the coefficients. An orthonormal set extends to a maximal orthonormal set (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Orthonormal.lean`).

Gram–Schmidt, indexed by a well-ordered type, subtracts the projections onto the lines already produced. It preserves span. On a linearly independent family the normalized output is orthonormal, and a basis is sent to a basis. Dropping the vectors that become zero produces an orthonormal family without a linear-independence hypothesis. Ambient completeness is not required (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/GramSchmidtOrtho.lean`).

The \(\ell^2\) sum of a family of inner product spaces is an inner product space, with inner product the sum of the coordinate inner products. It is complete when each factor is. A Hilbert basis is a linear isometric equivalence with \(\ell^2\). Its coordinate functionals are the inner products against the basis vectors, the expansion \(x = \sum_i \langle b_i, x \rangle b_i\) converges unconditionally, and
\[
\sum_i \langle x, b_i \rangle \langle b_i, y \rangle = \langle x, y \rangle.
\]
Finite linear combinations of the basis vectors are dense. On a Hilbert space, an orthonormal family whose span is dense, or whose span has trivial orthogonal, is a Hilbert basis. Every Hilbert space has a Hilbert basis, extending any prescribed orthonormal set (`exists_hilbertBasis`). A complete subspace and its orthogonal form a Hilbert sum (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/l2Space.lean`). The classification of Hilbert spaces up to unitary equivalence by the cardinality of a basis, and the characterization of separability by a countable orthonormal basis, are not stated.

The separation quotient and the uniform completion of an inner product space are inner product spaces, and the inner product extends the original one (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Completion.lean`).

**Lax–Milgram.** A bounded coercive bilinear form on a real Hilbert space is represented by a continuous linear equivalence of the space with itself, so that every continuous linear functional is \(v\mapsto B(u,v)\) for a unique \(u\) (`IsCoercive.continuousLinearEquivOfBilin`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LaxMilgram.lean`). The complex sesquilinear form with \(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}a(u,u)\ge\alpha\|u\|^2\) is not treated. Its use for weak solutions of PDE is recorded in Sections 32 and 33.

*Supporting material.* A reproducing kernel Hilbert space is a Hilbert space of functions on which point evaluations are continuous. Its kernel is positive semidefinite, the kernel functions are dense, and conversely every positive semidefinite kernel determines such a space (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Reproducing.lean`); see also Section 42.

### TauCeti

Parseval's identity is stated in norm-square form \(\|x\|^2=\sum_i|\langle b_i,x\rangle|^2\) (`HilbertBasis.tsum_norm_sq_inner`, [Parseval](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Parseval.lean)). The existence-and-uniqueness form of the real Lax–Milgram theorem, for an arbitrary continuous linear functional, with a continuous linear solution map on the dual, is packaged in `IsCoercive.existsUnique_forall_eq` and `IsCoercive.solutionOfFunctionalCLM` ([Lax–Milgram](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LaxMilgram.lean)).

**Lions' representation theorem.** If \(j\colon\Phi\to H\) maps a vector space of test vectors into a Hilbert space, \(E(\varphi,\cdot)\) is continuous on \(H\) for each \(\varphi\), and \(c\,p(\varphi)^2\le E(\varphi,j\varphi)\) for a function \(p\) dominating \(\|j\varphi\|\), then every functional on \(\Phi\) bounded by \(p\) is \(\varphi\mapsto E(\varphi,u)\) for some \(u\in H\), without uniqueness ([Lions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Lions.lean)). It is applied to parabolic equations in Gelfand triples. TauCeti's "Stampacchia lemma" is the level-set iteration lemma, not the variational inequality of entry 15.44.

Partly covered: 15.38 (existence and the variational characterization in real spaces; uniqueness as a stated theorem and nonexpansiveness are absent), 15.42 (existence of a Hilbert basis and the isometry with \(\ell^2(I)\); classification by cardinality is not stated), 15.70 (Gram–Schmidt; the separability characterization and the isomorphism with \(\ell^2\) are not stated), 15.43 (real bilinear case; the complex sesquilinear form is absent). ## Adjoints, compactness, and the Fredholm alternative

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS15.md#adjoints-compactness-and-the-fredholm-alternative).

### Mathlib

**Hilbert adjoints.** The adjoint is defined for continuous linear maps between Hilbert spaces, so both the domain and the codomain are complete. It is a conjugate-linear isometric equivalence from the operators \(E \to F\) onto the operators \(F \to E\), characterized by \(\langle A^\dagger y, x \rangle = \langle y, A x \rangle\), involutive, reversing composition, and preserving the operator norm. The orthogonal of the range is the kernel of the adjoint. The orthogonal of the kernel is the closure of the range of the adjoint. A continuous endomorphism is self-adjoint if and only if it equals its adjoint, if and only if it is symmetric. On a Hilbert space, an everywhere-defined symmetric linear operator is continuous; this is the Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem, proved from the sequential closed graph theorem (`LinearMap.IsSymmetric.continuous`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Adjoint.lean`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Symmetric.lean`). In finite dimensions the algebraic adjoint satisfies the same inner-product identity, and the orthogonal of the kernel equals the range of the adjoint exactly. The star operation on continuous endomorphisms of a Hilbert space is the adjoint, and \(\|A^\dagger A\| = \|A\|^2\); the \(C^\star\)-algebra structure in the complex case is recorded with Section 23.

**Compact operators.** A map from a pointed topological space is a compact operator when the preimage of some compact set is a neighbourhood of zero. If the codomain is Hausdorff, this is equivalent to the closure of the image of some neighbourhood of zero being compact; for a continuous action of the scalars, images of balls then have compact closure. The zero map is compact. Compactness passes to pre- and post-composition with continuous linear maps. A continuous linear map into, or out of, a locally compact additive topological group is compact. The restriction of a compact operator to a closed invariant submodule is compact. From a seminormed space into a complete Hausdorff uniform topological vector space, the compact semilinear operators form a closed set, so a limit of compact operators is compact (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Compact/Basic.lean`). On a Hausdorff topological vector space over a complete nontrivially normed field, compactness of the identity implies finite dimensionality; if the scalar field is locally compact, the identity is compact if and only if the space is finite-dimensional (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Compact/FiniteDimension.lean`).

**Fredholm alternative.** The Fredholm alternative is stated for a compact endomorphism of a normed space over an arbitrary nontrivially normed field. If \(\mu \ne 0\) is not an eigenvalue, then \(T - \mu \mathrm{id}\) is antilipschitz, without completeness of the space. If the space is complete and \(\mu \ne 0\), then either \(\mu\) is an eigenvalue or \(\mu\) lies in the resolvent set, and the nonzero spectrum consists exactly of the nonzero eigenvalues (`IsCompactOperator.hasEigenvalue_or_mem_resolventSet`, `Analysis/Normed/Operator/Compact/FredholmAlternative.lean`).

**Compact self-adjoint operators.** For a compact self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space, eigenvalues are real, eigenspaces are mutually orthogonal, the eigenspaces have trivial joint orthogonal complement, and the eigenspaces at nonzero eigenvalues are finite-dimensional (`ContinuousLinearMap.orthogonalComplement_iSup_eigenspaces_eq_bot`, `ContinuousLinearMap.finite_dimensional_eigenspace`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Spectrum.lean`). The same file gives the finite-dimensional diagonalization with eigenvalues listed in decreasing order.

**Fredholm operators.** Fredholm operators are continuous linear maps between modules over a nontrivially normed field, carrying topologies; they need not be normed or complete. The definition asks that the map be strict, that the range be closed and of finite codimension, and that the kernel be finite-dimensional and topologically complemented (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Fredholm/Basic.lean`). When the scalar field is complete and both spaces are Hausdorff topological vector spaces, four conditions are equivalent: the definition; existence of a continuous quasi-inverse; an isomorphism, induced by the operator, between closed finite-codimension subspaces; and a Fredholm package. Under those hypotheses the range of a Fredholm operator is topologically complemented. The index is the difference of the dimensions of kernel and cokernel, and the index of a composition is the sum of the indices (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Fredholm/Basic.lean`, `Algebra/Module/LinearMap/Index.lean`). Openness of the Fredholm operators in the operator-norm topology and local constancy of the index are proved for continuous linear maps from a Banach space into a normed space, over a complete nontrivially normed field; completeness of the codomain is not among the variables, although the comments say “two Banach spaces” (`Analysis/Normed/Operator/Fredholm/Open.lean`, `Analysis/Normed/Operator/BoundedLinearMaps.lean`). An analytic choice of quasi-inverse near a given Fredholm operator is listed there as a TODO. The Fredholm index theory belongs to [Section 21](STRUCTURE21.md).

### TauCeti

**Banach adjoints and the closed range theorem.** For \(T\colon E\to F\) between normed spaces with Banach adjoint \(T^*\) (precomposition, `ContinuousLinearMap.precomp`), \((ST)^*=T^*S^*\) and, over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\), \(\|T^*\|=\|T\|\) (`ContinuousLinearMap.precomp_comp`, `ContinuousLinearMap.norm_precomp`, [functoriality](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Dual/Functoriality.lean)). Moreover \(\ker T^*=(\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}T)^\perp\), \(\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}T^*\subseteq(\ker T)^\perp\), and \(\ker T={}^\perp(\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}T^*)\) when the dual separates points of \(F\). Between Banach spaces, \(\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}T\) is closed if and only if \(\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}T^*\) is closed, and then \(\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}T^*=(\ker T)^\perp\); the key step is that a map whose adjoint is bounded below is surjective, which needs only a complete domain (`ContinuousLinearMap.isClosed_range_precomp_iff`, `ContinuousLinearMap.range_precomp_eq_orthogonalBilin_ker`, `ContinuousLinearMap.surjective_of_norm_le_norm_comp`, [Banach adjoint](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Dual/Adjoint.lean)). The inclusion of finitely supported sequences into \(\ell^2\) shows that completeness of the domain cannot be dropped ([counterexample](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Module/Dual/ClosedRangeCounterexample.lean)). The identity \(\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}T=(\ker T^*)_\perp\) for closed range and the converse “\(T\) surjective implies \(T^*\) bounded below” are not stated. For Hilbert spaces, the range of an operator is closed if and only if the range of its adjoint is closed; in that case \(\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}T^*=(\ker T)^\perp\), and a closed-range operator restricts to an isomorphism of \((\ker T)^\perp\) onto its range (`ContinuousLinearMap.isClosed_range_adjoint_iff`, `ContinuousLinearMap.orthogonalKerEquivRange`, [adjoint closed-range theorem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/Adjoint.lean)).

**Weakly compact operators.** For real or complex normed spaces, a bounded operator is weakly compact when the image of the unit ball is relatively weakly compact. Weakly compact operators are stable under sums, scalar multiples, and composition with bounded operators; compact operators are weakly compact; the identity is weakly compact exactly on a reflexive space. Gantmacher's criterion (\(T\) weakly compact iff \(T^{**}\) takes values in \(J_F(F)\)) and Gantmacher's theorem (for Banach \(F\), \(T\) weakly compact iff \(T^*\) is) are proved (`ContinuousLinearMap.isWeaklyCompact_iff_range_subset`, `ContinuousLinearMap.isWeaklyCompact_precomp_iff`, [weakly compact operators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/WeaklyCompact.lean)). The Davis–Figiel–Johnson–Pełczyński factorization is absent.

**Compact operators.** Over a locally compact nontrivially normed field, a bounded operator of finite rank is compact, and operator-norm limits of finite-rank operators into a Banach space are compact (`LinearMap.HasFiniteRange.isCompactOperator`, [finite rank](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/Compact/FiniteRank.lean)). The approximation numbers \(a_n(T)=\inf\{\|T-R\|:\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}R\le n\}\) are defined for bounded operators between seminormed spaces ([approximation numbers](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/Basic.lean)). A compact operator into an inner product space has \(a_n(T)\to0\), so it is a norm limit of finite-rank operators, and an operator into a Hilbert space is compact exactly when \(a_n(T)\to0\) (`IsCompactOperator.tendsto_singularValue`, `ContinuousLinearMap.isCompactOperator_iff_tendsto_singularValue`, [approximation and compactness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/Compact.lean)). Between Hilbert spaces \(a_n(T^\dagger)=a_n(T)\) (`ContinuousLinearMap.singularValue_adjoint`, [adjoint invariance](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/OperatorIdeal/ApproximationNumber/Adjoint.lean)); together with the previous result this yields Schauder's theorem for Hilbert spaces, but the statement is not recorded, and nothing is proved for Banach adjoints.

**Riesz–Schauder theory.** For a compact operator \(K\) on a Banach space, the kernel of \(1-K\) is finite-dimensional, the range is closed, and the cokernel is finite-dimensional, by Riesz's argument on the chain of ranges of the powers of \(1-K\) ([Riesz theory](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/Compact/RieszTheory.lean)). Hence \(1-K\) is Fredholm of index zero, so it is injective exactly when it is surjective, and adding a compact operator to a Fredholm operator preserves Fredholmness and the index (`ContinuousLinearMap.index_one_sub_eq_zero`, [compact perturbations](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/CompactPerturbation.lean), [finite-rank perturbations](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/FiniteRank.lean)). A compact operator on a normed space over a complete nontrivially normed field has finite-dimensional eigenspaces and finite-dimensional finite stages of generalized eigenspaces at every nonzero scalar ([eigenspaces](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Operator/Compact/Eigenspace.lean)). On the Hilbert-space side, the Fredholm alternative is proved for compact perturbations of a coercive bilinear form ([coercive forms](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Variational/Fredholm.lean)).

For real or complex Banach spaces, a continuous linear map with finite-dimensional cokernel has closed range, so Mathlib's Fredholm predicate is equivalent to finite dimensionality of kernel and cokernel (`TauCeti.isFredholm_iff_finite_ker_coker`, [Fredholm criterion](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/ClosedRange.lean)); further criteria, the \(\varepsilon\)-form of openness, and constancy of the index along continuous families are in [criteria](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/Criteria.lean), [small perturbations](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/SmallPerturbation.lean), and [continuous families](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fredholm/ContinuousFamily.lean).

**Spectral theory of compact self-adjoint operators.** A compact self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\) has a Hilbert basis consisting of eigenvectors, with no separability assumption (`ContinuousLinearMap.exists_hilbertBasis_forall_hasEigenvector`, [eigenbasis](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Spectrum.lean)). ## First spectral and fixed-point principles

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS15.md#first-spectral-and-fixed-point-principles).

### Mathlib

**Neumann series and resolvent.** In a complete normed ring, \(1-t\) is a unit when \(\|t\|<1\), with inverse the geometric series (`Units.oneSub`, `NormedRing.inverse_one_sub`, `Analysis/Normed/Ring/Units.lean`, `Analysis/SpecificLimits/Normed.lean`), and the units form an open set. In a complete normed algebra the resolvent set is open, the resolvent is differentiable on it (`spectrum.hasDerivAt_resolvent_const_left`, `Analysis/Normed/Algebra/GelfandFormula.lean`) and tends to zero at infinity, and the second resolvent identity \(R(a)-R(b)=R(a)(a-b)R(b)\) at a common point is stated (`spectrum.resolvent_sub_resolvent`, `Algebra/Algebra/Spectrum/Basic.lean`). The first resolvent identity in the spectral parameter is not stated in this form.

**Spectrum and spectral radius.** The spectrum of an element of a complete normed algebra is closed, bounded by the norm, and compact; over \(\mathbb C\) it is nonempty, and Gelfand's formula \(r(a)=\lim_n\|a^n\|^{1/n}\) holds, with \(r(a)\le\|a^n\|^{1/n}\) for every \(n\) (`spectrum.isCompact`, `spectrum.subset_closedBall_norm`, `spectrum.nonempty`, `spectrum.pow_nnnorm_pow_one_div_tendsto_nhds_spectralRadius`, `spectralRadius_le_pow_nnnorm_pow_one_div`; `Analysis/Normed/Algebra/Spectrum.lean`). These apply to bounded operators on a complex Banach space. The polynomial spectral mapping theorem holds over an algebraically closed field (`spectrum.map_polynomial_aeval_of_nonempty`, `FieldTheory/IsAlgClosed/Spectrum.lean`); the rational version is not stated.

**Self-adjoint, positive, and normal operators.** In a \(C^\star\)-algebra, in particular for bounded operators on a complex Hilbert space, a self-adjoint element has real spectrum and spectral radius equal to its norm (`IsSelfAdjoint.mem_spectrum_eq_re`, `IsSelfAdjoint.spectralRadius_eq_nnnorm`, `Analysis/CStarAlgebra/Spectrum.lean`); for a self-adjoint operator on a real or complex Hilbert space the latter is also in `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Rayleigh.lean`. An element is nonnegative exactly when it is self-adjoint with spectrum in \([0,\infty)\) (`nonneg_iff_isSelfAdjoint_and_quasispectrumRestricts`), and positivity of operators in the inner-product sense agrees with this order. The continuous functional calculus for normal elements is an isometric unital star homomorphism from \(C(\sigma(a))\) whose range is the closed star subalgebra generated by \(a\) (`cfcHom`, `range_cfcHom`, `Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Basic.lean`, `Analysis/CStarAlgebra/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Range.lean`). The positive square root is `CFC.sqrt`, unique among positive square roots (`CFC.sqrt_unique`, `Analysis/SpecialFunctions/ContinuousFunctionalCalculus/Rpow/Basic.lean`).

The von Neumann mean ergodic theorem, for a continuous linear contraction on a Hilbert space, is recorded in Section 10; the Banach and Schauder fixed-point theorems are in Section 35.

### TauCeti

**Modulus and polar decomposition.** For a bounded operator \(T\) between Hilbert spaces over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\), the modulus \(|T|=\sqrt{T^\dagger T}\) is defined through the continuous functional calculus, with \(\||T|x\|=\|Tx\|\) ([modulus](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Modulus.lean)). The polar factor \(U\) is a partial isometry with \(U|T|=T\), initial space \(\overline{\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}|T|}=(\ker T)^\perp\) and final space \(\overline{\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}T}\), and it is the unique bounded operator \(V\) with \(V|T|=T\) vanishing on \(\ker T\) (`ContinuousLinearMap.polarFactor`, `ContinuousLinearMap.polarFactor_comp_modulus`, [polar decomposition](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Polar/Bounded.lean)).

**Scalar spectral measures.** For a normal bounded operator \(a\) on a complex Hilbert space and \(\xi\in H\), the functional \(f\mapsto\langle\xi,f(a)\xi\rangle\) on \(C(\sigma(a))\) is integration against a unique finite Borel measure \(\mu_\xi\) of total mass \(\|\xi\|^2\), and \(\int|f|^2\,d\mu_\xi=\|f(a)\xi\|^2\) (`ContinuousLinearMap.diagMeasure`, [diagonal measure](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/BorelCalculus/DiagonalMeasure.lean)). The projection-valued measure and the bounded Borel functional calculus are not constructed. The spectral theorem for normal operators is proved in finite dimension only ([normal operators](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/NormalOperator.lean)).
