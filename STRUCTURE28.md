## Haar measure, convolution, and homogeneous spaces

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS28.md#haar-measure-convolution-and-homogeneous-spaces).

### Mathlib

**Existence of Haar measure.**

On a locally compact Hausdorff topological group, a left Haar measure exists (`MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/Basic.lean`). The construction follows Gleason’s account of the covering argument. For compact \(K\) and a neighborhood \(V\) of the identity, the index \((K:V)\) is the least number of left translates of \(V\) needed to cover \(K\). Relative to a fixed compact set \(K_0\) with nonempty interior, a cluster point of the functions \(K\mapsto(K:U)/(K_0:U)\), supplied by Tychonoff compactness as \(U\) shrinks to the identity, produces a content. Convergence of the entire family of ratios is not asserted. That content extends to a measure, which is then scaled so that \(K_0\) has measure \(1\).

The resulting measure `haarMeasure K₀` is left-invariant and regular. It gives finite mass to compact sets and positive mass to nonempty open sets, which is the library’s definition of a Haar measure. A choice of such a \(K_0\) gives a Haar measure `haar` on any locally compact Hausdorff group. The same statements hold, additively, for locally compact Hausdorff additive groups. On a second-countable group the measure is \(\sigma\)-finite. A Haar measure in this sense is automatically regular and inner regular when the group is second-countable and locally compact. Textbooks often impose regularity separately on groups that are not second-countable; the library makes the same distinction.

Steinhaus’ theorem: if \(\mu\) is an inner regular Haar measure and \(E\) is measurable with positive measure, then \(E E^{-1}\) is a neighborhood of the identity. If one assumes only inner regularity for sets of finite measure, the same conclusion holds when \(E\) itself has finite positive measure. The theorem is false for a merely regular Haar measure without an inner-regularity assumption, and the file records a counterexample of that shape.

On a finite-dimensional real vector space, a basis determines a Haar measure giving mass \(1\) to the parallelepiped it spans (`MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/OfBasis.lean`). On a finite-dimensional real inner product space the canonical volume is that measure for an orthonormal basis, and it is the measure of the `MeasureSpace` instance (`MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/InnerProductSpace.lean`). The pushforward of Haar measure on a finite-dimensional vector space by a surjective continuous linear map is an extended-nonnegative scalar multiple of Haar measure on the target. The factor includes the total Haar mass of the kernel and can be infinite (in particular for a nontrivial kernel over \(\mathbb R\)); the pushforward need not itself be a locally finite Haar measure. A property holds almost everywhere if it holds almost everywhere on the translates of a fixed subspace (`MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/Disintegration.lean`).

**Uniqueness.**

Write \(c(\mu',\mu)\) for the nonnegative scalar `haarScalarFactor`. It is strictly positive when both measures are Haar measures. On a locally compact group, two measures that are finite on compact sets, one of them left-invariant and the other a Haar measure, give the same integral to every continuous compactly supported function, up to this scalar. They give the same measure to every set whose closure is compact, up to the same scalar. They give the same measure to every open set, up to the same scalar (`MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/Unique.lean`).

If both measures are inner regular, or both are regular, and both are finite on compact sets, with one a Haar measure and the other only assumed left-invariant, then they coincide as measures up to \(c(\mu',\mu)\). On a second-countable locally compact group the regularity is automatic, so any two such measures coincide up to the scalar. On a compact group, left invariance and finiteness on compact sets already give the same conclusion. Two Haar probability measures are equal.

A second form of uniqueness assumes second-countability in place of finiteness on compact sets. On a second-countable locally compact group, every \(\sigma\)-finite left-invariant measure \(\mu\) satisfies \(\mu=(\mu K_0)\cdot(\text{haarMeasure }K_0)\). In particular \(\mu\) equals that Haar measure if and only if \(\mu(K_0)=1\). Such a \(\sigma\)-finite left-invariant measure is absolutely continuous with respect to any Haar measure (`MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/Basic.lean`).

**The modular character.**

Let \(G\) be locally compact and let \(\mu\) be an inner regular left Haar measure. Right multiplication by \(g\) pushes \(\mu\) forward to another left Haar measure. By uniqueness there is a positive real \(\Delta(g)\) with
\[
\mu(A g^{-1})=\Delta(g)\,\mu(A)
\]
for every measurable \(A\): the pushforward of \(\mu\) under \(x\mapsto xg\) equals \(\Delta(g)\cdot\mu\). The resulting map \(\Delta:G\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^\times\) is a group homomorphism, the modular character, and it does not depend on the choice of inner regular Haar measure (`MeasureTheory/Group/ModularCharacter.lean`). The module states explicitly that continuity of \(\Delta\) is still to be proved. Under the normalization \(\int_Gf(xy)\,dx=\Delta_G(y)^{-1}\int_Gf(x)\,dx\) used in the synopsis, `modularCharacter` is the reciprocal \(\Delta_G^{-1}\).

A group acting continuously by additive automorphisms of a locally compact abelian group has an analogous distributive character, recording how the action scales additive Haar measure (`MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/DistribChar.lean`). A topological-group automorphism of a locally compact group scales Haar measure by a positive constant (`MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/MulEquivHaarChar.lean`).

Right Haar measure is obtained from left Haar measure by inversion: \(\mu^{\mathrm{inv}}(A)=\mu(A^{-1})\). The construction `Measure.inv` and the instance `Measure.inv.instIsMulRightInvariant` implement the change from left to right invariance (`MeasureTheory/Group/Measure.lean`); inversion also preserves the topological regularity properties because it is a homeomorphism. Right invariance of a left Haar measure is the reading of the identity in which \(\Delta\) is identically \(1\). The quotient file notes that a group carrying a Haar measure invariant on both sides is called unimodular.

**Quotients and extensions.**

Let \(G\) be a Polish topological group and let \(\Gamma\) be a subgroup such that \(G/\Gamma\) is Hausdorff and second-countable. Suppose a measure \(\nu\) on \(G\) is left-invariant and the right action of \(\Gamma\) admits a fundamental domain. A measure \(\mu\) on the coset space that satisfies the projection identity — for a fundamental domain \(t\) and a measurable set \(U\subseteq G/\Gamma\), \(\mu(U)=\nu(\pi^{-1}(U)\cap t)\) — is then invariant under the left translation action of \(G\). If \(\Gamma\) is normal, \(\mu\) is left-invariant. The module docstring describes \(\nu\) as invariant on both sides; these two conclusions use left invariance of \(\nu\), together with the fundamental domain (`MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/Quotient.lean`).

If \(\Gamma\) is countable and normal, \(\nu\) is \(\sigma\)-finite and right-invariant as well as left-invariant, a fundamental domain exists, and \(\mu\) is a finite left-invariant measure whose total mass equals the covolume of \(\Gamma\), then the projection identity holds.

If, with \(G\) still Polish and \(G/\Gamma\) Hausdorff and second-countable, \(G\) is locally compact, \(\Gamma\) is countable and normal, \(\nu\) is a right-invariant Haar measure, a fundamental domain exists, and \(\mu\) is a finite measure satisfying the projection identity, then \(\mu\) is a Haar measure. The source leaves open whether finiteness of \(\mu\) can be removed. Countability of \(\Gamma\) is the hypothesis used; the docstring says discrete.

The unfolding trick holds for a countable subgroup \(\Gamma\) of a topological group \(G\) whose right action has a fundamental domain \(\mathcal F\) for a right-invariant measure \(\mu\): for integrable \(f\), \(\int_Gf\,d\mu=\int_{G/\Gamma}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(x\gamma)\,d\mu_{\mathcal F}\), where \(\mu_{\mathcal F}\) is the pushforward of \(\mu|_{\mathcal F}\), and the same holds after multiplying \(f\) by an essentially bounded function lifted from the quotient (`QuotientGroup.integral_eq_integral_automorphize`, `QuotientGroup.integral_mul_eq_integral_automorphize_mul`).

In the other direction, a short exact sequence \(1\to A\to B\to C\to 1\) of topological groups, with \(B\) locally compact Hausdorff, produces a Haar measure on \(B\) by integrating a Haar measure on \(A\) along the fibers and then a Haar measure on \(C\). The construction is the Riesz measure of that integrated functional (`MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/Extension.lean`).

**Convolution.**

Convolution of functions is defined on an additive group equipped with a Haar measure. For a continuous bilinear multiplication \(L\) of the codomains,
\[
(f\star_L g)(x)=\int L\bigl(f(t),g(x-t)\bigr)\,d\mu(t),
\]
when the integral exists. The usual applications are scalar multiplication and the product in a normed algebra. Existence everywhere, or at a point, is a separate predicate. The calculus of the convolution — continuity, support in the sum of the supports, differentiation under the integral — is developed when one factor has compact support and the other is locally integrable. On Schwartz space the convolution is available by the Fourier-side definition of Section 27. Sources: `Analysis/Convolution.lean`.

Convolution of measures on a measurable monoid is the pushforward of the product measure under multiplication. It is associative when multiplication is measurable in the sense required by the file, the Dirac mass at the identity is a two-sided unit, and the convolution of two \(s\)-finite measures is \(s\)-finite (`MeasureTheory/Group/Convolution.lean`). Integrability of a function against a convolution of measures is expressed by an integral over the product (`MeasureTheory/Group/IntegralConvolution.lean`).

**Translation on \(L^p\).** If a topological monoid acts continuously on a space by maps preserving a locally finite measure that is inner regular on sets of finite measure, the induced action on \(L^p\), \(1\leq p<\infty\), by \(f\mapsto f(c\,\cdot)\) is jointly continuous (`MeasureTheory.Lp.instContinuousSMulDomMulAct`, `MeasureTheory/Function/LpSpace/DomAct/Continuous.lean`). For a locally compact group with Haar measure this gives strong continuity of translation on \(L^p(G)\) by isometries. The norm \(\Delta_G(y)^{-1/p}\) of right translation is not stated.

**Unimodular cases.** Counting measure on a group is both left and right invariant (`MeasureTheory/Group/Measure.lean`), which is the discrete case of unimodularity. The abelian case is immediate from the definitions but is not stated as a theorem, and nothing is recorded for Lie groups.

### TauCeti

On a compact group, normalized Haar measure `TauCeti.haarProb` is left-invariant, right-invariant and inversion-invariant, so compact groups are unimodular ([compact Haar measure](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/Haar.lean)). ## Unitary representations and positive definite functions

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS28.md#unitary-representations-and-positive-definite-functions).

### Mathlib

Mathlib's `ContRepresentation` supplies continuous representations on topological vector spaces, used by the TauCeti files below. No unitary-representation theory of locally compact groups, group \(C^*\)-algebra, or Gelfand–Raikov theorem is recorded.

### TauCeti

**Integrated forms.** For a strongly continuous, uniformly bounded representation \(\pi\) of an additive topological group on a normed space and a measure \(\mu\), the integrated form \(\pi(f)v=\int f(g)\,\pi(g)v\,d\mu(g)\) is a continuous linear map \(L^1(G,\mu)\to\operatorname{End}(E)\) with \(\|\pi(f)\|\leq C\|f\|_1\) when \(\|\pi(g)\|\leq C\). Translating the weight composes with \(\pi\), convolution of weights becomes composition (`ContRepresentation.integratedOperatorL1_convolution`), and, for unitary \(\pi\) and inversion-invariant \(\mu\), adjoints correspond to \(f^*(g)=\overline{f(-g)}\) ([integrated form](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Continuous/Integrated/Basic.lean)). The closed star algebra generated by the integrated operators is constructed ([integrated algebra](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Continuous/Integrated/Algebra.lean)). The converse, that every nondegenerate representation of \(L^1(G)\) arises this way, is not stated.

**Schur's lemma and decomposition.** Schur's lemma is proved for continuous representations; the scalar half requires a finite-dimensional representation over an algebraically closed field ([Schur](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Continuous/Schur.lean)). For a unitary representation on an inner product space, the orthogonal complement of an invariant subspace with orthogonal projection is invariant, and the orthogonal projection is an intertwiner (`ContRepresentation.IsUnitary.orthogonal_mem_invtSubmodule`) ([invariant complements](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Continuous/InvariantComplement.lean)). Finite-dimensional unitary representations are orthogonal sums of irreducibles ([orthogonal decomposition](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Continuous/OrthogonalDecomposition.lean)). Decomposition into cyclic representations in infinite dimension is not stated.

**Positive-definite functions.** On an additive monoid with involution, `TauCeti.IsPositiveDefinite` is the Berg–Christensen–Ressel notion. Its value at \(0\) is real and nonnegative, it is conjugate-symmetric in the involution, it satisfies the \(2\times2\) Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and the class is closed under sums, nonnegative multiples and pointwise products ([positive-definite functions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PositiveDefinite/Basic.lean)). Positive-definite functions on abstract additive commutative groups have a unitary GNS representation in which the function is the matrix coefficient of the vector at \(0\) ([GNS](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PositiveDefinite/Function/GNS.lean)). Uniqueness of the cyclic representation is not stated. Bochner's theorem is proved on finite-dimensional real inner-product spaces: every continuous positive-definite complex function is the Fourier transform of a unique finite positive measure ([Bochner representation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Bochner/BochnerTheorem.lean)). The locally compact abelian case is under the next heading.

**Regular representations.** For a compact group, left and right translation on \(L^2(G,\text{haarProb})\) are unitary and strongly continuous (`TauCeti.leftRegularLp`, `TauCeti.rightRegularLp`) ([regular representations](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/RegularRepresentation.lean), [biregular representation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/BiregularRepresentation.lean)).

## Locally compact abelian groups

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS28.md#locally-compact-abelian-groups).

### Mathlib

**The dual group.** Mathlib defines the Pontryagin dual of a topological group as the continuous homomorphisms into the circle with the compact-open topology, with its group structure and functoriality (`Topology/Algebra/PontryaginDual.lean`). The dual of a locally compact group is locally compact; the dual of a compact group is discrete; and the dual of a discrete compact group is finite. That the dual of a discrete group is compact, and the converse implications, are not stated. The duality theorem is stated only for finite abelian groups (see the next heading).

**The circle and the torus.**

The additive circle \(\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z}\), with Haar measure of total mass \(1\), has characters \(e^{2\pi i n x/T}\). They are orthonormal in \(L^2\), their span is dense in the continuous functions and in \(L^p\) for \(1\leq p<\infty\), and they form a Hilbert basis of \(L^2\). The Fourier series of an \(L^2\) function converges to it in \(L^2\), and Parseval’s identity holds. Absolute summability of the coefficients of a continuous function gives uniform convergence. The same package — orthonormal Hilbert basis indexed by \(\mathbb{Z}^d\), \(L^2\) convergence, Parseval, density in \(C\) and in \(L^p\) for \(p<\infty\), and uniform convergence under summable coefficients — holds for the characters of the unit torus \((\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^d\) (`Analysis/Fourier/AddCircle.lean`, `Analysis/Fourier/AddCircleMulti.lean`). Details of the coefficient formulae are in Section 27.

Mathlib itself defines the Pontryagin dual of a topological group, the continuous homomorphisms into the circle with the compact-open topology, with its group structure and functoriality (`Topology/Algebra/PontryaginDual.lean`); the duality theorem is stated only for finite abelian groups.

### TauCeti

**Dual pairs.** The circle group is identified with the dual of \(\mathbb Z\) as a topological group (`TauCeti.circleEquivPontryaginDualInt`), and the dual of a second-countable locally compact group is Polish ([Pontryagin dual](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Topology/Algebra/PontryaginDual.lean)). The continuous characters of the circle \(\mathbb R/T\mathbb Z\) are exactly the Fourier monomials, giving a group isomorphism of \(\mathbb Z\) with the dual of the circle (`TauCeti.fourierPontryaginDualEquiv`) ([circle characters](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/AddCircle.lean)). The finite-dimensional irreducible continuous representations of the circle are exactly the Fourier monomials ([circle representations](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/Circle.lean)). The dual of \(\mathbb R^d\) is not identified.

**Fourier–Stieltjes transforms.** Evaluation of characters at a fixed group element is continuous on the dual and integrable against every finite measure ([evaluation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Pontryagin/DualEval.lean)). The Fourier–Stieltjes transform of a finite measure on a Pontryagin dual is defined (`MeasureTheory.FiniteMeasure.pontryaginMeasureTransform`), with its continuity and positive definiteness ([Fourier–Stieltjes transform](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Pontryagin/Measure.lean), [continuity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Pontryagin/Continuity.lean)). It is injective on finite inner regular measures on the dual of any locally compact abelian group, and on all finite measures when the dual is Polish ([uniqueness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Pontryagin/Uniqueness.lean)). The Riemann–Lebesgue lemma and injectivity on \(L^1(G)\) are not stated for general \(G\).

**Spectral theory and Bochner.** For a strongly continuous unitary representation of an abelian group and a complete star algebra containing its integrated operators, each character of the algebra that is nonzero on some integrated operator determines a unique continuous group character ([characters of the integrated algebra](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Continuous/Pontryagin/Basic.lean), [continuity](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Continuous/Pontryagin/Continuity.lean)). For a strongly continuous unitary representation \(\pi\) of a locally compact abelian group on a complex Hilbert space and \(\xi\in H\), the matrix coefficient \(g\mapsto\langle\xi,\pi(g)\xi\rangle\) is the Fourier–Stieltjes transform of a finite inner regular positive measure on the dual, with no second countability or separability assumption (`ContRepresentation.exists_pontryaginMeasureTransform_eq_inner`). This is the cyclic form of the Stone–Naimark–Ambrose–Godement theorem; no projection-valued measure is constructed ([cyclic spectral theorem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Pontryagin/StronglyContinuous.lean)).

Combined with the GNS construction this gives Bochner's theorem on every locally compact abelian group: a positive-definite function continuous at \(0\) is the Fourier–Stieltjes transform of a unique finite inner regular measure on the dual, conversely every such transform is continuous and positive definite, and when the dual is Polish (for example when \(G\) is second countable) the representing measures are arbitrary finite measures ([Bochner on LCA groups](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Bochner/LocallyCompactGroup.lean)). The discrete case, and Herglotz's theorem for \(\mathbb Z\) by Fejér means, are separate files ([discrete groups](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Bochner/DiscreteGroup.lean), [Herglotz](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Bochner/Herglotz.lean)). On the semigroup \(\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}\times V\), the Berg–Christensen–Ressel theorem is proved: a bounded continuous positive-definite function is the Laplace–Fourier transform of a unique finite measure ([Berg–Christensen–Ressel](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PositiveDefinite/SemigroupGroup/FourierLaplace/Existence.lean)).

## Compact and finite groups

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS28.md#compact-and-finite-groups).

### Mathlib

**Finite abelian groups.**

Characters of a finite abelian group, with values in \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), are orthogonal. The expectation of a character is \(1\) if the character is trivial and is \(0\) otherwise. The normalized inner product of two characters is \(1\) if they are equal and is \(0\) otherwise. The characters are linearly independent, so there are at most as many as there are group elements (`Analysis/Fourier/FiniteAbelian/Orthogonality.lean`).

For a finite abelian group \(A\), the number of complex characters equals the order of \(A\). The characters form a basis of the functions \(A\to\mathbb{C}\). The evaluation pairing embeds \(A\) into the character group of its character group, and that embedding is an isomorphism of groups: this is Pontryagin duality for finite abelian groups. Equivalently, \(\sum_\psi\psi(a)\) equals \(|A|\) if \(a=0\) and equals \(0\) otherwise. The group \(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\) is isomorphic to its own character group, by an isomorphism that is not claimed to be canonical (`Analysis/Fourier/FiniteAbelian/PontryaginDuality.lean`).

This duality is the finite abelian case only.

The discrete Fourier transform on \(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\), including the formula \(\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{F}\Phi)(j)=N\Phi(-j)\) and the identification of the transform of a primitive Dirichlet character with a Gauss sum, is recorded in Section 27 (`Analysis/Fourier/ZMod.lean`).

### TauCeti

**Peter–Weyl.** The Peter–Weyl theorem is implemented for compact groups: normalized matrix coefficients of irreducible continuous unitary representations form a Hilbert basis of \(L^2(G)\) (`TauCeti.peterWeylBasis`). The file constructs a standard exhaustive family of irreducible models as well as accepting a supplied family, so exhaustiveness is not left as an unresolved assumption ([Peter–Weyl](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/PeterWeyl.lean)). Representative functions are uniformly dense in \(C(G)\), and on a compact Hausdorff group finite-dimensional continuous representations separate points (`TauCeti.exists_contRepresentation_apply_ne`) ([representative functions](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/RepresentativeDensity.lean)). Convolution eigenspaces carry finite-dimensional representations, the step that places convolutions in the uniform closure of the representative functions ([eigenspace representations](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/EigenspaceRepresentation.lean)). For the circle, the basis is identified with Mathlib's Fourier basis, with the appropriate indexing convention ([circle comparison](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/FourierPeterWeyl.lean)).

**Schur orthogonality and the Plancherel decomposition.** Schur orthogonality holds for matrix coefficients of irreducible unitary representations, with the factor \(1/d_\pi\) ([Schur orthogonality](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/SchurOrthogonality.lean), [orthonormal coefficients](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/Orthonormal.lean), [matrix coefficients](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/MatrixCoefficient.lean)). Over an algebraically closed field, the normalized trace coefficients identify \(L^2(G)\) isometrically with the Hilbert sum of the Hilbert–Schmidt endomorphism spaces of the irreducible models (`TauCeti.peterWeylTraceEquiv`). The identification is equivariant for the biregular action of \(G\times G\), which acts on each summand by \(T\mapsto\pi(g)T\pi(h)^{-1}\), and an \(L^2\) function is the \(L^2\)-convergent sum of its trace coefficients ([trace coefficients](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/TraceCoefficient/Basic.lean), [Hilbert sum](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/TraceCoefficient/HilbertSum.lean), [isometry](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/TraceCoefficient/Isometry.lean)). ## Convolution algebras, amenability, and spherical analysis

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS28.md#convolution-algebras-amenability-and-spherical-analysis).

### Mathlib

**Følner filters.**

Let a group \(G\) act on a measure space, preserving the measure in the statements that need invariance. A family of sets \(F_i\), indexed along a filter, is Følner when the sets are eventually measurable and of finite positive measure, and when for every group element \(g\) the measure of the symmetric difference of \(F_i\) and \(g\cdot F_i\), divided by the measure of \(F_i\), tends to \(0\). There is a maximal Følner filter, and a family is Følner precisely when it tends to that filter (`MeasureTheory/Group/FoelnerFilter.lean`).

If a Følner filter is nontrivial, the ultralimit of the normalized measures of \(F_i\) is a finitely additive probability, defined on measurable sets, and invariant under the group action. The file calls this conclusion amenability of the action. It also records that a general definition of amenability has not been fixed, because several notions exist for groups and for actions. The theorem presently proved is the existence of this invariant finitely additive probability.
