## Holomorphy, integration, and power series

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS40.md#holomorphy-integration-and-power-series).

### Mathlib

**Cauchy–Riemann equations and power series.** A map \(f:\mathbb{C}\to E\) into a complex normed space is complex-differentiable at \(x\) if and only if it is real-differentiable there and its real derivative satisfies \(Df(x)\,i=i\,Df(x)\,1\), which is the Cauchy–Riemann system; the complex derivative is then the complexification of the real one (`differentiableAt_complex_iff_differentiableAt_real`, `Analysis/Complex/Conformal.lean`). Conversely the real derivative of a holomorphic function is multiplication by the complex derivative (`Analysis/Complex/RealDeriv.lean`). The radius of convergence of a power series is \(\liminf_n\|p_n\|^{-1/n}\), the Cauchy–Hadamard formula (`FormalMultilinearSeries.radius_eq_liminf`, `Analysis/Analytic/RadiusLiminf.lean`). A power series converges to an analytic function on its disc of convergence, and the derivative of its sum is the sum of the derived series on the same disc (`HasFPowerSeriesOnBall.fderiv`, `Analysis/Calculus/FDeriv/Analytic.lean`).

**The Cauchy integral formula.** The integral theorems allow a countable exceptional set. Throughout, \(E\) is a complex normed space. Where a value is recovered from a circle integral, or a power series is summed, \(E\) is complete (`Analysis/Complex/CauchyIntegral.lean`).

On a closed rectangle, if \(f\) is continuous up to the boundary and real-differentiable off a countable set in the open rectangle, and if the Cauchy–Riemann defect is integrable, the integral of \(f\) over the boundary equals the integral of \(2i\,\partial f/\partial\bar z\) over the rectangle. If instead \(f\) is complex-differentiable off a countable set, the boundary integral vanishes. This is the Cauchy–Goursat theorem for a rectangle; no continuity of the derivative is assumed. The same vanishing holds for a function complex-differentiable on the whole closed rectangle, and that case does not use completeness of \(E\).

On a closed annulus \(r\le |z-c|\le R\), continuity on the closed annulus and complex-differentiability off a countable set in the open annulus make the integrals of \((z-c)^{-1}f(z)\) over the two boundary circles equal. Letting the inner radius tend to zero gives the Cauchy formula at the center: if \(f\) is continuous on the closed disc except possibly at the center, complex-differentiable off a countable set in the punctured disc, and tends to a value \(y\) at the center, then
\[
\oint_{|z-c|=R}\frac{f(z)}{z-c}\,dz = 2\pi i\, y.
\]
If \(f\) is continuous on the whole closed disc, the same formula holds with \(y=f(c)\). Consequently the integral of \(f\) itself over the circle vanishes.

For a point \(w\) inside the disc, not merely the center,
\[
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z-c|=R}\frac{f(z)}{z-w}\,dz = f(w),
\]
again with continuity on the closed disc and complex differentiability off a countable set in the open disc. The same identity holds when \(f\) is complex-differentiable on the open disc and continuous on the closure, and when it is complex-differentiable on the closed disc.

The circle integral depends holomorphically on the point \(w\) off the circle of integration. Expanding it produces a power series. Thus a function continuous on a closed disc of positive radius and complex-differentiable off a countable set in the open disc is analytic on the open disc, with coefficients given by the Cauchy integrals. Complex differentiability on an open set in \(\mathbb{C}\) is therefore equivalent to complex analyticity on that set, the derivative is again complex-differentiable, and the function is continuously differentiable of every finite order. An entire function is given by its Cauchy series on every disc, and that series has infinite radius.

At the center, the derivatives themselves are circle integrals. If \(f\) is continuous on the closed disc and complex-differentiable off a countable set inside, then
\[
\oint_{|z-c|=R}\frac{f(z)}{(z-c)^{n+1}}\,dz = \frac{2\pi i}{n!}\,f^{(n)}(c).
\]
The corresponding formula at a point other than the center is not in this file. The first derivative at a general interior point is recovered after the removable-singularity theorem, as the circle integral of \((z-w)^{-2}f(z)\) (`Analysis/Complex/RemovableSingularity.lean`).

On any open disc on which \(f\) is complex-differentiable, with values in a complex Banach space, the Taylor series at the center converges to \(f\); the same holds on a disc of infinite radius, and therefore for every entire function at every center (`Analysis/Complex/TaylorSeries.lean`).

Cauchy’s estimates: if \(|f|\le C\) on the circle of radius \(R\) about \(c\), and \(f\) is holomorphic inside and continuous up to the boundary, then \(\|f^{(n)}(c)\|\le n!\,C/R^n\). The first-derivative bound does not need completeness of the codomain; the higher-derivative bound does (`norm_iteratedDeriv_le_of_forall_mem_sphere_norm_le`, `Analysis/Complex/Liouville.lean`).

**Primitives on discs and Morera’s theorem.** A function is conservative on a set when its integrals over rectangles contained in the set vanish, and exact when it is the complex derivative of some map on that set. Every complex-differentiable function is conservative. On a disc, a continuous conservative function is exact; this is Morera’s theorem for a disc, and it uses completeness of the codomain. In particular, a holomorphic function on a disc has a primitive, and an entire function has a primitive on \(\mathbb{C}\). The extension to simply connected domains is marked in the file as still to be done (`Analysis/Complex/HasPrimitives.lean`).

**Locally uniform limits.** On an open set in the plane, a locally uniform limit of holomorphic functions with values in a complex Banach space is holomorphic, and the derivatives converge locally uniformly to the derivative of the limit. A series of holomorphic functions dominated on the set by a summable sequence of constants may be differentiated termwise (`Analysis/Complex/LocallyUniformLimit.lean`).

**Parameter integrals.** Differentiation under the integral sign is proved over any nontrivially normed field, in particular \(\mathbb{C}\): if \(F(x,\cdot)\) is integrable, \(F(\cdot,a)\) is differentiable near \(x_0\) for almost every \(a\), and the derivatives are bounded near \(x_0\) by one integrable function, then \(x\mapsto\int F(x,a)\,d\mu(a)\) is differentiable at \(x_0\) with derivative \(\int\partial_xF(x_0,a)\,d\mu(a)\) (`hasDerivAt_integral_of_dominated_loc_of_deriv_le`, `Analysis/Calculus/ParametricIntegral.lean`). Holomorphy of the integral from domination of the integrand alone, without a bound on the derivative, is not stated.

**Abel’s limit theorem.** If a power series of radius \(1\) converges at \(1\), its sum is the limit of the function as \(z\to1\) within a Stolz angle (`Analysis/Complex/AbelLimit.lean`).

A continuous function whose integrals over closed piecewise-\(C^1\) curves in an open set \(U\) vanish has a primitive on \(U\) (`isExactOn_of_forall_closed_integral_eq_zero`). Hence every holomorphic function has a primitive on an open set in which every closed curve is null-homologous, in particular on an open set without holes (every component of \(\mathbb C\setminus U\) unbounded) (`isExactOn_of_forall_isNullHomologous`, `isExactOn_of_filledHull_subset`, [primitives](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Contour/Primitive.lean)). No primitive theorem is stated under the hypothesis `IsSimplyConnected` itself. An explicit global primitive for a holomorphic function on the upper half-plane is constructed by integration along horizontal–vertical paths ([primitive](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/UpperHalfPlane/Primitive.lean)).

Morera's theorem is stated by name: a continuous function on an open set whose rectangle integrals vanish is holomorphic (`TauCeti.morera`, [Morera](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/Morera.lean)).

The Cauchy–Pompeiu formula is proved for compactly supported \(C^1\) maps \(u:\mathbb C\to F\) into a complex Banach space: \(u\) is the Cauchy transform of \(2\partial_{\bar z}u\). Conversely the Cauchy transform \(w\mapsto(2\pi)^{-1}\int(w-z)^{-1}f(z)\,dA(z)\) of a compactly supported \(C^1\) map \(f\) satisfies \(2\partial_{\bar w}Tf=f\), so \(\pi^{-1}\int g(\zeta)/(z-\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)\) solves \(\partial_{\bar z}u=g\) ([Cauchy–Pompeiu](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Pompeiu.lean)). The version on a bounded domain with a boundary integral is not stated.

## Zeros, local mapping, and maximum principles

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS40.md#zeros-local-mapping-and-maximum-principles).

### Mathlib

**Isolated zeros and the identity theorem.** These are theorems about analytic functions of one variable on a nontrivially normed field, and they apply to holomorphic functions because complex differentiability on an open set is analyticity (`Analysis/Analytic/IsolatedZeros.lean`, `Analysis/Analytic/Uniqueness.lean`). A function analytic at a point is either identically zero on a neighborhood of the point, or else has no zero in some punctured neighborhood; in the latter case it has the form \((z-z_0)^n g(z)\) with \(g\) analytic and nonzero at \(z_0\), and the exponent is unique. On a preconnected set, two analytic functions that agree on a set accumulating at a point of the set agree throughout the set. The order of vanishing is an extended natural number, infinite exactly when the function vanishes on a neighborhood (`Analysis/Analytic/Order.lean`).

**Open mapping and inverse functions.** A function analytic at a point of a complex normed space, with values in \(\mathbb{C}\), is either eventually constant there or carries every neighborhood of the point onto a neighborhood of the value. On a preconnected set, an analytic function is either constant or open. A nonconstant complex polynomial is an open quotient map of the plane, and so are the maps \(z\mapsto z^n\) for \(n\neq 0\). A holomorphic function on a connected open set with constant real or imaginary part is constant (`Analysis/Complex/OpenMapping.lean`). If \(f\) is analytic at \(x\) with invertible derivative, its local inverse is analytic at \(f(x)\) (`Analysis/Calculus/InverseFunctionTheorem/Analytic.lean`).

**The maximum and minimum modulus principles.** The maximum modulus principle is stated for maps between complex normed spaces. If the norm attains a maximum at an interior point, the norm is constant on any closed ball about that point within the domain of differentiability, and constant on any preconnected open set on which the maximum is attained. If the codomain is strictly convex over \(\mathbb{R}\), as \(\mathbb{C}\) is, the function itself is constant. On a nonempty bounded set in a finite-dimensional complex space, a function holomorphic inside and continuous up to the closure attains its maximum modulus on the frontier, and two such functions that agree on the frontier agree on the closure. A local minimum of the norm of a holomorphic function with values in \(\mathbb{C}\) forces the function to be locally constant or to vanish at the point (`eventually_eq_or_eq_zero_of_isLocalMin_norm`, `Analysis/Complex/AbsMax.lean`).

**Liouville’s theorem and polynomials.** A bounded complex-differentiable map from a complex normed space into a complex normed space is constant; the same holds, when the domain is nontrivial, if the map tends to a finite limit at infinity. An entire function \(f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\) is \(O(|z|^n)\) at infinity if and only if it is a polynomial of degree at most \(n\) (`Differentiable.isBigO_pow_iff_exists_eq_polynomial_eval`, `Analysis/Complex/Liouville.lean`). Every nonconstant complex polynomial has a root, \(\mathbb{C}\) is algebraically closed, so complex polynomials split into linear factors, and an irreducible real polynomial has degree at most two (`Analysis/Complex/Polynomial/Basic.lean`). The Gauss–Lucas theorem places the roots of the derivative of a nonconstant complex polynomial in the convex hull of its roots (`Analysis/Complex/Polynomial/GaussLucas.lean`).

**Branches of the logarithm and of roots.** On a locally path-connected space, a continuous function \(g\) without zeros on an open simply connected set \(U\) has a continuous logarithm on \(U\) and, for every positive integer \(n\), a continuous \(n\)th root; if \(g\) takes values in the open unit disc, the root may be taken with values in the open unit disc (`Analysis/Complex/BranchLogRoot.lean`).

**Schwarz, Borel–Carathéodory, Phragmén–Lindelöf, and the three-lines theorem.** The Schwarz lemma is stated for analytic maps between complex normed spaces. If \(f\) sends the open ball of radius \(R_1\) about \(c\) into the closed ball of radius \(R_2\) about \(f(c)\), then \(\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(f(z),f(c))\le (R_2/R_1)\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(z,c)\) on the open ball, and the derivative at \(c\) has norm at most \(R_2/R_1\). If the norm of the difference quotient attains \(R_2/R_1\) at one interior point, and the codomain is strictly convex over \(\mathbb{R}\), then \(f\) is affine on the ball; the difference quotient at the center is the derivative, so equality in the derivative bound is included (`Analysis/Complex/Schwarz.lean`).

The Borel–Carathéodory theorem: if \(f\) is holomorphic on \(|z|<R\) and \(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}f\le M\) there, with \(M>0\), then \(\|f(z)\|\le 2M\|z\|/(R-\|z\|)+\|f(0)\|(R+\|z\|)/(R-\|z\|)\) for \(|z|<R\) (`Analysis/Complex/BorelCaratheodory.lean`).

The Phragmén–Lindelöf principle is proved for maps into a complex normed space on horizontal and vertical strips (growth \(O(\exp(B\exp(c|\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}z|)))\), \(c<\pi/(b-a)\)), on coordinate quadrants (growth \(O(\exp(B\|z\|^c))\), \(c<2\)), and on the right half-plane (`Analysis/Complex/PhragmenLindelof.lean`). The sectorial form is recorded in [Section 41](STRUCTURE41.md).

The Hadamard three-lines theorem: if \(f\) is bounded and continuous on the closed strip \(l\le\mathop{\mathrm{Re}} z\le u\), holomorphic inside, and bounded by \(A\) and \(B\) on the two edges, then \(\|f(z)\|\le A^{1-t}B^{t}\) with \(t=(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}} z-l)/(u-l)\) (`Analysis/Complex/Hadamard.lean`). The three-circles theorem on an annulus is not stated.

## Isolated singularities, residues, and counting

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS40.md#isolated-singularities-residues-and-counting).

### Mathlib

**Removable singularities.** If \(f\) is complex-differentiable on a punctured neighborhood of \(c\) and \(f(z)-f(c)=o\bigl((z-c)^{-1}\bigr)\), then \(f\) has a limit at \(c\), and redefining \(f\) at \(c\) to be that limit makes it complex-differentiable on the full neighborhood. Boundedness on the punctured neighborhood is enough for the same conclusion (`Analysis/Complex/RemovableSingularity.lean`).

**Meromorphic functions.** Meromorphy is developed for maps from a nontrivially normed field into a normed space over that field. A function is meromorphic at a point when, after multiplication by a high enough power of \((z-x)\), it agrees on a punctured neighborhood with a function analytic at the point. Sums, products, inverses, integer powers, and compositions on the right with analytic functions preserve meromorphy (`Analysis/Meromorphic/Basic.lean`). The order at a point lies in \(\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}\): a negative order is a pole, a positive order a zero, and order zero a nonzero finite limit (`Analysis/Meromorphic/Order.lean`). The divisor of a function meromorphic on a set is a locally finite integer-valued function, additive for products (`Analysis/Meromorphic/Divisor.lean`). Normal forms, trailing coefficients, isolated zeros, and the finite factorization \(\prod(z-u)^{d(u)}\) times a zero-free analytic function on sets with finitely many zeros and poles are in `Analysis/Meromorphic/NormalForm.lean`, `TrailingCoefficient.lean`, `IsolatedZeros.lean`, and `FactorizedRational.lean`; the logarithmic derivative turns products into sums (`LogDeriv.lean`). Essential singularities are not classified: meromorphy excludes them, and nothing is proved about a function that is not meromorphic at an isolated singularity.

**Rationality.** An entire function that is \(O(|z|^n)\) at infinity is a polynomial (`Analysis/Complex/Liouville.lean`, see the preceding section); the rationality of meromorphic functions on the Riemann sphere is not stated.

**Jensen’s formula.** If \(f\) is meromorphic on the closed disc of radius \(|R|\) about \(c\), and \(R\neq 0\), the circle average of \(\log\|f\|\) equals \(\log\) of the norm of the trailing coefficient at \(c\), plus a sum over the divisor in the closed disc; zeros and poles on the circle contribute \(\log(R/|u|)=0\) (`Analysis/Complex/JensenFormula.lean`). A function meromorphic on a closed disc is a finite Blaschke-type product of canonical factors, of modulus one on the boundary circle, times an analytic function without zeros (`Analysis/Complex/CanonicalDecomposition.lean`).

### TauCeti

## Harmonic functions and boundary values

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS40.md#harmonic-functions-and-boundary-values).

### Mathlib

**Harmonic functions and conjugates.** A function on a finite-dimensional real inner-product space is harmonic at a point when it is twice continuously differentiable and its Laplacian vanishes on a neighborhood of the point (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/Basic.lean`). The real and imaginary parts of an analytic function are harmonic, and \(\log|f|\) is harmonic where \(f\) is analytic and nonzero (`AnalyticAt.harmonicAt_re`, `AnalyticAt.harmonicAt_im`, `AnalyticAt.harmonicAt_log_norm`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/Constructions.lean`). On the complex plane, if a real-valued function is harmonic at a point, then \(\partial f/\partial x-i\,\partial f/\partial y\) is holomorphic there; on an open disc, and on the whole plane, a real-valued harmonic function is the real part of a holomorphic function, so harmonic functions \(\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}\) are real-analytic (`Analysis/Complex/Harmonic/Analytic.lean`). A global conjugate on a simply connected domain is not stated.

**Mean values and the Poisson formula.** A holomorphic function continuous on a closed disc has its circle average equal to its value at the center, and for \(w\) inside the disc the circle average of \(\frac{z-c}{z-w}f(z)\) equals \(f(w)\) (`Analysis/Complex/MeanValue.lean`). The Herglotz–Riesz kernel \(((z-c)+(w-c))/((z-c)-(w-c))\) has real part equal to the Poisson kernel on the circle, and a holomorphic function continuous on the closed disc is the Poisson average of its boundary values (`Analysis/Complex/Poisson.lean`). Harmonic functions on \(\mathbb{C}\) with values in a real Banach space have the circle mean-value property, and real-valued ones satisfy the Poisson integral formula, when harmonic near the closed disc or harmonic inside and continuous on the closure (`Analysis/Complex/Harmonic/MeanValue.lean`, `Analysis/Complex/Harmonic/Poisson.lean`). A bounded harmonic function on the whole plane is constant (`Analysis/Complex/Harmonic/Liouville.lean`).

### TauCeti

**Mean values and maximum principles.** In a finite-dimensional real inner product space, a function harmonic near a closed ball equals its averages over the sphere and over the ball ([mean value](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/MeanValue/Basic.lean)); conversely a continuous function equal to its ball averages for arbitrarily small radii is harmonic (`harmonicOnNhd_iff_continuousOn_setAverage_ball_eq`, [converse](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/MeanValue/Converse.lean)). The weak and strong maximum principles for the Laplacian hold in every dimension ([weak](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Laplacian/WeakMaximumPrinciple.lean), [strong](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Laplacian/StrongMaximumPrinciple.lean)).

**Poisson solution, Herglotz representation, and Harnack.** The planar Poisson integral of integrable real boundary data is harmonic in the disc, and for continuous data it tends to the prescribed boundary value (`harmonicOnNhd_planarPoissonIntegral`, `tendsto_planarPoissonIntegral`, [planar Poisson integral](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Poisson/Integral.lean)); the Dirichlet problem on a Euclidean ball is solved in every dimension (`exists_harmonicOnNhd_ball_continuousOn_closedBall_eqOn_sphere`, [ball](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/PoissonIntegral/Ball.lean)), and uniqueness follows from the weak maximum principle. A holomorphic function on the disc with nonnegative real part is the Herglotz transform \(\int(z+w)/(z-w)\,d\mu(z)+i\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}F(0)\) of a finite positive measure on the circle, and conversely (`exists_isFiniteMeasure_eq_herglotzTransform_add`, [Herglotz](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Herglotz.lean)); uniqueness of the measure is not stated. The sharp planar Harnack inequality \(\frac{R-|w-c|}{R+|w-c|}u(c)\le u(w)\le\frac{R+|w-c|}{R-|w-c|}u(c)\) holds for nonnegative harmonic \(u\) on a disc (`harnack_inequality_center`, [planar Harnack](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Harnack/Planar.lean)), and a monotone family of harmonic functions on a preconnected open set that is bounded above at one point converges locally uniformly to a harmonic function (`tendstoLocallyUniformlyOn_iSup_of_monotone`, [Harnack convergence](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Harnack/Convergence.lean)).

**Subharmonic functions and Perron’s method.** Continuous subharmonic functions, defined by the local sub-mean-value inequality, satisfy the comparison principle against harmonic and superharmonic functions on bounded open sets (`TauCeti.SubharmonicOn`, [subharmonic](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/Subharmonic.lean)). Upper semicontinuous subharmonic functions, and the subharmonicity of \(\log|f|\) and \(|f|^p\), are not covered. On a bounded open set in a finite-dimensional real inner product space, the Perron solution of boundary data bounded above is harmonic (`harmonicOnNhd_perronSolution`, [Perron](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Perron/Basic.lean)); it attains the boundary value at every point admitting a barrier where the data are continuous, and the exterior sphere condition (logarithmic barrier in the plane) supplies barriers (`tendsto_perronSolution_of_isBarrier`, [barriers](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/PDE/Perron/Barrier.lean)). The converse (regular points admit barriers) and the planar criterion that no complementary component is a point are not stated, and the Dirichlet problem on Jordan domains is not derived.

**Reflection.** The Schwarz reflection principle is proved across the real axis for a function continuous on the closed upper part of a conjugation-symmetric open set, holomorphic above, and real on the axis ([real axis](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/Reflection/Principle.lean)), and transported to affine lines, analytic arcs given by biholomorphic charts, and circles ([lines](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/Reflection/Line.lean), [analytic arcs](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/Reflection/Arc.lean), [circles](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/Reflection/Circle/Principle.lean)). Odd reflection of harmonic functions vanishing on a hyperplane is proved in the weak (Sobolev) form across the boundary of a half-space ([harmonic reflection](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Harmonic/Reflection.lean)).

## Normal families and conformal mapping

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS40.md#normal-families-and-conformal-mapping).

### Mathlib

**Toward the Riemann mapping theorem.** The file `Analysis/Complex/RiemannMapping.lean` states that it contains partial results toward the Riemann mapping theorem, with a complete proof in community pull request `33505` being merged in stages. For an open simply connected \(U\subsetneq\mathbb{C}\) it proves that there is an injective holomorphic function on \(U\) with nonvanishing derivative whose image is not dense, and one carrying \(U\) into the open unit disc. Neither statement produces a bijection onto the disc.

**The disc, the half-plane, and Möbius maps.** The open unit disc is the open unit ball of \(\mathbb{C}\) with its multiplicative semigroup structure (`Analysis/Complex/UnitDisc/Basic.lean`). The group \(\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{R})\) acts on the upper half-plane by fractional linear transformations, extended to negative determinant by conjugation (`Analysis/Complex/UpperHalfPlane/MoebiusAction.lean`), and \(\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\) acts by isometries of the Poincaré distance (`Analysis/Complex/UpperHalfPlane/Metric.lean`). The group \(\mathrm{GL}(2,K)\) acts on the projective line `OnePoint K` by Möbius transformations (`OnePoint.instGLAction`, `Topology/Compactification/OnePoint/ProjectiveLine.lean`), and the fixed points of a matrix are the roots of its fixed-point polynomial (`Matrix.GeneralLinearGroup.fixpointPolynomial`, `LinearAlgebra/Matrix/GeneralLinearGroup/FinTwo.lean`). That these maps form the automorphism group of the Riemann sphere, and that they preserve circles and cross-ratios, is not stated.

### TauCeti

**Normal families.** A locally bounded family of holomorphic functions is equicontinuous ([normal families](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/NormalFamilies.lean)), and Montel's selection theorem is proved for locally bounded holomorphic sequences with proper complex normed target, with holomorphic locally uniform limits (`TauCeti.montel`, [Montel](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/Montel/Basic.lean)). Vitali's theorem upgrades pointwise convergence on a set with an accumulation point to locally uniform convergence for locally bounded sequences on a connected open set (`TauCeti.vitali`, [Vitali](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/Vitali.lean)).

**The Riemann mapping theorem.** Every nonempty simply connected open proper subset of \(\mathbb C\) is biholomorphic to the disc (`riemannMapping`, [existence](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/RiemannMapping/Existence.lean), [conformal form](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/RiemannMapping/Conformal.lean)). Two Riemann maps differ by a disc automorphism ([uniqueness](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/RiemannMapping/Uniqueness.lean)), and for a base point \(a\) there is exactly one with \(f(a)=0\) and \(f'(a)>0\) (`riemannMapping_normalized`, [normalization](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/RiemannMapping/Normalization.lean)). Any two simply connected proper plane domains are biholomorphic ([equivalence](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/RiemannMapping/Equivalence.lean)).

**Characterizations of simple connectivity.** For a connected open set, simple connectivity is equivalent to the existence of holomorphic square roots of nowhere-zero holomorphic functions (`isSimplyConnected_iff_hasHolomorphicSquareRoots`, [existence](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/RiemannMapping/Existence.lean), [square roots](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/SquareRoots.lean)). An open set without holes has every closed curve null-homologous, every holomorphic function a primitive, and every nowhere-zero holomorphic function a logarithm ([primitives](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Contour/Primitive.lean)), and a connected open set without holes is simply connected ([no holes](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/SimplyConnected.lean)). The converse implications through cycles and harmonic conjugates are not stated.

**Automorphisms, Möbius maps, and boundary correspondence.** Every biholomorphic self-map of the unit disc has the form \(z\mapsto u(z-a)/(1-\bar az)\) with \(|u|=1\) (`exists_forall_unitDisc_eq_unitDiscStandardAutomorphismEquiv`, [classification](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/UnitDisc/Automorphism/Classification.lean); [Moebius factors](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/Moebius.lean)). For two conformal maps of the upper half-plane onto a Jordan domain, the transition map is a disc automorphism in Cayley coordinates and the boundary correspondence preserves cross-ratios; three matching boundary points force equality ([cross-ratio](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Conformal/Jordan/CrossRatio.lean)). ## Standard global examples and analytic techniques

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS40.md#standard-global-examples-and-analytic-techniques).

### Mathlib

**The Gamma and Beta functions.** The complex Gamma function is defined by the Euler integral for \(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}z>0\) and continued by \(\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)\) (`Complex.Gamma_eq_integral`, `Complex.Gamma_add_one`, `Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Gamma/Basic.lean`); it is meromorphic on \(\mathbb C\) (`Analysis/Meromorphic/Complex.lean`), differentiable off the nonpositive integers, not continuous at each \(-n\), and \(z\Gamma(z)\to1\) as \(z\to0\) (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Gamma/Deriv.lean`). It has no zeros, satisfies \(B(z,w)=\Gamma(z)\Gamma(w)/\Gamma(z+w)\) for \(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}z,\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}w>0\), and satisfies the reflection formula \(\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\pi/\sin\pi z\) (`Complex.Gamma_ne_zero`, `Complex.Gamma_mul_Gamma_eq_betaIntegral`, `Complex.Gamma_mul_Gamma_one_sub`, `Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Gamma/Beta.lean`). The residues \((-1)^n/n!\) at \(-n\) for \(n\ge1\) are not stated. Stirling's formula is proved for \(n!\) (`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Stirling.lean`), not for complex \(\Gamma\) in a sector.

**The Weierstrass \(\wp\)-function.** For a period pair, \(\wp\) and \(\wp'\) are defined by locally uniformly convergent lattice sums; \(\wp\) is even, periodic, meromorphic on \(\mathbb C\) with a pole of order two at each lattice point, and satisfies \(\wp'^2=4\wp^3-g_2\wp-g_3\) (`PeriodPair.weierstrassP`, `PeriodPair.derivWeierstrassP_sq`, `Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Elliptic/Weierstrass.lean`). That every elliptic function is rational in \(\wp\) and \(\wp'\), and the residue and zero–pole counting constraints for elliptic functions, are not stated.

**The disc and the upper half-plane.** The Poincaré distance on the upper half-plane is \(2\mathop{\mathrm{arsinh}}\bigl(|z-w|/(2\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}} z\mathop{\mathrm{Im}} w})\bigr)\); it induces the Euclidean topology, hyperbolic balls are Euclidean balls, and \(\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\) acts by isometries (`Analysis/Complex/UpperHalfPlane/Metric.lean`). The actions are jointly continuous and that of \(\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\) is proper (`ProperAction.lean`), fixed points are classified (`FixedPoints.lean`), the measure \(dx\,dy/y^2\) is \(\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{R})\)-invariant (`Measure.lean`), and the half-plane is a complex manifold on which Möbius maps of positive determinant act holomorphically (`Manifold.lean`); all in `Analysis/Complex/UpperHalfPlane/`.
