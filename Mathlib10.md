## Measure-preserving maps and ergodicity

A map is measure-preserving when it is measurable and pushes the source measure onto the target measure. Preimages of null sets are null, and a null-measurable set has the same measure as its preimage. Composition and iteration preserve the property, as do addition and scaling of measures (`Dynamics/Ergodic/MeasurePreserving.lean`). A map is quasi-measure-preserving, or nonsingular, when it is measurable and the preimage of a null set is null.

If \(f\) preserves \(\mu\) and \(\mu(X)<n\,\mu(s)\) for a null-measurable \(s\), then some point of \(s\) returns to \(s\) at a positive time strictly less than \(n\). In particular, a self-map preserving a finite measure is conservative: a nonsingular self-map is conservative when every measurable set of positive measure contains a point that returns at some positive time (`Dynamics/Ergodic/Conservative.lean`). From conservativity one gets the stronger statements. For infinitely many times \(m\), the intersection of \(s\) with its preimage under \(f^{m}\) has positive measure. Poincaré recurrence: for a null-measurable \(s\), almost every point of \(s\) returns to \(s\) infinitely often, and almost every point whose orbit meets \(s\) at least once meets it infinitely often. The topological form, on a second-countable space whose open sets are measurable, says that almost every point visits each of its neighbourhoods infinitely often. An iterate of a conservative map is conservative, and the identity is conservative.

A self-map is pre-ergodic when every measurable strictly invariant set is almost empty or almost the whole space. On a probability space this is a zero–one law. It is ergodic when it is measure-preserving and pre-ergodic, and quasi-ergodic when it is nonsingular and pre-ergodic. For a quasi-ergodic map, almost invariance of a null-measurable set is enough. For an ergodic map and a set of finite measure, an almost inclusion of the set in its preimage, or of the preimage in the set, or of the image in the set, forces the set to be almost empty or almost full; on a finite measure space the finite-measure hypothesis is free (`Dynamics/Ergodic/Ergodic.lean`). Ergodicity passes to iterates in the pre-ergodic direction (ergodicity of an iterate implies pre-ergodicity of the map), is preserved by measure-preserving semiconjugacy, and is invariant under conjugation by a measure-preserving measurable equivalence. The inverse of an ergodic measurable equivalence is ergodic.

An a.e. strongly measurable function from the space into a nonempty metrizable space, almost invariant under a quasi-ergodic map, is almost everywhere constant. The same holds for a null-measurable function into a countably separated measurable space, and, under strict invariance and strict measurability, for a pre-ergodic map (`Dynamics/Ergodic/Function.lean`).

A finite measure is ergodic if and only if it is an extreme point of the invariant measures of the same total mass. In particular, a probability measure is ergodic if and only if it is an extreme point of the invariant probabilities. If a finite invariant measure is absolutely continuous with respect to a finite ergodic measure, it is a scalar multiple of that measure; if both are probabilities, they agree (`Dynamics/Ergodic/Extreme.lean`).

If \(\mu\) is a finite invariant measure and \(\nu\) is a σ-finite invariant measure, then both the singular part of \(\mu\) with respect to \(\nu\) and the absolutely continuous part are invariant. If both measures are finite, the Radon–Nikodym derivative \(d\mu/d\nu\) is almost everywhere invariant under the map (`Dynamics/Ergodic/RadonNikodym.lean`). The file notes that these finiteness hypotheses may not be optimal, and neither a weakening nor a counterexample is given.

## The circle and group actions

On the additive circle of circumference \(T>0\), with Haar measure, \(y\mapsto n\cdot y\) is ergodic whenever \(n\in\mathbb{Z}\) satisfies \(|n|>1\), and so is \(y\mapsto n\cdot y+x\) for any fixed \(x\). The argument is a Lebesgue density argument: a null-measurable set that is almost invariant under a family of rotations whose additive orders tend to infinity is almost empty or almost full (`Dynamics/Ergodic/AddCircle.lean`). Rotation by a fixed \(a\) is ergodic if and only if \(a\) has infinite additive order. Equivalently, the integer multiples of \(a\) are dense. For a real representative, those multiples are dense if and only if the ratio of the representative to the circumference is irrational (`Dynamics/Ergodic/AddCircleAdd.lean`).

An action of a group on a measure space is ergodic when the measure is invariant and every null-measurable set that is almost invariant under every group element is almost empty or almost full. For the iteration action of a measurable self-map, this agrees with ergodicity of the map (`Dynamics/Ergodic/Action/Basic.lean`). The left action of a group on itself is ergodic for any s-finite left-invariant measure, and the right action is ergodic for any s-finite right-invariant measure, assuming multiplication and inversion are measurable (`Dynamics/Ergodic/Action/Regular.lean`).

If a monoid acts continuously and ergodically on an \(\mathrm{R}_1\) space, and \(\mu\) is a finite inner-regular measure, then an element whose natural powers are dense in the monoid acts ergodically. The same holds for integer powers in a group. Thus if \(N\) acts continuously and ergodically and \(M\) acts minimally on \(N\), the induced action of \(M\) is ergodic. On a second-countable topological group, left multiplication by \(g\) is ergodic for a finite, nonzero, inner-regular, left-invariant measure if and only if the integer powers of \(g\) are dense. One direction needs only that the measure of every nonempty open set is positive. A continuous surjective endomorphism of a compact second-countable group is ergodic for Haar measure when the preimages of the identity under its iterates are dense (`Dynamics/Ergodic/Action/OfMinimal.lean`).

## Birkhoff sums and the mean ergodic theorem

The Birkhoff sum of an observable \(g\) along \(f\) is \(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} g(f^{k}x)\), and the Birkhoff average is that sum divided by \(n\). If \(g\circ f=g\) and \(n\neq 0\), the average equals \(g\). The average is almost invariant:
\[
A_n(fx)-A_n(x)=n^{-1}\bigl(g(f^{n}x)-g(x)\bigr).
\]
If the orbit of \(g\) is bounded in a normed space, the difference of the averages along the orbits of \(fx\) and of \(x\) tends to zero. At a fixed point the averages tend to \(g(x)\), because they equal \(g(x)\) for every positive time; a periodic-orbit version is marked as future work and is not proved. If \(f\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz and \(g\) is uniformly continuous, the averages are uniformly equicontinuous, and the set of points at which they converge to a prescribed continuous limit is closed (`Dynamics/BirkhoffSum/Basic.lean`, `Dynamics/BirkhoffSum/Average.lean`, `Dynamics/BirkhoffSum/NormedSpace.lean`). Quasi-measure-preserving maps preserve almost-everywhere equality of sums and of averages. The comments in these files say that the averages appear in ergodic theorems. No pointwise convergence theorem, and no \(L^{p}\) convergence theorem for a measure-preserving transformation, is proved there.

What is proved is the von Neumann theorem for a linear contraction (`Analysis/InnerProductSpace/MeanErgodic.lean`). Let \(f\) be a linear self-map of a normed space over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\), Lipschitz with constant \(1\), and let \(g\) be a continuous linear projection onto the fixed subspace of \(f\), equal to the identity on that subspace. If the range of \(f-\mathrm{id}\) is dense in the kernel of \(g\), then for every vector \(x\) the averages \(n^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f^{k}(x)\) converge in norm to \(g(x)\). On a Hilbert space, if \(f\) is continuous and linear with \(\|f\|\le 1\), those averages converge to the orthogonal projection of \(x\) onto the fixed subspace of \(f\).

## Følner filters

Let a group act on a measure space. A family of sets \(F_i\), indexed along a filter, is Følner when the sets are eventually measurable and of finite positive measure, and for every group element \(g\)
\[
\frac{\mu\bigl((g\cdot F_i)\triangle F_i\bigr)}{\mu(F_i)}\to 0.
\]
The constant family equal to the whole space is Følner when the measure is finite and nonzero. There is a maximal Følner filter on the measurable sets of finite positive measure, characterised by the same ratio tending to zero, and a family is Følner if and only if it tends to that filter. Along an ultrafilter, the densities \(\mu(s\cap F_i)/\mu(F_i)\) converge. If the underlying measure is invariant, this mean is invariant under the action, the whole space has mean one, and the mean is additive for a measurable set disjoint from another set. Consequently, a nontrivial Følner filter produces a finitely additive \(G\)-invariant probability, with values in the extended nonnegative reals: total mass one, invariant under the action, and additive when one of the two sets is measurable and the sets are disjoint (`MeasureTheory/Group/FoelnerFilter.lean`). The file uses the name amenable for this existence statement and says explicitly that amenability itself has not been defined.

## Topological dynamics

An action of a monoid is minimal when every orbit is dense. A nonempty closed invariant set is then empty or the whole space, and a compact set can be covered by finitely many translates of any nonempty open set (`Dynamics/Minimal.lean`). A minimal set, as a subset, is not defined. The action is topologically transitive when every pair of nonempty open sets has some translate of the first meeting the second, equivalently when the saturation of every nonempty open set is dense. Minimality implies topological transitivity (`Dynamics/Transitive.lean`).

A flow of an additive topological monoid on a space is a continuous monoid action. Invariant and forward-invariant sets, orbits, restriction to an invariant set, time reversal for a group, and semiconjugacy of flows are defined (`Dynamics/Flow.lean`). The ω-limit of a set along a filter is the intersection of the closures of the forward images. It is closed. On a compact space, the ω-limit of a nonempty set along a nontrivial filter is nonempty. For a flow, if translation along the monoid preserves the filter, the ω-limit is invariant (`Dynamics/OmegaLimit.lean`).

A point is periodic of period \(n\) when its \(n\)th iterate returns to it; the definition allows \(n=0\). The minimal period divides every period, the map is bijective on its periodic points, and the fixed-point set of a continuous self-map of a Hausdorff space is closed. If the iterates of \(x\) converge to \(y\) and the map is continuous at \(y\), then \(y\) is fixed (`Dynamics/PeriodicPts/Defs.lean`, `Dynamics/FixedPoints/Topology.lean`).

Topological entropy is the Bowen–Dinaburg entropy of a uniformly continuous setting, stated for an arbitrary subset of a uniform space and taking values in the extended reals. For an entourage \(U\), two orbit segments of length \(n\) are distinguished when some iterate leaves \(U\). If \(N(T,F,U,n)\) is the least cardinality of a dynamical cover of \(F\), the entourage entropy is
\[
h(T,F,U)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log N(T,F,U,n),
\]
and the entropy \(h(T,F)\) is the supremum of these quantities over entourages. A parallel definition uses a liminf. The two agree when \(F\) is invariant. The empty set has entropy \(-\infty\); a nonempty set has entropy at least \(0\). Each fixed entourage has finite entropy on a compact invariant set, which does not by itself make \(h(T,F)\) finite. The same supremum is obtained from dynamical nets. Entropy is monotone in the set, the entropy of a finite union is the maximum, a uniformly continuous semiconjugacy does not raise entropy, and the entropy of the restriction of the map to an invariant set equals the entropy of that set (`Dynamics/TopologicalEntropy/CoverEntropy.lean`, `Dynamics/TopologicalEntropy/NetEntropy.lean`, `Dynamics/TopologicalEntropy/Semiconj.lean`, `Dynamics/TopologicalEntropy/Subset.lean`). The comparison with dynamical balls in a metric space is left open.

A degree-one lift is a monotone map \(f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\) with \(f(x+1)=f(x)+1\). Its translation number exists and is independent of the base point:
\[
\tau(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f^{n}(x)-x}{n}.
\]
It is monotone in \(f\), additive on commuting maps, changes sign under inversion, and is unchanged by semiconjugacy in this class. For continuous \(f\), \(\tau(f)=m/n\) in lowest terms if and only if \(f^{n}(x)=x+m\) for some \(x\), and two bijective lifts with the same translation number are semiconjugate by a degree-one lift. The same semiconjugacy holds for two group actions by such lifts whenever corresponding elements have equal translation numbers (`Dynamics/Circle/RotationNumber/TranslationNumber.lean`). The file says that the continuous lift of an orientation-preserving circle homeomorphism is not yet formalised, so the rotation number as a class in \(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\) is not constructed. Semiconjugacy of a circle homeomorphism of irrational rotation number to the corresponding translation is listed as future work, not as a theorem.

On a left-cancellative monoid, configurations are maps from the monoid to an alphabet, with the product topology and the left shift. Cylinders are clopen when the alphabet is discrete, and closed when it is \(T_1\). A subshift is a closed shift-invariant set of configurations. A pattern is a configuration that takes a default value off a finite support. Forbidding a family of patterns defines a subshift when the alphabet is discrete (`Dynamics/SymbolicDynamics/Basic.lean`). A finite forbidden family is allowed by this construction. The module comment names “subshift of finite type”, but no separate predicate for it is declared.

## Additional coverage in TauCeti

The following additions were checked in TauCeti revision `6e53de0d3ce9`; see the [revision, build evidence, and comparison scope](TauCetiCoverage.md). Source links below are pinned to that revision.

For a measure-preserving transformation, TauCeti specializes the Hilbert-space mean ergodic theorem to the Koopman operator on vector-valued \(L^2\): the averages converge in \(L^2\) to the orthogonal projection onto the fixed subspace ([mean ergodic theorem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6e53de0d3ce9ea24d7d487e656ac4590b0c45b3a/TauCeti/Probability/Ergodic/MeanErgodic.lean)). It then identifies that projection with conditional expectation on the invariant σ-algebra. The representative-level conditional-expectation statement assumes integrability and σ-finiteness of the trimmed measure, automatic in the finite-measure setting ([identification](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/6e53de0d3ce9ea24d7d487e656ac4590b0c45b3a/TauCeti/Probability/Ergodic/CondExpProjection.lean)). This fills the measure-preserving \(L^2\) specialization omitted in the Mathlib survey, but is not the almost-everywhere Birkhoff theorem or a general \(L^p\) theorem.

## Topics of Section 10 not found in either inspected library

- The pointwise Birkhoff ergodic theorem, in \(L^{1}\) or almost everywhere. The sums and averages are defined, and the norm convergence that is proved is the von Neumann theorem for a linear contraction.
- A general \(L^p\) mean ergodic theorem for measure-preserving transformations beyond the implemented \(L^2\) specialization and its conditional-expectation identification.
- Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy, and any entropy of a measure-preserving transformation. A variational principle was not found. Topological entropy, in uniform spaces, is proved.
- Multiple recurrence. Bergelson’s intersectivity lemma is proved for a finite measure space (`MeasureTheory/Function/Intersectivity.lean`); the file says that the ergodic theorem would give Bergelson’s refinement of Poincaré recurrence, and that argument is not given.
- A definition of amenability. A nontrivial Følner filter is shown to yield an invariant finitely additive probability.
- The rotation number of a circle homeomorphism, as opposed to the translation number of a degree-one lift of the line. The continuous lift is not constructed, and semiconjugacy to an irrational rotation is not proved.
- A dedicated theory of subshifts of finite type and their entropy. Finite forbidden families are already covered by the general subshift construction.
- Minimal sets of an action. Minimality of the whole action is defined.
- The identification of dynamical entourages with dynamical balls in a metric space.
