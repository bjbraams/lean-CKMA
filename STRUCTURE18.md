## Bochner integration, duality, and differentiation

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS18.md#bochner-integration-duality-and-differentiation).

### Mathlib

**The Bochner integral and dominated convergence.** The Bochner integral extends Lebesgue integration to Banach-valued functions by passing through integrable equivalence classes. A function is integrable when it is almost everywhere strongly measurable with finite integral of the norm; a function that is not integrable is assigned integral zero. On integrable functions the integral is linear, and its norm is at most the integral of the norm. The construction and the norm inequality are stated in [Section 04](STRUCTURE04.md) (`MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean`).

Dominated convergence: almost-everywhere strongly measurable functions with values in a real normed space, dominated in norm by one integrable real function and convergent almost everywhere, have integrals convergent to the integral of the limit. The same conclusion holds along a countably generated filter, and for a series whose norms are dominated by a summable family of integrable functions (`MeasureTheory/Integral/DominatedConvergence.lean`). Under the same domination, \(\int\|f_n-f\|\,d\mu\to0\) (`tendsto_lintegral_norm_of_dominated_convergence`, `MeasureTheory/Function/L1Space/HasFiniteIntegral.lean`); for \(1\le p<\infty\), convergence in \(L^p\) is proved in Vitali's form, under uniform integrability and tightness rather than domination (`tendsto_Lp_of_tendsto_ae`, `MeasureTheory/Function/UnifTight.lean`). Fubini's theorem holds for Banach-valued integrands with respect to s-finite measures: integrable functions on the product have iterated integrals in both orders equal to the product integral (`integral_prod`, `integral_integral_swap`, `MeasureTheory/Integral/Prod.lean`); the isometric identification \(L^p(S;L^p(T;X))\cong L^p(S\times T;X)\) is not formalized.

A continuous linear map between Banach spaces over \(\mathbb{R}\) or \(\mathbb{C}\) passes inside the Bochner integral of an integrable function (`ContinuousLinearMap.integral_comp_comm`). A continuous semilinear map does as well when both spaces are complete and the scalar homomorphism commutes with real scalars (`ContinuousLinearMap.integral_comp_commSL`). For a fixed continuous semilinear map, the integral of the image of an \(L^1\) class depends continuously on that class; that continuity statement does not add a completeness hypothesis on the codomain. When the target is complete, evaluation of an integrable map with values in continuous linear operators passes inside the integral (`ContinuousLinearMap.integral_apply`, `MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/ContinuousLinearMap.lean`).

**Strong measurability.** Pettis's measurability theorem is proved in the form used by Hytönen–van Neerven–Veraar–Weis with Borel measurability in place of weak measurability: a function into a pseudometrizable space is strongly measurable if and only if it is measurable and has separable range, and almost everywhere strongly measurable if and only if it is almost everywhere measurable and almost everywhere separably valued (`stronglyMeasurable_iff_measurable_separable` in `MeasureTheory/Function/StronglyMeasurable/Basic.lean`, `aestronglyMeasurable_iff_aemeasurable_separable` in `MeasureTheory/Function/StronglyMeasurable/AEStronglyMeasurable.lean`). Mathlib has no version in which measurability is tested only against continuous linear functionals.

**Banach-valued function spaces and duality.** The Lebesgue–Bochner spaces \(L^p(\mu;E)\) are the \(L^p\) spaces of [Section 04](STRUCTURE04.md) with a Banach space \(E\) of values: they are complete for \(1\le p\le\infty\) (`Lp.instCompleteSpace`, `MeasureTheory/Function/LpSpace/Complete.lean`), and simple functions are dense for \(p<\infty\) (`Lp.simpleFunc.isDenseEmbedding`, `MeasureTheory/Function/SimpleFuncDenseLp.lean`). A continuous bilinear map induces the Hölder pairing of \(L^p\) spaces, and for Hölder conjugate exponents the integration pairing gives a bounded linear map \(L^p(\mu;E^*)\to L^q(\mu;E)^*\) (`ContinuousLinearMap.lpPairing`, `MeasureTheory/Function/Holder.lean`). That this map is isometric, and the question of its surjectivity, are not formalized.

**Conditional expectation.** Conditional expectation onto a sub-σ-algebra (with σ-finite trimmed measure) is defined for functions with values in a real Banach space. It is characterized by its integrals over measurable sets (`ae_eq_condExp_of_forall_setIntegral_eq`), it is contractive on \(L^p\) for \(1\le p\le\infty\) (`eLpNorm_condExp_le_eLpNorm`, `MeasureTheory/Function/ConditionalExpectation/Real.lean`), and it commutes with continuous linear maps (`ContinuousLinearMap.comp_condExp_comm`, `MeasureTheory/Function/ConditionalExpectation/Basic.lean`). See also [Section 06](STRUCTURE06.md).

**Differentiation.** Lebesgue's differentiation theorem holds for locally integrable functions with values in a normed group: for a Vitali family on a second-countable metric space with a locally finite measure, almost every point satisfies \(\fint_a\|f(y)-f(x)\|\,d\mu(y)\to0\) as \(a\) shrinks to \(x\) (`VitaliFamily.ae_tendsto_average_norm_sub`, `MeasureTheory/Covering/Differentiation.lean`); for a uniformly locally doubling measure, such as Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb R^d\), the same holds along closed balls (`IsUnifLocDoublingMeasure.ae_tendsto_average_norm_sub`, `MeasureTheory/Covering/DensityTheorem.lean`). For locally integrable \(f:\mathbb R\to E\), \(E\) a real Banach space, the indefinite integral \(x\mapsto\int_c^x f\) is differentiable with derivative \(f(x)\) at almost every \(x\) (`LocallyIntegrable.ae_hasDerivAt_integral`, `IntervalIntegrable.ae_hasDerivAt_integral`, `MeasureTheory/Integral/IntervalIntegral/LebesgueDifferentiationThm.lean`).

### TauCeti

**Weak measurability.** For a separable inner product space \(E\), \(h\colon\alpha\to E\) is almost everywhere strongly measurable exactly when every \(t\mapsto\langle v,h(t)\rangle\) is (`TauCeti.aestronglyMeasurable_iff_forall_inner`); for a separable Hilbert space \(E\), a function into \(E^*\) is a.e. strongly measurable in operator norm exactly when every \(t\mapsto g(t)v\) is (`TauCeti.aestronglyMeasurable_strongDual_iff_forall_apply`, [Pettis](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/StronglyMeasurable/Pettis.lean)). This is Pettis's measurability theorem for separable Hilbert targets, not for general Banach spaces.

**Duality.** For scalar-valued functions (over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\)) and Hölder conjugate exponents, the \(L^q\) norm is bounded by the supremum of the pairings \(|\int hg|\) over simple \(g\) in the unit ball of \(L^{q'}\): for every \(q<\infty\) on any measure space, and for \(q=\infty\) on a σ-finite one. For functions with values in an inner product space a version for \(q<\infty\) is proved against bounded test functions of finite-measure support (`MeasureTheory.MemLp.eLpNorm_le_of_forall_enorm_integral_mul_le`, `MeasureTheory.AEStronglyMeasurable.eLpNorm_le_of_forall_enorm_integral_inner_le`, [Lᵖ duality](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/Lp/Duality.lean)). This is the norming half of \(L^{q'}\hookrightarrow(L^q)^*\) in the scalar and Hilbert cases.

**Convolution and approximate identities.** On a finite-dimensional real normed space with an additive Haar measure, convolution with an integrable real kernel \(k\) is a continuous linear operator on \(L^p(\mu;F)\), \(1\le p<\infty\), \(F\) a real normed space, with Young's bound \(\|k*f\|_p\le\|k\|_1\|f\|_p\) (`TauCeti.norm_convolutionLp_le`, [convolution](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/Lp/Convolution.lean)). Normalized smooth bump averages have norm at most one on \(L^p\) and converge strongly to the identity for \(1\le p<\infty\) and values in a real Banach space (`TauCeti.norm_normedBumpLp_le_one`, `TauCeti.tendsto_normedBumpLp`, [Banach-valued approximate identities](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/Lp/ApproximateIdentity.lean)). The exponent \(p=\infty\) and the general Young exponent relation are not covered.

## Vector measures and the Radon–Nikodým property

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS18.md#vector-measures-and-the-radonnikodým-property).

### Mathlib

A vector measure is a countably additive map from a σ-algebra into a topological additive monoid. Signed measures are the real-valued case. Hahn's decomposition, the Jordan decomposition into a unique pair of mutually singular finite measures, and the Radon–Nikodym theorem for a signed measure absolutely continuous with respect to a σ-finite measure are proved and are recorded in [Section 04](STRUCTURE04.md) (`SignedMeasure.withDensityᵥ_rnDeriv_eq`; `MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/Decomposition/Hahn.lean`, `MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/Decomposition/Jordan.lean`, `MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/Decomposition/RadonNikodym.lean`). Transport of a Banach-valued density along a scalar density is proved as well (`withDensityᵥ_smul_eq_withDensityᵥ_withDensity`, `MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/WithDensity.lean`). It is not a Radon–Nikodym theorem for a general Banach-valued vector measure.

The variation of a vector measure is the supremum of sums of norms over measurable partitions, and it dominates the norm of the measure of each set (`MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/Variation/Basic.lean`). For a vector measure with values in a real normed space, not necessarily complete, the semivariation of a set is the supremum of the variations of the real pushforwards by continuous linear forms of norm at most \(1\). The norm of \(\mu(s)\) is at most the semivariation of \(s\), which is at most the variation (`enorm_apply_le_semivariation`, `semivariation_le_variation`). Any number strictly below the semivariation of a measurable set is strictly below twice the norm of the measure of some measurable subset (`exists_subset_lt_enorm_apply_of_lt_semivariation`). The semivariation of the whole space is finite, so \(\|\mu(s)\|\) is bounded independently of \(s\) (`VectorMeasure.bound`, `norm_apply_le_bound`). The comparison uses the norm-controlled dual vector from the Hahn–Banach theorem (`MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/Variation/Semivariation.lean`). Relative weak compactness of the range, control measures, and the Orlicz–Pettis theorem are not formalized.

An \(E\)-valued function that is integrable with respect to the total variation of an \(F\)-valued vector measure may be integrated through a continuous bilinear pairing \(E \times F \to G\), provided \(G\) is complete. The integral is \(G\)-valued, with set integrals modelled on the Bochner API (`MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/Integral.lean`, `MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/SetIntegral.lean`). The vector measure with density \(f\) with respect to a vector measure \(\mu\) through such a pairing has variation at most the measure with density \(\|f\|\) with respect to the variation of \(\mu\), with equality when the pairing satisfies \(\|B(x,y)\|=\|x\|\,\|y\|\), as scalar multiplication does (`variation_withDensity`, `variation_withDensity'`, `MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/WithDensityVec.lean`). The product of two vector measures through a pairing exists when one of them has finite variation, and Fubini theorems hold when both do (`MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/Prod.lean`). The total variation distance \(|\mu-\nu|(X)\) between vector measures is an extended distance (`VectorMeasure.etvDist`, `MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/TotalVariation.lean`).

A function of bounded variation on a dense linear order with compact segments, with values in a complete normed group, has an associated Stieltjes vector measure giving \([a,b]\) the mass \(f(b^+)-f(a^-)\); its variation is finite and controlled by the variation of \(f\) (`BoundedVariationOn.vectorMeasure`, `MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/BoundedVariation.lean`). Integration by parts \(\int_a^b f\,dg=f(b)g(b)-f(a)g(a)-\int_a^b g\,df\) is proved for such measures, with the four interval conventions accounting for one-sided limits (`MeasureTheory/VectorMeasure/IntegrationByParts.lean`).

Lipschitz maps between finite-dimensional real spaces are differentiable almost everywhere (Rademacher's theorem, `LipschitzWith.ae_differentiableAt` in `Analysis/Calculus/Rademacher.lean`; `LipschitzWith.ae_differentiableAt_real` in `Analysis/BoundedVariation.lean`); this is the finite-dimensional instance of the Lipschitz characterization of the Radon–Nikodým property. The martingale convergence theorems are stated for real-valued processes: an \(L^1\)-bounded real submartingale converges almost surely, a uniformly integrable one also converges in \(L^1\), and a uniformly integrable martingale is the conditional expectation of its limit (`Submartingale.ae_tendsto_limitProcess`, `Submartingale.tendsto_eLpNorm_one_limitProcess`, `Martingale.ae_eq_condExp_limitProcess`, `Probability/Martingale/Convergence.lean`). The limit of an \(L^p\)-bounded submartingale lies in \(L^p\) (`Submartingale.memLp_limitProcess`); convergence in \(L^p\) norm is not stated. Banach-valued martingale convergence is not formalized.

## Fourier analysis and randomized Littlewood–Paley theory

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS18.md#fourier-analysis-and-randomized-littlewoodpaley-theory).

### Mathlib

For a finite-dimensional real inner product space \(E\) and a complex Hilbert space \(F\), the Fourier transform extends from Schwartz functions to a linear isometry equivalence of \(L^2(E;F)\), with Mathlib's normalization \(e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\) (`MeasureTheory.Lp.fourierTransformₗᵢ`, `MeasureTheory.Lp.norm_fourier_eq`, `Analysis/Fourier/LpSpace.lean`). Bessel potential spaces \(H^{s,p}\) of tempered distributions are defined (`TemperedDistribution.MemSobolev` in `Analysis/Distribution/Sobolev.lean`, bundled in `Analysis/FunctionalSpaces/BesselPotentialSpace.lean`).

### TauCeti

**Calderón–Zygmund theory.** Let \(T:L^2(\mathbb R^n;E)\to L^2(\mathbb R^n;F)\) be bounded, \(E\) a real Banach space and \(F\) a real normed space, and suppose \(T\) satisfies the Calderón–Zygmund cancellation condition, for instance because it is represented off the support of compactly supported functions by a kernel \(K(x,y)\in\mathcal L(E,F)\) satisfying Hörmander's condition. Then \(T\) is of weak type \((1,1)\) and, by Marcinkiewicz interpolation, satisfies \(\|Tf\|_p\le C_p\|f\|_p\) for \(1<p<2\) and \(f\in L^2\), with explicit constants (`ContinuousLinearMap.mul_volume_lt_enorm_le_of_setLIntegral_compl_closedBall_le`, `ContinuousLinearMap.eLpNorm_le_of_hormander`; [weak type](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Integral/CalderonZygmund/WeakType.lean), [strong type](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Integral/CalderonZygmund/StrongType.lean)). For real Hilbert spaces \(H,K\) in place of \(E,F\), the cancellation condition for the adjoint gives \(2<p<\infty\) by duality, and when both \(T\) and \(T^*\) satisfy it, \(T\) extends to a bounded operator \(L^p(\mathbb R^n;H)\to L^p(\mathbb R^n;K)\) for every \(1<p<\infty\), agreeing with \(T\) on \(L^2\cap L^p\) (`ContinuousLinearMap.exists_strongTypeExtension_of_setLIntegral_compl_closedBall_le`, [extension](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Integral/CalderonZygmund/Extension.lean)). Versions for standard kernels in the Euclidean distance on a finite-dimensional inner product space are in [inner product spaces](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Integral/CalderonZygmund/InnerProductSpace.lean). The initial bound is always on \(L^2\); a general exponent \(p_0\) is not allowed.

**Mihlin–Hörmander theorem.** On a finite-dimensional real inner product space \(V\) of dimension \(n\ge1\), if a scalar symbol \(m\), smooth away from the origin, satisfies \(\|D^km(\xi)\|\le A|\xi|^{-k}\) for \(k\le N\) with \(2N>n\), then the \(L^2\) Fourier multiplier \(T_m\) on functions with values in a complex Hilbert space \(F\) satisfies \(\|T_mf\|_p\le C\,A\,\|f\|_p\) for \(1<p<\infty\) and \(f\in L^2\) (`TauCeti.exists_eLpNorm_fourierMultiplierCLM_le_of_norm_iteratedFDeriv_le`). For symbols of temperate growth, the tempered-distribution multiplier maps every \(f\in L^p(V;F)\) to an \(L^p\) function with the same bound (`TauCeti.exists_fourierMultiplierCLM_eq_and_norm_le_of_norm_iteratedFDeriv_le`, [Mihlin–Hörmander](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/MihlinHormander.lean)). The symbols are scalar and the value spaces are Hilbert spaces; there is no operator-valued multiplier theorem, no \(R\)-boundedness, and no UMD condition. The dyadic Littlewood–Paley decomposition of the symbol is used in the proof ([Littlewood–Paley pieces](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Distribution/SchwartzSpace/LittlewoodPaley.lean)), but no Littlewood–Paley square-function equivalence is proved.

**Sobolev and Bessel potential spaces.** For \(1<p<\infty\) and every natural \(k\), a real-valued \(L^p\) function on \(\mathbb R^n\) lies in the Bessel potential space \(H^{k,p}\) exactly when it is the value of an element of the weak-derivative Sobolev space \(W^{k,p}\) (`MeasureTheory.Lp.memSobolev_natCast_iff_exists_wkp_value_eq`, [Calderón's theorem](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/BesselPotential/Basic.lean)).

## Laplace transforms, functional calculus, and maximal regularity

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS18.md#laplace-transforms-functional-calculus-and-maximal-regularity).

### Mathlib

Complex analysis in Mathlib is largely Banach-valued ([Section 40](STRUCTURE40.md)). For a complex Banach space \(E\), a complex-differentiable \(f:U\to E\) satisfies the Cauchy integral formula and has a convergent power series on every disc in \(U\) (`DifferentiableOn.hasFPowerSeriesOnBall`, `Analysis/Complex/CauchyIntegral.lean`); bounded entire maps are constant (`Differentiable.apply_eq_apply_of_bounded`, `Analysis/Complex/Liouville.lean`); and locally uniform limits of holomorphic maps are holomorphic (`TendstoLocallyUniformlyOn.differentiableOn`, `Analysis/Complex/LocallyUniformLimit.lean`). The characterization of holomorphy by weak holomorphy (Dunford) is not formalized. The Laplace transform of Banach-valued functions, sectorial operators, and the holomorphic functional calculus are absent.

### TauCeti

**Evolution spaces.** For a Gelfand triple \(V\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow V^*\), the space of \(u\in L^2(a,b;V)\) with \(u'\in L^2(a,b;V^*)\) is a Banach space with a bounded linear map to \(C([a,b];H)\) giving the continuous representative, and the energy identity \(\|u(t)\|^2-\|u(s)\|^2=2\int_s^t\langle u',u\rangle\) holds for it (`TauCeti.EvolutionSpace.toContinuousMap`, `TauCeti.EvolutionSpace.norm_sq_sub_norm_sq_eq_integral`, [evolution space](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/EvolutionSpace/Basic.lean)), together with integration by parts ([integration by parts](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/5cdecf40e99d5a5ad0833afff31f5b007d68a149/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/EvolutionSpace/IntegrationByParts.lean)). Lions' representation theorem ([Section 15](STRUCTURE15.md)) gives weak solutions of parabolic equations in this setting. Strict convexity of \(L^p(\mu;E)\), \(1<p<\infty\), for strictly convex \(E\) is recorded in [Section 15](STRUCTURE15.md).

TauCeti's Banach-space evolution theory, with strongly continuous semigroups, generators, generation theorems, and unique mild and classical solutions of the associated abstract Cauchy problem, is described in [Section 24](STRUCTURE24.md). TauCeti's Laplace transforms are those of positive measures on \([0,\infty)\), used for Bernstein's theorem on completely monotone functions; they are scalar.

**Synopsis entries.** Partly covered: 18.53 (Banach-valued Cauchy formula, power series, Liouville and Vitali-type convergence; not Dunford's weak characterization). Not covered: 18.54–18.56, 18.58–18.60, 18.65, 18.66.
